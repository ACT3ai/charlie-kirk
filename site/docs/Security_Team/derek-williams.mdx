## The correction: not Chester Barnes

The man in the **hat and plaid shirt** standing on Charlie Kirk's protective detail on
**September 10, 2025** was earlier identified on air as **Chester (Claude) Barnes** of
[Integrity Security Solutions](./integrity-security). That identification was **wrong**,
and **[Candace Owens](/Influencers/podcasts-candace-owens)** has corrected it **on the
record**.

Per that correction, the identification came in through her **tips inbox**: the man in the
hat and plaid is **Derek "Pepper" Williams**. He goes by **Pepper**.

This site treats the correction the same way it treats the original claim — as an
**attributed identification**, not a finding. What changed is which name is attached to a
figure in a photograph. Nothing about the correction says that either man did anything
wrong. Chester Barnes appears to have been **misidentified**, and the fair reading of that
is stated plainly on [Security Team](./overview): **he may well not have been there at
all.**

## Reported background

Two things are reported about Williams, both of them lawful occupations:

- **Naval Special Warfare.** He is described as a **former Navy SEAL**.
- **Perimeter Global Logistics (PGL).** After the teams, he is reported to have gone to
  PGL — a **Texas-based government defense contractor** that moves **freight across the
  border** and handles **military shipping for the Department of Defense**.

Cross-border freight and DoD shipping are ordinary contract work performed by many firms.
Naming an employer here is a **biographical detail**, not an allegation, and no inference
about the assassination should be drawn from either line.

## What is still open

1. Who confirms the identification? The tips inbox is a single, unnamed source. A firm
   roster, a credential list, or a photograph with provenance would settle it.
2. Was Williams on the **paid roster** for September 10, or present in another capacity?
   Appearing in a frame near the detail is not the same as being contracted to it.
3. What is the actual **date range** of his reported PGL employment, and was he there in
   September 2025 or before it?
4. Where did the original **Chester Barnes** label come from, and how many other names on
   the annotated group photograph came from the same source?
5. Does the corrected identification change any of the [screening and credential
   questions](./screening-credentials) already documented, or none of them?

## How the misidentification fits the wider pattern

Crowd-photo naming has produced repeated errors in this case. The
[Integrity Security Solutions](./integrity-security) page already lists a set of
**unverified** photo matches circulated on X — a light-blue-plaid figure, a
square-sunglasses figure, an older bearded figure — and says on its face that none of them
is confirmed by the firm or by law enforcement. The Barnes-to-Williams correction is
another instance of exactly that, and it arrived the way the others will have to: someone
who actually knew wrote in.

The lesson researchers draw from it is procedural rather than sinister. A **label on a
photograph is a lead**, and it stays a lead until a roster, a credential, or the person
themselves confirms it.
