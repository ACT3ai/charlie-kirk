## What This Image Shows

A screenshot of a post from an account shown as **Coyote of Wallstreet**, handle @YoteOfStreet. Below the text is an embedded low-resolution video player at 0:23 showing a man in a white t-shirt speaking, with tour banner lettering behind him.

The post reads: "What exploded underneath [Charlie](/People/charlie-kirk)'s t-shirt around the neckline? The effect of that explosion is what caused a laceration to his neck. Before the explosion is audibly heard, Charlie's t-shirt ripples at the neckline forming a visible shockwave!!"

This is the exploding-microphone argument in its compressed form, and it is one of the most consequential lines in the whole investigation. The claim rests on frame timing: if fabric moves before the report is audible, the energy source has to be closer than a distant rifle. This archive tracks the microphone hardware, the shirt movement analysis and the wound description in detail, including the government-side claim that a bullet's shockwave can arrive before the muzzle report, which investigators answer with the [Canon XA55 audio](/Mic/Canon_XA55_Audio) separating a louder event at the tent from the distant report.
