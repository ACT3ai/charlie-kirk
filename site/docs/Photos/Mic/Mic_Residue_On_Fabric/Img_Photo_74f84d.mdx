## What This Image Shows

This is an explanatory infographic on a pale grey background, titled “Likelihood of Explosive Residue Transfer &amp; Detectability on Synthetic Fibers”. Three panels sit side by side, each with a heading, a coloured rating pill and an upward arrow icon. The first, “TRANSFER”, is rated HIGH and illustrated with cupped hands, a detonation and a drifting particle cloud; its caption says handling or proximity to detonation readily transfers particles to synthetic surfaces and that fingerprint-level and post-blast residues are common. The second, “TRAPPING IN FIBERS”, also rated HIGH, shows a magnified circular inset of particles lodged between carpet fibres; its caption says nylon, polyester and auto carpet (typically nylon or polyester blends) are porous and fibrous so particles lodge between fibres and in the pile, giving better retention than many smooth surfaces. The third, “TESTABILITY / RECOVERY”, rated MEDIUM-HIGH, shows a pipette, flask and an HPLC trace; its caption says solvent extraction (acetone/methanol) with HPLC-UV or LC-MS recovers residues effectively, that nylon often shows higher recovery than cotton with polyester/auto carpet also workable, and that detection at low µg/g to ng levels is possible when properly sampled. A horizontal gradient bar beneath ranks Nylon (high recovery), Polyester / Auto Carpet (Good-High) and Cotton (Lower). A footnote states the graphic is based on forensic studies of post-blast and handling residues (RDX, PETN, TNT-related compounds) and that actual results depend on amount deposited, time, weathering, washing and extraction method, with outdoor or heavily soiled carpet reducing likelihood.
