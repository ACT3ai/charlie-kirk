## What This Image Shows

Active groundwork running along the base of the Sorensen Student Center, under the building's overhang and glass upper floors. Working left to right:

- A **tracked skid-steer loader** with its bucket raised, parked on the excavated surface at the left edge.
- A worker **screeding gravel** with a long-handled rake or lute, pulling material toward the camera across the prepared sub-base.
- A **second worker** standing further up the run, near the walkway.
- A **plate compactor** sitting mid-frame on the graded material.
- A **surveyor laser level on a tripod** set at the edge of the surviving lawn, with a yellow equipment case on the grass beside it.

A **trench line** runs along the grass margin on the right, and a windrow of loose aggregate sits along the left. The exposed ground extends the full length of the frame. An **SPN** watermark appears at lower right.

## Why Researchers Point To It

Two details drive the discussion. First, the **laser level**: grade-control equipment of that kind is used to set a precise engineered elevation across a run. Researchers reading this frame argue it depicts a **planned hardscape installation** with surveyed grades — a different category of work from an emergency biohazard wash-down. That framing matters because UVU text messages released through the Judicial Watch GRAMA request show that as late as **Friday, September 12, 2025**, the stated plan was to hose the area down and that *"we're not gonna need to cut out any sod."*

Second, the **depth of the excavation and the size of the machine**. A tracked loader and a compactor are equipment brought in for a substantial dig, not for surface cleaning.

The counter-reading is equally available in the frame and is recorded here: this is **exactly what competent paver installation looks like**. Merrell himself has described over-excavating as standard practice — *"to do pavers correctly you over-excavate, you get rid of the top… you want to get a good base."* A laser level is ordinary equipment on such a job.

## What It Does Not Establish

The photograph carries **no timestamp**, so it cannot by itself be placed inside the September 13–14 window. The workers are **not identifiable** at this resolution and are **not named** on this page; nothing here suggests any of them did anything other than the job they were hired to do. It is not established which of the two crews described in the record — the unidentified state crew that removed the soil, or the hardscape crew that laid the pavers — appears in this frame.
