## What This Image Shows

A vertical frame taken from the upper plaza level looking out across campus. A **mound of loose aggregate** — coarse gravel or road base — sits on the concrete apron in the middle distance. Someone has **circled it by hand in blue marker**; the annotation is the poster's, not part of the original photograph.

To the right of the mound, a **white flatbed dump truck** is parked with its bed empty. In the foreground, **stacked metal crowd-control barricades** lie on their sides against a low retaining wall, with more construction material piled behind them at left. Beyond the mound the **campus fountain** is running, light poles and trees line the walkway, and the Wasatch range fills the horizon under a partly clouded sky. An **SPN** watermark sits at lower right.

## Why Researchers Point To It

Of the three photographs in this set, this is the only one that shows **the material itself and a vehicle capable of moving it**. That combination is what the blue circle is calling attention to. The open question on [10 Inches of Dirt Removed Before the Pavers](/Tent/Dirt_Removed_Before_Pavers) is where roughly ten inches of courtyard soil went — no **soil-disposal manifest** or hauling record has entered the public record through the Judicial Watch GRAMA release or any other channel.

The **stacked barricades** are the second detail noted. Crowd-control barricades of that type were used for the September 10 event, and their presence stacked and set aside places the frame in the post-event cleanup period rather than during the event itself.

## What It Does Not Establish

The photograph **cannot distinguish between imported and excavated material.** A clean, uniformly graded mound of light-colored aggregate is far more consistent with **road base delivered for the paver installation** than with dark blood-soaked topsoil removed from under the tent — and the truck bed is empty, so nothing in the frame shows material being hauled away. Readers should treat the "this is the removed soil" interpretation as **unsupported by the image itself**.

There is **no timestamp**, no visible license plate or company marking on the truck, and no person in frame. Nothing here identifies who ordered, delivered, or removed any material, and no court has made a spoliation finding in this matter.
