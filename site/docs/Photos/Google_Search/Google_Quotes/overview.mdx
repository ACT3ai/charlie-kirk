## About This Cluster

These screenshots are quoted-string searches — the search term is wrapped in quotation marks so Google Trends measures only the exact phrase, not loose word matches. The strongest of them tests the Egyptian aircraft tail number [**SU-BTT**](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) in Israel across a custom June 11 to September 1, 2025 window, and it spikes on exactly **July 20, 2025**, the date researchers treat as the anchor for the wider search-pattern argument.

The other captures run personal names taken from unsourced crew lists that circulated online for [**N1098L**](/Planes/N1098L/overview). The crew of that aircraft is not public, those lists carry no documentation, and this site does not repeat them or identify anyone as having been aboard. Common personal names produce search interest for reasons that have nothing to do with this case, and Trends reports relative interest inside a bucket rather than raw volume, so a single spike against a near-empty baseline can represent a very small number of queries.

Open the **[SU-BTT capture](/Photos/Google_Search/Google_Quotes/Img_BTT_a7fd5b)** first — a six-character aircraft registration is not an ordinary search string. For the written analysis, see the [planes search patterns](/GoogleSearches/planes-search-patterns) page, the [July 20, 2025 anchor](/GoogleSearches/july-20-2025-anchor), and the aircraft sections at [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) and [N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview).
