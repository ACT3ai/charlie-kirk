## Overview
This page describes the role of **photographs and still images** in the [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) investigation. It includes reference to official photos, media images, and pictures captured by attendees, and explains how they are used to understand the scene, movements, and claims about the handling of physical items. Because many photos are sensitive and subject to legal or privacy constraints, not all images are publicly available.

## Types of photos

Photographs relevant to the case include:

- **Event and media photos** – Still images from professional photographers or media outlets covering the event, capturing the stage, crowd, and surrounding environment at different moments.
- **Eyewitness photos** – Pictures taken by attendees on phones or cameras, sometimes providing unique angles on specific individuals, vehicles, or details near the tent and courtyard.
- **Post‑incident site photos** – Images showing changes to the scene, such as landscaping, paving, or structural modifications, which are often cited in discussions about scene preservation and possible cover‑up.

These images can complement video by offering higher resolution or different perspectives.

## Uses and limitations

Photographs are used in several ways:

- To **document the layout** of the event site and positions of key people and objects at specific moments.
- To support or challenge **shooter‑location and trajectory theories** by showing sight lines, obstructions, and physical damage.
- To illustrate **before‑and‑after comparisons** of the site where physical changes may have occurred.

However, still images have limitations: they capture only a moment in time, can be subject to perspective distortion, and may lack reliable metadata if not preserved carefully.

## Access and sensitivities

Some photographs, especially those that are graphic or closely tied to medical findings, may be restricted or not released publicly due to:

- **Privacy, dignity, and legal protections** for the victim and family.
- Ongoing **investigative or evidentiary needs**, including chain‑of‑custody concerns.
- Platform or publication standards regarding sensitive content.

Where specific photos are discussed in this project, they are referenced descriptively rather than reproduced unless they are already widely public and appropriate to include.

## When originals stop existing

Everything above assumes the original file still exists somewhere. Often it does not, and the clearest account of how that happens comes from a witness who filmed the event on his own phone.

He says he sent his footage to the [FBI](/FBI/overview) as a tip because he thought he was helping. His contact escalated from a local field office to what he describes as the national level, and that contact called him back and advised him to erase the video from his phone — telling him, in his account, that it would only give him PTSD because the victim had been his friend. He says a second call came later asking him to confirm that he had erased it. That second call is what changed his mind. Instead of deleting the footage he began posting it and copying it "on many different hard drives, in many different places."

That account matters on a page about photographs and stills for a practical reason. The frames citizen investigators argue over — sight lines, the tent, the rooftop, trajectory geometry — are almost all screen-grabs pulled from footage that has been re-uploaded and re-compressed across several platforms. Each pass discards detail and strips metadata. When an original is deleted, what survives is not the evidence but a copy of a copy, and resolution disputes of exactly the kind described on this page become unresolvable.

This is one man's account of two phone calls. The FBI has not publicly addressed it, and there are ordinary welfare and evidence-handling explanations that fit the same described facts; nothing here asserts that any individual acted improperly or unlawfully.
