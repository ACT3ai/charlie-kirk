# Groyper Wars

The **"Groyper Wars"** (also **"Groyper War"** or **"the Groypening"**) were a coordinated questioning-and-heckling campaign by followers of **Nick Fuentes** — self-described **Groypers** or the **"Groyper Army"** — against [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk), [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview), and other mainstream conservative speakers during fall **2019**, with organizational spillover into **2020**. The name comes from **Groyper**, a rotund green-frog meme (a Pepe the Frog variant, often drawn with chin on interlocked fingers). Fuentes's fans were earlier called **"Nickers."** The term is often mis-transcribed as "Graper Wars."

Peak in-person disruption ran **October–December 2019** on TPUSA's **"Culture War"** campus tour and related events. A separate online **"Groyper War 2"** in **2024** targeted Trump campaign personnel and positions and is not the same as the 2019 campus campaign.

:::caution Date correction — this was 2019, not 2023
The campaign is sometimes misdated to **2023** in secondary retellings and in post-2025 social media threads. The documented in-person campus campaign against Kirk and TPUSA ran in **fall 2019**, with 2020 organizational aftermath. There is **no evidence in contemporary sources** of the main 2019 activity continuing as a major university campaign into 2023 — though online factional tension persisted continuously, and a separate digital/meme **"Groyper War 2"** ran in **2024** against Trump campaign elements.
:::

Fuentes and allies framed the campaign as exposing **"Conservative Inc.,"** **"Israel First"** loyalty, and insufficiently nationalist positions. Kirk, TPUSA, and their allies framed the participants as **white nationalists, antisemites, racists, and internet trolls**. Activity peaked **October–December 2019** across multiple campuses, with Fuentes livestreaming reactions, declaring sequential "victories," and directing followers via social media. **Written guides circulated** — including on **4chan** and in *Daily Stormer*-adjacent contexts — advising participants to dress well, stay polite and calm, and stick to a defined list of question topics; some versions explicitly warned against overt **"Jewish question"** phrasing in the room, in favor of coded or search-bait framings.

:::note Why this page is here
This is **pre-event background**, not evidence about September 10, 2025. The campaign is the origin of a durable right-wing factional dispute over Charlie Kirk's positions on **Israel, immigration, and LGBT inclusion** — fault lines that reappear in [Israel friction](/Israel/Charlie_Israel_Friction) and [motive analysis](/Motive/overview) years later. Nothing here asserts that any person or group from 2019–2020 had any connection to Charlie Kirk's death. After September 10, 2025, online speculation sometimes re-linked Groypers to the case; investigators have **not** publicly reported a connection between [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) and the Groyper movement, and Fuentes publicly disavowed armed action by followers.
:::

## Who the Groypers were (as reported)

Contemporaneous reporting and later encyclopedic summaries describe Groypers as a **loosely defined online/offline network** around Fuentes's *America First* show — not a formal membership organization with a roster. Mainstream and watchdog sources (ADL, Vox, NBC, Wikipedia synthesis) have characterized the movement as far-right, white nationalist, antisemitic, and homophobic. Fuentes and participants often self-described as **"America First,"** traditionalist, Christian, and nationalist, and rejected "alt-right" or "white nationalist" labels as left-wing smears. Both framings are **claims of the parties that made them**.

**Operational pattern (2019):**

* Attend TPUSA and allied campus events, usually during **Q&A**.
* Present as ordinary MAGA/Trump students (red hats, TPUSA-friendly pins) so organizers would not filter them out.
* Ask **leading questions** on Israel aid and loyalty, legal immigration and demographics, and LGBT conservatives in the movement.
* Extract short clips for **Twitter memes**, hashtags (e.g. `#dancingisraelis`), and Fuentes livestreams.
* If denied the mic, **heckle, boo, and chant** ("America First," "Q&A").

Guidance circulated on message boards and extreme sites (including posts Vox attributed to *Daily Stormer*) told attendees to stay calm and polite in line, and if ejected to shout phrases such as **"GOOGLE THE USS LIBERTY,"** **"GOOGLE DANCING ISRAELIS,"** **"AMERICA FIRST NOT ISRAEL FIRST,"** or **"NICK FUENTES."**

### The circulated guides (as reported)

Copies and paraphrases of the coordination material appeared on **4chan** boards, in Telegram channels, and in *Daily Stormer*-style write-ups. Recurring instructions:

* **Dress the part** — collared shirt, sport coat, or plain MAGA merchandise; look like an ordinary campus Republican, not a protester.
* **Be polite and calm** — no shouting while holding the mic; the optics of a well-dressed, soft-spoken questioner versus an agitated speaker were treated as the whole point.
* **Stick to the approved question set** — foreign aid to Israel, dual loyalty, legal immigration volume, demographic change, LGBT inclusion in the movement, the Ashley St. Clair severance.
* **Avoid overt "Jewish question" phrasing** in some versions — use policy framing or **search bait** ("Google X") instead of explicit statements that would let hosts label the questioner instantly.
* **Use memes and hashtags afterward** — the clip mattered more than the answer.
* **Saturate the line** — send enough people that the Q&A becomes majority-Groyper rather than a single hostile question.
* **If removed by security**, shout the standing phrases above so the ejection itself becomes content.

**Note on sourcing:** these guides are known primarily through **journalistic quotation and participant description**, not from an authenticated single master document. Treat the list as **reported practice**, consistent across multiple accounts of what happened in the rooms.

## Hybrid media: how Twitter and livestreams amplified campus nights

The campaign was designed as much for **online remediation** as for the room. Diggit Magazine's contemporaneous analysis (Ico Maly, Oct. 31, 2019) described Groyper Q&A as a **digital practice performed offline**:

* TPUSA livestreamed events; Groyper questions therefore reached Kirk's own audience.
* Fuentes dual-screened the official stream on **DLive**, adding live commentary. At Ohio State, Diggit reported roughly **~4,000** watching Fuentes's stream versus **~400** on the official TPUSA stream.
* Clips were cut within minutes and circulated on Twitter by Groyper accounts, many of which used anodyne bios ("Christian conservative") while coordinating harassment and talking points.
* Questions that sounded absurd offline (e.g. "dancing Israelis") pointed online viewers into **search data voids** full of conspiracy content.

NBC News (Ben Collins, Nov. 12, 2019) reported Fuentes building anticipation on stream when "his guys" reached the mic: **"Here we go. Here we go… Groyper time."** Harvard Shorenstein Center researchers Joan Donovan and Brian Friedberg, quoted by NBC, framed the goal as putting "shocking language around race… back on the table" after post-Charlottesville platform crackdowns left some far-right voices looking for new entry points into pro-Trump spaces.

## Immediate triggers (September–October 2019)

### Ashley St. Clair photo and TPUSA severance

In **September 2019**, TPUSA "brand ambassador" **Ashley St. Clair** was photographed at an event with Fuentes and others described in reporting as alt-right figures, including **Jacob Wohl** and **Anthime Gionet** ("Baked Alaska"). After **Right Wing Watch** publicized the images, TPUSA **severed ties** (reports cluster around **September 30, 2019**) and stated that **white nationalism is "abhorrent and un-American."**

Vox later quoted a source saying the St. Clair firing — not only ideology in the abstract — helped drive Fuentes's campaign, and that some chapter officers more aligned with Fuentes's views **resigned**. Kansas State University's TPUSA officers were reported to have quit en masse, saying the national group no longer reflected their conservative values. TPUSA's response, per that source, amounted to **"don't let the door hit you"** and insistence on a **"big tent"** message that excluded Fuentes's project.

### Politicon

At **2019 Politicon**, Fuentes tried to reach Kirk at TPUSA-linked events: waiting in photo lines and attempting to attend Kirk's debate with **Kyle Kulinski** (*The Young Turks*). Security repeatedly blocked him. Fuentes publicly claimed Kirk was **avoiding confrontation** because Kirk's positions could not survive open challenge. Kirk's side treated Fuentes as an extremist to be kept away from stages and students.

## Event-by-event: early/mid-October 2019 — the first infiltrations (Reno, GCU, Colorado State, Iowa, New Hampshire)

The tour's opening weeks are where the pattern was **invented and then scaled**. These stops get little mainstream coverage because nothing was shut down — but Fuentes's own retrospectives treat them as the ignition point.

### University of Nevada, Reno (~Oct 7, 2019)

Tour opener. No documented Groyper saturation. Establishes the format: Kirk keynote plus open-mic Q&A, livestreamed by TPUSA — the livestream being the vulnerability the campaign later exploited.

### Grand Canyon University / Phoenix (~Oct 21, 2019)

Early Q&A clips began circulating as viral-content targets. Phoenix is Fuentes-adjacent territory (TPUSA is headquartered in the Phoenix area), and period accounts treat this as one of the first stops where hostile-from-the-right questions were deliberately farmed for clips.

### Colorado State University, Fort Collins (~Oct 23, 2019) — the credited origin

Fuentes later **credited two early Groypers at Colorado** with kicking the campaign off — described in stream and follower shorthand by their online personas (a **"Rosary"/"Han"-style** account and a **"Patrick Bateman"**-type account; these are pseudonymous handles, not verified identities). Their questions set the two durable themes:

1. **Foreign aid to Israel** — why American money and diplomatic priority go abroad.
2. **Legal immigration** — not illegal immigration, which mainstream conservatives already opposed, but the **volume and composition of legal immigration**, including Kirk's support for stapling green cards to diplomas.

The choice to attack **legal** immigration was strategically deliberate: it put Kirk on ground where his answers would sound pro-immigration to a nationalist audience while sounding reasonable to a mainstream one.

### University of Iowa (~Oct 23–24, 2019)

Period accounts describe **four or more** Groyper questions at this stop — the first evidence the tactic could be repeated by strangers who had only seen it online. Fuentes's stream treated Iowa as proof of **replicability**.

### University of New Hampshire (~Oct 25, 2019)

Again **4+** Groyper questions reported. By this point the escalation curve was explicit in Fuentes's own framing: a handful of questions at Colorado, several at Iowa and New Hampshire, then the claimed **near-total Q&A saturation** at Ohio State a few days later. Diggit documented **Google Trends spikes** for Groyper-related search terms across the **Oct 25 – Nov 2** window, matching this ramp.

**Why the ramp matters:** it shows the campaign was not a single viral night. It was an iterated tactic with a feedback loop — stream the result, recruit more questioners, saturate a bigger room.

## Event-by-event: October 29, 2019 — Ohio State University

**Format:** Culture War event with co-host **Rob Smith** (Black, gay Iraq War veteran and TPUSA-aligned influencer).

**Numbers:** Reporting counted **11 of 14** audience questions as coming from Groypers/nickers — the first night widely treated as proof the campaign could dominate a TPUSA Q&A.

### Questions documented in period sources

1. **Demographic / "white European ideals"**  
   *"Can you prove that our white European ideals will be maintained if the country is no longer made up of white European descendants?"*  
   Kirk and allies treated this as a white-nationalist demographic frame; Groyper-aligned accounts treated it as exposing "civic nationalism."

   The question was built on a specific chain of premises the questioner did not state aloud: **Census projections** of a non-white-majority United States around **2045**; the observed **Democratic voting margins** among non-white voters; and therefore the claim that immigration levels determine political outcomes permanently. **Kirk called it a racist question**, rejected the premise outright, and pivoted to **Trump's improved performance with Black and Latino voters** relative to prior Republicans — the standard civic-nationalist answer that demographics are not destiny because persuasion works. Groyper-aligned accounts described him as **squirming and changing the subject**; Kirk-aligned accounts described him as **correctly refusing a racist frame**. Both readings come from the same clip.

2. **Homophobic question aimed at Rob Smith**  
   *"How does anal sex help us win the culture war?"*  
   Hosts and much of the room rejected it; the clip still circulated as Groyper content. The point of the question, in Groyper framing, was TPUSA's inclusion of gay speakers.

   Variants of the question reported from this and nearby stops framed **sexual liberation as a mechanism of political control**, or challenged the movement for treating **homosexuality as normative** — sometimes referencing prior on-stage advocacy for LGBT inclusion.

   **Rob Smith's response:** he called the question **BS, irrelevant, and bad faith**, and invoked his **military service** — a Black, gay Iraq War veteran being asked about anal sex at a conservative event was, in his framing, the entire tell about the questioners' motives.

   **Kirk's response:** he deferred to Smith initially, then said he **does not care what consenting adults do privately**, that **hyperfocus on gay sex is "weird,"** and that **America's Western values allow gays and lesbians to contribute to society**. He separately maintained that **marriage is between one man and one woman** while refusing to expel people from the coalition over personal values.

   Groyper-aligned commentary on the clip claimed the responses were **evasive**, and some posts insinuated the *asker* was closeted — an argument that circulated on both sides of the clip.

3. **"Dancing Israelis" question (full exchange as transcribed by Diggit)**  
   The questioner dressed as a TPUSA/MAGA supporter: red MAGA hat, **"I love ISR"** pin, **"socialism sucks"** pin. He opened by seeming to agree that a prior question was racist ("What a racist question that was"), then:

   > "I have a quick and fun, lighthearted question for you, Charlie. So, you gave a speech in Jerusalem earlier this year? Were there any awesome fun dancing parties that you guys hit afterwards? Cause I heard that Israelis are some of the best dancers in the world. I mean, if you don't believe me, Google 'Dancing Israelis,' – it is insane how good their dancing is. Would you agree or disagree with that?"

   **Kirk:** "Israel is a beautiful country, a great country too."  
   **Questioner:** "It is our greatest ally."  
   **Kirk:** "Correct."

   Online, "Google Dancing Israelis" pointed to long-running antisemitic 9/11 conspiracy material. Groyper Twitter pushed **`#dancingisraelis`** the next day. Diggit documented Google Trends spikes for Groyper-related searches in the Oct 25–Nov 2 window.

   **Why the "lighthearted" framing mattered:** the question was engineered so that any answer worked. Agreement produced a clip of Kirk endorsing a phrase tied to 9/11 conspiracy material; refusal produced a clip of Kirk refusing a "fun" question about dancing. The real payload was the **search instruction** delivered to the livestream audience.

4. **Ashley St. Clair severance / "blackballing" question**  
   Questioners pressed Kirk on the **September 2019 St. Clair firing** over a photograph taken in proximity to Fuentes:

   * Do you support **blackballing** people for controversial opinions, or for appearing in a photo in the same room as someone?
   * If you are the **pro-debate, free-speech** side, will you debate on **immigration, identity, and demographics**?

   **TPUSA's position:** internal personnel matters were resolved **privately**, and the organization's line was that **white nationalism is abhorrent and un-American** — association with figures promoting it was disqualifying for a brand ambassador. Groyper framing: this proved TPUSA operated a **guilt-by-photograph purge system** while advertising itself as the free-speech faction.

5. **Israel policy priority**  
   A direct-choice question: would Kirk support a policy that **benefits America to the detriment of Israel**? Kirk treated this as a **false choice**, holding that American and Israeli interests are broadly aligned and that the framing itself was the trap. Groypers treated the refusal to answer the hypothetical as an admission of **dual loyalty** — which was the purpose of constructing it as a forced binary.

### Framing goals at OSU (Groyper side, as summarized by Diggit)

Interventions aimed to paint Kirk as **"pro-Israel, anti-white, anti-American, loyal to a different country, anti-Christian, pro-drag queen and an anal sex supporting fake conservative."** All of those phrases are **participant/advocate characterizations**, not findings of this site.

### Response at OSU

Kirk, Smith, and TPUSA figures including **Benny Johnson** labeled the questioners **white supremacists and antisemites**. Reports described Kirk as rattled; TPUSA distanced itself from members who spoke sympathetically about Fuentes. Fuentes celebrated the night on stream as validation of the tactic.

### Fuentes's OSU stream claims

Fuentes streamed a celebration episode framed around **"Charlie Kirk DECISIVELY ROUTED"** and described the night as **"white-pilled,"** claiming:

* **Nearly 100% saturation** of the Q&A by America First questioners at some stops.
* A standing offer of **accountability**: answer the questions, or the next event gets disrupted instead.
* A **historic tipping point** — young nationalists rejecting mainstream conservatism in favor of European-heritage preservation and America First against globalism.
* **"Total victory"** language that would be reused, stop by stop, for the rest of the tour.

### Claimed TPUSA reaction after OSU (Groyper-side allegations)

Follower accounts and sympathetic posts claimed that in the days after Ohio State, TPUSA:

* **Reached out to influencers** to coordinate messaging against the movement.
* **Purged members** who had spoken about Fuentes politely or neutrally.
* Began building **photo databases of known questioners** for door screening at later events.

These are **unverified participant claims**. TPUSA's public posture was that it was excluding extremists and non-students, not policing curiosity.

### Twitter aftermath: "the Battle of OSU"

Period X posts show the vocabulary of the campaign being minted in real time:

* Posts noting the word **"Groyper"** spreading rapidly after Ohio State, and that **"the Groyper Wars started"** here.
* **"Battle of OSU"** framed as "based kings vs. Charlie Kirk."
* Claims of **humiliation** and of TPUSA **purging** members afterward.
* Repeated citation of Kirk's **green-card-to-diploma** position as irreconcilable with America First.
* Kirk-side and neutral posts comparing the tactic to **Antifa-style disruption** applied from the right.

## Event-by-event: November 8–9, 2019 — Arizona State / Dan Crenshaw

**Event title:** "Prove Me Wrong ft. Congressman Dan Crenshaw" (Tempe).

**Crenshaw's own characterization (email to Vox, Jane Coaston):** questions were **"almost exclusively about Israel."** He wrote that questions often began as **"a legitimate policy difference, perfectly debatable,"** then revealed **"deeper and darker"** motives with **"hints along the way."**

**USS Liberty:** Questioners repeatedly raised the **1967 Israeli attack on the USS Liberty** (34 Americans killed). Israel and the U.S. governments treated it as mistaken identity and paid reparations; far-right forums use it as a standing claim that Israel is not an ally. Period reporting (Vox citing Ellie Hall / BuzzFeed) treated Liberty and "dancing Israelis" questions as **antisemitic dog whistles** when used in this pattern.

**NBC (Collins):** After an antisemitic 9/11 reference, Crenshaw asked, **"What do you guys call yourselves? There's a name for your group, right?"** He got sarcastic answers, then chants of **"America First! America First!"**

Crenshaw later stated publicly that conservatives are **"100% different"** from **"vehement racists, anti-semites & ethnic-nationalists."** GQ's contemporaneous essay said Crenshaw had been heckled by Fuentes-aligned attendees **multiple times** in that period.

**Context — the ASU stop as escalation:** the Arizona State event sat in the same series as **Donald Trump Jr.** appearances, and the Groyper questions there were described as **"almost exclusively about Israel"** — a narrowing from the earlier three-theme mix (Israel / immigration / LGBT) to the single theme that produced the most usable clips. This is also the point at which targeting **expanded beyond Kirk** to **YAF** and **Daily Wire** figures, turning a Kirk-specific campaign into a "Conservative Inc." campaign.

## Event-by-event: November 10, 2019 — UCLA (Trump Jr. *Triggered*)

**Bill:** Promotional appearance for Donald Trump Jr.'s book ***Triggered***, with **Charlie Kirk** and **Kimberly Guilfoyle**.

**Planned format (Vox source):** brief interview by Kirk, then book signing; originally scheduled about **two hours**.

**Countermeasure:** Organizers **canceled the Q&A in advance** specifically to block Groyper questions.

**What happened:** A largely pro-Trump crowd — some accounts estimated roughly **one-third Groypers** — **booed, heckled, and chanted "Q&A" and "America First."** Some reports described attendees **imitating Joker-style laughter** as a mocking heckle. The event **ended after roughly 20–30 minutes** — cut down from the planned ~2 hours; Trump Jr. left the stage early. National print and cable coverage followed; this night is the standard citation for when the "Groyper War" became a mainstream story.

**Guilfoyle's reported reaction:** Kimberly Guilfoyle is reported to have shouted from the stage that the disruptors needed to **try online dating** — a line that became one of the most-clipped moments of the night and was reused by Groyper accounts as evidence that the panel had no substantive answer.

**Fuentes's stream:** an episode framed as **"UCLA Nationalist REVOLT Shuts Down TPUSA Don Jr. Event,"** treated as the campaign's largest scalp to that date.

**Groyper-side allegations about security:** follower accounts claimed TPUSA security **waved through suspicious left-wing attendees** while **threatening or screening America First attendees** — cited as proof that the organization feared questions from its right more than protest from its left. **Unverified.**

**Mainstream and Kirk-side X reaction:** posts asked directly whether **Fuentes had shut down a Trump Jr. event**, and characterized the group as **racists hijacking young conservative events** — the framing that carried into national coverage.

**Free books:** period posts claimed copies of *Triggered* were handed out free after the program collapsed.

**Spectator USA (Chadwick Moore):** argued the night exposed a **generational split** on the American right. Groypers saw Kirk and similar figures as having **"snatch[ed] the baton and appoint[ed] themselves the guardians of 2016's spoils"** while holding positions (on immigration, Israel, LGBT inclusion) that Fuentes's cohort treated as betrayal of MAGA. Another *Spectator* writer, **Ben Sixsmith**, argued TPUSA had sold itself as the **"debate guys"** yet used insults and Q&A cancellation instead of answering.

**Twitter discussion:** Moore posted (Nov 11, 2019) that a friend thought Kim and Jr. may **not have been warned** — "as if TPUSA thought the groypers wouldn't dare show up" — and that Guilfoyle could have "legitimately thought it was leftists in the crowd." Period posts also claimed free copies of *Triggered* were distributed after the shutdown.

## Event-by-event: November 12, 2019 — University of Florida

**Bill:** Culture War with **Graham Allen**, University Auditorium, evening program.

**Tactics:** Organizers reportedly ran **separate question lines** for left-wing and right-wing questioners — described in some accounts as one line for the left and one for the **"alt-right."** Kirk still took many questions and was **booed at points**, including when he condemned white nationalism.

**Groyper-side account of Kirk's answers:** sympathetic accounts claimed Kirk answered **extensively** and **backtracked toward immigration restriction** under pressure, while **holding firm against white identitarianism** — and described him as **"humbled"** by the exchange. Kirk-side accounts describe the same night as Kirk taking hostile questions at length and being booed for calling white nationalism evil, i.e. holding the line.

**Fuentes's stream:** episode titled around **"Groyper Wars: DECISIVE VICTORY AT THE BATTLE OF FLORIDA."**

**X reaction:** posts framed it as a **massive America First showing**, "hard questions" for Kirk, and rising **#GroyperWars** momentum — e.g. "Groyper War: Charlie Kirk Hammered With Hard Questions by America First Youth At UF" and "The Groypening gathered even more momentum… massive showing."

### TPUSA at UF chapter public statement (Nov 12–13, 2019)

Local coverage (WCJB and chapter social posts) preserved the chapter's message:

* The near-full house had been intended for **"sincere discourse."**
* The event was **"hijacked by non-students with an agenda."**
* Infiltrators allegedly disguised themselves with **MAGA hats** and even **Bernie Sanders memorabilia**.
* They **booed Kirk for calling white nationalism evil**.
* They rejected the Christian premise that **God can love and use all people** regardless of sexual orientation.
* The chapter reaffirmed **socially conservative values, natural rights, limited government, capitalism, and national sovereignty**, and said the non-students had **"espoused evil."**

This statement is one of the clearest **primary-source chapter reactions** from the campaign: it concedes social conservatism while drawing a hard line against racial ideology and non-student disruption.

## Event-by-event: ~November 14, 2019 — University of Houston

**Format:** Kirk **"Prove Me Wrong"** table event (capitalism vs. socialism billing). GQ described a small table with a poster reading roughly **"I'm pro-choice. You can pick your gun."**

**Kirk counter-tactic:** He tried to confront Groypers by **unveiling a concealed monitor** to play a clip of Fuentes criticizing Trump — initially hampered by **technical problems**.

**Outcome:** The crowd chanted **"America First,"** booed, and **followed and heckled Kirk** as he left campus. Groyper-aligned accounts and sympathetic blogs posted **"booed off campus"** victory videos (widely shared on Twitter Nov 14–16, 2019). GQ (Talia Lavin, Nov 25) described a **"hostile throng"** that **"chased him bodily from the area."**

**Date note:** Some later timelines list Houston as **November 25**; contemporaneous "booed off campus at UH" posts and GQ's "last week" framing (published Nov 25) point to **mid-November (~Nov 14)**. Both dates appear in secondary compilations; treat mid-November Houston as the well-attested disruption and flag the variance.

**Twitter:** *"WATCH: #CharlieKirk 'Booed Off Campus' at UH to Chants of 'America First'…"* circulated widely, with Groyper accounts claiming they had **successfully run him off campus**.

**On the monitor stunt:** the concealed-screen reveal is worth noting as a **tactical inversion** — Kirk attempting to use the Groypers' own weapon (a damning clip of the opponent) in the room rather than online. Fuentes's criticism of Trump was the chosen wedge, on the theory that Groypers claimed Trump loyalty while following someone who attacked him. The **technical failure** of the reveal is the detail every Groyper retelling foregrounds.

## Event-by-event: ~November 13, 2019 — North Carolina State University

NCSU is the stop that complicates the victory narrative, because **even Groyper-aligned scorekeeping split on it**.

* Fuentes's stream ran an episode titled around **"Unhinged Charlie Kirk CHEATS His Way to Stalemate at NCSU"** — a **stalemate**, not a win, with the "cheating" charge referring to question screening and format control.
* A follow-up episode then reframed the same stop as **"Total Victory"** / **"BATTLE OF NORTH CAROLINA."**
* Meanwhile the far-right blog **Occidental Dissent** published **"Groypers Take a Loss In North Carolina"** (Nov 13, 2019).

**Why this matters analytically:** the same night was published by allied sources as a stalemate, a total victory, and a loss within days. That is direct evidence that the "battle" scoreboard was **rhetorical production, not measurement** — and it is the strongest single reason to treat every "decisive victory" claim on this page as a claim rather than a finding.

## Mid-to-late November 2019 — the reaction phase

By mid-November the campaign's center of gravity moved from **campus rooms** to **argument about the campaign itself**.

### Fuentes stream themes in this window

* **"Gatekeepers EXPOSED"** — an episode on the **Michelle Malkin firing** by YAF, treated as the establishment proving the thesis by punishing its own most respected immigration hawk.

## Did anyone "win a debate"? What Twitter said

### First: there was no formal debate

A basic factual point often lost in victory rhetoric: **Charlie Kirk and Nick Fuentes never held a formal, moderated debate** in 2019–2020 (nor did Fuentes get a formal stage debate with Ben Shapiro). The "Groyper Wars" were **Q&A packing, heckling, livestream dual-screens, meme warfare, and refusal-to-debate discourse** — not Oxford Union-style contests with a declared winner. Almost every claim that someone "won a debate" is really a claim about **optics, virality, institutional power, or moral legitimacy**.

### The "debate guys" thesis (Spectator, Ben Sixsmith, Nov. 5, 2019)

*Spectator* writer **Ben Sixsmith** published a widely cited essay arguing Groypers had beaten mainstream campus conservatives **at their own brand**:

* Kirk, **Steven Crowder** ("Change My Mind"), and Shapiro ("facts don't care about your feelings") had spent years branding themselves as open, rational **"debate guys"** against an intolerant left.
* Groypers inserted themselves into that format with "sharp, civil and, in general, hilariously bad faith questions" from the *right*.
* Sixsmith's core claim: debating leftists who reject *all* of your premises is **easier** than debating people who share some premises but go further — the latter makes establishment figures sound **"weak, defensive and spineless."**
* He wrote that Kirk, especially, was "**painfully easy to outflank**" on basic conservative positions (citing Kirk-style capitalism cheerleading and "nation as placeholder for ideas" civic-nationalist language).
* Excluding Fuentes from events, after years of denouncing leftist closed-mindedness, looked **"limp and hypocritical."**
* Example used: **Crenshaw** answering a free-speech/anti-BDS law question by focusing on antisemitism without, in critics' view, meeting the constitutional free-association point (Columbia Bugle and related Twitter criticism).

Sixsmith still warned that Fuentes's distance from open Nazi branding was often **strategic/optics-based**, not a clean moral break — and hoped young men attracted by the vulnerability of Kirk-type figures would not "mire" themselves in racialism. That essay became a stock citation for the Groyper claim that **canceling Q&A or smearing questioners = losing the debate you advertised**.

## Further context and downstream effects

* **Chapter fallout:** some TPUSA chapters reportedly **dissolved** or saw **leaders resign**, citing free-speech objections or sympathy with the questioners. **Kansas State's** officer exodus is the best-documented case.
* **New organizations:** **America First Students** groups formed afterward, founded in at least one case (**KSU**) by a **former TPUSA chapter leader** (Jaden McNeil) — the campaign converting disruption into institutions.
* **Target expansion:** from Kirk to **Ben Shapiro** (including the family street confrontation that drew condemnation from **Nikki Haley**, **Meghan McCain**, and others), **YAF**, *Daily Wire* figures, and *Dispatch* writers.
* **Rival events:** Fuentes held **competing events near CPAC** and launched **AFPAC** — parallel infrastructure rather than continued infiltration.
* **What observers concluded:** commentators on both sides and in mainstream media described the episode as exposing a **generational and ideological split on the right** — **big-tent civic conservatism** versus a **harder nationalist / Christian nationalist / ethnonationalist** current. That framing, not any single campus night, is the episode's durable legacy.
* **Method summary:** coordination emphasized **memes, politeness for optics, and saturation** — not argument won on the merits in a room, but volume, virality, and the forcing of defensive institutional behavior that could then be filmed.
* **Long tail:** later Fuentes streams and follower accounts continued invoking "the wars" as **precedent**, and some claimed long-term influence on mainstream-right rhetoric about Israel aid and immigration levels.

## Why it appears in this investigation

The 2019–2020 conflict created a **public, timestamped record** of who attacked Charlie Kirk from his own right flank, and on what grounds — chiefly **Israel loyalty and aid**, **immigration volume and composition**, and **LGBT inclusion in conservative institutions**. Treat those framings as **interpretation, not evidence**. This page documents a political conflict. It does **not** establish motive for the assassination, and it does **not** assert that Fuentes, any Groyper participant, TPUSA, or any named living individual had involvement in or foreknowledge of Charlie Kirk's death. Post-assassination online speculation about Groyper ties to [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) remains **unproven in official reporting**; Fuentes publicly condemned the shooting and told followers not to take up arms.
