# Trained Roof Drop / Egress

:::caution Charged, not convicted
**[Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is charged, not convicted.** Smooth movement on video
can mean rehearsal, athleticism, panic with lucky footwork, or over-reading a short clip. This page
documents the **trained-egress claim**, not a finding that any living person is a professional
operator.
:::

Alongside the [lateral crawl](./military-lateral-crawl) debate, citizen investigators focus on
**how the figure left the roof**. The affidavit-layer language is that the person **lowered
himself** onto the grass (not a free-fall "jump") after placing a dark item — often described as a
**rifle wrapped in a towel** — on the rooftop. [Sam Parker](/Influencers/sam-parker) and others
argue the body mechanics look **rehearsed**: no pause at the edge, no scouting of the corner, fluid
transfer of the carried object, perfect foot placement on a **~16–20 foot** drop.

## The Claim in Parker's Words

On **July 29, 2026**, Parker wrote
([@BasedSamParker](https://x.com/BasedSamParker/status/2082286655189516559)):

> "That roof is 16-20 feet high. At no point does the jumper hesitate, or pause to look around and
> take stock of the situation. Without rehearsal and practice, most people would take a beat here…
> He headed straight for that corner and did a Peter Pan like he'd done it a hundred times
> before… He didn't even fumble around with the gun. His foot placement was perfect… You simply
> won't convince me that these are the actions of a scrawny kid who's never killed anyone before…"

He also notes that public hearing summaries did not show the prosecution establishing that Tyler
had **scoped that corner as an egress** beforehand — and that an untrained person might simply
have **retreated down the stairs** the way they came.

In a related reply the same day, Parker tied the drop to the crawl: including the lateral crawl
*"at the other end of the roof, that's not Tyler"*
([status/2082337862037746148](https://x.com/BasedSamParker/status/2082337862037746148)).

## How This Fits the Roof Clock

From the [Roof Timeline](./roof-timeline):

* **~12:23:29–12:23:54** — four stills are said to show movement from firing position to the
  **northeast corner in about 25 seconds**.
* **~12:24** — dark item placed; figure **lowers** to grass and runs north.

The trained-egress claim is about **quality of motion** in that window, not about whether the
window exists. Feasibility of the physical drop is also discussed on
[Roof Jumping Timeline](/Timeline/roof-jumping-timeline) in the Timeline section.

the perch and the exit, not like a first-time campus shooter inventing a path under adrenaline.

## Counterpoints Worth Stating

* **Adrenaline compresses decision time.** People do reckless, fast things after a gunshot without
  Special Forces training.
* **Short clips hide hesitation.** Cuts between stills can remove a pause that existed in
  continuous video.
* **"Lowered himself"** (affidavit language) already implies controlled hang-and-drop, not a blind
  leap — which can look "trained" while still being civilian parkour-ish movement.
* **Prior recon** is alleged in the broader case (morning campus movement); if true, the corner
  need not have been brand-new to the figure even if never proven in a hearing clip.
* Height estimates of **16–20 feet** are citizen measurements; a surveyed height could change how
  extreme the move looks.

## Open Questions

1. What is the **surveyed height** from roof gravel to grass at the northeast corner?
2. Does **continuous video** (not four stills) show any pause, look-down, or re-grip?
3. Was the **carried object** clearly a rifle/towel wrap through the entire drop, or ambiguous?
4. Did any **prior visit** by the charged defendant to that corner appear in discovery?
