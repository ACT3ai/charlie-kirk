# The Screwdriver and the Crime-Scene Tape

:::caution Nobody here is accused of wrongdoing
This page compares **reported courtroom testimony** against **publicly aired footage**. Discrepancies between a
witness's recollection and a video record are ordinary and have many innocent explanations. Nothing on this page
asserts that any officer lied, mishandled evidence, or committed any offense.
:::

A **screwdriver** sits at the center of one of the more concrete evidentiary disputes on the
[Losee Center](/UVU/Losee_Center) roof. According to the account described by
[Sam Parker](/Influencers/sam-parker) on X, **Officer Bagley** testified that he found the screwdriver
**15–20 feet past the railing** — that is, past the point where the figure is said to have crossed from the
public walkway onto the roof.

## Why a Screwdriver Matters at All

The tool is significant only because of what the State's sequence asks it to explain. In that account the rifle
was carried onto the roof and **assembled there**, which requires a tool, which is why a screwdriver on the roof
is corroborative rather than incidental. It also fixes a location: if the assembly happened 15–20 feet past the
railing, then the runner's path, timing and stopping point are all constrained by that spot. That is the link
worked through on [The Tactical Roof Run](./tactical-roof-run).

## The Tape Discrepancy

Parker's post raises a second reported point:

> "Officer Bagley testified he put up yellow crime scene tape at the railing to protect the screwdriver evidence,
> but the KSL helicopter showed conclusively that there was no crime scene tape around the railing, and no
> screwdriver can be seen on their footage, either."

Two separate claims are bundled there, and they should be kept apart:

* **The tape claim** — whether yellow tape was strung at the railing at the time the helicopter passed overhead.
  Timing matters here: aerial footage captures one moment, and tape placement on an active scene changes over
  hours.
* **The screwdriver claim** — whether the tool is visible in helicopter footage. A hand tool on a gravel roof
  photographed from a circling news helicopter is a very small object, and its absence from that footage is weak
  evidence either way.

The photograph above shows the roof surface with **orange evidence markers and a white placard already placed**,
and a strand of yellow tape visible beyond the roof edge near the courtyard walkway — which is a different
location from the railing Parker refers to. The image is consistent with a processed scene; it does not resolve
where tape was or was not strung at the moment the helicopter filmed.

## What Would Settle This

None of this is unresolvable. The **evidence log** for the roof would record what was collected, from where, and
when. The **scene photographs** taken by the processing team would show marker placement and tape lines directly.
The **full KSL aerial footage** with a timecode, rather than the broadcast excerpt, would establish what was
visible and when. All three exist; none has been published. That pattern — checkable records that would end an
argument in a paragraph, held back — is the recurring subject of the [Cover-Up](/CoverUp/overview) section and a
target of the proposed [disclosure laws](/Fix/overview).

Officer Bagley's rooftop presence is also relevant to a separate reported issue: that his **body-worn camera
stopped recording while he was still on the roof**. That is covered on
[Officer Bagley — Body Cam Died on Roof](/Law_Enforcement/officer-bagley-bodycam).

## Open Questions

1. What does the **evidence log** record for the screwdriver — recovery time, recovery location, and collecting
   officer?
2. Was the screwdriver **tested for prints or DNA**, and were results produced in discovery?
3. What is the **timestamp** on the KSL helicopter footage relative to the reported tape placement?
4. Do the **scene-processing photographs** show tape at the railing, and have they been produced?
5. Does the screwdriver **match the rifle's hardware** — that is, was it the right tool for that assembly?
