# Military Lateral Crawl

:::caution Charged, not convicted
**[Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is charged, not convicted.** This page documents how
citizen investigators and commentators describe a movement on surveillance footage. It does not
establish who the figure is, whether the movement required formal military training, or that any
living person is a professional assassin.
:::

In late **July 2026**, [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens) and accounts amplifying her release
circulated rooftop surveillance from the **[Losee Center](/UVU/Losee_Center)** that they say shows
the figure **running onto the roof**, then executing a **lateral (sideways) crawl** into a prone
firing position. Commentators immediately framed that motion as a **military tactical crawl** —
and used it to argue that a 22-year-old without known combat training is an unlikely match for
the figure.

## What Commentators Say the Footage Shows

The claim cluster, as repeated across X in late July 2026, is consistent enough to list cleanly:

* The figure **sprints or runs** across the gravel roof toward a corner or vent area.
* After most of the run, the figure **drops** and crawls **laterally** (sideways relative to the
  direction of the run) several feet into a final prone position.
* Some posts pair the clip with side-by-side video of a trained soldier performing a similar
  low crawl, arguing the body position and control match.
* Several accounts assert the crawl shows **no limp**, which they contrast with earlier
  affidavit-layer descriptions of a limping figure on the approach path (see
  [Roof Timeline](./roof-timeline)).

[Sam Parker](/Influencers/sam-parker) had already baked the same idea into his five-step sequence
on **July 29, 2026**: after the alleged assembly stop and sprint, the figure *"dropped & executed a
lengthy tactical lateral crawl several feet to the left to get into position"*
([@BasedSamParker](https://x.com/BasedSamParker/status/2082500145359503672)).

Representative amplification posts include accounts such as
[@RT_Breaking](https://x.com/RT_Breaking/status/2082330386504286425) ("lateral crawl — a move
KNOWN in the MILITARY"), [@LBELVIN6](https://x.com/LBELVIN6/status/2082344790348206171)
("What 22 yr old that's not in the military has this training?"), and
[@Omaruncovered](https://x.com/Omaruncovered/status/2082412314527985716) (side-by-side soldier
comparison, claim the person "does not resemble Tyler Robinson").

## Why the Crawl Is Used as an Identity Argument

The crawl is not treated as proof of guilt by skeptics — it is treated as **proof of skill profile**.
The argument runs:

1. Lateral low crawl into a supported prone is a **taught tactical skill**, not something most
   civilians practice.
2. The charged defendant is publicly described as a young civilian without a published military
   career.
3. Therefore, either the figure is **not** that defendant, or the defendant had undisclosed
   training, or the "military" label is overstated.

That chain is only as strong as its middle premise. Low crawling on gravel to stay below a roof
lip is also what many people would do if they believed they were about to be seen — a point made
by skeptics of the military reading, including
[@scottybhoy1988](https://x.com/scottybhoy1988/status/2082505815433392130), who argued a real
trained marksman would have stayed lower the entire time and might not have used that roof at all.

## How This Fits the Official Sequence

The crawl claim does not contradict the existence of a roof figure. It sits inside the same
sequence already on [The Tactical Roof Run](./tactical-roof-run): railing → alleged assembly stop →
sprint → **lateral crawl** → prone behind the lip. The prosecution package summarized in public
commentary also includes a **rooftop crawl / prone position** near shot time as part of campus
surveillance evidence. The dispute is not "was there a crawl?" so much as **"what does that crawl
imply about training and identity?"**

## Counterpoints Worth Stating

* **Civilians crawl.** Getting prone and sliding sideways behind cover does not require Ranger
  school. Over-labeling ordinary low movement as "special forces" is a common online pattern.
* **Video quality and angle.** Distant campus cameras compress posture. Side-by-side comparisons
  with drill videos can force a match that is not unique.
* **Hearing evidence cuts both ways.** Public summaries of the July 2026 preliminary hearing
  describe rooftop positioning and flight; they do not, on the public record, turn a crawl into a
  finding that the figure is a foreign operator.
* **Limp contradiction is testable.** If approach footage shows a limp and roof footage does not,
  that is either a clothing/gait change, a different person, or misreading of one of the clips —
  not automatic proof of a professional hit team.

## Open Questions

1. Does any **released continuous clip** show the full drop-to-crawl sequence with authenticated
   timestamps, or only edited excerpts?
2. Has any **court-admitted expert** characterized the movement as military training, or is that
   label entirely from social media?
3. How do **frame rates and resolution** affect claims about limp presence or absence during the
   crawl?
4. Is the crawl direction consistent with getting **behind the roof lip** for cover rather than
   with a textbook low-crawl drill?
