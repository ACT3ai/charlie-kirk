# Fired for Questioning the Narrative

> **What this page does and does not say.** This page records **one woman's
> account of her own firing, in her own words.** Aubrey Laitsch says she was
> terminated by Turning Point USA after questioning the official account of
> Charlie Kirk's assassination. That is her statement about her own employment,
> and it is reproduced here in full.
>
> **She does not name Erika Kirk anywhere in the video.** The headline
> *"ERIKA KIRK FIRED HER"* is the framing added by the X account that circulated
> the clip — an account whose own bio describes it as a *"Commentary & satire
> account"* whose content is *"opinion, fictional commentary, and satire only."*
> This site does **not** adopt that framing and makes **no claim** that
> [Erika Kirk](/People/erika-kirk) personally made, ordered, or knew of any
> termination decision.
>
> Turning Point USA has not, as far as this site can find, publicly responded to
> Laitsch's account. **No wrongdoing by any person or organization named here is
> alleged or established.** An employer's decision to end an at-will employment
> relationship is lawful in itself; what is documented here is what a former
> employee says the reason was.

## The Clip

On **September 1, 2026**, a nearly three-minute video was circulated on X in which
a woman identified as **Aubrey Laitsch**, a former Turning Point USA employee,
speaks directly to camera about the end of her employment. Within hours the post
had roughly **21,000 views, 2,400 likes and 430 reposts**.

The posting account captioned it:

> "ERIKA KIRK FIRED HER.
>
> Former TPUSA employee Aubrey Laitsch says she was FIRED after questioning
> Charlie Kirk's assassination.
>
> She says under Erika Kirk's leadership, there's an unwritten rule:
>
> Question the official narrative and YOU'RE GONE."

**The caption goes further than the video does.** In the clip itself Laitsch
refers to *"Turning Point"* and to *"the leadership there."* She does not use
Erika Kirk's name at any point. Readers comparing the two should treat the
headline as the poster's characterisation and the transcript below as the
primary record.

## What She Says

Laitsch's account has four parts.

**1. The stated reason for her termination.** She opens by tying the firing
directly to her questions about September 10:

> "I was terminated from Turning Point because I am questioning the narrative of
> what happened to my role model and CEO Charlie Kirk on the day of his
> assassination. What is being told to us in the mainstream media just really
> doesn't add up to me."

**2. That she believes it was a condition of employment.** She frames this as her
own experience rather than as a written policy:

> "It is, from my own experience, that you can't question the narrative and work
> at Turning Point. That is how I feel, that is how other people I've talked to
> feel."

**3. That Kirk himself modelled the opposite.** This is the argument she returns
to most:

> "It goes against everything that Charlie ever stood for. Charlie was someone
> who did question things, who did challenge the narrative."

She adds that if any staffer had been killed the way Kirk was, *"Charlie would be
doing the same thing... He would have never lived it down."*

**4. That she is not the only one.** She says others have been terminated, and
offers her own tenure as a measure of what the organisation lost:

> "All of these people who have been terminated were the most hard-working,
> loyal, honest employees that Turning Point has ever had. And I know that
> because I've actually been at Turning Point longer than most people."

## The Turnover Claim — and How to Check It

The one part of Laitsch's account that is **externally checkable** is her claim
about staff turnover. She points readers at public data herself:

> "That's public record. You can see it for yourself on these job websites.
> Turning Point's average length of employment is about a year and a half to 2.
> And so to be there for almost 5 years is actually quite some time. I've seen
> the media and marketing team, for example, turn over at least twice, consisting
> of about 40 people."

Three specific, falsifiable figures sit in that passage:

| Claim | Stated figure | How it could be checked |
|---|---|---|
| TPUSA average tenure | ~1.5 to 2 years | Aggregated tenure data on public employment-listing sites |
| Laitsch's own tenure | ~5 years | Her own public employment history |
| Media/marketing turnover | ~2 full cycles, ~40 people | Comparison of staff listings across years |

*(source needed — this site has not independently verified any of the three
figures. They are recorded as her claim, with the verification route she herself
proposed.)*

## Why This Matters to the Investigation

Laitsch's account, **if accurate**, speaks to a narrow but relevant question: how
the organisation Kirk founded has treated internal doubt about his death. It does
not bear on who fired the shot, and this page should not be read as evidence
about the assassination itself.

It does connect to material already documented elsewhere on this site. TPUSA's
post-death direction is covered in
[TPUSA Contradicts Kirk's Legacy](./TPUSA_Opposite_Charlie), which catalogs
reported policy reversals against Kirk's own stated positions. The succession
questions are traced in
[TPUSA Replacement Timeline](./TPUSA_Replacement_Timeline). The disappearance of
Kirk's own back catalogue is documented in
[Were Charlie's Podcasts Deleted?](./were-charlie-podcasts-deleted) and
[Apple Podcasts Removed Charlie](./apple-podcasts-removed-charlie). The internal
financial review Kirk reportedly ordered days before his death is covered at
[DOGE Audit](./doge-audit).

The obvious counterargument deserves stating plainly: **staff at any organisation
are let go for many reasons, and a departing employee's account of why is one
side of a dispute the employer has not answered.** Laitsch's statement is
uncorroborated on this site. She names no other terminated employee, produces no
document, and TPUSA has offered no public response for this page to weigh against
her. It is recorded because she said it publicly, on camera, under her own name.

   reason did it give for the termination?

   how many of those individuals have made similar statements?

   cites?
4. Did Laitsch raise her concerns internally before her termination, and to whom?
5. Is there any written policy, or any document, bearing on discussion of the
   assassination by staff?
