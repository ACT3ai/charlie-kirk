# Were Charlie's Podcasts Deleted?

**The Charlie Kirk Show** ran from 2019 until [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk)'s
death on **September 10, 2025** — a daily show distributed through **Salem Media
Group's** podcast network and carried on Spotify, Apple Podcasts, YouTube, and
Rumble. It was one of the largest conservative podcasts in the country, and every
episode Kirk recorded is investigative material: his guests, his positions on
Israel and Iran, his final weeks of commentary, and the on-air record of what he
actually said versus what has been attributed to him since.

In **February 2026**, users began reporting that the pre-death episodes were no
longer where they used to be. This page separates what is established from what
is alleged.

## What Appears To Be Established

Multiple independent users, and reporting that followed, describe the same
observation: **the back catalog of episodes recorded while Kirk was alive stopped
appearing on Spotify and Apple Podcasts, and older episodes were largely missing
from the YouTube channel.** Episodes remained reachable on **Rumble** and on
TPUSA's own website. The show itself continued publishing new episodes under the
same name and the same artwork, hosted by the co-hosts who took over after Kirk's
death — a detail several posters flagged as the strange part: *the brand stayed,
the episodes did not.*

No platform — Spotify, Apple, or YouTube — has published a statement explaining
what happened, and neither has [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview) in any formal public
release.

On **February 15, 2026**, [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens) posted on X that
*"one of the early actions his wife took as the new CEO was to have ALL of the
Charlie Kirk Show episodes removed from Spotify, Apple, and YouTube."* Her stated
reasoning was inferential rather than documentary: she argued it was implausible
that three independent platforms would drop the same archive at the same time
without a request from the rights holder.

Owens later narrowed her own claim in a follow-up post, writing that the episodes
*"were removed from Spotify and Apple only as they were never available in full on
YouTube"* — a meaningful correction, because it concedes that the YouTube half of
the original allegation described a catalog that had never been complete there in
the first place.

**This site does not allege that Erika Kirk, TPUSA, or anyone else ordered the
removal.** No document, platform statement, or contract has been produced showing
who initiated it. Erika Kirk has not been shown to have directed any takedown, and
publicly she has rejected Owens' broader commentary on the assassination — during a
CBS News town hall she responded to it with, *"Stop. That's it. That's all I have
to say. Stop."*

## TPUSA's Stated Explanation

Listeners who emailed the organization reportedly received a response attributing
the change to platform mechanics rather than to any editorial decision — described
as **"a product of how they choose to handle shows like ours with a high number of
episodes."** That explanation is secondhand, relayed by recipients rather than
issued as a public statement, and it has not been confirmed on the record by TPUSA
or by any platform.

## The Ordinary Explanations, Stated Fairly

Before treating this as suppression, an investigator has to clear the mundane
causes — several of which are well documented in podcasting generally:

* **Feed and episode-count limits.** Podcast hosts and directories commonly cap how
  many episodes a single RSS feed exposes. A daily show running six years produces
  thousands of episodes; truncating the feed to recent items is a routine hosting
  configuration, and the archive disappearing from Spotify and Apple simultaneously
  is exactly what a **single upstream feed change** looks like. Those platforms do
  not host the audio — they read the same feed. One change, two platforms.
* **Distribution and licensing.** The show was distributed via **Salem Media
  Group**. A change in that relationship — expiry, renegotiation, or a move to a
  different host — can pull a back catalog off directories without anyone deciding
  to erase anything.
* **YouTube was never the archive.** As Owens herself conceded, the full episode
  catalog was not on YouTube to begin with. A gap there is not evidence of removal.
* **Estate and rights questions.** Control of a deceased host's recorded work is
  legally messy, and material is sometimes pulled while ownership is sorted out.

None of these are proven either. They are the alternatives that have to be
excluded before the archive's disappearance can be treated as a deliberate act.

## Why It Matters To This Investigation

The reason this is a TPUSA question and not a media-business trivia question is
**what those episodes contain**. Kirk's final months are the period in which his
public positions were reportedly shifting — on Israel and donor pressure, on Iran,
on the Epstein files, on which voices he would platform. Those shifts are the
subject of [Fourteen Ways TPUSA Went the Opposite Direction After
Kirk](./TPUSA_Opposite_Charlie) and of [TPUSA Replacement
Timeline](./TPUSA_Replacement_Timeline). The single strongest rebuttal to any claim
about "what Charlie really believed" is the recording of Charlie saying it.

An archive that is hard to search is not the same as an archive that was destroyed —
and nothing here indicates the recordings were destroyed. But **accessibility is
the whole point of an evidentiary record.** A citizen investigator who cannot pull
the July 2025 episodes on the platform where they were published cannot check a
quote against the tape. That is a real loss to the record regardless of who caused
it or why, and it is the same concern documented at
[Archive.today](/CoverUp/Archive_Is_Purge) and
[Videos Deleted Remotely](/CoverUp/Videos_Deleted_Remotely).

## How To Check This Yourself

This is a claim with a **verifiable present-tense answer**, which makes it unusual
on this site. Anyone can test it:

1. Open the show's Apple Podcasts page and use **See All** to page back past
   **September 10, 2025**. Confirm whether pre-death episodes are listed and
   playable, not merely counted.
2. Do the same on Spotify, sorting oldest-first.
3. Compare against **Rumble** and TPUSA's own site, which were reported to still
   carry the older material.
4. Check the **Wayback Machine** for captures of the Apple and Spotify listing
   pages from late 2025 and early-to-mid 2026 — those captures are what would
   actually establish when the catalog changed, which is the piece nobody has
   produced.

As of a check on **July 29, 2026**, the Apple Podcasts listing for the show
reported roughly **2,200 episodes** available behind **See All**, which is
consistent with a large historical catalog being present in the directory. Whether
those entries include and play the pre-death episodes, and whether the catalog was
restored after the February 2026 dispute, is **not resolved here** — step 1 above
is how a reader settles it for themselves.

## Open Questions

1. On what date did the pre-death episodes stop appearing on Spotify and Apple
   Podcasts? No archived capture establishing the transition has been published.
2. Who holds distribution rights to the pre-September 2025 catalog now — TPUSA,
   the Kirk estate, Salem Media, or some combination?
3. Was there a takedown request at all, or a feed or hosting change? These leave
   different records, and neither has been produced.
4. Is the catalog complete today, and if it was restored, when and at whose
   request?
5. Does a complete master archive of every episode exist in a form investigators
   can obtain?

## Laws (Charlie Kirk)

* Who controls the Charlie Kirk Show master archive, whether any takedown request
  was made to Spotify or Apple, and the distribution contracts governing the
  pre-death catalog are things that the [Charlie Kirk Investigation
  Laws](/Fix/overview) may result in powerful truths coming out that aren't out
  yet.

## What This Page Does Not Claim

This page does not claim that any person deleted, ordered the deletion of, or
destroyed Charlie Kirk's recorded work. It records that **users observed the
pre-death catalog become unavailable on two or three major platforms**, that
**Candace Owens publicly attributed that to new TPUSA leadership**, that **no
verified proof of who initiated it has been produced**, and that **ordinary
technical and contractual explanations remain unexcluded**. No wrongdoing by anyone
named is alleged or established.
