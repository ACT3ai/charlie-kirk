# 42. Immediate blame for TPUSA vandalism on "the left"

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page claims as fact that any person, group, or political movement committed any act of
vandalism, or that anyone who assigned blame acted in bad faith. This site makes no claim that TPUSA
had any role in or knowledge of Charlie Kirk's death. [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is
charged, not convicted, and presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

Turning Point USA events have drawn protests for years, and some of those protests turned destructive.
After [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) was killed on September 10, 2025, each new incident of damage
at or near a [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview) event landed in a very different climate. One example captured in
this site's search logs: on October 8, 2025, AL.com reported that The Movement Church in Madison,
Alabama, was spray-painted ahead of a Turning Point Faith event. The messages named the scheduled
speaker, Bryan Dawson of 1819 News, as serving "the Anti Christ," and added "Beware his falsehoods."
The Madison County Sheriff's Office said the damage happened between 5:00 p.m. on October 7 and 8:00
a.m. on October 8, and asked the public for help identifying who did it.

Incidents like that were quickly described as the work of "the left." The question on this page is not
who did any given act of vandalism. It is whether assigning blame before any evidence came in served a
second purpose: reinforcing the idea that Charlie Kirk was killed by one lone leftist.

## The fed slop side, in full

The history is real. TPUSA tabling events and speakers faced disrupted events, torn-down posters, and
damaged equipment on campuses before September 2025, often by people who openly opposed Kirk's views.
When damage appears at a TPUSA event, the people with the most obvious motive are the organization's
open political opponents. On this view, pointing at them is common sense, and the same culture of
hostility is the world the State says Tyler Robinson came from.

Official-side commentators ran that line from day one. They treated the killing as left-wing political
violence, folded the roommate texts and the engraved casings into a single "fascist-hater" motive, and
read every later spray-paint job as confirmation. When a bronze statue of Kirk at TPUSA's Phoenix
headquarters was defaced with red paint in mid-September 2026, days after the one-year mark, TPUSA
called the act "diabolical," said it happened in the middle of the night, and noted that the vandals
also crossed out the name of Jesus on a nearby sign. [Erika Kirk](/People/erika-kirk) wrote that they
thought a bullet would silence her husband's voice and that spray paint would not do it either. Accounts
repeating the charging package posted the photos with captions about "the tolerant left" and a country
that "cannot coexist" with it. From this chair, asking for a vehicle description before assigning a
political tribe is stalling.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators do not deny that protesters have damaged TPUSA property. Their point is about timing and
use. They say the blame arrived before any arrest, any suspect, or any evidence, and that it was folded
straight into the story of the killing: one angry young man from the left, acting alone, with nothing
else to look at. That is the [lone-gunman fed claim](/Narrative/fed_narrative/lone-gunman) in miniature.

According to investigators, that frame does two jobs. First, it makes the lone-leftist account feel
confirmed by every new incident, even though vandalism elsewhere says nothing about who killed Charlie
Kirk in Orem. Second, it sorts people into sides. Anyone still asking about the thin security at UVU,
the missing rooftop footage, or the Panguitch timeline can be cast as siding with the people doing the
damage. Investigators point to the wider climate the site documents, including reports that
[hundreds of people were fired or suspended](/CoverUp/Purge_600_Fired) over comments after September 10,
as the setting in which that sorting happens. Reuters later put the punished-comments figure near 600.

The statue episode is the 2026 version of the same argument. On September 14, 2026, [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens)
asked in public whether Turning Point had shared any video or a vehicle description from a campus it
calls heavily surveilled: male or female, one person or two. "All I'm seeing," she wrote, "is them
promoting Turning Point Action and blaming the Left." [Project Constitution](/Influencers/x/project-constitution)
amplified the demand to drop the footage, arguing that independent researchers would identify a
suspect faster from a raw clip than from a press line. This site does not claim that TPUSA, any staff
member, or any political movement committed the vandalism. The open question investigators actually
press is narrower: if cameras caught the incident, why is the public being asked to accept a tribe as
the identification?

The [alleged Robinson motive](/Motive/Tyler_Robinson_Alleged_Motive) is the criminal-case twin of this
messaging. The [chain that the lone-gunman finding cannot absorb the intelligence indicators](/Narrative/chains/not-a-lone-gunman)
is the investigative twin. [Narrative-control claims](/CoverUp/Narrative_Psyop) collect the coordinated
talking-point version. None of those pages needs a spray-painted church in Alabama to stand. That is
the investigators' point: vandalism is being asked to do work the charging package has not done.

## When this fight ran

* **September 10–16, 2025.** Official-side commentators locked the "leftist assassin" frame within days
  of the killing, before the Alabama church, before the statue, and before the public had the
  steakhouse clock or the 911 kit. Governor Spencer Cox's early "acted alone" characterization sits on
  [its own issue page](/Narrative/fed_slop_issues/Spencer_Cox_Acted_Alone).
* **October 7–11, 2025.** The Movement Church graffiti. TPUSA Faith's Lucas Miles condemned it as
  cowardly. Conservative outlets carried the story as hostility toward a Kirk-aligned event. No
  public arrest was attached to the first wave of coverage this site has seen.
* **September 11–14, 2026.** A statue unveiled around the one-year mark; TPUSA's Phoenix headquarters
  statue spray-painted overnight; official-side posts blaming "the left" the same afternoon; Owens
  and Project Constitution asking for the tape the next day.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

The State's case leans on motive: the claim that Robinson acted from left-wing hostility to Kirk. Every
unrelated act of vandalism blamed on "the left" makes that motive feel stronger to a future jury pool,
without adding a single fact about what happened on September 10. That is how a frame can harden before
evidence is tested. [Proof Not Tyler](/Proof_Not_Tyler/overview) and
[Tyler Robinson is not the assassin](/Tyler_Robinson_Not_Assassin/overview) exist because the physical
file — no public firing video, a contested wound, a Panguitch ticket — does not close on ideology.
Painting the courtyard as one more left-wing crime scene is a substitute for that file.

## What would settle it

* Police reports for each vandalism incident blamed on "the left," and whether anyone was identified or charged.
* A timeline comparing when blame was assigned with when any suspect was identified.
* Whether any incident was later attributed to someone outside the political frame first offered.
* For the Phoenix statue: the raw camera files, a vehicle description, and whether law enforcement
  named a suspect before the political label went out.
* Records of whether any vandalism case was ever linked by investigators to the UVU killing.
