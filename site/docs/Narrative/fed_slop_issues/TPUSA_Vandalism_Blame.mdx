# 42. Immediate blame for TPUSA vandalism on "the left"

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page claims as fact that any person, group, or political movement committed any act of
vandalism, or that anyone who assigned blame acted in bad faith. This site makes no claim that TPUSA
had any role in or knowledge of Charlie Kirk's death. [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is
charged, not convicted, and presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

Turning Point USA events have drawn protests for years, and some of those protests turned destructive.
After Charlie Kirk was killed on September 10, 2025, each new incident of damage at or near a TPUSA
event landed in a very different climate. One example captured in this site's search logs: on October
13, 2025, a news report said a church in Alabama was vandalized ahead of a Turning Point USA event, with
a message calling the speaker the Antichrist.

Incidents like that were quickly described as the work of "the left." The question on this page is not
who did any given act of vandalism. It is whether assigning blame before any evidence came in served a
second purpose: reinforcing the idea that Charlie Kirk was killed by one lone leftist.

## The fed slop side, in full

The history is real. TPUSA tabling events and speakers faced disrupted events, torn-down posters, and
damaged equipment on campuses before September 2025, often by people who openly opposed Kirk's views.
When damage appears at a TPUSA event, the people with the most obvious motive are the organization's
open political opponents. On this view, pointing at them is common sense, and the same culture of
hostility is the world the State says Tyler Robinson came from.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators do not deny that protesters have damaged TPUSA property. Their point is about timing and
use. They say the blame arrived before any arrest, any suspect, or any evidence, and that it was folded
straight into the story of the killing: one angry young man from the left, acting alone, with nothing
else to look at.

According to investigators, that frame does two jobs. First, it makes the lone-leftist account feel
confirmed by every new incident, even though vandalism elsewhere says nothing about who killed Charlie
Kirk in Orem. Second, it sorts people into sides. Anyone still asking about the thin security at UVU,
the missing rooftop footage, or the Panguitch timeline can be cast as siding with the people doing the
damage. Investigators point to the wider climate the site documents, including reports that
[hundreds of people were fired or suspended](/CoverUp/Purge_600_Fired) over comments after September 10,
as the setting in which that sorting happens.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

The State's case leans on motive: the claim that Robinson acted from left-wing hostility to Kirk. Every
unrelated act of vandalism blamed on "the left" makes that motive feel stronger to a future jury pool,
without adding a single fact about what happened on September 10. That is how a frame can harden before
evidence is tested.

## What would settle it

* Police reports for each vandalism incident blamed on "the left," and whether anyone was identified or charged.
* A timeline comparing when blame was assigned with when any suspect was identified.
* Whether any incident was later attributed to someone outside the political frame first offered.
* Records of whether any vandalism case was ever linked by investigators to the UVU killing.
