# 52. "Digital assassin" labeling threads

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page claims as fact that Erika Kirk, TPUSA, any government body, or any commentator
directed, coordinated, or paid for any online campaign, or acted improperly. This site makes no claim
that Erika Kirk or TPUSA had any role in or knowledge of Charlie Kirk's death. Harassment of anyone,
including Erika Kirk, is wrong. [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is charged, not convicted, and
presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

"Digital assassination" is a phrase for destroying someone's reputation online. It is not new to this
story: Charlie Kirk's own podcast used it, in a June 2020 episode title and in an April 2021 episode
called "Unpacking the Digital Assassination of Project Veritas," both listed on this site's record of
removed podcast episodes.

After his death, the phrase came back pointed in a new direction. Threads began labeling people who
keep investigating the case as "digital assassins" of his widow, [Erika Kirk](/People/erika-kirk). The
label lumps together two very different things: people who harass a grieving woman, and people who
post 911 audio, court filings, and flight data. The question is whether the label protects her, or
turns ordinary evidence-gathering into something shameful.

## The fed slop side, in full

Erika Kirk lost her husband in front of a crowd and then became a target. Some online content about
her has been cruel, personal, and accusatory with no evidence. Naming that behavior is a way to push
back. There is also a real legal concern: in a capital case, a flood of accusatory content can taint
the people who will sit on the jury. At the preliminary hearing the defense itself submitted a public
opinion survey about prejudicial publicity. On this view, calling out the worst actors protects both the
widow and the fairness of the trial.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators agree that harassment is wrong and that Erika Kirk should not be accused of crimes
without evidence. This site's own rules forbid it. Their objection is to scope. They say the label is
not kept for harassers; it is applied to anyone who asks about the security plan, the missing rooftop footage,
the Panguitch receipt, or the custody clock, so that asking becomes an attack on a widow.

They note the jury-pool argument runs both ways. A steady public message that doubters are cruel,
paired with official statements that the case is settled, shapes a jury pool too. They also point to
the wider pattern on this site: reported [legal threats](/Censorship/TPUSA_Legal_Threats), takedowns,
and [reach drops on X](/Censorship/X_Deboosting_Investigators) aimed at the same researchers.
Investigators call the labeling threads state-adjacent because, in their view, they repeat the
official case and target its critics, not because any document shows government direction. None has
been produced.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If collecting evidence is branded as cruelty, fewer people will do it, and fewer witnesses will speak
up. The defense of a man presumed innocent depends on outsiders being free to question the State's
account without being cast as villains. A label that makes every question look like an attack narrows
what a future jury will ever hear.

## What would settle it

* Who started and amplified the main labeling threads, and whether any were paid or coordinated.
* Whether the threads named specific people and which posts they cited as harassment.
* A clear line, from the platforms or the court, between harassment and reporting on court records.
* Whether any labeled researcher was ever found by a court to have harassed anyone.
