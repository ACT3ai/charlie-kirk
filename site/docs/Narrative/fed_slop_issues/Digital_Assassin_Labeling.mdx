# 52. "Digital assassin" labeling threads

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page claims as fact that Erika Kirk, TPUSA, any government body, or any commentator
directed, coordinated, or paid for any online campaign, or acted improperly. This site makes no claim
that Erika Kirk or TPUSA had any role in or knowledge of Charlie Kirk's death. Harassment of anyone,
including Erika Kirk, is wrong. [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is charged, not convicted, and
presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

"Digital assassination" is a phrase for destroying someone's reputation online. It is not new to this
story: Charlie Kirk's own podcast used it, in a June 2020 episode title and in an April 2021 episode
called "Unpacking the Digital Assassination of Project Veritas," both listed on this site's record of
[removed podcast episodes](/TPUSA/apple-podcasts-removed-episode-list).

After his death, the phrase came back pointed in a new direction. Threads began labeling people who
keep investigating the case as "digital assassins" of his widow, [Erika Kirk](/People/erika-kirk). The
label lumps together two very different things: people who harass a grieving woman, and people who
post 911 audio, court filings, and flight data. The question is whether the label protects her, or
turns ordinary evidence-gathering into something shameful.

## The fed slop side, in full

Erika Kirk lost her husband in front of a crowd and then became a target. Some online content about
her has been cruel, personal, and accusatory with no evidence. Naming that behavior is a way to push
back. There is also a real legal concern: in a capital case, a flood of accusatory content can taint
the people who will sit on the jury. At the preliminary hearing the defense itself submitted a public
opinion survey about prejudicial publicity.

On this view, calling out the worst actors protects both the widow and the fairness of the trial.
Official-side commentators at the July 2026 hearing said evidence-free speculation helps the accused
and poisons a potential jury pool. One hearing attendee, after describing enhanced courtroom video,
told "the tormenters of the Kirk family" they could "go to Hell where you belong." The argument is
moral as well as legal: a widow should not have to relive the worst day of her life on a timeline
that also accuses her of complicity.

Harassment of anyone, including Erika Kirk, is wrong. This site's own rules forbid accusing her of
crimes. The official side says the label exists to draw that line in public.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators agree that harassment is wrong and that Erika Kirk should not be accused of crimes
without evidence. This site's own rules forbid it. Their objection is to scope. They say the label is
not kept for harassers; it is applied to anyone who asks about the security plan, the missing rooftop
footage, the [Panguitch receipt](/Tyler_Robinson/cowboys-smokehouse-receipt), or the
[custody clock](/court/mirandize/custody-timeline-fake-confession), so that asking becomes an attack
on a widow.

[Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens) said on **23 August 2026** that during the Tyler Robinson
preliminary hearing an official-narrative account created a thread accusing her of being a "digital
assassin" responsible for death threats against Erika Kirk *because she was investigating her
husband's murder*. She called that thread propaganda meant to criminalize noticing gaps in the
charging package. This site does not adopt her charge that any named commentator is a state actor.
It does record that the phrase was aimed at a researcher posting receipts, not only at people sending
threats.

They note the jury-pool argument runs both ways. A steady public message that doubters are cruel,
paired with official statements that the case is settled, shapes a jury pool too. They also point to
the wider pattern on this site: reported [legal threats](/Censorship/TPUSA_Legal_Threats), takedowns,
[reach drops on X](/Censorship/X_Deboosting_Investigators), and
[engagement bans on Owens](/Censorship/X_Candace_Engagement_Bans) aimed at the same researchers.
The [censorship hub](/Censorship/overview) and the
[investigator-censorship page](/CoverUp/Censorship_Investigators) collect those reports. Investigators
call the labeling threads state-adjacent because, in their view, they repeat the official case and
target its critics. No document produced so far shows government direction of those threads.

Charlie's own friendship with Owens is part of the record at
[Candace Owens Friendship](/Charlie/Candace_Owens_Friendship). Investigators say treating her later
work on his death as a crime against his widow inverts that history.

## When this fight ran

* **October 2025 – early 2026.** Copyright strikes, reported cease-and-desist letters, and reach
drops hit researchers posting 911 audio, campus clips, and restaurant interviews. See
[copyright strikes](/Narrative/fed_slop_issues/Copyright_Strikes_Against_Investigators).
* **7–11 July 2026.** Preliminary hearing week. Official-side commentators in Provo used "jury pool"
and "tormenters of the family" language in the same posts that claimed 4K rooftop video.
* **Late July – 23 August 2026.** The "digital assassin" thread Owens named, then her public reply.
The label was no longer a metaphor. It was a charge aimed at a named investigator.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If collecting evidence is branded as cruelty, fewer people will do it, and fewer witnesses will speak
up. The defense of a man presumed innocent depends on outsiders being free to question the State's
account without being cast as villains. A label that makes every question look like an attack narrows
what a future jury will ever hear.

The [Fix](/Fix/overview) section exists because investigators treat this as a structural problem:
discovery, media access, and the right to publish court records. A capital file that cannot be
examined in public is a file that cannot be checked. See also [court gag orders](/Censorship/Court_Gag_Orders).

## What would settle it

* Who started and amplified the main labeling threads, and whether any were paid or coordinated.
* Whether the threads named specific people and which posts they cited as harassment.
* A clear line, from the platforms or the court, between harassment and reporting on court records.
* Whether any labeled researcher was ever found by a court to have harassed anyone.
* Release of the exhibits the labeled researchers keep asking for: the card digits, the custody
video, the unedited 911 audio, the fragment bench notes.
