# 39. FBI direction not to interview witnesses until agents arrived

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page claims that the FBI, the Utah State Bureau of Investigation, any officer, or any
agent suppressed evidence, acted illegally, or knew anything in advance. The radio line is a short
snippet whose full context has not been published. Tyler Robinson is claimed to be the shooter; he is
charged, not convicted, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

After a shooting, the first officers on scene normally talk to people who saw what happened. Those
first statements are valuable because they are fresh. Nobody has had time to read the news, compare
stories, or be shown a photo.

On September 10, 2025, police radio at UVU carried a different instruction. A unit says that, per the
FBI, officers have been given "clear direction not to interview" witnesses until federal agents get
there. Another unit acknowledges, "we were not going to interview." Some renderings of the audio have the
first line as "As per SBI and FBI." In another transmission, the FBI asks local officers to stand by with
a private individual at a café until agents reach them. That person has not been charged in this case and
is not named here.

This is the context for a simple fact. Very few first-hour witness statements exist in any public record.

## The fed slop side, in full

The official side says this is how major cases are run. One lead agency controls the interviews so
each witness is questioned once, carefully, by trained investigators. If several agencies question the
same people separately, the statements drift and a defense lawyer can later use the differences. "Do not
interview" can simply mean "the lead agency will handle it." A single radio snippet also does not say
which witnesses, for how long, or why. On this view nothing was lost, only organized.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators say the timing is the problem. In those same first minutes, dispatch was broadcasting a
suspect in "all black," with a "black tactical helmet," a "black mask," and "possibly a tactical vest."
A man who says he filmed the figure on the Losee Center roof before the shot described all-black
tactical clothing and a face mask. He says he reported it on campus that day. He has not testified, and
his video and report have not been released.

A construction-crew witness described a man in a black trench coat and black mask near campus after the
shooting. According to the account circulating, he said the photo officers first showed his crew did not
look like the image the FBI released the next day.

Investigators argue that holding these first accounts until federal agents arrived let the public
description move from a masked, all-black figure to the T-shirt figure later tied to Tyler Robinson.
They present this as a question about how the witness record was gathered, not as proof of any act.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

The earliest witness descriptions are the ones least shaped by the official story, and several of them
do not describe the man charged. If those accounts were never formally taken, or were taken only after a
suspect image had been released, the defense loses the evidence most likely to support Robinson's plea of
not guilty.

## What would settle it

* The full radio recordings and CAD log, showing who issued the direction, when, and to whom.
* Any written FBI or SBI instruction on witness handling that day.
* FBI interview reports for the roof witness, the 911 callers, and the construction crew, with dates and times.
* The photo arrays shown to witnesses on September 10 and 11, and when each was shown.
