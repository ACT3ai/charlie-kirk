# 17. Body doubles and the 2022 bodycam gait

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page says any officer, agent, or other living person fabricated video, planted anyone, or had foreknowledge of the killing. A body camera that stopped recording is described as an undocumented gap, not as proof of anything. Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

The State says campus cameras followed one man from a grassy area near **800 West** at about **11:50 AM** on September 10, 2025, to the Losee Center roof. The pictures are not sharp enough to show a face clearly. So the identification leans on other things — clothing, a backpack, and above all the man's **walk**.

The probable-cause affidavit says the figure *"walks with a distinctive gait,"* with *"a stiff, right leg"* and a *"restricted"* ability to bend it. Supporters of the official account say that same walk appears in a **2022 traffic-stop bodycam** of Robinson. Citizen investigators ask why the walk became the identifier, and whether lookalikes on campus were ever ruled out.

## Side by side

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fed Slop Side** | The same gait as the 2022 traffic-stop bodycam proves the campus walker is Robinson. Body-double talk is panic after Owens' own clips showed him on campus. |
| **Citizen Investigators** | Maroon-shirt lookalikes, bad stills, and sudden high-quality 2022 bodycam next to missing station and roof tape are the tell. The official side moved to gait when 4K roof ID failed. |

## The fed slop side, in full

Stated fairly: gait is a real forensic signal. A stiff right leg is unusual, and it is hard to fake for a whole walk across campus. If older footage of Robinson shows the same movement, that is independent support for the identification. Candace Owens' own clips were said to show him on campus that day. On this view, body-double theories are what people reach for once they have to admit he was there.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators first point to the State's own testimony. At the hearing, Agent David Hull described a *"noticeable gait or limp"* on the walk in. Asked about the figure on the roof after it stood up, he said: *"there is no longer a visible limp or gait"* ([Day 2 transcript](/court/Preliminary_Hearing/Day_2)). A walk offered as a signature disappears in the minute that matters most.

Second, the clothing does not stay still. Earlier that day the figure attributed to Robinson wears a **maroon T-shirt**; the affidavit's campus and rooftop description is a **long-sleeve black shirt with an American flag and eagle**. Early witness accounts describe **black tactical clothing** ([stairs figure identity gap](/Proof_Not_Tyler/Stairs_Guy_Identity_Gap)). A maroon shirt is also common enough that other young men on campus fit it.

Third, the footage that would settle identity is the footage that is missing. The officer who reached the roof testified his **body camera had stopped recording**. The surrender video from the Washington County station was later described as deleted under a retention policy ([sheriff video withheld](/CoverUp/Sheriff_Video_Withheld)). Investigators say a clear 2022 clip surfacing while those recordings stay absent is the wrong way round. This site has not verified the 2022 bodycam comparison itself.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

The identity case, by the defense's own account, rests on the roommate texts, the Discord messages, and a surveillance identification. If the surveillance leg depends on a walk that changes on the roof and a shirt that changes on the video, it is weaker than it is presented, and an innocent lookalike becomes a real possibility.

## What would settle it

* The full, native-resolution campus video, continuous rather than cut to stills.
* A published gait analysis by a named examiner, with its method and error rate.
* The 2022 bodycam, its source, and when it entered the case file.
* The officer's rooftop body-camera device logs and the WCSO arrival video.
