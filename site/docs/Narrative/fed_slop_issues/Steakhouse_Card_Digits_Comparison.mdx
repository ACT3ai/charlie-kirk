# 60. Who benefits if the steakhouse last-four are never compared in public

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page says the FBI, any officer, the Robinson family, the restaurant, its owner or its staff committed a crime, lied, or had foreknowledge of the killing. The restaurant's staff and owner are private people, and nobody should contact or harass them. Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

Cowboy's Smokehouse is a restaurant in **Panguitch, Utah**, about **209 road miles** south of Orem, roughly three and a half hours' drive from Utah Valley University. A photographed receipt from that restaurant, **Check #211, Table 24**, is dated **9/10/25**, ordered at **8:55 PM** and paid at **9:47 PM**, for **$38.51**, on a **Visa Debit** card. The card number is blurred in the source.

Staff have said on camera that they served Tyler Robinson that night. If true, he was far from the Orem area on the evening of the killing, which does not fit the state's timeline. The simplest way to test it is to compare the last four digits of that card with Robinson's cards. That comparison has never been published.

## The fed slop side, in full

Stated fairly: this is a small family business that did nothing but serve dinner. The owner has said the store received harassing phone calls. He was not there that night, and he has said that as far as he knows he does not think it was Tyler Robinson, though it could be. The restaurant later declined to stand behind a confirmation. Mistaken identity is the most common way small-town sightings go wrong, especially after a suspect's photo has been on every screen. From this view, pressing the story further hurts real people over a lead that has already fallen apart.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators agree the restaurant should be left alone, and say that is exactly why the card matters. A card record needs no one's memory. According to the [full visit video](/Tyler_Robinson/cowboys-smokehouse-full-visit), recorded on **July 11, 2026**, the owner handed over the physical receipt and said it was the ticket in question, and a staff member said he was certain he served Robinson. Staff say the tip reached the FBI, that the last four digits of the card were given to agents, and that nobody came back.

The stakes, as laid out in the [Panguitch chain](/Narrative/chains/panguitch-breaks-the-evening), are high. The state's evening places Robinson in the Orem area, including a claimed Dairy Queen stop around 6:38 PM and the text messages attributed to him that night. A diner in Panguitch at 9 PM could not also be sending those messages from Orem. Either the card is his, and the timeline breaks, or it is not, and the alibi story ends. Investigators say the comparison is cheap for anyone who holds the records, and that its absence is itself worth noting.

The [Goforth midnight stop](/Tyler_Robinson/goforth-midnight-stop), a reported vehicle contact around 12:30 AM, is examined against the same receipt.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

This is one of the few pieces of evidence that can be checked objectively and quickly. If he was in Panguitch at 9 PM, much of the evening case against him cannot stand, and investigators say that would point strongly toward a frame. If he was not, the question closes cleanly. Leaving it unanswered helps nobody who wants the truth, including Robinson.

## What would settle it

* The last four digits of the card on Check #211, compared with every card on Robinson's accounts.
* The merchant processor's record for approval code 010864, which ties the transaction to a card without relying on anyone's memory.
* FBI records of when the restaurant's tip was received and what was done with it.
* Cell-site location data for Robinson's phone between 8 PM and 10 PM on September 10.
* Any road or fuel camera on the routes between Orem and Panguitch that evening.
