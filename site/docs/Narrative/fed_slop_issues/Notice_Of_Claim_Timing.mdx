# 43. Notice-of-claim timing against Utah

:::caution Attributed claims only
Filing a notice of claim is a lawful, ordinary legal step. Nothing on this page claims as fact that
Erika Kirk, TPUSA, the Kirk family, their lawyers, or any Utah official acted improperly or had any
knowledge of or role in Charlie Kirk's death. This site makes no claim that Erika Kirk or TPUSA did.
[Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is charged, not convicted, and presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

In Utah, a person who wants to sue a government body, such as the State or a public university, first
has to file a written notice of claim. It is a formal warning that a lawsuit may follow, and it has to
be filed within a set time. Charlie Kirk was killed on the campus of Utah Valley University, which is a
state school, so any claim about campus security would run through this process.

Citizen investigators say a notice of claim tied to the killing was filed against Utah, and they ask
why it landed when it did: close to the September 1, 2026 bind-over argument in the criminal case, and
alongside public messaging about "justice for Charlie." This site has not obtained the notice itself,
so its filer, date, and contents are not documented here.

## The fed slop side, in full

Lawyers file notices of claim to protect their clients' rights before a deadline runs out. Missing the
deadline can bar a lawsuit forever, so a careful lawyer files on the calendar, not on the news cycle.
A notice of claim does not accuse anyone of a crime and says nothing about who killed Charlie Kirk. On
this view, reading meaning into its date is pattern-hunting: the family is simply keeping its civil
options open, as any family would after a killing at a public venue.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators agree that the step is ordinary. Their interest is in what the filing would say. A civil
claim against the State has to describe what the government did wrong, and that description may not
match the public "justice" message, which centers on convicting one man.

They point to the questions this site has documented about the day itself: the
[thin security plan](/Security_Team/overview), campus camera coverage, and the handling of the scene.
A civil claim that blames the State for security failures sits in a different place from a criminal
case that says one lone actor on a roof is the whole story. Investigators say that if the family's own
lawyers see a state failure worth claiming, the public should be able to read what that failure is.
Hence their phrase: follow the filing, not the press line.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If a civil filing describes security gaps, lapses in scene handling, or facts about the day that the
criminal case does not mention, that material could be relevant to the defense. A claim written by the
victim's side that points at the State's failures would at least show the story is larger than one
rooftop.

## What would settle it

* The notice of claim itself: who filed it, the date, and the entity named.
* The factual allegations it makes about security, campus response, or the scene.
* Any response or denial from the State or Utah Valley University.
* Whether a lawsuit followed, and whether any part of it was sealed.
