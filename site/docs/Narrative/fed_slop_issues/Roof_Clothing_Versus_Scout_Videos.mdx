# 9. The clothing and weapon on the roof versus the campus scout videos

:::caution Attributed claims only
Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent.
Witness descriptions here come from 911 audio, dispatch radio, and interviews, not sworn testimony. This site
makes no claim that any witness, officer, or agent said anything untrue, and it does not claim to identify
the rooftop figure.
:::

## The issue in plain words

On the morning of September 10, 2025, campus cameras recorded a young man the state says was Tyler
Robinson walking around UVU. Investigators call these the **scout videos**, because the state treats them as
him looking over the site. In them he wears casual clothes: a maroon shirt and shorts in the morning, and a
black long-sleeve shirt and jeans later.

The first voices after the shot described someone very different. So this issue asks: **does the person on
the roof match the person in the scout videos?** And does the rifle described on the roof match the rifle in
court?

## Side by side

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fed Slop Side** | T-shirt-and-jeans scout, a clothes change, then the roof. The 911 tactical language is reconcilable or mistaken. The court rifle is the rifle. |
| **Citizen Investigators** | 911 callers and the pre-shot witness say all-black tactical, helmet, mask, vest, and a long gun that does not match the towel-wrapped Mauser. The shorts-and-T-shirt scout and the firing kit are two different people, or a costume change the state never proved. |

## The fed slop side, in full

The fed-slop side says the sequence is simple. A **T-shirt-and-jeans scout** walks the campus, changes
clothes, then goes to the roof. The tactical language in the 911 calls is either reconcilable with ordinary
dark clothing or simply mistaken, because frightened callers get details wrong. And the rifle in court, the
towel-wrapped Mauser found in the woods, **is** the rifle.

That has force. Eyewitness descriptions under stress are notoriously unreliable, video compression shifts
colors, and one caller's "tactical" can be another's "black shirt." The reported texts even mention a change
of outfits in the woods.

## What citizen investigators say

Citizen investigators say the descriptions are too consistent to dismiss. Dispatch radio in the first
minutes described a suspect in **"all black, black long gun, black tactical helmet, a black mask, possibly a
tactical vest."** Another unit put the person on the far north side of a building east of the library in
**jeans, black shirt, black mask, long rifle**.

The man who filmed the roof figure said **"It was all tactical. He definitely had a face mask on,"** that all
skin was covered, and that he was about **175 yards** away. According to Candace Owens, the witness, who she
says teaches people to shoot, says the rifle he saw **does not match** the one presented in court. A worker at
a nearby construction site, relayed through a coworker, described a man **after** the woods still in **all
black**, with a trench coat, a mask, and a small backpack, even though the texts say he changed outfits there.

Investigators also question the rifle. A Mauser 98 is roughly **44 inches** long, and they argue it does not
fit the backpack seen in the stills. Their conclusion: the shorts-and-T-shirt scout and the firing kit are
**two different people**, or a costume change **the state has never proved** with footage.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If the person on the roof was not dressed like Robinson and did not carry the gun found in the
woods, then the video identification and the physical evidence point at different people. That is the
classic shape of a setup: one person seen, another person acting. Investigators say the state has to show the
change of clothes, not assume it.

## What would settle it

* Native-resolution video of every camera between the scout sightings and the roof, showing any clothing change.
* The full 911 and dispatch recordings with times, released by the agencies.
* The rooftop witness's video and campus report, and his testimony under oath.
* Evidence logs for any tactical clothing, helmet, mask, or vest recovered.
