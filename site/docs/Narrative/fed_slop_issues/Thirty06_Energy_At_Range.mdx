# 33. Expected behavior of a .30-06 from 142-175 yards (~415 ft)

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page claims that any medical examiner, laboratory, agent, or prosecutor misstated
evidence or acted improperly. Tyler Robinson is claimed to be the shooter; he is charged, not convicted,
has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent. The rooftop .30-06 is the government's narrative. Most
citizen investigators conclude Charlie Kirk was killed by an [exploding microphone](/Mic/overview).
:::

## The issue in plain words

The government's narrative says Charlie Kirk was killed by one .30-06 Springfield round fired from a
Mauser bolt-action rifle on the Losee Center roof. Estimates of the distance on this site run from about
142 to 175 yards, around 415 feet. The witness who filmed the roof figure put it at about 175 yards.

A .30-06 is a large hunting and military rifle cartridge. It leaves the muzzle at roughly 2,700 to 2,900
feet per second with around 2,800 to 2,900 foot-pounds of energy. For comparison, a common 9mm handgun
round carries about 356 foot-pounds. At under 200 yards, very little of the rifle round's energy is
lost before it arrives.

So the question is simple physics. What should that round do to a human neck at that distance, and does
the official wound picture look like that?

## Side by side

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fed Slop Side** | A neck hit with fragments is what happened. Internet ballistics are not the medical examiner. |
| **Citizen Investigators** | That cartridge at that distance should not produce the official wound picture. Energy, exit and tissue damage are off, so the official gun or the official range is off. Investigators conclude an explosive at the microphone. |

## The fed slop side, in full

The official side says real wounds do not follow gun-forum rules. A bullet can strike bone, break apart,
and stay inside. The medical examiner recorded a gunshot wound of the neck, and fragments consistent
with a .30-caliber jacket were recovered during the autopsy. Watermelon tests and energy charts are not
autopsies. People without medical training should not overrule the examiner who actually looked at the
body.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators say this is not a matter of impression. A round with that energy passing through a neck
normally produces massive tissue disruption and, in most cases, a large exit. The record as described
instead shows a contained track down the left side of the neck, fragments inside, and no clean exit. A
widely shared line said the treating surgeon called it "an absolute miracle" that the bullet stopped, but
that reached the public only second-hand, with no named doctor on record.

The fragment did not settle it either. The ATF examined it and reported a class-level result consistent
with a .30-caliber projectile. It could not identify or exclude the seized Mauser.

From September 2025 onward, citizen investigators posting on X kept making the same point. In their
words, it is "impossible" for a neck to stop a .30-06 at that range without an exit. Eleven months later,
the large majority had still not gone back to the rifle story. Their conclusion is that the official gun
or the official range is wrong, and that the death was caused by an explosive at the microphone on
Charlie's chest and collar.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

The charge against Robinson depends on the rooftop rifle being the thing that killed Charlie Kirk. If
that round at that distance cannot produce the recorded wound, the rifle, even if it was fired, may not
be the cause of death. That would break the link between the man on the roof and the killing itself.

## What would settle it

* The full autopsy report, with the wound track, fragment locations, and any exit, reviewed by independent pathologists.
* The fragments themselves, weighed and examined by defense experts.
* Controlled ballistic tests of the seized rifle and ammunition at the stated range, into realistic tissue simulants.
* The raw multitrack audio from the event, to test whether the decisive sound came from the roof or the tent.
