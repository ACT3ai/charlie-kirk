# 48. Early Google searches for the exact name Tyler James Robinson

:::caution Attributed claims only
Search-trend data does not identify who searched. Nothing on this page claims as fact that any person,
agency, or country searched for Tyler Robinson, or that any searcher had foreknowledge of or any role
in Charlie Kirk's death. This site makes no claim that [Lance Twiggs](/People/lance-twiggs) knew about
or was involved in the killing. [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is charged, not convicted, and
presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

Tyler Robinson's name reached the public on September 12, 2025, two days after [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk)
was killed at Utah Valley University. Before that, he was an unknown 22-year-old in southern Utah. If
anyone was searching Google for him, his partner, or his home address months earlier, investigators
ask why.

Google Trends is a public tool that shows how much a phrase was searched, where, and when, in relative
terms. Citizen investigators captured Trends graphs they say show interest in phrases such as "Tyler
Robinson Discord/Reddit/FBI/LDS/TPUSA/Israel" running from about June to November 2024, an interest blip
around [July 20, 2025](/GoogleSearches/july-20-2025-anchor), and nonstop interest in the St. George
townhome address he shared with his partner from December 2024 to September 2025. A researcher
interviewed on a podcast also claims his exact apartment address was searched in Hebrew on April 29,
2025. The [search-pattern hub](/GoogleSearches/overview) collects those captures.

## The fed slop side, in full

"Tyler Robinson" is a common name. Thousands of people share it, including athletes and local figures,
so searches for it prove nothing. Google Trends is sampled: at very low volumes it shows noisy spikes
that can change between pulls, and Google has told the media it is not a historical record of who
typed what. Trends does not expose IP addresses, accounts, or absolute counts. A handful of queries
for a rare exact phrase can draw a 100 on the relative index. After a famous event, people also go
back and read old graphs with new eyes, seeing a pattern that was never there.

Official-side commentators treat the Hebrew-address and DC-metro claims the same way: a volunteer
network's screenshot is not a subpoena return. The [Discord search-traffic page](/Tyler_Robinson/discord/google-trends-discord-searches)
on this site already records the hard limit: regional interest in "Tyler Robinson Discord" after the
story broke is what a confession story produces, and a June–November 2024 range for a name that was
not public until September 2025 is a reason to doubt the capture, not a reason to assume a plot. On
this view, early-search claims are statistical noise dressed up as evidence, and they pull attention
off DNA, texts, and a bind-over.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators answer that the claims are not about the bare name. They are about long, specific
strings that pair the name with Discord, the FBI, TPUSA and Israel, and about a specific street
address, which no famous namesake shares. A common name explains searches for "Tyler Robinson"; it
does not explain searches for his townhouse, or for "Tyler James Robinson" in Washington, D.C. and
Huntsville, Alabama on September 9, 2025 — the day before the killing, and three days before the
name was public. Attorney Baron Coleman walked that spreadsheet in September 2026 and said he does
not know what it means. His point, which investigators repeat, is that someone with subpoena power
should find out.

James Li of the 5149 Show told *Redacted* in October 2025 that Trends showed interest, from Washington
D.C. and from Israel, in terms including Tyler James Robinson, the Losee Center rooftop, the hospital,
and the medical examiner, in two clusters: one in late July 2025 around AmFest, and one on September
9–10. He said some of those graphs later listed as unavailable. The site's
[Trends deletion page](/GoogleSearches/trends-deletion-suppression) and the
[Google Search & Trends scrubbing](/Censorship/Google_Search_Trends_Scrubbing) page record those
reports. That is why investigators archive the captures, and why this site does not soften or remove
them.

The April 29, 2025 Hebrew-address claim is the sharpest single data point. Commentators say the
Hebrew form of Robinson's exact St. George apartment address showed a relative spike in the Lincoln,
Nebraska metro while an Egyptian-registered jet, [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), sat parked there.
The dedicated page is [the Hebrew search](/Planes/critical/google_search_in_hebrew). Trends cannot
tie a query to an aircraft. Investigators still want the raw logs, because an unknown apprentice's
home address being searched in Hebrew four and a half months before he was named is, they say, the
definition of a question that needs an account, not a shrug. See also the
[Israel IP search-pattern](/GoogleSearches/israel-ip-search-patterns) and
[DC metro](/GoogleSearches/dc-metro-search-patterns) pages, and the
[Israeli search-pattern claims](/Israel/Israeli_Search_Patterns_Claims) file.

They also point to what [recruitment](/Tyler_Robinson/Recruited) would look like from the outside:
months of watching a quiet young man, his partner, his address, and his online handles, then a
public unveiling of that same man as a lone hater two days after the killing. If the logs show
nothing, that answer should also be public.

## When this fight ran

* **October 2025.** James Li / *Redacted* airs the first widely watched Trends compilation: D.C. and
  Israel, July cluster and September 9–10 cluster, then "unavailable" graphs. Official-side accounts
  mostly ignore it or call it noise.
* **April–August 2026.** The Hebrew-address / Lincoln / SU-BTT claim circulates hard. Skeptics answer
  that Trends is relative and anonymized, and that a few queries can draw a spike. Investigators
  answer that the string is the address, in Hebrew, in the metro where the jet sat.
* **September 2026.** Baron Coleman publishes a spreadsheet walk: "Tyler James Robinson" in D.C. and
  Huntsville on September 9, plus years of hits on Lance Twiggs in the same key locations. The
  one-year mark of the killing puts the captures back in circulation. Official-narrative accounts
  still treat it as after-the-fact mythology.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If people were searching for Tyler Robinson, his home, and his online accounts months before
September 10, someone may have been watching him before any crime. That would fit monitoring,
recruitment, or preparation of a patsy far better than it fits a lone young man who decided on his
own. It is one of the files behind [Tyler Robinson Not Assassin](/Tyler_Robinson_Not_Assassin/overview)
and [Proof Not Tyler](/Proof_Not_Tyler/overview). Google holds the raw logs. A subpoena under
[digital-evidence rules](/Fix/Digital_Evidence) could answer who searched and when. A shrug cannot.

## What would settle it

* Google's raw query logs for the specific strings and address, with timestamps and locations.
* Account or IP-level records for those queries, obtained by subpoena under court supervision.
* Independent re-pulls of the same Trends queries archived with date and method.
* Whether the FBI's digital investigation reviewed pre-event searches for Robinson, and what it found.
* Ground-handling and passenger records for SU-BTT's Lincoln stop on April 29, 2025, so the jet
  coincidence can be tested rather than asserted.
