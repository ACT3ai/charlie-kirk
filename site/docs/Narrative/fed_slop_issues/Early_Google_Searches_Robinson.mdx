# 48. Early Google searches for the exact name Tyler James Robinson

:::caution Attributed claims only
Search-trend data does not identify who searched. Nothing on this page claims as fact that any person,
agency, or country searched for Tyler Robinson, or that any searcher had foreknowledge of or any role
in Charlie Kirk's death. This site makes no claim that [Lance Twiggs](/People/lance-twiggs) knew about
or was involved in the killing. [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is charged, not convicted, and
presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

Tyler Robinson's name reached the public on September 12, 2025, two days after Charlie Kirk was killed.
Before that, he was an unknown 22-year-old in southern Utah. If anyone was searching Google for him,
his partner, or his home address months earlier, investigators ask why.

Google Trends is a public tool that shows how much a phrase was searched, where, and when, in relative
terms. Citizen investigators captured Trends graphs they say show interest in phrases such as "Tyler
Robinson Discord/Reddit/FBI/LDS/TPUSA/Israel" running from about June to November 2024, an interest blip
around July 20, 2025, and nonstop interest in the St. George townhome address he shared with his
partner from December 2024 to September 2025. A researcher interviewed on a podcast also claims his
exact apartment address was searched in Hebrew on April 29, 2025.

## The fed slop side, in full

"Tyler Robinson" is a common name. Thousands of people share it, including athletes and local figures,
so searches for it prove nothing. Google Trends is sampled: at very low volumes it shows noisy spikes
that can change between pulls, and Google has told the media it is not a historical record. After a
famous event, people also go back and read old graphs with new eyes, seeing a pattern that was never
there. On this view, early-search claims are statistical noise dressed up as evidence.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators answer that the claims are not about the bare name. They are about long, specific
strings that pair the name with Discord, the FBI, TPUSA and Israel, and about a specific street
address, which no famous namesake shares. A common name explains searches for "Tyler Robinson"; it
does not explain searches for his townhouse.

They also point to what happened to the evidence. The site's [Trends deletion page](/GoogleSearches/trends-deletion-suppression)
records reports that some Trends graphs later vanished from Google's interface, and one related query
tied to his father's business reportedly disappeared after a spike. That is why investigators archive
the captures, and why this site does not soften or remove them. Their demand is simple: Google holds
the raw logs, with timestamps and accounts, and a subpoena could answer who searched and when.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If people were searching for Tyler Robinson, his home, and his online accounts months before
September 10, someone may have been watching him before any crime. That would fit monitoring,
recruitment, or preparation of a patsy far better than it fits a lone young man who decided on his own.
If the logs show nothing, that answer should also be public.

## What would settle it

* Google's raw query logs for the specific strings and address, with timestamps and locations.
* Account or IP-level records for those queries, obtained by subpoena under court supervision.
* Independent re-pulls of the same Trends queries archived with date and method.
* Whether the FBI's digital investigation reviewed pre-event searches for Robinson, and what it found.
