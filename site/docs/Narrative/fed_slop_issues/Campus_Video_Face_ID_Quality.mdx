# 59. Whether public campus video quality can identify a face

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page says the FBI, any agency, or any living person committed a crime, altered evidence, or had foreknowledge of the killing. Claims about enhancement and resolution are reported as the claims of the people making them. Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

The identification of Tyler Robinson rests heavily on video. At **7:55 PM on September 11, 2025**, the FBI released rooftop and stairwell stills of a person of interest. Those stills were described as enhanced. The rooftop figure in the public clips is small and distant, seen from campus cameras well over a hundred yards away.

So a basic question matters a great deal: can the footage the public has actually show whose face it is? And if not, is there a better version that can?

## The fed slop side, in full

Stated fairly: what circulates online has been screen-recorded, compressed and re-uploaded many times over. The originals held by investigators are higher quality. Prosecutors showed video at the July 2026 preliminary hearing, and people who attended said it was far clearer than anything online. Courts routinely withhold exhibits before trial to protect the jury pool. The public being unable to see a face in a compressed clip says nothing about what the originals show.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators say the "better version" has never been produced in a way anyone can check. According to posts collected on the [more reasons page](/Tyler_Robinson_Not_Assassin/more-reasons), a radio host covering the hearing said spectators who claimed to have seen 4K had in fact seen the same blurry sequence, zoomed and circled in red, that was already public. Other posters stated there is no true 4K footage of Robinson on any roof around the event. The claim of a face-clear set, they note, rests on a small number of attendees rather than on the exhibit.

They add that low resolution cuts both ways. This site's own [chain on the rooftop figure](/Narrative/chains/rooftop-figure-not-robinson) says plainly that pixel-level exclusion is weak evidence. But the same weakness applies to pixel-level identification. The stronger points, investigators say, are that the stills were [enhanced](/CoverUp/Enhanced_Suspect_Photos) before release, that the [clothing conflicts](/Roof/black-clothing-vs-jeans) with the all-black description from 911 callers, and that [roof eyewitnesses have not been called](/Suspicious/Trial_Legal/uncalled-roof-eyewitness).

There is also a footage question. A witness has said the FBI repeatedly called him to confirm he had deleted his own close-up 4K clip of the moment ([eyewitness mobile video](/cameras/Eyewitness_Mobile_Video)), and campus HD footage has not been released ([UVU HD footage withheld](/CoverUp/UVU_HD_Footage_Withheld)).

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If no footage the public or the defense can test shows his face on that roof, the identification rests on inference: clothing, movement and timing. Those can be matched to the wrong person. A frame works best when the key image is too blurry to disprove. That is why investigators treat the face-ID question as central, not technical.

## What would settle it

* The native-resolution, untrimmed campus camera files, with their metadata and chain of custody.
* The FBI image-enhancement work product: what was enhanced, how, when and by whom.
* A list of every video exhibit shown at the preliminary hearing, with its resolution.
* Testimony from the people who saw the roof figure in person.
* Independent authentication of any witness phone video of the moment.
