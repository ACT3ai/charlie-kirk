# 23. Engraved meme casings as authentic motive artifacts

:::caution Attributed claims only
The possibility that items were planted is raised by investigators as a question about chain of custody, not as an accusation. This site makes no claim that any officer, examiner, or other living person planted, altered, or mishandled evidence, or had foreknowledge of the killing. Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent. Lance Twiggs has been charged with nothing.
:::

## The issue in plain words

When the recovered rifle was made safe, a sergeant testified, it held **one fired cartridge case** and **three unfired cartridges**, and **all four** carried engraved text. Utah's governor described them at a press conference on **September 12, 2025**. The unfired rounds reportedly read *"Hey fascist! CATCH!"*, *"O Bella ciao, Bella ciao"*, and *"If you read this..."*; the fired case reportedly read *"NoTices Bulge OWO What's This?"*

Within hours the engravings became the public's explanation of the killing. This issue asks whether they are the defendant's authentic voice, or objects whose origin still needs to be proved.

## Side by side

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fed Slop Side** | "Hey Fascist! Catch!" and the other engravings are Robinson's voice. They prove ideology and close motive. |
| **Citizen Investigators** | Could be planted props. Too on-the-nose for a quiet apprentice. Treat them as narrative garnish until chain of custody and tool origin are public. |

## The fed slop side, in full

This side has more than a press conference behind it. At the July 2026 hearing, an ATF tool-mark report was read into the record: a **Dremel engraving bit** seized from Robinson's home *"was identified as having made tool marks"* on the fired case and the three unfired cartridges. An ATF biology report said DNA on the Dremel's buttons, cord, and bits was *"at least one trillion times more likely"* to come from Robinson as a major contributor. In the recorded interview played in court, Twiggs said Robinson had asked for a Dremel, saying *"he wanted to engrave messages on bullets,"* about a month before ([Day 4 transcript](/court/Preliminary_Hearing/Day_4)). On this view, the tool, the DNA, and the witness all point one way.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators separate two questions: **who engraved the rounds**, and **what the engravings mean**. On meaning, the fired case's text is furry-fandom copypasta, not a slogan, and Robinson reportedly called the engravings *"mostly a big meme"* ([engraved casings](/Narrative/fed_narrative/engraved-casings-motive)). In the same recorded interview, Twiggs placed the Dremel request alongside talk of a planned hunting trip with his family. Anti-fascist song lyrics on a round are ideology-flavoured, but they are not a stated grievance against Charlie Kirk.

On origin, investigators want the full record of how the rifle and its rounds moved from the woods to the laboratory. The rifle turned up only after early local searches and a K-9 pass found nothing, and after federal agents reportedly directed junior officers to search again ([gun discovery sequence](/Proof_Not_Tyler/Gun_Discovery_Sequence)). Photographs of all four casings have not been released in full. Until they are, investigators say, the engravings are a story the public was told, not an exhibit it has seen.

Finally, the engravings sit on a rifle that the ATF could not tie to the fatal wound, and most investigators have concluded the killing itself was an [exploding microphone](/Mic/overview), not the government's .30-06 narrative.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

The engravings do two jobs for the State: they connect Robinson to the rifle, and they supply a motive. If the tool-mark and DNA results hold, the first job is real. The second is an inference from memes. For a defendant who may be framed, the gap between engraving a round and firing it at a person is the whole case.

## What would settle it

* Full-resolution photographs of all four engraved casings and the "test shot" case from the home.
* The complete chain of custody for the rifle and each cartridge, with handlers and times.
* The ATF tool-mark and biology reports in full, with bench notes.
* The date and location the rifle was first photographed in place.
