# 45. ROTC-linked circulation of the only roof-runner videos

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page claims as fact that any student, cadet, ROTC program, university, or official
filmed, edited, or released any video improperly, or had any knowledge of or role in Charlie Kirk's
death. No person is named. [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is charged, not convicted, and
presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

On September 10, 2025, a figure was seen on the roof of the [Losee Center](/UVU/Losee_Center) at Utah
Valley University, then seen dropping off the roof and running. The State says that figure was Tyler
Robinson. Almost everything the public knows about that figure comes from a handful of short clips: a
view of someone on the roof, a ground-side view of someone coming off the corner, and surveillance cuts
of a runner.

Citizen investigators say the earliest of these roof-runner clips reached the public through people
connected to the campus ROTC world. This site has not documented who first posted each clip. What it
has documented is that the UVU campus map labels a building near that area "Army ROTC," and that the
claimed date of the ground-side roof-jumper video [changed as it circulated](/Roof/roof-jumper-video-dating).
The question: when so few videos exist, does it matter whose hands they passed through?

## The fed slop side, in full

UVU is a big campus with a military-affiliated student community, and its campus map places an Army
ROTC building near the route investigators trace from the roof. It would be surprising if nobody connected to ROTC had a phone
out that day. People share what they film. A clip's first poster tells you nothing about whether the
figure in it was Robinson, and treating a student's military ties as a clue is guilt by association.
On this view, the footage matters; the uploader does not.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators say the uploader matters because there is so little footage. Thousands of people were in
the courtyard, yet the public has only a few seconds of the roof and the run. When the whole identity
case rests on a few clips, investigators say three facts about each clip have to be known: who filmed
it, who trimmed it, and who decided to release it.

They point to gaps the site already records. A widely shared claim says the released rooftop-view
surveillance clip [begins at 12:23:34 pm](/CoverUp/Rooftop_Video_Trimmed), after the runner was already
moving. The ground-side jumper video's claimed date shifted as it spread. And the one person who
[says he filmed the roof figure](/Witnesses/911-calls-rooftop-witness) describes all-black tactical gear,
not the t-shirt and jeans in the State's account. Investigators say that if the clips that did circulate
came through a narrow, military-adjacent channel while other footage stayed unreleased, that is a
source-control question, not a detail.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

The roof figure is the heart of the identification. If the only public clips were selected, trimmed,
or dated by a small set of hands, the public cannot check whether the person on the roof and the person
running are the same, or whether either is Tyler Robinson. Provenance is how any video becomes evidence.

## What would settle it

* The original files of each roof and runner clip, with device metadata and first upload times.
* The identity of each original filmer, and whether police or the FBI interviewed them.
* The full, untrimmed surveillance files for the Losee Center roof and the runner path.
* Whether any other bystander footage of the roof was collected and never released.
