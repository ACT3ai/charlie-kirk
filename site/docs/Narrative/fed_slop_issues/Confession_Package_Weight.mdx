# 7. How much weight the confession package really carries

:::caution Attributed claims only
Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has pleaded **not guilty**, and is presumed innocent.
This site makes no claim that Lance Twiggs, any family member, or any officer lied, forged anything, or
committed a crime. The question here is what the evidence is, how it was gathered, and how it has been
described.
:::

## The issue in plain words

People often say Tyler Robinson "confessed." It is worth asking what that word is standing on.
There is **no signed, sworn confession** from Robinson to police. What exists is a **package** of other
material, mostly gathered from or through other people.

The state's hearing exhibits, as quoted in its memorandum, include a **transcript of the police interview
with Lance Twiggs** (Exhibit 16), **text messages** between Robinson and Twiggs (Exhibit 16.1, at least 17
pages), **Discord messages** (Exhibit 16.2, 2 pages), and a **handwritten note** (Exhibit 16.3). This issue
asks how heavy that package really is.

## The fed slop side, in full

The fed-slop side says the package is **devastating**, and denying it is bad faith. The texts
reportedly include "I am, I'm sorry" when asked if he did it, and "I had enough of his hatred. some hate
can't be negotiated out." A note left for Twiggs reportedly reads that the writer "had the opportunity to
take out Charlie Kirk" and took it. There is the recorded roommate interview, and there are reported family
admissions. The state's memorandum summarizes it this way: that Robinson "admitted to Mr. Twiggs and other
friends that he had shot Mr. Kirk."

Taken at face value, that is strong evidence, and it deserves to be stated plainly rather than waved
away.

## What citizen investigators say

Citizen investigators say every piece of the package runs through **someone else's device, a photo
of a screen, or a roommate's account**, not a contemporaneous statement by Robinson himself.

* **Unsigned notes.** A handwritten note is not a signed statement, and its authorship has not been tested
  in open court.
* **A cooperating roommate.** Twiggs cooperated and reportedly received **limited immunity**. He is not charged
  as the shooter. Investigators say that is exactly the kind of witness whose account needs testing.
* **Screens, not seizures.** Investigators were reportedly allowed to **photograph the roommate's screen**, and
  the state has indicated it will **not offer forensic extractions** of the devices.
* **No signed confession and no guilty plea.** Robinson has maintained innocence.

The Discord post has a further timing problem, covered in the custody issue: a Miranda warning logged at
**6:25 PM**, before the message went out. Investigators also say commentators who said he "admitted guilt"
**overstated what the charging documents say**. The documents allege admissions through messages. They are
not a confession under oath.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If the confession is really a set of messages that passed through other people's phones, then its
weight depends on whether those messages were authentic, complete, and sent by him at the times claimed.
The Panguitch dinner and the custody clock both put that in doubt. A framing, investigators say, would not
need a signed statement. It would need exactly this: messages that read like a confession and that no one
has been allowed to test at the source.

## What would settle it

* Full forensic extractions of Robinson's and Twiggs's phones, offered in court with timestamps and time zones.
* Discord's server-side records for the posting account.
* Handwriting analysis of the note by an examiner the defense can cross-examine.
* The complete Twiggs interview recording and the terms of his immunity agreement.
