# 3. Does court or hearing video show Tyler Robinson on the roof in 4K?

:::caution Attributed claims only
Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent. This
page does not say any commentator, agent, or prosecutor said anything untrue. It compares public
descriptions of hearing video with the hearing transcript and with the footage that is public. This site
makes no claim that any living person or organization committed a crime or had foreknowledge.
:::

## The issue in plain words

The state says a figure seen on the roof of the **Losee Center** at UVU on September 10, 2025 was
Tyler Robinson, and that the rifle shot came from there. The public has only seen grainy, distant clips of
that figure.

During and after the **July 2026 preliminary hearing**, a claim spread: that the courtroom had been shown a
far better copy, enhanced HD and some 4K, with a clear face, a clear plate, clear clothing, and rooftop
actions timed to the shot. If that were true, the blur in public clips would not matter.

So this issue asks one factual question. **What did the hearing video actually show, and did any of it
show a clear face firing a rifle?**

## Side by side

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fed Slop Side** | Commentators who attended the hearing (Benny Johnson among them) say they saw enhanced HD and some 4K: a crystal-clear face, plate, clothes, and rooftop actions timed to the shot. The public blur does not matter because the courtroom saw the good copy. |
| **Citizen Investigators** | Other same-day attendees disputed it: most clips were already public and none was face-clear 4K of a shot. The circulating roof footage is grainy, with no gun, no recoil, and no firing. |

## The fed slop side, in full

The fed-slop side says commentators who attended the hearing, Benny Johnson among them, described
seeing enhanced HD and some 4K footage: a crystal-clear face, a license plate, clothes, and rooftop actions
timed to the shot. On this view, the public blur is irrelevant because the court saw the good copy, and
people who argue from the blurry clips are arguing from the wrong evidence.

In fairness, the state did play an enhanced compilation at the hearing, and a prosecutor argued that the
enhanced version makes it easier to "truly see what's going on." That part is real.

## What citizen investigators say

Other attendees on the same day disputed the description. Their account is that most clips shown were
**already public**, and that **none was face-clear 4K of a shot being fired**.

The hearing transcript supports a narrower picture than "crystal-clear 4K." FBI Agent David Hull described
**State's Exhibit 12.1** as a **compilation** of UVU camera video, prepared by the Utah County Attorney's
Office, showing the movements of the person he believes is Robinson around campus from September 10 into
the morning of September 11. He testified that it has **zooms that were not a function of the cameras**,
that some people were **blurred**, and that **red circles** were added. That is an edited compilation of
campus surveillance, not a new high-resolution camera.

The rooftop footage that circulates is grainy. Reviewers report **no visible rifle, no recoil, and no
muzzle flash** at the moment of the shot. Investigators say that if a clear 4K recording of the figure
firing existed, it would be the single most powerful exhibit in the case, and it would not need to be
described secondhand.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

Identification is the heart of the case against Robinson. If the only rooftop images are distant,
compressed, and enhanced after the fact, the identification rests on interpretation, not on a clear
picture. Claims that "the courtroom saw 4K" can close public debate without anyone ever seeing that
footage. Investigators say a framing case would need exactly that: confidence about images the public is
not allowed to check.

## What would settle it

* Public release of State's Exhibits 12.1 and 12.4, with the native-resolution source files behind them.
* The camera model, resolution, and frame rate of each UVU camera used in the compilation.
* The enhancement work product: what was zoomed or altered, by whom, and when.
* Any footage, from any camera, that shows the figure aiming or firing.
