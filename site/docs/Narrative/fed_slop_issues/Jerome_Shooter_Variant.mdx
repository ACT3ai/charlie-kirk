# 36. The "Jerome" alternate-shooter variant

:::caution Attributed claims only
This site makes no claim that any named person, including anyone called Jerome, fired, was present in
any role, or was involved in Charlie Kirk's death in any way. Tyler Robinson is claimed to be the
shooter; he is charged, not convicted, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

Soon after September 10, 2025, a family of online claims grew up around one idea: the shot, if there
was one, came from a different angle than the Losee Center roof. Some of these claims point to the
south, some to a closer position, and some to the stage area itself.

A few versions attach a name to that different origin. One of those is the "Jerome" variant, which
names a person called Jerome as an alternative source of the shot.

This site has not found any documented fact that supports the Jerome variant, and it does not repeat
any identifying detail about the person named. This page records it because it is part of the public
argument, and because the fed slop side often uses it to dismiss every question at once.

## The fed slop side, in full

The official side says there is one person seen jumping off the roof, one rifle found in the woods, and
one defendant. The judge bound Tyler Robinson over for trial on September 1, 2026. Alternate-shooter
names, on this view, are invented by people who reject the charging package and will attach a name to
anyone. Treating them as serious gives oxygen to harassment of private people.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators say the Jerome variant is not their main line, and they do not rely on it. Their main
conclusion is that Charlie Kirk was killed by an [explosive at his microphone](/Mic/overview), not by a
rifle round from any angle.

What they do say is that the general "different angle" question has not been answered. The state's own
brief describes a downward path, yet the damage it quotes runs down the left side of the neck from C2 to
C7, which critics say does not fit the roof. One acoustic analysis argues the decisive sound came from
the south. Another argues the sound and impact appear at the same moment on video, which would put any
source much closer than 150 yards.

A bind-over is a probable-cause finding. It means there is enough evidence to go to trial. It does not
rule out other angles or other mechanisms, and it is not a verdict. That is why investigators say the
angle question, in every form, stays open until the evidence is produced.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If the wound or the sound points away from the roof, the case against the man on the roof weakens,
whoever else is or is not involved. The fair way to test the angle is with the wound record and the
audio, not by naming someone online.

## What would settle it

* The medical examiner's wound track, with direction and angle, reviewed by independent experts.
* The raw multitrack audio from every device at the event, for acoustic direction-finding.
* The state's 3D scan of the courtyard, with line-of-sight tests from the roof and other positions.
* Full native video of the tent at the moment of the shot.
