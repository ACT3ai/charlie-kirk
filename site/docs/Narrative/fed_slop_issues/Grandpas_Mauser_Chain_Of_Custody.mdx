# 24. Grandpa's Mauser and its chain of custody

:::caution Attributed claims only
Questions about how the rifle was found and handled are chain-of-custody questions, not accusations. This site makes no claim that any officer, agent, laboratory, or family member planted, moved, or mishandled anything, or had foreknowledge of the killing. The family's views are reported second-hand and have not been confirmed on the record. Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

The rifle in this case is a scoped **Mauser Model 98** in **.30-06**, described as belonging to Tyler Robinson's **grandfather**. The State says it was found in a **wooded area** near campus after the shooting, **wrapped in a dark towel**. DNA consistent with Robinson was reported on the trigger, the rifle, the fired case, two unfired cartridges, the towel, and a screwdriver.

For supporters of the official account, that ends the argument about the weapon. Citizen investigators say a family rifle carrying family DNA answers a different question from who carried it to campus, and who fired it, on September 10, 2025.

## The fed slop side, in full

Stated at full strength: this is the heaviest part of the State's case, and this site says so. The texts attributed to Robinson have him worrying about *"what my old man would do if I didn't bring back grandpas rifle,"* and, as read by an agent in court, saying *"Only thing I left was the rifle wrapped in a towel. I'll bet that canine sniffed it out"* ([Day 4 transcript](/court/Preliminary_Hearing/Day_4)). DNA on the **towel** is harder to explain as ordinary household contact than DNA on a stock, and DNA on the **fired case** points to whoever loaded it. An ATF report identified other cartridge cases from his home as fired in that rifle.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators start with how the rifle was found. According to the master case notes, a first arm-to-arm search by local officers found nothing, and a K-9 search found nothing. Then, they say, federal agents told three junior local officers to search again, and the rifle was found ([gun discovery sequence](/Proof_Not_Tyler/Gun_Discovery_Sequence)). A weapon found where searchers had already been is a chain-of-custody question that needs times and handlers.

Second, DNA does not carry a date. A rifle that lived in a family home has been touched by the family. At the hearing, the defense called the ATF's DNA chief, who testified on **transfer and error-rate limits**, and an FBI analyst who described **mixture** profiles on the towel and screwdriver ([hearing witnesses](/court/hearing-witnesses)). No latent fingerprint match and no gunshot-residue result on Robinson have been publicly reported ([DNA and the rifle](/Narrative/fed_narrative/dna-and-the-rifle)).

Third, provenance itself is disputed. Analysts describe the rifle as a **commercial Mauser 98**, not a military K98, and note that Mauser 98s were not chambered in .30-06 during World War I ([the Mauser page](/Gun_Bullet/Mauser_30_06_Rifle)). Investigator Baron Coleman says, citing people who have spoken with the family, that they deny Robinson confessed to them ([family denial](/Tyler_Robinson/baron-coleman-family-denial)). And the ATF could not tie the autopsy fragment to this rifle at all.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If the rifle came from his family, his DNA on it is expected. The live questions are who had it that week, how it reached the woods, and whether it fired the fatal round — which most investigators doubt, having concluded the killing was an [exploding microphone](/Mic/overview) rather than the government's .30-06 narrative. A towel is not a firing record.

## What would settle it

* The full chain of custody from discovery to laboratory, with every handler and time.
* Search logs and body-camera video for each pass through the wooded area, including the K-9 search.
* The complete DNA reports, with number of contributors and statistics on each item.
* Latent-print results, including exclusions, and whether GSR sampling was attempted.
* Records of where the rifle was kept in the weeks before September 10.
