# 24. Grandpa's Mauser and its chain of custody

:::caution Attributed claims only
Questions about how the rifle was found and handled are chain-of-custody questions, not accusations. This site makes no claim that any officer, agent, laboratory, or family member planted, moved, or mishandled anything, or had foreknowledge of the killing. The family's views are reported second-hand and have not been confirmed on the record. Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

The rifle in this case is a scoped **Mauser Model 98** in **.30-06**, described as belonging to [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson)'s **grandfather**. The State says it was found in a **[wooded area](/Locations/Wooded_Area_Gun_Recovery)** near campus after the shooting, **wrapped in a dark towel**. DNA consistent with Robinson was reported on the trigger, the rifle, the fired case, two unfired cartridges, the towel, and a screwdriver.

For supporters of the official account, that ends the argument about the weapon. Citizen investigators say a family rifle carrying family DNA answers a different question from who carried it to campus, and who fired it, on September 10, 2025. Presence in a towel is not a firing video. Most investigators have concluded [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) was killed by an [exploding microphone](/Mic/overview), so even a perfectly documented family Mauser would still be the government's gun, not the investigators' mechanism.

## The fed slop side, in full

Stated at full strength: this is the heaviest part of the State's case, and this site says so.

The texts attributed to Robinson have him worrying about *"what my old man would do if I didn't bring back grandpas rifle,"* and, as read by an agent in court, saying *"Only thing I left was the rifle wrapped in a towel. I'll bet that canine sniffed it out"* ([Day 4 transcript](/court/Preliminary_Hearing/Day_4)). DNA on the **towel** is harder to explain as ordinary household contact than DNA on a stock, and DNA on the **fired case** points to whoever loaded it. An ATF report identified other cartridge cases from his home as fired in that rifle.

At the July 2026 hearing, FBI analyst Amanda Bakker testified that mixed DNA on the towel and on a screwdriver from the Losee roof had two contributors. Robinson was described as the majority donor; Lance Twiggs as the minority. Likelihood ratios were read in court as **1.7 octillion** times more likely (towel) and **30 quintillion** times more likely (screwdriver) if both were contributors than if Twiggs and an unknown unrelated person were. Official-side accounts treated that as the federal period at the end of the sentence: grandpa's gun, Robinson's DNA, roommate's DNA on the wrap, case closed. The rifle itself was described in the report as item seven, serial **8863**, "towel from around Mauser."

Official-narrative commentators also used a negative: **.30-06 ammunition was found in the home, and no .30-06 rifle was found there.** Where was it, they asked, if not in the woods? Combined with the [engraved casings](/Gun_Bullet/Engraved_Bullets_Towel_Implausibility) and the Dremel from the house, the official package is: family rifle, family ammo, family DNA, dumped where the texts said it would be.

Kash Patel, as FBI Director, publicly confirmed DNA on the towel and screwdriver. Official-side accounts treated that federal seal as the end of provenance.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators start with how the rifle was found. According to the master case notes, a first arm-to-arm search by local officers found nothing, and a K-9 search found nothing. Then, they say, federal agents told three junior local officers to search again, and the rifle was found ([gun discovery sequence](/Proof_Not_Tyler/Gun_Discovery_Sequence)). A weapon found where searchers had already been is a chain-of-custody question that needs times and handlers ([Mauser custody page](/Law_Enforcement/rifle-chain-of-custody)). **Candace Owens** and Baron Coleman have alleged, as commentary, that federal agents "lied about the gun location." Those are reported allegations against institutions, not findings of this site.

Second, DNA does not carry a date. A rifle that lived in a family home has been touched by the family. That is the [DNA-is-not-a-shot chain](/Narrative/chains/dna-does-not-prove-a-shot). At the hearing, the defense called the ATF's DNA chief, who testified on **transfer and error-rate limits**, and an FBI analyst who described **mixture** profiles on the towel and screwdriver ([hearing witnesses](/court/hearing-witnesses)). Caitlin Oliver, as reported, said a "major contributor" speaks only to quantity of DNA, not to any activity or when it was deposited, and that a profile can be developed where the contributor never touched the item — secondary transfer. She also said the headline "one trillion times" figure is a **reporting cap**: it could be quintillions or octillions; the lab chose a trillion "because it has meaning to people." Swabs were mixtures of as many as four or five contributors. Elimination samples reportedly placed Robinson's **father's** DNA on the rifle and Twiggs's DNA on the engraving tool. No latent fingerprint match and no gunshot-residue result on Robinson have been publicly reported, and no evidence that the rifle had been recently fired was entered ([DNA and the rifle](/Narrative/fed_narrative/dna-and-the-rifle); [no GSR](/Proof_Not_Tyler/No_GSR_And_Physical_Tests)).

Third, provenance itself is disputed. Analysts describe the rifle as a **commercial Mauser 98**, not a military K98, and note that Mauser 98s were not chambered in .30-06 during World War I ([the Mauser page](/Gun_Bullet/Mauser_30_06_Rifle); [weapon file](/Tyler_Robinson/Weapon_Rifle)). Investigator Baron Coleman says, citing people who have spoken with the family, that they deny Robinson confessed to them ([family denial](/Tyler_Robinson/baron-coleman-family-denial)). **Project Constitution** asked after the hearing photos whether a military wing safety would even clear the low-mounted scope.

Fourth, the ATF could not tie the autopsy fragment to this rifle at all ([ATF fragment](/Gun_Bullet/ATF_Fragment_Inconclusive)). Karner's notes, investigators say, recorded **dust and debris inside the barrel** ([barrel dust](/Gun_Bullet/Barrel_Dust_And_Diameter)). A recently fired .30-06, they argue, would have blown that dust out. Official-side accounts say debris is compatible with a single shot and a woods dump.

## When this fight ran

* **10–12 September 2025.** Rifle described as grandpa's Mauser, towel wrap, woods north of UVU. Official-side accounts treated provenance as closed on day two.
* **April 2026.** Unsealed warrants and the media fight over the ATF fragment. Official line: DNA plus casing. Investigators: the fragment still does not tool-mark.
* **7–10 July 2026.** Hearing DNA week. Towel and screwdriver mixtures. Official-side accounts: "avalanche," "direct DNA all over the rifle." Investigators: mixtures, transfer, father's DNA, no GSR, dust in the barrel.
* **13–14 July 2026.** Official-side "ammo in the house, no rifle in the house" posts. Same days, Project Constitution and Candace Owens on barrel dust and the wing-safety question.

Kash Patel's public confirmation of towel and screwdriver DNA is the federal seal official-side accounts wanted. Investigators call a federal seal on a Utah charging theory the opposite of independent review. See [Kash Patel and the federal seal](/Narrative/fed_slop_issues/Kash_Patel_Federal_Seal) and [DNA and the rifle](/Narrative/fed_narrative/dna-and-the-rifle).

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If the rifle came from his family, his DNA on it is expected. The live questions are who had it that week, how it reached the woods, and whether it fired the fatal round — which most investigators doubt, having concluded the killing was an exploding microphone rather than the government's .30-06 narrative. A towel is not a firing record. The [FBI](/FBI/overview) seal on household DNA does not convert contact into a shot.

For a defendant who may be framed, a family hunting rifle is the easiest object in the world to wrap and leave where a K-9 will later be sent.

## What would settle it

* The full chain of custody from discovery to laboratory, with every handler and time.
* Search logs and body-camera video for each pass through the wooded area, including the K-9 search.
* The complete DNA reports, with number of contributors and statistics on each item.
* Latent-print results, including exclusions, and whether GSR sampling was attempted.
* Records of where the rifle was kept in the weeks before September 10.
* Photographs of the rifle in place, before it was moved, and a published note on barrel debris.
