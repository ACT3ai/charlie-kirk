# 21. The rooftop drop and gun handling as proof of an untrained apprentice

:::caution Attributed claims only
Smooth movement on video can mean rehearsal, athleticism, panic with lucky footwork, or over-reading a short clip. Nothing on this page says any living person is a trained operator, committed a crime, or had foreknowledge of the killing. Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

Seconds after the shot at **12:23 PM** on September 10, 2025, video shows a figure leave the firing position on the **[Losee Center](/UVU/Losee_Center)** roof, cross to the **northeast corner**, and drop to the grass below. Citizen measurements put that drop at about **16 to 20 feet**. The *New York Times* timeline, published September 12, 2025, said the gunman "jumped at least 15 feet onto the ground." The figure then runs toward Campus Drive and a wooded area.

The State says that figure is [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson), a 22-year-old electrical apprentice. This issue asks whether the way the figure moved fits that description, or points to someone who had practiced the exit. Most citizen investigators already conclude [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) was killed by an [explosive at the microphone](/Mic/overview), so the roof figure's skill is a separate identity question: even if someone left that roof, was it the man charged?

## Side by side

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fed Slop Side** | A 16-20 ft drop with clean foot placement is what a 22-year-old electrical apprentice can do in the moment. No rehearsal required. |
| **Citizen Investigators** | The egress looks practiced: no hesitation, a gun transfer, the exact corner. The prosecution never showed prior scouting of that roof. |

## The fed slop side, in full

This side has fair points, and it is the story official-side commentators repeated from the first stills through the July 2026 hearing.

Adrenaline compresses decision time. People do fast, reckless things after a gunshot without any training. The charging affidavit says the figure **lowered himself** onto the grass — a hang-and-drop, not a blind leap — which can look controlled while still being ordinary movement. Short clips and still frames can hide a pause that existed in continuous video. The State also alleges morning movement on campus, so the corner need not have been new.

At the July 2026 preliminary hearing, Agent David Hull walked the courtroom through campus surveillance. He testified that after the reported shot the figure stood up, moved north across the roof, headed to the northeast corner, **lowered himself**, jumped onto grass, and moved toward Campus Drive carrying "some kind of an object." Prosecutors told the court the clip does **not** show the shot itself. Official-side accounts still posted the drop as "the moment" and treated the hang-and-run as identity-closed: same body, same day, same roof.

A former Secret Service campus-security commentator, quoted by network coverage in October 2025, said a 22-year-old who found an open roof access "got extremely lucky" if he had not scouted it — which the official side reads as luck plus adrenaline, not a trained team. Height estimates of 16–20 feet are citizen measurements. One later citizen still put a straight drop closer to **12 feet**. Either number is survivable for a young adult who hangs from a ledge.

On this view, the drop proves nothing about rehearsal. A 22-year-old electrical apprentice can hang, drop, and run.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators point to the clock and the quality of the motion.

Four stills are said to show the figure moving from the firing position to the northeast corner in **about 25 seconds**, between roughly **12:23:29 and 12:23:54**, with the drop at about **12:24** ([roof timeline](/Roof/roof-timeline); [roof-jumping timeline](/Timeline/roof-jumping-timeline)). Commentator **Sam Parker** wrote on July 29, 2026 that the jumper *"headed straight for that corner,"* never paused to look around, and *"didn't even fumble around with the gun."* His line, quoted on the [trained roof drop](/Roof/trained-roof-drop) page: *"You simply won't convince me that these are the actions of a scrawny kid who's never killed anyone before."* He also noted that public hearing summaries did not show the prosecution establishing that Robinson had scoped that corner as an exit. An untrained person, he argued, might have gone back down the stairs the way he came.

Second, the State's own witness describes a carried object through the whole move. Hull testified it was *"very apparent he has an item in his hand"* at the corner, and that on landing *"it's almost like he either drops it or goes into a crouched position,"* then rises with the item again ([Day 2 transcript](/court/Preliminary_Hearing/Day_2)). Hull also testified that after the figure went over the railing to the position, *"there is no longer a visible limp or gait"* — the stiff right leg the affidavit used to identify the walker. That limp-then-no-limp splice is the same identity fight as the [roof figure claims](/Roof/roof-figure-not-tyler) and the [tactical roof run](/Roof/tactical-roof-run).

Third, investigators note what was not shown. Public hearing summaries did not show prior scouting of that corner. **Project Constitution** circulated a December 2025 visual map of the route and asked how a lone 22-year-old with a visible limp navigated a busy campus, fired, dropped, and vanished. **Ryan Matta** posted on September 13, 2025 that a different clip showed a man leaving the crowd with a gun, and wrote that Tyler *"didn't jump off the roof with a rifle."* **Candace Owens** said from October 2025 onward that Charlie was set up and that the rooftop-rifle story does not match the metal recovered from the wound.

Fourth, the [military lateral crawl](/Roof/military-lateral-crawl) into prone, the [missing muzzle flash](/Roof/no-muzzle-flash-or-recoil), and the [decoy / no-shot-from-roof](/Roof/decoy-no-shot-from-roof) claims sit on the same seconds. If the roof figure never fired, the drop is still a practiced exit — just not the shot that killed Kirk.

## When this fight ran

* **10–12 September 2025.** First stills and the NYT "at least 15 feet" drop. Official-narrative accounts treated the jumper as Robinson from the first night.
* **October 2025.** Network rooftop-access pieces. Official side: easy access, lucky apprentice. Investigators: a waist-high rail is a known perch, not a surprise.
* **18 December 2025.** Project Constitution's route map. Citizen push on how the figure vanished after the drop.
* **7–10 July 2026.** Preliminary hearing. Hull's object-in-hand and limp-gone testimony. Official-side accounts posted the roof video as closed identity.
* **29 July 2026.** Sam Parker's "Peter Pan" thread on the 16–20 ft corner. That is the date the untrained-apprentice fight went loud on X.
* **August 2026.** Citizen re-measurements of the drop height (some ~12 ft) and continued identity arguments.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If the roof figure moved like someone who had rehearsed the perch and the exit, that is a skill profile the charging documents never proved for a first-time campus shooter. Combined with the limp that vanishes on the roof, investigators say it raises the possibility that the rooftop figure and the man charged are not the same person. That is the [chain that the rooftop figure is not identified as Robinson](/Narrative/chains/rooftop-figure-not-robinson), and it is one of the pillars of [Proof Not Tyler](/Proof_Not_Tyler/overview) and [Tyler Robinson Not Assassin](/Tyler_Robinson_Not_Assassin/overview).

A practiced drop also does not prove a rooftop .30-06 killed Kirk. Investigators conclude the fatal event was the microphone. The drop still matters, because the State's identity case uses that body as Robinson.

## What would settle it

* A surveyed height from the roof gravel to the grass at the northeast corner.
* The continuous, native-resolution video of the drop, not four stills.
* Any evidence the State holds of prior visits to that roof or that corner.
* A published gait and movement comparison by a named examiner.
* Uncut footage showing whether the carried object is a rifle, a towel wrap, or something else through the whole hang.
