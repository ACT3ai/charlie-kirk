# 21. The rooftop drop and gun handling as proof of an untrained apprentice

:::caution Attributed claims only
Smooth movement on video can mean rehearsal, athleticism, panic with lucky footwork, or over-reading a short clip. Nothing on this page says any living person is a trained operator, committed a crime, or had foreknowledge of the killing. Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent.
:::

## The issue in plain words

Seconds after the shot at **12:23 PM** on September 10, 2025, video shows a figure leave the firing position on the **Losee Center** roof, cross to the **northeast corner**, and drop to the grass below. Citizen measurements put that drop at about **16 to 20 feet**. The figure then runs toward Campus Drive and a wooded area.

The State says that figure is Tyler Robinson, a 22-year-old electrical apprentice. This issue asks whether the way the figure moved fits that description, or points to someone who had practiced the exit.

## Side by side

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fed Slop Side** | A 16-20 ft drop with clean foot placement is what a 22-year-old electrical apprentice can do in the moment. No rehearsal required. |
| **Citizen Investigators** | The egress looks practiced: no hesitation, a gun transfer, the exact corner. The prosecution never showed prior scouting of that roof. |

## The fed slop side, in full

This side has fair points. Adrenaline compresses decision time, and people do fast, reckless things after a gunshot without any training. The affidavit says the figure **lowered himself** onto the grass — a hang-and-drop, not a blind leap — which can look controlled while still being ordinary movement. Short clips and still frames can hide a pause that existed in continuous video. The State also alleges morning movement on campus, so the corner need not have been new. And the height estimates are citizen measurements, not a survey.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators point to the clock. Four stills are said to show the figure moving from the firing position to the northeast corner in **about 25 seconds**, between roughly **12:23:29 and 12:23:54**, with the drop at about **12:24** ([roof timeline](/Roof/roof-timeline)). Commentator Sam Parker wrote that the jumper *"headed straight for that corner,"* never paused to look around, and *"didn't even fumble around with the gun"* ([trained roof drop](/Roof/trained-roof-drop)).

Second, the State's own witness describes a carried object through the whole move. Agent David Hull testified it was *"very apparent he has an item in his hand"* at the corner, and that on landing *"it's almost like he either drops it or goes into a crouched position,"* then rises with the item again ([Day 2 transcript](/court/Preliminary_Hearing/Day_2)). Hull also testified that after the figure went over the railing to the position, *"there is no longer a visible limp or gait"* — the stiff right leg the affidavit used to identify the walker.

Third, investigators note what was not shown. Public hearing summaries did not show the prosecution establishing that Robinson had scouted that corner as an exit. An untrained person, they argue, might simply have gone back down the stairs the way he came.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

If the roof figure moved like someone who had rehearsed the perch and the exit, that is a skill profile, not a description of a first-time campus shooter. Combined with the limp that vanishes on the roof, investigators say it raises the possibility that the rooftop figure and the man charged are not the same person.

## What would settle it

* A surveyed height from the roof gravel to the grass at the northeast corner.
* The continuous, native-resolution video of the drop, not four stills.
* Any evidence the State holds of prior visits to that roof or that corner.
* A published gait and movement comparison by a named examiner.
