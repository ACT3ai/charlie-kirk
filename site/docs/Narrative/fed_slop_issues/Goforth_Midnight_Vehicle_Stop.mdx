# 27. Officer Goforth's midnight vehicle stop

:::caution Attributed claims only
Nothing on this page claims that Officer Goforth, Agent David Hull, or any other officer invented,
altered, or misstated anything. Tyler Robinson is claimed to be the shooter; he is charged, not
convicted, has not entered a guilty plea, and is presumed innocent. The questions below are about gaps in
the public record, not accusations against any person.
:::

## The issue in plain words

About twelve hours after [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) was killed at
[Utah Valley University](/Locations/Public-Speaking-Event-UVU), a gray Dodge Challenger reportedly
pulled up to the corner of Campus Drive and 800 South, on the edge of campus. A Spanish Fork
police officer named Goforth, working security that night, spoke with the driver for a short time.
Hearing coverage describes the contact as roughly **20 seconds**.

The prosecution uses that contact, placed at about **12:30 AM on September 11, 2025**, to put
[Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) back in Orem after midnight. It is the only item in the
public record that puts his car at UVU after dark with a named officer attached. The full hearing
exchange is on [The Goforth Midnight Stop](/Tyler_Robinson/goforth-midnight-stop).

The trouble is timing. Staff at Cowboy's Smokehouse in Panguitch, about **209 road miles** south,
say a man they recognized as Robinson paid for dinner at **9:47 PM** the same night. Both accounts
cannot comfortably be true on an ordinary drive. That collision is
[Chain: Panguitch breaks the evening](/Narrative/chains/panguitch-breaks-the-evening).

## The fed slop side, in full

The official-side argument is simple and, stated fairly, it has a real hook. An officer saw a car
trying to get back onto campus after midnight, felt something was off, and wrote down part of the
plate. When the case broke, he ran it, and the registration came back to Tyler Robinson and a
relative. That is independent of the family bringing Robinson in, and it came before the surrender.
It also fits the messages the state says were recovered about retrieving the rifle from the wooded
area once police left.

On Day 2 of the July 2026 preliminary hearing, Agent David Hull walked the court through State's
Exhibit 12. The last clip, as he described it, shows a vehicle at Campus Drive and 800 South
attempting to turn onto campus. Hull said Officer Goforth, Spanish Fork Police, was on security
detail. The contact was "approximately 0:30, 12:30 a.m." on September 11. Hull's phrase for why
Goforth made a note was **"cop intuition."** Two registered owners came back: Tyler Robinson and
Amber Robinson. Hull said the driver was a male Goforth "believed" to be Robinson.

The same night, Hull testified, a Ring doorbell at the Noble household showed a vehicle consistent
with Robinson's Challenger parking at about **12:38 AM**, with a later departure around **1:33–1:35 AM**.
License-plate readers later put plate **T455CE** in Cedar City at about **7:16 AM**. On this view the
Panguitch story is a mistaken identification, and the stop is one more pin in a timeline that already
holds: Orem after midnight, then south toward Washington County in the morning. Official-side
commentators treated the hearing clip as a closed identification of the car and the driver.

## What citizen investigators say

Investigators point first to how thin the record is. Officer Goforth **did not testify**. Everything
the court heard came from Hull relaying what Goforth told him, on [Day 2](/court/Preliminary_Hearing/Day_2).
He said Goforth ran a **"partial plate"** only after a vehicle "or a vehicle matching the description"
was linked to the case. No bodycam clip, written stop report, citation, or field-interview card was
shown. There is no statement about what the driver said.

On cross-examination, defense attorney Kathryn Nester had Hull confirm that the Noble household,
which supplied Ring doorbell video of the car, told investigators the driver was **bald** and that
**three other people** were in the car. Robinson is not bald, and the state's account has him alone.
Project Constitution posted the Ring clip the same day and called the quality too grainy to carry
the identification.

Then there is the drive. From a 9:47 PM card approval in Panguitch, a normal drive to Orem takes
about **3 hours 9 minutes to 3 hours 40 minutes**, arriving roughly **12:56 to 1:27 AM**. Reaching a
12:30 AM contact needs about **77 mph** with no stops, starting on two-lane US-89 during a statewide
manhunt. Investigators say that is not strictly impossible, but it is not the evening the state
describes. Either the Panguitch customer was someone else, the Goforth driver was someone else, or
the night-of timeline has a hole. The [Cowboy's Smokehouse receipt](/Tyler_Robinson/cowboys-smokehouse-receipt)
is the other clock. The [25-hour driving day](/Tyler_Robinson/twenty-five-hour-driving-timeline) is
what the state's story requires if every pin is his.

Citizen investigators also note that gray Challengers are not unique. Footage from KSL's live
broadcast on September 10 shows at least one gray Challenger in the exit line after the shot.
That does not prove a decoy. It does mean a partial plate plus "cop intuition," without the officer
on the stand, is a thin way to lock a capital identification. Candace Owens has walked the same
clock in public: if the Panguitch card is his, he is not behind the wheel when Goforth stops that
car at midnight, he is not the bald man at 12:38, and he is not starting a Maps route off the Noble
street at 1:33 AM.

The Cedar City Maverik stop the next morning, around **7:15–7:17 AM**, is a separate pin. See
[Cedar City Maverik](/Locations/Cedar_City_Maverik). It sits on the southbound I-15 run toward
Washington County. It does not rescue a 12:30 AM Orem contact if the 9:47 PM Panguitch ticket is
his. The longer write-up is
[Panguitch — the town that breaks the timeline](/Tyler_Robinson_Not_Assassin/panguitch-breaks-the-timeline).

## When this fight ran

* **25 September 2025.** National outlets reported that Robinson had circled back near the rifle
  drop and "made contact" with an officer. No named officer yet in most of that coverage.
* **January 2026.** The Panguitch steakhouse story went national. That is when the midnight-Orem
  pin and the 9:47 PM receipt first collided in public.
* **7 July 2026.** Day 2 of the preliminary hearing. Hull names Officer Goforth, 12:30 AM, partial
  plate, "cop intuition." Live blogs (East Idaho News, Post Millennial) ran the clip in real time.
* **11 July – 11 August 2026.** Citizen investigators argued the stop was a late insert to hold
  the Orem-home timeline. Project Constitution called the named encounter a detail that appeared
  only at the hearing.

## Why it matters for Tyler Robinson

The stop carries the whole post-midnight half of the state's timeline, including the rifle-retrieval
story. If the Panguitch receipt is his, the stop cannot be him on any normal drive, and a key
independent identification becomes a second-hand belief about a partial plate. If a central
identification can be this thin here, readers are entitled to ask what else in the package rests on
the same kind of footing. That is why [Proof Not Tyler](/Proof_Not_Tyler/overview) treats geography
as a test, not a color item.

## What would settle it

* Officer Goforth's own report, notes, and bodycam for the contact, and his sworn testimony.
* The partial plate exactly as written, and the full list of vehicles it returned.
* The native video file for the final clip of State's Exhibit 12, with its embedded timestamp.
* The Noble interview report and the full Ring export with metadata.
* Bank records for the Visa debit card that paid the Panguitch check, plus phone location data for
  the evening.
* A public match or mismatch on the last-four card digits. Until that comparison is made, the
  collision stays a collision.
