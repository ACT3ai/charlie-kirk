# Chains of Reasoning

:::caution Attributed claims only
Tyler Robinson is **charged, not convicted**. No person, agency, company, or government named across these chains has been charged with or found responsible for anything relating to Charlie Kirk's death. These are **arguments built by named citizen investigators from public material**, and every chain prints its own strongest objection.
:::

A **chain of reasoning** is a numbered sequence of facts that, taken in order, supports one conclusion at the bottom. It is the opposite of how most of this case gets argued online, where a single striking detail is presented as if it settled everything.

The rules every chain on this site follows:

* **Each link is stated separately**, with its source and a marked **strength** — strong, moderate, weak, or contested.
* **A chain is not stronger than its weakest link merely because it is long.** Where a link is weak, the chain says so and says whether it can stand without it.
* **The strongest objection to the whole chain is printed on the page**, not buried or omitted.
* **The conclusion says what it does and does not reach.** Chain 3 in particular stops short of the attribution people expect, and says why.

## How the three fit together

Chains 1 and 2 are a **pair**, and they should be read in order. Chain 1 is destructive: it takes the official cause of death apart link by link and shows what the .30-06 account has to absorb. Chain 2 is constructive: it builds the alternative from the video, the audio, the burnt object, and the way the scene was treated. Neither is complete alone. Chain 1 without Chain 2 leaves a reader with a hole where a mechanism should be, and Chain 2 without Chain 1 looks like a leap.

Chain 3 is doing a different job. It is not about the mechanism at all — it is about **who had the reach**, and it is the chain most likely to be misused. The first five links rest on recovered position data anyone can reproduce. The sixth link, the step from foreign-registered airframes to a named country, rests on motive rather than on the aircraft, and the page says so in plain language. Getting that distinction right is the difference between an investigation and a rumour mill.

## Why chains are the right format for this case

Almost nothing in the Charlie Kirk record is individually decisive. There is no confession that survives its own timestamp, no ballistic match that individualizes, no autopsy the public has read, no unedited campus footage, no named accomplice. What there is, in quantity, is **converging weak-to-moderate evidence** — and converging weak evidence is exactly the thing human beings reason about worst.

The failure modes run in both directions. Stack twenty soft items and it feels like proof when it is not. Dismiss each soft item individually and you can talk yourself out of a real pattern one item at a time. A chain forces the honest middle: name every link, grade it, show what the conclusion needs, and state what would break it.

Each chain therefore ends with a **"what would settle it"** list. Every item on those lists is a document, a record, or a test that exists or could exist and is not in public hands. That is the same argument the [four proposed federal disclosure laws](/Fix/overview) make, arrived at from the evidence side rather than the legislative side.
