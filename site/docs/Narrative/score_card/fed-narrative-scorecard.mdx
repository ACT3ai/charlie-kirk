# Fed Narrative Score Card

:::caution This scores statements, never people
This card measures **what someone said about a claim**, not their character, honesty, or loyalties. A person can repeat every point in good faith because they trusted a wire report — that is a low score on this card and no accusation at all. **Nothing here suggests any commentator or outlet is paid, controlled, compromised, or acting in bad faith.** Tyler Robinson is charged, not convicted.
:::

## What you are scoring

Ten claims make up the [Fed Narrative](/Narrative/fed_narrative/overview). This card asks, for one commentator, outlet, official, or podcast episode: **for each of the ten, did they repeat it, examine it, or ignore it?**

## The scoring key

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0** | **Repeated as fact.** Stated the official claim without qualification, as settled. |
| **1** | **Repeated with a hedge.** Used it, but with "reportedly," "police say," or similar. |
| **2** | **Named the dispute.** Told the audience the claim is contested and what the counter-evidence is. |
| **3** | **Went to the document.** Cited the underlying filing, booking sheet, transcript, or recording. |
| **—** | **Did not address it.** Not scored; note it separately. |

Maximum on ten items is **30**. A score is meaningful only when the number of "—" items is recorded beside it, because silence is not the same as scrutiny.

## Reading a score

| Band | What it indicates |
|---|---|
| **0–5** | Reproducing the official account. Usually not deception — usually a newsroom working from wire copy on deadline. |
| **6–12** | Aware something is contested but not working from primary material. The largest band in practice. |
| **13–20** | Actively examining the claims. Naming the disputes and their evidence. |
| **21–30** | Working from the documents themselves. Rare, and it is what this card exists to find. |

## Two traps to avoid when using this card

**First, a high score is not a truth score.** Someone can cite every filing and still reach a wrong conclusion, and someone who repeats the official account can turn out to be right. This card measures **method**, not correctness. That distinction is the entire point of it.

**Second, do not score a person on one clip.** Score a **named episode, article, or broadcast**, with a date and a link. Commentators change position as evidence arrives, and several in this case have — publicly and to their credit. A card without a date and a source is gossip with a number attached.
