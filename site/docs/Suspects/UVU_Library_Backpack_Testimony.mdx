Of all the places on the UVU campus that officers treated as a potential crime scene on
September 10, 2025, the **Fulton Library** is the one that has produced the least public
explanation. Officers reportedly recovered a **backpack, a jacket, and gloves** left unattended
at the library bus stops, away from the courtyard where Charlie Kirk was killed. Those items were
sent to an FBI laboratory. Testing on them was then stopped.

This page holds the primary-source audio for that sequence. It matters because the claim had
previously circulated on this site and elsewhere only as a **third-party characterization** of
the hearing — see [Locals Told Not To Investigate](/Suspicious/FBI/locals-told-not-to-investigate),
which lists "obtain the transcript and read the testimony in full" as an open action item. The
video below is a step toward that: it plays the testimony rather than describing it.

**Nothing on this page is a finding that any officer, agent, laboratory examiner, or member of
Charlie Kirk's security detail did anything improper.** Every person named here is living. The
testimony is quoted; the inferences drawn from it by the video's narrator are labeled as such,
and the ordinary-explanation counterpoints are set out at the bottom.

## The dispatch audio

The first segment is UVU dispatch radio traffic from the minutes around the shooting. The
description broadcast over the air places an armed man on a rooftop immediately **east of the
library** — the same end of campus where the abandoned items were later found. Transcribed from
the video:

> "What's going on at UVU? … the CS building is just east of the library. He's wearing jeans,
> black shirt, black mask, long rifle. … There was one shot. We are staying at the front of the
> school by the roundabout. … is on top of the building on the far north side, just east of the
> library, wearing jeans, black shirt, black vest."

Two details in that traffic recur across this investigation: the **mask** and the **long rifle**.
Both belong to the early scanner description of a black-clad figure, which is developed at length
on [Black-Clad Fugitive Escape Path](./Black_Clad_Fugitive_Escape_Path) and in the
[dispatch radio scanner timeline](/Timeline/dispatch-radio-scanner-timeline). The audio is
also the reason the library end of campus was canvassed at all.

## Testimony: why the library was a crime scene

The second segment is cross-examination of **Sergeant Jennifer Faumuina** of UVU Public Safety,
who wrote the September 22 report documenting the overview of the crime scenes. Asked why the
Fulton Library was treated as a potential crime scene, she testified (transcribed from the video):

> "When officers were canvassing the scene, they were also trying to see, right, the route of the
> shooter as well … they determined that the Fulton Library stood out because there were
> unattended items there at the bus stops that were away from the courtyard area. And so to them
> they thought it was suspicious."

Asked what was seized:

> "Yes, you had a backpack, a jacket, as well as some gloves, and there may have been some other
> things. I just don't recall them right now."

She confirmed the items went to the FBI laboratory, and confirmed they were sent along with
information that **the suspect may have shed some of these items as he fled**. The exchange then
turned to why testing stopped:

> **Q:** "At some point, did you decide to pull the plug on that?"
>
> **A:** "At the time when it was already sent, we didn't have more information as far as whether
> or not the evidence that was collected there were probative."
>
> **Q:** "But eventually you communicated to the lab that those items were not in fact connected
> with the case, and there was no need to do further testing."
>
> **A:** "I don't remember communicating that."
>
> **Q:** "If that's in the notes of the FBI or the ATF, would that be inconsistent with what you
> know?"
>
> **A:** "No."

Asked to confirm the bottom line — that the items were eventually determined not to be related
to the case — she answered that this would be fair. So the determination is not in dispute in
her testimony. What she did not recall is **communicating it**.

## Testimony: the FBI laboratory side

The video then plays testimony from **Amanda Baker**, an FBI laboratory examiner, described in
the video as testifying on day 2 about the same items. She confirmed receiving the backpack,
logged as **exhibit 9**, along with a number of items from inside it, and described her own
starting position:

> "So initially, we didn't have any information. So we processed that. Those are items that are
> good for DNA. Not having any information from the investigators, we process them for DNA. As
> the investigation continued, it was determined that that backpack was potentially left behind
> by a bystander, and there was no comparisons needed at that time."

Counsel then read from the laboratory's own notes:

> **Q:** "Well, left behind by a bystander — or, according to your notes at page 20, the backpack
> belongs to Kirk's detail?"
>
> **A:** "…that was not my communication log. That was someone else within the laboratory. That
> was the information that they received."

That is the specific new element this video contributes. Per the testimony as transcribed, the
recorded basis for standing down on the backpack was not simply "a bystander" but a note
attributing the bag to **Charlie Kirk's own security detail** — and the examiner on the stand
identified that note as written by someone else in the laboratory, not by her.

## What the video's narrator argues

The narrator's commentary is opinion and inference, not testimony, and is reproduced here as
such. The argument runs:

* If the backpack is the one recovered at the bus stop, it was found **on the route investigators
  believed the fleeing suspect took** — which is why it was collected in the first place.
* A member of the security detail would have no obvious reason to be at a library bus stop far
  from the [Losee Center](/UVU/Losee_Center) and the courtyard, and no obvious reason to
  leave a backpack, jacket, and **gloves** there on a hot September day.
* Knowing the victim is not, in the narrator's phrasing, a reason to stop forensic testing:
  "Since when is 'I know the victim' accepted as an alibi?" The narrator notes that a large
  share of homicide victims are killed by someone known to them.
* Two witnesses gave, in the narrator's characterization, the same shape of answer — the
  determination stands, but neither recalls originating it. The narrator asks who authored the
  note.

The narrator's central question is a documentary one and can be answered from records rather
than argument: **who wrote the page-20 note, and on whose information?**

## Counterpoints

These ordinary explanations are not rebutted by anything in the video, and a reader should hold
them alongside the above:

* Unattended bags, jackets and gloves accumulate at a campus library bus stop in ordinary life.
  Triaging them out of a capital case is routine, not suppression.
* Laboratory capacity is finite. Once investigators concluded the items were unconnected,
  stopping comparison work is a normal resourcing decision.
* A note written by one laboratory staffer recording what they were told is a **secondhand
  paraphrase**, not a finding. "Belongs to Kirk's detail" may be an imprecise shorthand for
  something narrower.
* Being unable to recall, months later, which supervisor communicated a particular triage
  decision is ordinary memory, not evasion.
* No testimony quoted here establishes whose the items actually were, and none of it establishes
  that any test was improperly cancelled.

## Open questions

1. Who authored the page-20 laboratory note, and what was their source?
2. What is the full evidence log for the backpack, jacket and gloves — date received, tests
   requested, tests completed, and the written basis for cancelling any test?
3. Were the items ever returned, and to whom?
4. Does the recovered jacket or glove set match the clothing described in the dispatch audio
   quoted above?
5. Has the backpack been photographed and disclosed in discovery? See
   [Discovery and Brady Evidence Timeline](/Timeline/discovery-brady-evidence-timeline).

## Laws (Charlie Kirk)

The evidence log for the Fulton Library items, the laboratory communication logs naming who
cancelled testing, and the full unedited UVU dispatch audio are among the disclosures the
