# Charlie's Injuries

A court filing circulated in late July 2026 quotes the medical examiner's findings directly, and
the list of injuries it recites is substantially longer than the single neck wound the public
narrative described. The filing text below is quoted as it appears in the screenshot shared on X;
this page does not assert a cause for any of the listed injuries, and the
[full autopsy report remains non-public](./autopsy_report_not_public).

* **Hemopericardium** — blood in the sac surrounding the heart.
* **Left and right hemothorax** — blood in the chest cavity on *both* sides, around both lungs.
* **Pulmonary apical hemorrhages / hematomas** — bleeding and blood pockets at the tops of the lungs.
* **Bilateral apical and posterior intercostal hemorrhages** — bleeding between the ribs, on both
  sides, at the top and toward the back.
* **Subarachnoid hemorrhage around the cerebellar vermis and parietal regions** — bleeding beneath
  the membrane covering the brain, at the base of the cerebellum and over the upper brain.
* **Multiple disruptions of the thyroid, cricoid, and tracheal cartilages** — the cartilage
  structures of the voice box and windpipe were broken in more than one place.

The second image is the poster's own visual mapping of those terms onto anatomical diagrams —
anterior torso, posterior torso, internal chest, and two brain views. Its own caption reads
*"Illustrative anatomical locations only — no assumed cause."* It is an interpretation aid, not a
medical document, and it does not come from the medical examiner.

## Why this matters to the case

Bilateral findings — blood on both sides of the chest, bleeding between ribs on both sides, brain
hemorrhaging in two separate regions — are the part readers have focused on, because they sit
uneasily against a description of a single localized neck wound. Blast overpressure, secondary
cavitation from a high-velocity round, resuscitation efforts, and simple post-injury blood
tracking are all offered as competing explanations, and the filing itself assigns no cause. That
question connects directly to the [ballistics and wound analysis](./ballistics_and_wound_analysis)
debate and to the [exploding mic theory](/Mic/overview), which argues the chest was involved
before the neck was.

The airway cartilage findings are also cited in the [chest-versus-neck witness
conflict](./Witness_Account_Conflicts): several early witnesses described chest trauma before the
public account settled on the neck.

None of this can be resolved from a quoted excerpt. The underlying autopsy report, imaging, and
forensic analysis remain non-public — the central complaint of the post and the reason the
[autopsy secrecy pages](./autopsy_report_not_public) exist. Full document and naming context:
[Charlie Kirk Autopsy Report](./charlie_kirk_autopsy_report),
[Charlie Kirk Medical Examiner Report](./charlie_kirk_medical_examiner_report),
[Charlie Kirk Medical Examiner Name](./charlie_kirk_medical_examiner_name).

## The same filing page also states the rooftop account

The screenshot's lower portion is the state's narrative, and it belongs on the record alongside
the injury list rather than cropped out of it. Per the filing:

> When the bullet struck Mr. Kirk, Officer Christopher Bagley of the UVU police was stationed on
> the south end of the walkway roof of the Hall of Flags above Mr. Kirk's canopy. ... Based on his
> experience with firearms, Bagley believed that the bullet came from a rifle fired from a
> position east of Mr. Kirk's canopy.

The filing goes on to describe Officer Bagley proceeding to the Losee Building roof, where he
reportedly observed a screwdriver 10 to 15 feet from the guardrail and disturbances in the gravel
at the southwest edge consistent with a "sniper pad" — a position where someone had been "laying
in a prone position" with a line of sight to the canopy. It states that surveillance video shows a
male running to that southwest edge, dropping into a prone position, then at approximately
**12:23:28 p.m.** — the moment of the gunshot — standing, running northeast carrying "a long,
skinny, black object," and dropping off the northeast side of the roof to the ground.

That account is the prosecution's, presented in its own filing. [Tyler
Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is the **accused** and has not been convicted. Readers weighing
the injury list should note it arrives inside a document arguing the state's case, which is part
of why the underlying medical records — not excerpts of them — are what the post demands.
