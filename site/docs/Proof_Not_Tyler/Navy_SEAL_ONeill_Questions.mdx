On **August 17, 2026**, the news program *Redacted* — hosted by Clayton Morris and Natali Morris —
aired an interview with **Robert J. "Rob" O'Neill**, the former US Navy SEAL publicly known as the
man who says he fired the shots that killed Osama bin Laden. **Status: Alive.** In it he says the
official account of the [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) assassination does not survive contact
with what he knows about **ballistics** and **shooting people at close range**.

His argument is not that he knows what happened. He is explicit that he was not there. It is that a
man with his particular professional background looks at the same public material everyone else has
and cannot make the pieces fit — and that saying so publicly gets a person **called an antisemite
rather than answered**.

O'Neill is relevant here for one narrow reason: **the wound**. He states his own qualification
plainly — *"I've killed a lot of people closer than that cameraman, and I've seen what happens.
I've seen entry wounds and exit wounds."* That is an experience claim, not a forensic credential,
and this page records it as such. It is not a substitute for the [autopsy](/Medical/overview) that
has still not been released.

## The ballistics objection

The heart of it is the [.30-06](/Gun_Bullet/Mauser_30_06_Rifle) round the state says was used, set
against what O'Neill says such a round does to a human neck:

> "I know ballistics. I know what it looks like when someone's shot, and this story they're spinning
> doesn't make any sense... You know what a 30-06 would do to a man's neck? It would blow his head
> off. And their response is, oh, he was a superhuman. No, he wasn't."

He describes the first thing he noticed watching the video: **no exit wound where one should be**,
and a shirt that moved as though *"there was an impact nearby."*

> "The moment I saw it was, why? That an exit wound coming out of his neck. And the way his shirt
> moved, something, there was an impact nearby... Immediately it didn't make sense."

He also walks through the explanations offered and says they contradicted each other:

* First, that the round **ricocheted off body armor**. He says he rejected that immediately because
  a .30-06 does not behave that way.
* Then, that **there was no body armor**.

Both cannot be the explanation. That contradiction is the same one tracked on
[Trajectory & Wound Mismatch](./Trajectory_Wound_Mismatch) and
[Alternative Kill Mechanisms](./Alternative_Kill_Mechanisms), and it is why the majority view among
citizen investigators on X is that the wound is **not consistent with a rifle round at all** — see
[Cause of Death](/Cause_of_Death/overview) and [Mic](/Mic/overview).

## The rooftop and the rifle

O'Neill's second objection is **tactical**, and it is the argument this section already builds out
on [Backpack & Rifle Impossibility](./Backpack_Rifle_Impossibility):

> "The guy running on the roof, the 30-06 — how this dude, he took it apart, he put it together. He
> got a scope on there, took a cold war shot, took it apart, jumped on a roof, put it together,
> wrapped it up."

Reassembling a bolt-action rifle, **mounting and zeroing a scope**, making a single long shot, then
breaking it down and escaping with it, is a sequence he treats as implausible for the person
described. He adds the texts: *"All of the texts he's sending that don't sound like any 22-year-old
I know"* — the same objection raised on
[Discord & Custody Timeline](./Discord_Custody_Timeline).

## The video the FBI has not released

> "Why the FBI not release the first 10 seconds of that video that would have shown this guy — your
> guy, Tyler — laying in a prone position, firing the shot. You release a video of a guy running
> across the roof, but we never see him actually fire the shot. But we got our guy."

He states that no video evidence has been published placing [Tyler
Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) on campus at all. That claim is tracked independently on
[Tyler on Surveillance Cameras](/cameras/Tyler_On_Cameras) and
[Stairs Guy Identity Gap](./Stairs_Guy_Identity_Gap) — where the figure on the roof and the
camera-tracked Tyler do not match on clothing or pack.

He characterizes the announcement sequence as: *"the FBI saying, hey, we got him. Hey, we don't got
him. Hey, his mom turned him in."* See [FBI](/FBI/overview) and
[Gun Discovery Sequence](./Gun_Discovery_Sequence).

## The scene, the mic, and the camera

O'Neill raises the crime-scene questions as questions, and says he cannot verify them:

> "Is it true that as soon as he was down, his security team took his lapel mic off, gave it to
> someone else that ran off? What do they do with the SIM card? Did someone take that camera that
> was right behind him down? Why did they contaminate crime scene immediately? Is it paved over? Did
> they rebuild it? Is all of that true? I don't know."

Each of those is covered here: the lapel mic on [Mic](/Mic/overview), the
[Security Team](/Security_Team/overview) handling, and the paving of the scene under
[Cover-Up](/CoverUp/overview).

## "The second you ask, I'm anti-Semitic"

The part of the clip that drew the most engagement is about **what happens to the person asking**,
not about the evidence:

> "Even with — if I said it doesn't make sense — like, why are you saying Israel did it? I'm like, I
> didn't say that. I'm just saying it doesn't look good... But the second you ask, I'm anti-Semitic.
> And I'm like, I don't even bring up Israel. Why are you bringing up Israel? Is someone getting
> paid to defend them for something I'm not even talking about?"

Read carefully, that is **not an accusation against Israel**. It is a complaint about a **reply
pattern**: that the accusation arrives before the answer does. This site tracks that pattern as its
own subject under [Censorship](/Censorship/overview) and
[Narrative](/Narrative/overview), separate from the substantive question of foreign involvement
handled in [Israel — Main Suspect](/Israel_Main_Suspect/overview).

He closes on the standing he thinks it should buy him:

> "No, I've killed a bunch of people for the country. A great American was killed in front of
> everyone in America. He was for this country. I would like to know why. And stop bullshitting me."

## Erika Kirk's forgiveness, and a trial not yet held

One further point he raises, which he himself flags as a tangent:

> "With Erika Kirk immediately saying, I forgive him — it's like, well, you just made him guilty. So
> now he's guilty. Have we had a trial yet?"

The observation is about **legal posture**, not about [Erika Kirk](/People/erika-kirk)'s sincerity:
that a public act of forgiveness presumes the guilt of a defendant who has not been tried. Robinson
is **charged, not convicted**. Court progress is tracked at [Court](/court/overview) and
[Legal Investigation](/legal_investigation/overview).

## What this is and what it is not

* **It is** a named, on-the-record expert-adjacent objection from a person with combat shooting
  experience, published to a large audience, and preserved here in full.
* **It is not** a forensic finding. O'Neill did not examine the body, the scene, or the rifle, and
  says so.
* **It does not** identify who fired anything, or assert that any named person or government was
  involved.
* **It does** line up with the physical objections already documented across this section, which is
  why it sits here rather than in [Influencers](/Influencers/overview).
