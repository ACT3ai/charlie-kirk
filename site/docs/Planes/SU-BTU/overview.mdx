# SU-BTU Israel/Egyptian Jet

SU-BTU is an Egyptian-registered jet — described by independent trackers as a **Dassault Falcon 7X** — named as one of the five aircraft in the **"Israel/Egyptian Plane Armada"** that public researchers say cycled through Provo, Utah during 2025. Unlike the two lead jets in that cluster — **[SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview)** ("the yellow plane") and **[SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview)** ("the blue plane") — there is very little standalone reporting on SU-BTU. Its significance in the public commentary comes almost entirely from its membership in the fleet pattern, not from any single documented event tied to this tail alone.

**Read the Israel/Egyptian Plane Armada hub page first** — the bulk of the analysis, the fleet timeline, the source list, and the counterarguments live there. This page collects only what is specific to SU-BTU, and is honest that the standalone record is thin. None of it establishes any link to the afternoon Charlie Kirk was assassinated at Utah Valley University; the [cover-up questions](/CoverUp/overview) raised elsewhere on this site rest on far stronger material than a single foreign tail number's Provo landings.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** SU-BTU
* **Type:** Egyptian-registered jet; described by trackers as a Dassault Falcon 7X (long-range business jet)
* **Registry / operator:** Israel/Egyptian Government / Israel/Egyptian Air Force use (per the framing in public coverage)
* **Color label:** None. [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens)'s color-coded shorthand ("yellow plane," "blue plane") was applied to SU-BTT and SU-BND; the master flight-log notes state **no color label** was attributed to SU-BTU.
* **Role in coverage:** One of three additional Israel/Egyptian tails (with [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview) and [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview)) added to the "armada" narrative after the original SU-BTT / SU-BND timeline.

## Flight history relevant to the case

Every item below comes from open-source researchers working from public flight-record screenshots. None has been independently verified; treat the dates and routes as reported claims.

* **May 27, 2025:** Investigation notes record SU-BTU flying into **Provo**, and those notes allege that one of the passengers aboard was **[Walid Mahmoud](/People/walid-mahmoud)** (see Open questions — this is an unverified online claim about a living person).
* **June 2, 2025:** The notes record the Egyptian passenger "Walid" departing Provo.
* **Feb 14, 2025:** Provo → Wilmington, DE (per the "five-plane armada" log thread).
* **Mar 2, 2025:** Detroit → Provo.
* **Aug 8, 2025:** Minot, ND → Provo.

The log thread that introduced SU-BTU summarized it as making **"multiple NE/UT hops tied to [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview) Faith tours."** That phrasing is the researcher's characterization, not a confirmed itinerary.

## Alleged Israel trips

Separately from the U.S. overlaps, a flight-log thread **compiled by [@nihilox](/Influencers/x/nihilox) and shared by [@ProjectConstitu](/Influencers/podcasts-project-constitution)** claims SU-BTU made a long run of trips to **Israel** across one year. The researchers allege the public logs show these as Cairo-to-Cairo (CAI → CAI) or origin-N/A legs, and argue the jets actually landed in Israel with the transponder switched off — so the listed dates are, by their own framing, an inference from a claimed blackout pattern, not confirmed Israel landings. None of this has been independently verified, and the same Cairo-to-Cairo logs are equally consistent with the routine in-country flying of a government VIP jet.

Dates the thread attributes to SU-BTU (one calendar year):

* **February:** Feb 05, Feb 11, Feb 16, Feb 23
* **March:** Mar 02, Mar 25
* **April:** Apr 15
* **May:** May 10, May 17
* **June:** Jun 14 (two flights), Jun 21, Jun 24

The same researchers posted parallel Israel-trip lists for **[SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview)** and **[SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview)**, and present the three lists together as a "consistent and undeniable" pattern. That characterization is the thread's, not an established finding. The only Israel leg with outside corroboration in this material is an **SU-BTT** flight (not SU-BTU) reported by Israeli Channel 12 News; no comparable corroboration has been published for any SU-BTU date above.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

These are framed as open theories raised by independent commentators, not conclusions:

* **Fleet-pattern surveillance** — that SU-BTU, as part of a recurring multi-jet Israel/Egyptian presence, fits the broader "intel-vacuum" theory that a foreign government may have been monitoring TPUSA events and the Kirk family. This theory lives at the fleet level on the Armada hub; SU-BTU's individual contribution to it is limited.
* **A passenger of interest** — because the notes place a named Egyptian passenger aboard SU-BTU's May 27 Provo arrival, some threads treat the aircraft as part of the "who was being shuttled through Provo" question. The same name has been floated by other threads in connection with the SU-BTT Wilmington leg, so the sourcing is inconsistent (see below).

### What the maintenance record does and does not account for

Provo Municipal Airport hosts **Duncan Aviation**, a full-service maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO) facility, and **Duncan has held an Egyptian Air Force maintenance account since 1999** — sourced and documented at length on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview). That is a record fact, and on SU-BTU it has a visible footprint: the rotation table below puts this airframe at **Lincoln, Nebraska** — Duncan's other plant — in three of five rotations, which is the shape a service relationship makes on a tracker.

It accounts for less than it is often asked to. No dated work order, invoice or release-to-service for SU-BTU has been published by anyone, so the contract explains why the operator's aircraft come to Provo and Lincoln at all — not what any individual leg was for, and not the days the airframe was on the ground doing nothing visible. Two further cautions belong beside it rather than under it: transponder gaps and trackers "snapping" an origin to the nearest public field are ordinary data artifacts and should not be read as evidence of concealment, and equally, an airframe that has legitimate business at a field can still be somewhere for a second reason. The tests this page can actually run are the ones below — the primary traces we hold, the rotation record, and the overlap register.

## Open questions

1. Is SU-BTU's individual flight record (May 27 arrival, June 2 departure, and the 2025 NE/UT hops) borne out by independent ADS-B history, or are some legs misattributed — as a skeptic's audit alleged for several of the SU-BTT overlaps?
2. Who were the passengers on SU-BTU's 2025 Provo visits, and is there any manifest for the May 27 arrival?
3. The investigation notes name **Walid Mahmoud** as a passenger. This is an unverified online claim about a living person who has not been charged with any crime, and the same name appears elsewhere tied to a different jet (SU-BTT). Which, if either, attribution is accurate?
4. What is the operational or contractual relationship between SU-BTU and the other four armada tails (SU-BTT, SU-BND, SU-BTV, SU-BGM)?

### What the template shows

Read as a set, these five rotations are the same trip flown five times in six months, and the regularity is the finding:

* **Every rotation is Cairo → Paris-Le Bourget → a US arrival airport → a short repositioning hop → sit → Wilmington → Cairo.** Not once does it vary in shape.
* **The repositioning hop is always 15 to 19 minutes.** Omaha→Lincoln three times; St. Louis Lambert→St. Louis Downtown once; Minot→Provo is the outlier at 1:43. The short hops are what an aircraft does when the international arrival airport is not the destination — it clears customs at the big field and then moves to the facility.
* **St. Louis Lambert (STL) → St. Louis Downtown (CPS) is a 15-minute hop across the Mississippi.** Both are St. Louis. Any overlap spreadsheet that logs "St. Louis, MO" as a location visit is logging a customs stop and a taxi-equivalent flight, not a destination.
* **The dwell is six to eight days every time**, and **Wilmington (ILG) is on the outbound leg every time and only the outbound leg**.
* **Lincoln, Nebraska is the most frequent endpoint — three of five rotations.** As set out on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), **Duncan Aviation's main plant is at Lincoln, and it has held the Egyptian Air Force maintenance account since 1999**, under a five-year direct contract signed in October 2015. That is the plain reading of this table.

### The one visit that is not Lincoln

**April 8–14, 2025 is SU-BTU's only Provo stay in the export.** It routes Paris → Minot, North Dakota → Provo — the same Minot-then-Provo entry sequence later flown by [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview) on 27 May and by [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) on 4 September. Three different aircraft, three different months, the same two-stop approach into Utah.

That repeated Minot routing is the detail worth pursuing, because **Minot is not a normal transatlantic entry point for a business jet** — it is a small North Dakota field adjacent to a nuclear-mission Air Force base, and the natural customs stops for a Cairo–Utah trip would be east-coast or Canadian. Whether Minot International (the civil field) or Minot AFB is meant in each case is not resolved by the export, which lists only "Minot (MOT)" — the **civil** airport code. Claims elsewhere that these aircraft "arrived via Minot Air Force Base" go beyond what MOT in a Flightradar24 export supports; the base is a separate facility with a separate identifier.

### The consequence for the "purple plane" identification

Candace Owens's account places a passenger in Provo aboard SU-BTU on **27 May 2025**, departing **2 June**. This export puts SU-BTU at Provo on **8–14 April**, and puts **[SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview)** at Provo on exactly **27 May – 2 June**. The discrepancy, and what rides on it, is set out in full on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview). It is unresolved, it is checkable, and it should be resolved before the person-across-aircraft inference built on top of it is relied on by anyone.


## All five US rotations, tested against the TPUSA event calendar

SU-BTU has the cleanest test case of the five Egyptian tails, because its record is five near-identical rotations across six months. If the shadowing claim is right about this aircraft, the rotations should land where TPUSA was. They do not.

The calendar used is `site/docs/Planes/following/tpusa_events.csv` — **139 dated rows, 4 January 2022 to 29 October 2025**, built from TPUSA postings, campus press, event listings and the Charlie Kirk Show archive, each row flagged `EVENT_DATE` (118 rows), `PODCAST_PROXY` (8), `SCHEDULED_NOT_HELD` (4) or `AFTER_DEATH` (9). It is the spine of the [Following section](/Planes/following/overview).

The test is deliberately generous: a ground window counts as an overlap if **any** event in the calendar happened **in the same US state** within **three days either side**. That is roughly the criterion [Liz Wheeler](/People/liz-wheeler) says the original overlap spreadsheet used, and far looser than "same city, same day."

The 8–14 April 2025 Provo stay is the one that gets cited, because Provo is where the September story happens. Here is the same week on the Kirk side:

He was working the Midwest campus circuit — Illinois, Indiana, Michigan — for the entire week SU-BTU was parked in Utah. Meanwhile [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview) was also at Provo on 10 and 16 April: **two Egyptian jets, same small field, same week, and no TPUSA presence within 1,000 miles.**

### What this table means for the SU-BTU case

* **It is the strongest evidence on this page for the maintenance reading.** The rotations track **Lincoln, Nebraska** — Duncan Aviation's main plant, holder of the Egyptian Air Force maintenance account since 1999 — three times out of five. They do not track Charlie Kirk at all. A pattern that correlates perfectly with a maintenance contract and not at all with an itinerary is a maintenance pattern.
* **It removes SU-BTU from the following claim on its own record.** SU-BTU is not in Candace Owens's 68/73 tally to begin with; it was added later by [@WeAre_TheElite](/Influencers/x/weare-theelite) to make the five-plane framing. This table is why that addition should be treated as fleet-context rather than as evidence.
* **It does not touch the September question.** Nothing here bears on [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview)'s 4–10 September Provo stay, which is a separate aircraft on a separate date and is the one window in the whole fleet record that does produce a same-city hit.

### The limits of this test

* **It tests Charlie's side, not Erika's.** Of 139 rows, exactly **one** places Erika Kirk at a TPUSA event before 10 September 2025 (the June 2025 Young Women's Leadership Summit, logged as "Both"). The overlap tallies were counted against *her* claimed locations, and her flight logs are reported erased — see [Erika's Flight Logs Erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased). Ten clean misses here do not refute those tallies; they establish that **no public source reproduces them for this tail**.
* **The calendar records public events only** — not private travel, donor meetings or studio days.
* **The flight record is a tracker export, not a manifest.** It shows where the airframe was, not who was aboard or why.

## Flight data recovery

This tail is the cleanest example in the fleet of two volunteer networks having different coverage of the same aircraft. **adsb.lol holds nothing at all for SU-BTU, while globe.airplanes.live holds three days.** That is ordinary and is **not** evidence of anything: the two networks run different feeders, with different antennas, in different places, so an aircraft heard by one can be missed entirely by the other. The monthly ADS-B Exchange sample reaches furthest back, to December 2022.

### The 2022 trace, and a correction to this site

`samples.adsbexchange.com/traces/` publishes a free full-day global sample for **the first of every month, back to 2016** — no account, no key. It reaches this tail once.

**2022-12-01.** The trace opens at **Paris–Le Bourget (LFPB), 0.73 km from the field**, and closes **on final approach to Spirit of St Louis Airport (KSUS), Missouri — 150 feet, 18:40:48 UTC, 1.35 km out.** 1,245 position points. The file self-identifies as `"r": "SU-BTU"`, `"t": "FA7X"`. It is `site/docs/Planes/SU-BTU/data/recovered/SU-BTU_2022-12-01_adsbexchange-samples_trace_full.json`.

**Missouri is one of the five states in the clustering claim** — Missouri, Delaware, Utah, Nebraska, Kansas — which this site carries marked *unverified*. This is **the first primary position data supporting that part of the claim for this tail**, as opposed to a reading of a tracker page.

**It also corrects a standing statement on this site.** We have published that "the archive does not reach 2022; every 2022 claim rests on a screenshot." **That is no longer true.** Primary 2022 position data now exists for this aircraft, and we found it with a free public dataset that was available the whole time.

**The sampling caveat has to travel with this fact every time it is cited.** The sample is **one day per month, the 1st**. For SU-BTU that is a single day in the whole of 2022. It establishes that the aircraft flew Paris to St Louis on 1 December 2022. **It cannot support a rate, a frequency or a pattern**, and nobody should build one on it.

And it locates the airframe only. **No source in this repo places Charlie Kirk or Erika Kirk in Missouri on or near that date.**

### What the Internet Archive actually holds for this tail

Three snapshots exist of the ADS-B Exchange globe page for hex `0101D0`, spanning **December 2022 to May 2023**. They contain **no aircraft data**.

The Archive's own content digest for all three, `AJQHJML54QE6X2IE4TMCM24E3DPGKFD6`, is **byte-identical across SU-BGM, SU-BTV and SU-BTU**. Three different hex codes cannot produce three identical files if the files contain per-aircraft data. What the Archive captured is the **empty JavaScript application frame** that loads before the map fetches anything. **These snapshots must never be cited as though they held evidence about this aircraft.**

Two related points, both from the re-run of the Archive query on 24 August 2026:

* **Every CDX row across every URL in this dataset returns `statuscode=200`.** There is no 200-to-404 transition anywhere in it. Any page claiming one for these aircraft is wrong.
* Where the Archive has nothing, it is because **nobody ever asked it to crawl the page**. Absence of archival interest is not suppression, and we do not report it as such.

The live FlightRadar24 URL for this tail returns HTTP 403 to a script today. That is not a removal either: FlightRadar24 returns the identical 403 to unrelated control aircraft and **to its own home page** — see [What A 403 Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means).

### The overlap register does not name this aircraft at all

We tested all **85 rows** of `site/docs/Planes/following/overlaps.csv` against both free archives, on the claimed date and the day either side, measuring the aircraft's closest approach in kilometres to the claimed field. Across the whole register: **24 rows put the claimed aircraft at the claimed field, 12 put it somewhere else entirely**, 22 were not heard, 20 fall outside any free archive's coverage, and 7 name no tail or no date.

**None of those 85 rows names SU-BTU.** Nor SU-BTV, nor SU-BGM. The register is 57 rows naming [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), 16 naming [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview), 3 naming both, 5 naming "SU-BTT or SU-BND", and 4 naming no aircraft at all.

That is worth saying out loud on a page this long: **this site gives SU-BTU considerable space, and the overlap claim itself does not rest on this aircraft in any way.** There is nothing here to verify, and correspondingly nothing here that has been verified. The disputed tallies — 73 and about 23, or 72, or "70-plus", or 68 and 29 — were never counted against this tail. Those figures conflict with one another and this site does not reconcile them; it reports the conflict.

### What the record on this tail actually supports

Stated from the primary data rather than from either side's framing: **SU-BTU's own file is thin, and where it is legible it points at the maintenance geography.**

**Duncan Aviation has held the Egyptian Air Force maintenance account since 1999**, with plants at **Lincoln, Nebraska** and **Provo, Utah** — documented on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview). The rotation table earlier on this page puts SU-BTU at **Lincoln in three of five rotations** and at Provo once, with Wichita — home of Yingling Aviation's FAA Part 145 Falcon shop — carrying the same role for [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview). A movement pattern that tracks the operator's two maintenance plants, and not a person's itinerary, is what the twelve aircraft-days we hold for this tail look like. **Not one of the 85 rows in the overlap register names SU-BTU**, so there is no overlap claim here to confirm or break.

Two things that follow from that, and they should not be blurred together. First, **this tail carries none of the weight of the September 2025 window.** That window belongs to [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) and [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) — confirmed Provo ramp positions on the days either side of 10 September, a departure the morning of the event, and a transponder left up on the airframe that stayed — and nothing on the SU-BTU page reaches it in either direction. Second, **a maintenance geography is not a clearance.** The paperwork that would turn "the operator has a contract" into "this leg was that contract" is exactly the paperwork nobody outside the operator and the shop can obtain, and no manifest, crew list or authorisation for the foreign-military use of a non-international US municipal field has ever been produced for any of these five tails.

The counterargument to an empty archive is equally plain: **an empty archive means the archive had nothing.** It does not mean the aircraft was hidden, and it does not mean a transponder was off. These are volunteer receiver networks, not registries — they hold whatever a feeder happened to hear.

### What all of this locates, and what it does not

A confirmed airframe is one half of an overlap. ## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — SU-BTU](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/su-btu) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.

## Note on Registration & Control

This aircraft carries an Egyptian registration — the **SU-** prefix on its tail number is Egypt's national civil registry, so the Egyptian registration itself is real and not in dispute. What is *not* settled is **who ultimately controlled the jet.** If an Egyptian front company was registered as the aircraft's operator, that registration — Egyptian on paper — may have been arranged on behalf of an intelligence service from another country. That open possibility is why this page labels the aircraft **"Israel/Egyptian"**: the label flags the unresolved question of control. It is **not** a claim that the jet was Israeli-registered or Israeli-owned — the public record does not establish that, and nothing here asserts as fact that any government or person controlled it.
