# N582MM TPUSA Aircraft

Tail number **N582MM** is an aircraft that public commentary describes as frequently used by **[Turning Point USA](/TPUSA/overview) (TPUSA)** insiders. It is named in the proposed Charlie Kirk disclosure laws over a reported **October 2025 movement from Fort Huachuca, Arizona to Kalispell, Montana**. Standalone sourcing on this tail number is thin — the TPUSA-insider association is a **reported characterization**, not an established fact, and naming the aircraft is not an accusation against any person or organization. The page exists because citizen investigators have flagged the tail in OSINT threads and because the disclosure-law draft lists it among the aircraft for which it seeks records.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** N582MM
* **Type:** Not confirmed in public reporting; described in commentary as a likely business jet
* **Registry / operator / owner:** Not independently confirmed on this page; described in public commentary as an aircraft frequently used by TPUSA insiders
* **Program / callsign:** None known; this is treated as a civil aircraft, not a military or government tail
* **Why it draws attention:** The reported [Fort Huachuca](/US_Intelligence/Fort_Huachuca/overview) stop and immediate Kalispell leg overlap two locations that recur across the case's intelligence angle

## Flight history relevant to the case

The flight history below is drawn from the disclosure-law draft and OSINT commentary, not from a flight-tracking record reproduced in the master investigation file. Treat the legs as **reported** rather than independently verified here.

* **Reported October 4–5, 2025:** N582MM is described as present at **Fort Huachuca, Arizona** — the U.S. Army Intelligence Center and Counterintelligence school in Sierra Vista. Fort Huachuca recurs throughout this investigation: roughly a dozen lieutenant colonels are reported to have met there on September 8–9, 2025, and the **[SAM 99-0404](/Planes/SAM-99-0404/overview)** and **[SAM-702](/Planes/SAM-702/overview)** government flights are tied to the same post in the days before the assassination.
* **Reported immediate onward leg to Kalispell, Montana:** The aircraft is reported to have flown directly to **Kalispell** (Glacier Park International Airport area), said to coincide with a TPUSA security-team gathering nearby. Kalispell is notable because the **[N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview)** Bombardier Global 6500 — analyzed elsewhere on this site — also landed at Glacier Park International (KGPI) the morning of September 10, 2025. The recurrence of the same small Montana airport across two separately flagged aircraft is what investigators ask the disclosure laws to explain.
* **Reported broader pattern:** Citizen investigators say they have spotted the tail in patterns around Kirk events and locations, framing those sightings in the context of pre- and post-September 10, 2025 timelines.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

Each item below is an **open theory** raised in public commentary, not a finding. None is established, and several have innocent explanations.

* **Organizational-travel theory.** The simplest reading is that N582MM is ordinary TPUSA-associated business-jet travel and that any overlap with Fort Huachuca or Kalispell is coincidental — a routine logistics movement that happens to touch locations the investigation is already watching. This is the null hypothesis and should be stated first.
* **Fort Huachuca overlap theory.** Some commentators ask whether an aircraft linked to TPUSA travel touching the Army's counterintelligence post — the same post tied to the September 8–9 lieutenant-colonel meeting and the SAM flights — points to a coordination angle. This is posed as a **question**, not a conclusion; presence at an airfield is not evidence of any operation.
* **Kalispell convergence theory.** Because N1098L also flew to the Kalispell area around September 10, a few investigators ask whether the two aircraft and a reported TPUSA security gathering converged there for a reason. The counterpoint is that Glacier Park International is a normal destination and that two flights to one airport, weeks apart, can be unrelated.

## TPUSA event-location patterns

Citizen investigators describe N582MM, **per X OSINT discussions**, as a TPUSA-associated jet whose reported movements trace **various TPUSA event locations** and form alleged **patterns around Utah and other Kirk event sites**. Commentators frame these reported sightings in the context of the pre- and post-**September 10, 2025** timelines. None of this is independently verified here — it reflects how OSINT commentators on X read the tail's reported movements, not an established flight record. The simplest explanation remains routine organizational travel to and from TPUSA events.

## Questions about organizational aviation and surveillance overlap

A recurring theme **per X discussions** is the question of whether Turning Point USA's organizational aviation resources overlap in any way with the surveillance or operational flights flagged elsewhere in this case. This is posed as an **open question**, not a conclusion. The mere presence of an aircraft, or its appearance near a flagged location or event, is not evidence of any such overlap, and TPUSA has not been shown to have done anything wrong. These are exactly the kinds of records the disclosure laws would clarify.

## Broader TPUSA-asset scrutiny

N582MM is one of several **TPUSA assets** that citizen investigators have placed under scrutiny in **X OSINT threads** following the assassination. That scrutiny extends to the organization's fleet and aviation logistics generally, with commentators **alleging** patterns they say warrant explanation. This page treats that scrutiny as **reported public commentary**, not as a substantiated claim against TPUSA. Naming the aircraft is not an accusation against the organization or any individual.

## Open questions

1. Who was aboard N582MM on the reported Fort Huachuca and Kalispell legs, and who chartered or owns the aircraft?
2. What was the purpose of the reported Fort Huachuca stop and the immediate move to Kalispell?
3. What is the registered owner and operator, and what is the documentary basis for the TPUSA-insider characterization?
4. Does the reported Kalispell leg connect in any way to N1098L's separate Kalispell arrival, or is the shared airport coincidental?
5. Does the timing connect to any other Fort Huachuca activity already flagged in the case?



## What the FAA registry says about this tail

Public commentary describes N582MM as "the TPUSA aircraft." The FAA Civil Aviation Registry does not support that description of its **ownership**:

* **Type:** **Learjet 60**, built 2001, serial 227 — an 11-seat midsize business jet. Earlier descriptions on this site called the type "not confirmed in public reporting." It is confirmed now.
* **Registered owner:** **DOJA Contracting LLC**, Mesa, Arizona. Also listed on the registration: TK Enterprises LLC and JRO Aviation LLC.
* **Registered to Turning Point USA?** **No.** A search of the complete FAA registration database returns **no aircraft registered to Turning Point USA, Turning Point Action, or any related Turning Point entity** — see [TPUSA Aircraft](/Planes/TPUSA-Aircraft/overview).

This narrows the claim rather than refuting it. "TPUSA insiders used this plane" is a statement about **who was aboard**, and private aircraft manifests are not public — the registry can neither confirm nor refute it. What the registry does establish is that the aircraft is owned by an Arizona LLC, not by the organisation. **Naming a registered owner is not naming a wrongdoer**, and nothing on this page asserts that DOJA Contracting LLC or anyone associated with it did anything improper.

## What this aircraft would cost to buy new today

* **Type (FAA-confirmed):** Learjet Inc 60, built 2001, serial 227
* **Registered owner:** DOJA CONTRACTING LLC (Mesa AZ)
* **Still built today?** No — Learjet production ended 2022
* **Priced against:** Embraer Praetor 500
* **Cost to buy new today:** **~$18.4 million**

Learjet 60 out of production and the Learjet brand is retired. The Praetor 500 (~$18.4M) is the nearest new midsize jet. The Learjet 60 listed around $13M when new.

List price is for a green airframe — interior completion, avionics options and any mission equipment are extra, and fleet buyers do not pay list. ## Flight data recovery

N582MM came back well from the archives: **7 Internet Archive captures** of its tracking pages
yielded **40 recovered flight rows**. Nothing was found to be missing from any tracking site. The one
thing worth stating carefully is that airplanes.live coverage for this tail stops on
**16 October 2025** — on the honest reading that means the volunteer receiver network heard nothing
after that date, not that anything was deleted.

| Source | What it holds for N582MM |
|---|---|
| adsb.lol `globe_history` | **19 days** of track, 1 September 2025 to 11 October 2025 |
| globe.airplanes.live | **24 days** of track, 1 September 2025 to 16 October 2025 |
| ADS-B Exchange monthly sample | **23 days**, 1 April 2022 to 1 December 2025 |
| FlightRadar24 page | **HTTP 200** in a browser on 24 August 2026; **0 history rows** on the free seven-day tier because the aircraft had not flown in the previous seven days; older history paywalled |
| Internet Archive | **7 page snapshots** held; **40 flight rows** recovered off them |

**No tracking site was found to have removed this aircraft's page.** Our scripts recorded HTTP 403
from FlightRadar24, RadarBox and Planespotters, but the same script recorded the identical 403 for
five unrelated control aircraft, so those are robot blocks and establish nothing about this tail:
[What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means). FlightAware and
ADS-B Exchange serve scripts normally and returned HTTP 200 for N582MM.

Where a day is absent from an archive, that means the volunteer receiver network heard nothing on
that day. It does not mean the aircraft did not fly, and it does not mean a transponder was off.
Both free daily archives begin in 2023, so no 2022 date is testable at all.

Raw payloads are saved at `site/docs/Planes/N582MM/data/recovered/`, with the source named in every
filename and a `.meta.json` sidecar per file recording the source, URL, HTTP status, byte count and
date.

The whole of this page above this line is characterization: what commentators say the aircraft is,
who they say used it, where a draft law says it went. This section is the opposite kind of material
— raw position data this investigation pulled itself from free public archives, plus archived
tracking pages the Internet Archive already held, all saved to disk at
`site/docs/Planes/N582MM/data/recovered/` with a `.meta.json` sidecar beside every payload recording
the source, the URL, the HTTP status, the byte count and the retrieval time.

Counts from `site/docs/Planes/following/apis/public_open_source/data/provenance/tail_provenance.json`.

**Nineteen of the forty-six days are held by both daily archives independently.** That is the
highest agreement rate of any Kirk-side tail in this investigation, and agreement between two
separate volunteer receiver networks is the strongest form free ADS-B evidence takes.

### The 2022 trace, on this site for the first time

`samples.adsbexchange.com` publishes a free full-day sample for **the first of every month**, back
to 2016, with no account and no key. One of those sampled days falls in 2022 for N582MM.

| Sampled day | First fix | Last fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 April 2022 | North Las Vegas (KVGT, 0.44 km) | Minden-Tahoe, NV (KMEV, 0.60 km) |

Positions are the nearest airport and the distance to it — **geometry, not a landing record.** And
the sampling caveat travels with the row: **this is one day per month.** A single sampled day
establishes where the aircraft was on 1 April 2022. It supports no claim about any other day of
that year.

It does, however, correct something this page said a few paragraphs up. The recovery section above
states that "both free daily archives begin in 2023, so no 2022 date is testable at all." **That is
too strong.** The two *daily* archives do begin in 2023 — but the ADS-B Exchange monthly sample
reaches back to 2016, and it produced this 2022 position. The 2022 wall this site has published is
not solid; it is a wall with one day per month cut out of it.

### What the archived pages put back: 40 flight legs, and Provo in 2015

The Internet Archive holds **seven captures** of this tail's FlightAware page, from **2011 and
2015**. Our own provenance files previously recorded all seven as yielding nothing, because the
extractor never parsed FlightAware's older table markup at all. `code/extract_wayback_flights.py`
re-read them. **We were wrong, and this is the correction.** The seven captures yield **40 flight
legs**:

| Capture | Legs | Fields named |
|---|---|---|
| 20 Dec 2011 | 4 | Fort Lauderdale (KFLL), Caracas (SVMI), Albuquerque (KABQ), Atlanta (KATL) |
| 14 Jan 2015 | 8 | KMEV, KSDL, KLGB, KBUR, KSAN |
| 28 Mar 2015 | 7 | KMEV, KSDL, KBVS, KHOT |
| 29 Apr 2015 | 7 | KMEV, KBFI, KBVS, KLAS, KADS |
| **31 May 2015** | **2** | **KMEV and Provo Muni (KPVU)** |
| 4 Jul 2015 | 6 | KMEV, KSDL, KCRQ, KBUR, KLVK |
| 18 Oct 2015 | 6 | KMEV, KSDL, KMQY, KOAK |

The 31 May 2015 capture is the one that matters to this page. It records **KMEV to KPVU on 17 May
2015** and **KPVU back to KMEV on 30 May 2015**. **This airframe was flying into Provo in 2015 —
ten years before the assassination, and years before the ownership and organizational associations
this page describes.** Provo is not a novel destination for this tail.

The 2011–2015 legs also show a base that is not the one it has now: **Minden-Tahoe, Nevada (KMEV)
recurs in every 2015 capture**, while across the 46 modern days the most-touched fields are
Scottsdale and Phoenix. An aircraft changing home base across fourteen years and more than one
registrant is unremarkable. It is stated because it means **the 2015 legs are evidence about the
airframe, not about anyone associated with it today**, and we do not carry a former operator's
routes forward onto a current one.

Files: `site/docs/Planes/N582MM/data/recovered/N582MM_2015*_wayback_flightaware_flights.json` and
the matching `.html` captures.

### This tail is not in the overlap sheet

None of the 85 claimed overlap rows in `site/docs/Planes/following/overlaps.csv` names N582MM. The
sheet names SU-BTT, SU-BND, or no aircraft at all. N582MM sits on the **Kirk-side / TPUSA-adjacent**
list — the tails said to carry the people, not the tails alleged to have followed them. **Do not
merge those threads.** The 18-month following pattern, the 10 September day-of timeline, the
[N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview) thread and the [N888KG](/Planes/N888KG/overview) Wendover
departure are four separate claims about four separate aircraft sets.

And the limit that applies to every line above: **position data locates an aircraft, not a person.**
Nothing here establishes who was aboard N582MM on any date — not in 2015, not in 2022, and not on
the reported Fort Huachuca and Kalispell legs. Private manifests are not public, which is precisely
what the [disclosure laws](/Fix/overview) are proposed to change.

### The counterargument

The plain reading of all of it is that N582MM is an ordinary midsize business jet with a fourteen-year
public flight record of ordinary business routes, operated over that period by more than one
registrant, and that its Provo appearances — in 2015 and since — are the same unremarkable thing.
Nothing we pulled shows otherwise, and the 2015 Provo legs cut directly against reading a Provo
arrival by this tail as meaningful in itself. **On this evidence the null hypothesis is the
best-supported one**, and the reported Fort Huachuca–Kalispell leg remains reported rather than
verified: we hold no primary trace of it.

### Why pulling this data matters at all

The claim this section answers is not about N582MM. It is the broader claim that this category of
record has been erased. **We pulled four years of primary track and recovered fourteen-year-old
flight tables for this aircraft, from free archives, on demand.** What came back is unremarkable
business aviation. That is a finding in itself: a record that reconstructs this easily has not been
scrubbed, and the honest way to report a recovery is to report what it actually shows rather than
what we hoped it would.

## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — N582MM](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n582mm) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.
* [ADS-B Exchange — N582MM](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/?reg=N582MM) — volunteer-fed, unfiltered tracker that does **not** honor LADD, so it often shows blocked aircraft and tracks the filtered sites leave out.

## Dates we looked for and did not find

Not every claimed N582MM flight could be checked against a primary ADS-B trace. **Every date we queried and got nothing back for is listed — with the exact HTTP status and the reason we looked — on [ADS-B gaps for N582MM](/Planes/N582MM/data/adsb/MISSING_DATA).** That page is published for the same reason the recovered traces are: a gap that is written down can be argued with, and a gap that is quietly dropped cannot.

**An absence there is not a finding.** A 404 means a volunteer receiver network heard nothing on that UTC day — parked and silent, outside coverage, or a wrong claimed date all come first, and [what a 403 means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means) sets out why a refusal and a deletion look identical from outside. The four free archives that did produce data, and the control test that separates a retention boundary from a removal, are on [Flight data recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview) and [per-aircraft recovery status](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Per-Aircraft-Status).

## Where this page fits

A tail number written into draft legislation deserves more scrutiny than the sourcing behind it currently supports, and documenting that gap is this page's job — an aircraft described in commentary as TPUSA-adjacent, named in the [disclosure-law drafts](/Fix/overview) over a reported [Fort Huachuca](/US_Intelligence/Fort_Huachuca/overview) to Kalispell leg, catalogued alongside the [FAA answer about TPUSA-owned aircraft](/Planes/TPUSA-Aircraft/overview). Both endpoints of that reported leg recur elsewhere: Fort Huachuca as the Army counterintelligence school running through the [US intelligence section](/US_Intelligence/overview), and Kalispell as the destination [N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview) was repositioning toward when it dropped over Utah — a coincidence of geography that is not by itself a connection, and a separate claim about a separate aircraft. [Law 2, the intelligence services disclosure act](/laws/US_Intel/Law_2_US_Intel) is where this tail actually does work: it names specific numbers Congress would be forced to explain, the Egyptian aircraft SU-BTT, SU-BND, SU-BTU and SU-BGM alongside the HADES jet, which turns a rumour about an aircraft into a testable demand for a record — the same move [Law 1 makes against the DoJ and FBI](/laws/DoJ_FBI/Law_1_DoJ_FBI) and [Law 3 makes against the investigation itself](/laws/Require_to_Investigate/Law_3_Require_to_Investigate).
