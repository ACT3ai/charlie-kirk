# N560TW Scottsdale Jet

A **Cessna Citation XLS** twin-jet registered **N560TW** is reported to have flown a short out-and-back route through **Provo, Utah** on the morning of September 10, 2025, the day [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) was assassinated at Utah Valley University. Commentator [**Candace Owens**](/People/candace-owens) publicly flagged the flight on her program as one she was "now very interested in." No wrongdoing has been established, and the flight is presented here as a publicly reported lead, not a conclusion.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** N560TW
* **Type:** Cessna Citation XLS (twin-engine business jet)
* **Reported registered owner:** **T.W. Lewis Aviation LLC**, which independent commentators say is tied to the **T.W. Lewis Foundation** — a conservative donor entity that has publicly listed [Turning Point USA](/TPUSA/overview) among its partners. This ownership link is drawn from public registration and commentary, not from any official case record.

The ownership and donor links described here are **reported allegations** circulated by Candace Owens and other commentators. There is **no evidence in the public record** that the aircraft's owner, operator, or any associated person had any involvement in the assassination. Naming the flight is not an accusation against any individual.

## Flight history relevant to the case

Candace Owens described the route on her live program. As publicly summarized by Owens and the researchers amplifying her:

* **Departed Scottsdale, Arizona** on the morning of September 10, 2025.
* **Landed at Provo (PVU) at ~10:06 a.m.** MDT. Some posts instead cite a **~10:26 a.m.** arrival; the exact minute differs between accounts, but both place the touchdown in the mid-morning window before the shooting.
* **Immediately turned around and departed for Santa Barbara, California** (reported departure ~11:37 a.m.) — an onward leg that, per some X timelines, left Provo **before the shooting** later that afternoon.
* **Returned to Scottsdale by ~12:44 p.m.**

The interest in the flight stems from its timing and shape — a quick land-and-turn at Provo on the morning of the event, then a leg to Santa Barbara and back. Provo was the same airport that drew a cluster of private arrivals scrutinized by OSINT investigators that morning. A separate Santa Barbara connection runs through tail **[N872RA](/Planes/N872RA/overview)**, another aircraft researchers logged in the Provo arrival timeline that departed Santa Barbara the same day.

Beyond Owens's interest, N560TW also appears in the broader catalogs of "anomalous aircraft near the UVU/Utah corridor" that X/OSINT researchers assembled from public ADS-B data in the September 10 window — the same researcher-compiled lists that group it with tails such as **[N40JD](/Planes/N40JD/overview)**, **[N872RA](/Planes/N872RA/overview)**, and **[N102DZ](/Planes/N102DZ/overview)** for manifest review. Per those researchers, inclusion on the list reflects attention to timing and routing drawn from public flight-tracking data, not any established connection to the assassination.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

Researchers covering N560TW have floated several interpretations, framed here as open theories rather than conclusions:

* **Donor-pressure angle** — Owens and others have noted that the reported owner entity is linked to a major TPUSA donor, and have referenced post-assassination reporting that Kirk faced donor pressure over his shifting public positions on Israel and his refusal to criticize [Tucker Carlson](/People/tucker-carlson). Owens has raised the question of whether the flight is connected to that tension. These donor-relationship and pressure claims are **reported by commentators and unproven**; they are recorded only to explain why the flight drew attention.
* **Personnel transport / planning** — Independent investigators building **arrival timelines** around Provo have flagged the flight as **potentially relevant to pre-event movements, personnel transport, or planning** on the morning of September 10. Per those X timelines, the morning arrival and the pre-shooting onward leg to Santa Barbara are what drew the attention. This too is a publicly reported lead, not an established fact.
* **Innocent coincidence** — A short business out-and-back by a donor-affiliated aircraft may have no connection to the events at UVU. No manifest, passenger list, or stated purpose has been made public to rule this in or out.

Owens herself framed the flight as a question, not an answer, telling viewers "somebody saw something" and asking the public for tips rather than naming a culprit.

## Open questions

1. Who was aboard N560TW on each leg of the September 10 Scottsdale–Provo–Santa Barbara route?
2. What was the stated business purpose of the Provo land-and-turn and the Santa Barbara stop?
3. Who is the beneficial owner of the aircraft, as distinct from the registered LLC?
4. Is there any documentary basis for the donor-pressure narrative beyond commentary?
5. Does the Santa Barbara leg connect in any way to tail N872RA, which also touched Santa Barbara and Provo that day?

## Flight data recovery

N560TW is still served by every source that carries it, and it is one of the two aircraft here whose
FlightRadar24 page had actually flown recently enough to render rows on the free tier. **Nine flight
rows** were recovered off archived pages, and the ADS-B Exchange monthly samples reach back to
**January 2022** — the deepest reach of any free archive for this tail.

| Source | What it holds for N560TW |
|---|---|
| adsb.lol `globe_history` | **9 days** of track, 1 September 2025 to 9 October 2025 |
| globe.airplanes.live | **27 days** of track, 1 September 2025 to 5 June 2026 |
| ADS-B Exchange monthly sample | **23 days**, 1 January 2022 to 1 August 2026 |
| FlightRadar24 page | **HTTP 200** in a browser on 24 August 2026; **28 history rows** on the free seven-day tier; older history paywalled |
| Internet Archive | **2 page snapshots** held; **9 flight rows** recovered off them |

**No tracking site was found to have removed this aircraft's page.** Our scripts recorded HTTP 403
from FlightRadar24, RadarBox and Planespotters, but the same script recorded the identical 403 for
five unrelated control aircraft, so those are robot blocks and establish nothing about this tail:
[What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means). FlightAware and
ADS-B Exchange serve scripts normally and returned HTTP 200 for N560TW.

Where a day is absent from an archive, that means the volunteer receiver network heard nothing on
that day. It does not mean the aircraft did not fly, and it does not mean a transponder was off.
Both free daily archives begin in 2023, so no 2022 date is testable at all — the 2022 monthly
samples above are the only window into that year, and they are monthly snapshots, not a record of
every flight.

Raw payloads are saved at `site/docs/Planes/N560TW/data/recovered/`, with the source named in every
filename and a `.meta.json` sidecar per file recording the source, URL, HTTP status, byte count and
date.

Method and fleet-wide results: **[Flight Data Recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview)** ·

## The primary flight data we hold on this aircraft, and where it came from

Everything above this line rests on commentary, a registry lookup, and screenshots of somebody
else's screen. This section does not. It is raw position data this investigation pulled itself,
from free public archives anyone can query, and saved to disk at
`site/docs/Planes/N560TW/data/recovered/` with a `.meta.json` sidecar beside every payload
recording the source, the URL, the HTTP status, the byte count and the retrieval time.

| Measure | N560TW |
|---|---|
| Days of primary track held | **49** |
| Span of those days | **1 January 2022 – 1 August 2026** |
| From adsb.lol `globe_history` | 9 days |
| From globe.airplanes.live (the backup network) | 27 days |
| From the ADS-B Exchange monthly sample | 23 days |
| Corroborated by **both** free daily archives | **9 days** |
| Recovered **only** from the backup network | **18 days** |
| Held **only** by the monthly sample | **22 days** |
| Days before 2023 | **6** |
| Field touched most often across all days | **Scottsdale (KSDL) — 77 times** |

Counts from `site/docs/Planes/following/apis/public_open_source/data/provenance/tail_provenance.json`.

Eighteen of those forty-nine days exist only because we asked a second archive. That is ordinary,
not sinister: the two networks run on different volunteer receivers, with different antennas, in
different places, and a day one hears and the other does not is a coverage difference. It is worth
stating because a single-source check of this tail would have under-reported its record by more
than a third.

### The 2022 traces, on this site for the first time

`samples.adsbexchange.com` publishes a free full-day sample for **the first of every month**, back
to 2016, with no account and no key. Six of those sampled days fall in 2022 for N560TW. Positions
are given as the nearest airport and the distance to it — **that is geometry, not a landing
record.**

| Sampled day | First fix | Last fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Jan 2022 | Scottsdale (KSDL, 0.18 km) | Scottsdale (KSDL, 0.18 km) |
| 1 Feb 2022 | Scottsdale (KSDL, 0.18 km) | Phoenix Sky Harbor (KPHX, 4.92 km) |
| 1 Apr 2022 | Jackson Hole, WY (KJAC, 0.18 km) | Scottsdale (KSDL, 0.17 km) |
| 1 May 2022 | Scottsdale (KSDL, 0.20 km) | Scottsdale (KSDL, 0.18 km) |
| 1 Jul 2022 | Scottsdale (KSDL, 0.19 km) | Double Eagle II, Albuquerque, NM (KAEG, 20.85 km) |
| 1 Nov 2022 | Scottsdale (KSDL, 0.17 km) | Phoenix Deer Valley (KDVT, 7.80 km) |

**The sampling caveat has to travel with every one of those rows: this is one day per month.** Six
sampled days establish where the aircraft was on six days. They cannot support a rate, a frequency,
or a claim about how often it flew anywhere.

Within that limit, one thing is clear and it bears directly on this page. **Five of the six sampled
2022 days start or end at Scottsdale, and KSDL is the field this aircraft touches more than any
other across the whole 49-day record — 77 times.** Scottsdale is this aircraft's home base, and has
been since at least January 2022. The route this page is built around — Scottsdale to Provo to
Santa Barbara and back on 10 September 2025 — begins and ends where this aircraft always begins and
ends. **A Scottsdale-based jet departing Scottsdale is the ordinary case, not the anomaly.**
Whatever is interesting about that morning, the Scottsdale end of it is not.

### What an archived page put back: a 2016 precedent for the same routing

The Internet Archive holds two captures of this tail's FlightAware page. Both were previously
recorded in our own provenance files as yielding nothing — the extractor never parsed FlightAware's
older table markup at all. `code/extract_wayback_flights.py` re-read them. **We were wrong, and this
is the correction.**

The capture of **29 November 2016** yields **nine flight legs**, naming **KSDL, KPVU, KSLC, KELP,
KTUS and MMSD**. Two of those nine are a single day:

* **18 November 2016, 09:24 MST — Scottsdale (KSDL) to Provo Muni (KPVU), landing 10:33 MST.**
* **18 November 2016, 16:42 MST — Provo Muni (KPVU) back to Scottsdale (KSDL), landing 17:47 MST.**

That is a Scottsdale–Provo land-and-turn, flown by this exact airframe, **nine years before the
assassination**, with no possible connection to this case. It is close to conclusive that the
Scottsdale–Provo shape attributed to N560TW on 10 September 2025 is ordinary flying for this
aircraft rather than a signature of anything. File:
`site/docs/Planes/N560TW/data/recovered/N560TW_20161129055521_wayback_flightaware_flights.json`.

The second capture, **15 April 2026**, returns no rows — but not because the record was gone. The
archived page is a **JavaScript shell**: 502,766 bytes of page chrome with the flight table rendered
client-side and therefore never captured. It is labelled
`JAVASCRIPT_SHELL_NO_SERVER_RENDERED_ROWS` in its sidecar, **not "0 rows"**, because those two
things mean completely different things and reading one as the other is how an absence of parsing
gets published as an absence of evidence.

### This tail is not in the overlap sheet

None of the 85 claimed overlap rows in
`site/docs/Planes/following/overlaps.csv` names N560TW. The sheet names SU-BTT, SU-BND, or no
aircraft at all. N560TW belongs to the **Kirk-side** aircraft — the tails that fix where the Kirk
party and its associates were, not the tails alleged to have followed them. **Do not merge those
two threads.** The 18-month following pattern, the 10 September day-of timeline, the
[N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview) thread and the [N888KG](/Planes/N888KG/overview) Wendover
departure are four separate claims about four separate aircraft sets.

And the harder limit: **position data locates an aircraft. It does not locate a person.** Nothing
above establishes who was aboard N560TW on any date, in 2016, in 2022 or on 10 September 2025.
Manifests for private aircraft are not public.

### The counterargument

The plainest reading of this whole section is that N560TW is an ordinary corporate light jet based
at Scottsdale that flies ordinary corporate routes, that Provo is one of many fields it has used
over a decade, and that its appearance on 10 September 2025 has an unremarkable business
explanation nobody has published because nobody is obliged to. Nothing in the data we pulled
contradicts that reading; in the case of the 2016 Provo turn, the data actively supports it. The
counterargument is not a formality here — it is the better-supported position.

### Why pulling this data matters at all

The claim this section answers is not about N560TW. It is the broader claim that this category of
record has been erased. **We pulled four and a half years of ordinary flying for this aircraft, on
demand, from free archives, in an afternoon.** What came back is unremarkable business aviation.
That is a finding: a record that can be reconstructed this easily is not a record that has been
scrubbed, and the honest way to report the recovery is to report what it actually shows.

## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — N560TW](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n560tw) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.
* [ADS-B Exchange — N560TW](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/?reg=N560TW) — volunteer-fed, unfiltered tracker that does **not** honor LADD, so it often shows blocked aircraft and tracks the filtered sites leave out.

## Dates we looked for and did not find

Not every claimed N560TW flight could be checked against a primary ADS-B trace. **Every date we queried and got nothing back for is listed — with the exact HTTP status and the reason we looked — on [ADS-B gaps for N560TW](/Planes/N560TW/data/adsb/MISSING_DATA).** That page is published for the same reason the recovered traces are: a gap that is written down can be argued with, and a gap that is quietly dropped cannot.

**An absence there is not a finding.** A 404 means a volunteer receiver network heard nothing on that UTC day — parked and silent, outside coverage, or a wrong claimed date all come first, and [what a 403 means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means) sets out why a refusal and a deletion look identical from outside. The four free archives that did produce data, and the control test that separates a retention boundary from a removal, are on [Flight data recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview) and [per-aircraft recovery status](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Per-Aircraft-Status).

## Where this page fits

One sentence on a broadcast can make an aircraft famous, and this page is the check on that — the Scottsdale to Provo to Santa Barbara morning route [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens) said she was now very interested in, set against primary position data and read next to the other tails on the [Provo arrival list](/Planes/Sept10-Flight-Timeline), [N40JD](/Planes/N40JD/overview) and [N872RA](/Planes/N872RA/overview). The reported owner link runs to a conservative donor entity that has publicly listed [Turning Point USA](/TPUSA/overview) among its partners, which is why the tail keeps surfacing in the [donor-pressure material](/Amfest/donor-pressure-bookings) and the [political context](/political_context/overview) rather than in anything about the shooting itself, and no wrongdoing by any owner or operator is established. The registry answer is blunter than the rumour: [TPUSA aircraft checked against the FAA](/Planes/TPUSA-Aircraft/overview) shows Turning Point USA and Turning Point Action own no registered aircraft at all, which reframes every TPUSA plane claim on this site as a claim about who chartered rather than who owned.
