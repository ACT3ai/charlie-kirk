## The identifier table — match on hex, never on the registration

Almost every unresolvable dispute in this section traces to an aircraft being misidentified. Registrations get retyped by hand and a character slips; **[N59906](/Planes/N59906/overview) and [N55906](/Planes/N55906/overview)** differ by one digit, and **[SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) and [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview)** differ by one letter each while being three consecutive serial numbers of the same aircraft type flying near-identical routes.

The **ICAO 24-bit address** — the Mode S hex code — is the field that does not slip, because it is copied out of tracker output rather than typed from memory. It is burned into the transponder and it is what raw ADS-B archives index on. **Any claim in this section should be checkable against this table.**

| Tail | Hex (Mode S) | Type | MSN | Build | Listed operator |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) | **0101D3** | Dassault Falcon 7X | 239 | Nov 2013 | Egypt - Air Force |
| [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) | **0101D0** | Dassault Falcon 7X | 243 | 2015 | Egypt - Air Force |
| [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview) | **0101D1** | Dassault Falcon 7X | 246 | 2014 | Egypt - Air Force |
| [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) | **01003E** | Gulfstream 4SP | 1332 | 1998 | *"Private owner"* — none listed |
| [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview) | **010070** | Gulfstream IV | 1048 | — | *"Egypt Goverment"* (sic) — none listed |
| [N888KG](/Planes/N888KG/overview) | **AC3C75** | Bombardier Challenger 300 | 20402 | 2013 | Private owner |
| [N59906](/Planes/N59906/overview) | **A7C14D** | Piper PA-31 Navajo Chieftain | 31-7552027 | **1974** | MARC INC |
| [N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview) | **A0299E** | Bombardier Global 6500 | 60098 | Feb 2023 | *(none listed)* |

Three things fall out of that table on sight:

* **The three Falcon 7Xs are consecutive fleet serials** — 239, 243, 246 — delivered 2013–2015, with hex codes in a contiguous block. They are one purchase, and they are functionally interchangeable in any account written from memory.
* **The two Gulfstreams carry no operator entry at all.** SU-BND is listed as a *private owner* aircraft. That is a real weakening of the "Egyptian military plane" framing as applied to that specific airframe, and it should be stated wherever that framing is used.
* **N59906 is a 1974 piston twin.** Any account that describes it as a modern surveillance platform is describing something else.

### The UVU trainer aircraft in Michigan

On **September 28, 2025**, **@MorganC000** posted a Flightradar24 collage under the text:

> The same small UVU aircraft seen loitering over Utah Valley University at the time Kirk was allegedly shot were also in Michigan today, patrolling before, during, and after the latest incident at the LDS church.
>
> No one has had anything to say about the flight activity of these aircraft.

The collage's Utah panels show two identified aircraft:

* **N114UV** — a **Diamond DA40 Diamond Star**, departing and returning to **PVU (Provo)**.
* **N908PA** — a **Diamond DA20-C1**, departing and returning to **QNK (Spanish Fork)**.

The Michigan panels, over **Grand Blanc** (map labels: NCG Cinema, Buffalo Wild Wings, McCandlish Rd, Holly Rd, Sherwood Dr, Interstate 75, Henry Ford Health Genesys), show a track attributed to an aircraft the tracker labels **"N/A · C172"** — a Cessna 172 with **no registration displayed**.

This does not hold, for reasons visible in the poster's own images:

* **N114UV is a Utah Valley University flight-school trainer.** The `UV` suffix is a UVU fleet marking; UVU runs a large aviation program based at Provo Municipal Airport. **N908PA** operating out of Spanish Fork is likewise a local flight-training aircraft. Single-engine trainers flying circuits out of their home fields all day is what a flight school is.
* **The Michigan aircraft is a different type.** A **Cessna 172** is not a Diamond DA40 or a DA20. The "same aircraft" claim fails on the airframe type printed in the poster's own screenshot.
* **The Michigan aircraft has no registration shown.** The tracker field reads **N/A**. There is nothing to match against anything.
* **A Cessna 172 over Michigan is the most common aircraft in the most common configuration in American aviation.** There are more than 44,000 of them.

Recorded here so that anyone encountering the claim can see why it fails, and so that the two Utah tail numbers — which are real, and are ordinary — are not carried forward as anomalies.

### The "investigation dossier" people-ranking app

A set of screenshots circulating in this material shows a mobile interface headed **"INVESTIGATION DOSSIER"** and **"PEOPLE DATABASE,"** listing named individuals with colour-coded numerical ratings ("10/10," "9/10," "4/10"), role labels ("Security Lead," "Official Shooter," "Person of Interest," "Board Member"), and status flags ("Missing," "Active," "Incarcerated").

**This is not evidence of anything and it is not an aircraft record.** It is somebody's self-built app assigning suspicion scores to living people — including TPUSA staff, board members, event attendees, a Q&A questioner, and Erika Kirk — with no stated methodology, no sourcing, and no accountability. Several people on it are labelled "Missing" and several are given roles that amount to accusations.

**None of the people in that database has been charged with or accused of anything by any authority**, and the ratings are one anonymous person's opinion rendered as a user interface. The names are not reproduced here. It is described only so readers who encounter these screenshots recognise what they are looking at: a mock-up, not a record.

### Screenshots that are not about this case at all

Some captures circulating inside the plane-research folders are simply unrelated material caught in the same screenshot sweep — in one instance, a domain registrar's account dashboard listing dozens of unrelated web domains. Anything of that kind that reaches these pages should be discarded rather than interpreted. It is noted only because the presence of off-topic captures in a shared research set is a reason to check the provenance of *every* screenshot in that set rather than treating the batch as uniformly relevant.
