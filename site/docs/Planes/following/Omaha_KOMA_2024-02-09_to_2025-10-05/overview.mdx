# Omaha (KOMA) — February 2024 to October 2025

**Egyptian-registered jets landed at Omaha six times between February 2024 and October 2025. On every single occasion, the aircraft took off again for Lincoln within twenty minutes.** Not once did one of them stay.

That single fact is what this page exists to establish, because **"Omaha, Nebraska" appears repeatedly in the shadowing counts as though it were a place these aircraft went.** It is not. It is where they cleared into the country before flying a quarter of an hour west to [Lincoln](./Lincoln_KLNK_2024-02-09_to_2025-10-11).

## The airport

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Eppley Airfield |
| **Identifiers** | **KOMA** (ICAO) / **OMA** (IATA) |
| **City / State** | Omaha, Douglas County, Nebraska |
| **Class** | Commercial-service **international** airport — the largest airport in Nebraska |
| **Distance to Lincoln (KLNK)** | ~50 miles, ~15–20 minutes by air |
| **Why it matters** | It has **US Customs and Border Protection** facilities. Lincoln's are limited. This is the operational reason for the split. |

### How unusual is a foreign government jet here

Not unusual at all, and that is the point. Eppley is Nebraska's international gateway with full customs handling. A transatlantic business jet arriving from Paris and clearing into the United States at Omaha is precisely what the airport is for. **An arrival here is not an anomaly requiring explanation; it is the ordinary front door.**

**Every hop is between 15 and 19 minutes.** Eight arrivals, eight quarter-hour departures for Lincoln, over twenty months, by three different aircraft.

The ground time at Omaha ranges from about an hour to about five and a half — which is the range you get from a customs clearance plus a crew break, and nothing like the range you get from a visit.

### Why a 15-minute flight instead of a car

This question is worth answering directly because it looks odd. Omaha to Lincoln is about an hour's drive on Interstate 80. Why fly?

Because the aircraft has to get to Lincoln. **The people are not the cargo — the airframe is.** If a jet is going into the shop at Duncan Aviation's Lincoln plant, the jet must physically be at Lincoln. Clearing customs at Omaha and repositioning empty or near-empty to Lincoln is standard practice for exactly this reason, and it is why every one of these hops exists.

That is also why **the Omaha hop is the strongest single piece of evidence for the maintenance reading anywhere in this section.** There is no surveillance interpretation of a 15-minute positioning flight between two Nebraska cities. It is an aircraft moving itself to a facility.

## Where the audit puts these dates

The [independent line-by-line audit](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika) of the overlap spreadsheet is unusually harsh on the Omaha rows, and the pattern of its objections is instructive:

* **Index 20 (9 Feb 2024, "Cairo→Paris→Omaha"):** *"Accurate-ish, flew from Omaha to Lincoln."* The route is right as far as it goes; the sheet stops at Omaha and misses that the aircraft immediately left.
* **Index 21 (14 Feb 2024, "Omaha→Wilmington→Cairo"):** ***"Flew from Lincoln to Wilmington, not Omaha."*** The sheet has the departure from the wrong city entirely.
* **Index 47 (15 Mar 2023, "Paris→Omaha, Nebraska"):** *"in Egypt or Jordan."*
* **Index 48 (27 Mar 2023, "Omaha→Wilmington→Cairo"):** *"In Egypt."*
* **Index 55 (15 Apr 2024, "Paris→Omaha→Cairo"):** *"Cairo 5 Apr – 16 Apr."*
* **Index 62 (8 Feb 2025, "Paris→Omaha→Cairo"):** *"Cairo."*

**Index 21 is the most revealing.** It shows the source sheet treating Omaha as the place the aircraft was, when the aircraft had left Omaha five days earlier and was at Lincoln. The error is not random — it is what happens when a compiler records the arrival airport and never checks whether the aircraft stayed there.

## The counterargument

There is barely a claim here to argue against, which is itself the finding:

* **Eppley Airfield is Nebraska's international port of entry.** A transatlantic arrival clearing customs here is the ordinary use of the airport.
* **Every arrival departs for Lincoln within twenty minutes.** No visit occurred. There is nothing to explain except a customs stop.
* **[Duncan Aviation's main plant is at Lincoln](./Lincoln_KLNK_2024-02-09_to_2025-10-11)**, and it has held the Egyptian Air Force maintenance account since 1999. The hop is the aircraft moving to the shop.
* **The one documented Kirk appearance in Omaha has no aircraft within two months of it.**

## What we do not know

1. **Who disembarked at Omaha, if anyone.** Customs was cleared here. The entry records for every person aboard all eight arrivals are **CBP records at Omaha**, not Lincoln and not Provo. None has been requested publicly.
2. **Whether the hop to Lincoln carried passengers or was flown empty.** Repositioning flights frequently carry only crew. Nothing in the public record says which.
3. **The 20 July 2025 five-and-a-half-hour ground time** is longer than the others by a factor of four. Nobody has explained it, and nobody has asked.
4. **The Kirk-side event dates are uncited**, coming from a researcher timeline on X.
