## What was actually on the ground near this event

A **blind geographic sweep** streams an entire UTC day of the open ADS-B archive — 74,000 to 95,000 aircraft — and keeps only what fell inside a 50-mile circle on **Gainesville, FL**. It is not given a tail number, so it cannot be steered toward a wanted answer. Here is what it found around this event.

| Sweep day (UTC) | | Aircraft entering the circle | On the ground | Flagged on the ground |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2025-02-26 | Day before | 1886 | 22 | 9 |
| 2025-02-27 | **Same day** | 2010 | 25 | 3 |
| 2025-02-28 | Day after | 104 | 1 | 1 |

### What "flagged" is actually made of

| Category | 2025-02-26 | 2025-02-27 | 2025-02-28 | What it means |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Unregistered / non-ICAO address | 0 | 1 | 0 | No registration resolved, or a transponder address outside the ICAO block. |
| FAA privacy program (LADD) | 9 | 2 | 1 | **Ordinary and not suspicious.** Thousands of US owners file for this; at a training field it is mostly Cessna 172s. |

**12 of the flagged aircraft here carry nothing but the FAA privacy flag.** They are excluded from the named table below. Counting them as suspicious is the single easiest way to inflate a number on this subject, and this site will not do it.

### The same days, at cities with no event

On these same sweep dates the run also covered six control cities with no Kirk or TPUSA event. Averaged per circle-day:

| | On the ground | Flagged on the ground |
|---|---:|---:|
| **This event circle** | 16.0 | 4.3 |
| Control circles (18 circle-days) | 28.6 | 5.4 |

**Do not read a bigger number here as a finding.** Event cities are large metros with large airports; several control cities are not. A raw per-circle comparison measures airport size, and once it is normalised for that, event circles hold a *lower* share of flagged aircraft than the controls. See [Investigating Deleted Flights](/Planes/investigating_deleted_flights) §4.5.

**What this section cannot tell you.** A trace proves presence — never purpose, and never occupancy. Military aircraft near Salt Lake City are routine: the Utah Air National Guard's refuelling wing is based at the airport and the Army Guard flies helicopters from South Valley Regional. An aircraft being in a circle is not an aircraft being *at* an event.

## Where this page fits

This stop was not in the source spreadsheet at all — it was added from later sourcing, and a tour date the compilers never had is a direct test of whether the [73-overlap tally](/Planes/following/73_overlaps) was built on a complete event list or a partial one, which is the question the [speaking index](/Planes/following/speaking/overview), the [TPUSA event ledger](/Planes/following/TPUSA_events) and the [Following](/Planes/following/overview) file were created to answer. An incomplete denominator inflates any coincidence rate, and that is the arithmetic behind the on-air 0.000000001 percent figure — the [broadcast record](/Planes/following/Candace_Owens_Broadcasts) sits beside the [AI-assistant answers page](/Planes/following/AI_Assistant_Answers) showing what Grok and Gemini return to the same question, and beside the [influencer material](/Influencers/overview) and [social-media analysis](/social_media_analysis/overview) that carried it. For a worked case of the denominator problem, the [Florida State stop the next day](/Planes/following/speaking/20250228_tallahassee) makes three Florida campuses in nine days — three chances for a coincidence the original sheet counted as zero.
