# St. Louis (KSTL / KCPS) — December 2022 to February 2025

**Missouri is one of the five states in the geographic clustering offered as evidence of the shadowing pattern, and St. Louis is where it comes from.** Three aircraft — [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) and [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) — visited across the window, including one stay that ran nearly three months.

**This is also where the closest documented Kirk-side pairing in the entire pre-2025 record sits**, and where the compiler of the source timeline was honest enough to footnote the distance rather than hide it.

## The airports — and why there are two

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Lambert–St. Louis International Airport |
| **Identifiers** | **KSTL** / **STL** |
| **Class** | Major commercial-service international airport, US customs port of entry |
| | |
| **Name** | St. Louis Downtown Airport |
| **Identifiers** | **KCPS** / **CPS** |
| **Location** | **Cahokia, Illinois** — across the Mississippi from downtown St. Louis |
| **Class** | General aviation reliever airport |
| **Distance apart** | ~15 miles; **a 15-minute flight, which is exactly what the record shows** |

The two-airport structure matters for the counts. **The February 2025 rotation logs "St. Louis (STL) → St. Louis Downtown (CPS)" as a 15-minute flight** — the same repositioning signature this fleet produces at [Omaha→Lincoln](../Omaha_KOMA_2024-02-09_to_2025-10-05/overview). Lambert is the customs port; Cahokia is where the aircraft actually parks. Anyone tallying "St. Louis visits" from a leg list will find two entries for one arrival.

Note also that **KCPS is physically in Illinois**, not Missouri. A "Missouri overlap" logged against a CPS arrival is a state-level match to the wrong state.

### How unusual is a foreign government jet here

Lambert is unremarkable — a major international airport with full customs. **St. Louis Downtown is the more interesting field**: a general aviation reliever with a substantial corporate and maintenance presence, of the kind business jets use when they want to be near a city without being at its airline hub. Neither is anomalous for this aircraft type.

### A third St. Louis-area airport appears in the audit

The audit's note on index 46 reads: *"1 week in St. Louis **(KSUS)**."* **KSUS is Spirit of St. Louis Airport, in Chesterfield, Missouri** — a third field in the metro area, roughly 20 miles west of downtown, and one of the busiest general-aviation airports in the region.

So the record touches **three separate St. Louis-area airports**: Lambert (KSTL, Missouri), Downtown/Cahokia (KCPS, Illinois), and Spirit of St. Louis (KSUS, Missouri). **A state-level tally counting "Missouri" cannot distinguish them, and a leg-level tally counting arrivals will multiply one visit into several.**

### The 1–6 April 2023 pairing, weighed honestly

**This is the strongest thing in this section outside Utah, and the compiler of the timeline flagged its weakness himself.**

The pairing: SU-BTT flies Cairo → Paris → St. Louis, landing at 11:02 am on 1 April 2023. The next day, 2 April, **TPUSA Faith launches its tour with a Sean Feucht event in Jefferson City, Missouri.** The aircraft stays until 6 April, then leaves for Wilmington and Cairo. The audit marks both the arrival and the departure **accurate** — the flights happened as described.

**And the timeline's own footnote reads: *"Event is in Jefferson City, MO (2 hrs from St. Louis)."***

That is a compiler marking his own strongest row as a 130-mile, two-hour-drive match. It is the single most creditable act of sourcing anywhere in this material, and it is worth saying so: **the person building the case wrote down the distance that weakens it.**

What survives, stated at its real strength: **an Egyptian-registered jet was on the ground in the same US metropolitan area for the five days spanning the launch of a TPUSA Faith tour, and a second Egyptian jet had been parked there since February.** Two foreign aircraft in one metro area across a period of TPUSA activity is a real co-location. It is not a same-airport, same-day overlap, and no one has claimed either Kirk was in Missouri on any of these dates.

### The February 2023 cluster

The most concentrated period in the pre-2025 record: **SU-BND lands at St. Louis on 21 February 2023 and stays until May** — nearly three months — while **SU-BTT arrives on 25 February** and stays until 2 March. **Two Egyptian jets in one metro area for a week**, overlapping the dates Sean Feucht was announced as a TPUSA Faith contributor (27 Feb) and as head of the "Kingdom to the Capitol" tour (28 Feb).

The counter-reading is the same one that explains SU-BND's later 113-day stay at [Provo](../Provo_KPVU_2024-04-19_to_2025-09-13/overview): **a three-month stay is a maintenance stay.** A 1998-build Gulfstream IV sitting for eleven weeks and then flying home via Goose Bay is what a heavy check looks like, not what an operation looks like. The audit marks that departure **accurate** (index 15: *"Overall yes, STL to Goose Bay, GB to Paris, Paris to Cairo"*).

## The duplicate that is easiest to see

**Index 56 is index 14 with the year advanced.** Both read *"Test flight" → Missouri*, both attributed to SU-BND; index 14 is dated 3 May **2023** and index 56 is dated 3 May **2024**. The audit marks the 2023 entry accurate — the aircraft did fly at St. Louis that day — and the 2024 entry inaccurate, noting the plane was on the ground at Provo and calling it **"a duplicate of number 14 plus one year."**

**A single real event has become two rows in the tally, one of which is a year off and describes the aircraft being 1,200 miles away.** It is the cleanest illustration in the whole audit of how a mechanical error inflates a count, and it sits on this page.

## The counterargument

* **The 2023 pairing is a two-hour drive away, and the compiler said so.** Jefferson City is not St. Louis.
* **Neither Kirk is placed in Missouri on any of these dates by any source.** The pairings are all with TPUSA organisational events, not with a person's presence.
* **A three-month stay is a maintenance stay.** SU-BND's February–May 2023 St. Louis stay is the same signature as its 113-day Provo stay in 2025.
* **The February 2025 rotation is the standard template** — Cairo → Paris → US entry → 15-minute repositioning hop → six-day dwell → Wilmington → Cairo. Identical in shape to the nine [Lincoln](../Lincoln_KLNK_2024-02-09_to_2025-10-11/overview) rotations.
* **Three separate airports, one metro area.** Leg-level counting multiplies a single visit.
* **Four of the claimed Missouri overlaps fail outright** — two put the aircraft in Egypt, two put it in Utah, and one of those is a duplicate.

## What we do not know

1. **Which St. Louis-area airport each aircraft actually parked at**, and which facility worked on them. The audit's own KSUS reference suggests the published summaries are not tracking this correctly.
2. **Whether anything was being done to SU-BND during its eleven weeks on the ground in 2023.** No shop record has been produced.
3. **Whether Charlie or Erika Kirk was in Missouri on any of these dates.** No source claims either was.
4. **The Kirk-side event dates are uncited**, coming from a researcher timeline circulated on X.
5. **Whether the 2 April 2023 Jefferson City event has any documented connection to a St. Louis arrival**, beyond proximity in time and state.
