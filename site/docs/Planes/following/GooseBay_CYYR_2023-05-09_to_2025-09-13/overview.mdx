# Goose Bay (CYYR) — May 2023 to September 2025

**[SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) left the United States on 13 September 2025 — three days after the assassination — and its first stop was a Canadian Forces base in Labrador. It was on the ground there for about fifty minutes.**

That sentence contains the only thing about Goose Bay that will make a reader sit up, and the honest answer to it is on this page: **fifty minutes is what a fuel stop looks like**, and the aircraft that used Goose Bay used it because of what they were, not because of where they had been.

**Only the two Gulfstreams ever stopped here.** [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) and [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview) route through Goose Bay; [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) and [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview) — the three Falcon 7Xs — never do, not once, in any direction, in the entire captured record. **That is a range split, not a mission split, and it is the single cleanest non-conspiratorial explanation available anywhere in this investigation.**

## The airport

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Goose Bay Airport |
| **Identifiers** | **CYYR** (ICAO) / **YYR** (IATA) |
| **Town / Province** | Happy Valley–Goose Bay, Labrador, Newfoundland and Labrador, Canada |
| **Class** | **Joint civil / military field.** It is co-located with — and operated in connection with — **5 Wing Goose Bay**, a Royal Canadian Air Force wing. It is a Canadian airport of entry with CBSA facilities. |
| **History** | Built in **1941** as a staging field on the North Atlantic ferry route. Through the Cold War it hosted **United States Air Force Strategic Air Command** operations and later NATO low-level flight training. |
| **Runway** | Over 11,000 feet — long enough for the heavy bombers and transports it was built for, and therefore for anything in this fleet. |
| **Why anyone stops here** | It sits on the great-circle line between north-western Europe and the North American interior, at roughly the point where a range-limited aircraft runs out of fuel. It is the classic **North Atlantic technical stop.** |

### How unusual is a foreign government jet here

**Not unusual. Goose Bay handles this traffic every week, and has since 1941.**

This is the point at which the "foreign state aircraft on a military installation" framing has to be handled carefully, because it is technically true and analytically weak.

**True:** Goose Bay is a military-operated field. A foreign government aircraft landing there is landing at a Canadian Forces wing.

**Weak:** that is the *only* thing at that latitude with a runway, customs, fuel and 24-hour handling. The base is there because the geography is there. Business jets, ferry flights and airliners on diversions have used Goose Bay as a technical stop for eighty years for one reason, and it is not the base — it is the map.

The [Minot](./Minot_KMOT_2025-04-08_to_2025-09-04/overview) argument is different and stronger, because Minot had genuine alternatives and the aircraft chose it anyway four times. **Goose Bay has no meaningful alternative.** A Gulfstream IV that cannot make Paris from Utah in one leg stops at Goose Bay or it stops at Reykjavík, and that is the whole list.

## The Gulfstream-only pattern

This is the finding on this page, and it is a finding that works against the surveillance reading rather than for it.

| Tail | Type | Transatlantic routing | Goose Bay stops |
|---|---|---|---|
| [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) | Dassault Falcon 7X | Cairo → Paris → US; **Wilmington → Cairo nonstop** | **0** |
| [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) | Dassault Falcon 7X | Cairo → Paris → US; **Wilmington → Cairo nonstop** | **0** |
| [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview) | Dassault Falcon 7X | Cairo → Paris → US; **Wilmington → Cairo nonstop** | **0** |
| [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) | Gulfstream IV-SP (1998 airframe) | **Paris → Goose Bay → US; US → Goose Bay → Paris → Cairo** | **3 confirmed** |
| [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview) | Gulfstream IV | **US → Goose Bay → Paris** | **1 confirmed** |

The Falcon 7X is a modern long-range trijet; it crosses the Atlantic westbound with a Paris stop for crew-duty reasons and eastbound nonstop from Delaware. **The Gulfstream IV is an older, shorter-legged airframe** — the SU-BND example is a 1998 build, 27 years old at the time of these flights — and it needs a North Atlantic tech stop that the Falcons do not.

**The two aircraft in the 73-overlap tally are one of each.** SU-BTT is a Falcon; SU-BND is a Gulfstream. They fly different routes across the Atlantic, and the difference has nothing to do with either Kirk. Anyone reading the route strings as behaviour has to account for that first.

## The 13 September 2025 departure

Stated plainly, because it is the reason a reader arrives at this page:

* SU-BND had been on the ground at [Provo](./Provo_KPVU_2024-04-19_to_2025-09-13/overview) for **113 continuous days**, since 23 May 2025.
* It was still there on **10 September 2025**, transponder reportedly on, and it **did not take off that day** — unlike [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), which left Provo for [Wilmington](./Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) that morning.
* It departed Provo on **13 September at 12:57 PM**, flew **5:10** to Goose Bay, landed **9:07 PM**, and was airborne again for Paris at **9:57 PM**.
* From Paris it went home to Cairo on **15 September**.

**A fifty-minute turn is a fuel-and-go.** There is no time in fifty minutes to do anything else — the aircraft is refuelled, the crew files, and it leaves. If a reader is looking for a handover, an offload or a transfer, the flight record does not offer one here.

**What the record does show is that a foreign state aircraft that had sat in Utah for four months left North America seventy-two hours after the assassination, through a military field, on a Saturday night.** Both halves of that sentence are true and neither is evidence without the other. The [independent audit](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika) marks this departure **accurate** — one of the small number of rows on the spreadsheet that survives review.

## The two claimed transits that are not confirmed

**17 July 2024 — "BND leaves for Cairo via Goose Bay and Paris."** From the researcher chronology circulated on X. The same audit that examined this period notes that SU-BND had been parked at Provo since 19 April 2024 and **left around 12 July** — five days before the claimed Goose Bay leg. The routing is plausible; the date is not confirmed and the two accounts disagree. Recorded here unresolved.

**7 July 2025 — "GOOSEBAY → PARIS → CAIRO."** This appears **twice** on the spreadsheet, as index 37 and again as index 66, and both are wrong. The auditor's note is blunt: ***"The plane was in Provo from 5/23 until 9/13"*** and ***"ONLY LOCAL FLIGHTS WERE FLOWN IN AND AROUND PROVO FROM 10 JUNE–13 SEPT."***

That single row is a compact illustration of what the audit found across the whole sheet: **a leg that never happened, entered twice, and counted twice.** It is also, awkwardly, a claim that the aircraft *left* Utah on a date when the aircraft's continued presence in Utah is the strongest fact the following theory has.

## The counterargument

* **Goose Bay is the North Atlantic tech stop.** Its use by a range-limited aircraft requires no explanation beyond fuel.
* **The military co-location is a 1941 artefact.** The base is at Goose Bay because the geography suited a ferry route; the civil traffic is there for the same reason.
* **Only the Gulfstreams used it, and only the Gulfstreams needed it.** If the routing were mission-driven the Falcons would show up here too. They never do.
* **A fifty-minute turn is a refuelling stop and nothing longer fits in the time.**
* **The spreadsheet's Goose Bay rows are among its worst.** One is a duplicated leg that never happened; another conflicts with the audit by five days. Goose Bay is a place where the source material demonstrably fails.

## What we do not know

1. **Whether anyone boarded or left any of these aircraft at Goose Bay.** No manifest for any leg has ever been published.
2. **The CBSA records** for all three confirmed transits exist and have never been requested.
3. **What the 9 May 2023 timings were.** The audit confirms the routing; no captured screenshot gives departure or arrival times for that transit.
4. **Whether the 17 July 2024 transit happened at all**, and if so on what date.
5. **Whether SU-BND's fifty-minute turn on 13 September 2025 was fuel only.** It fits the time available; it has not been confirmed.
6. **Whether the fleet used Goose Bay before May 2023.** The captured histories do not reach back that far, and the auditor's *"Data doesn't go back this far"* note on the 2022 rows applies here too.
