# Government Flight Records

**This is the only pass that produces records instead of readings.**

Everything on the rest of this site about where an aircraft was comes from somebody's radio receiver
hearing a transponder. That is useful and it is checkable, but it is an observation. A landing-fee
invoice, a fuel ticket, a customs entry, an airport badge log or a registry entry is a **record** —
and on this site a record outranks every reading and every social-media post about the same flight.

It is also the slowest pass. A download takes a minute. A records request takes weeks, and some of
them come back refused.

## What downloads freely, right now, with no request at all

### The FAA Releasable Aircraft Database

United States government work, public domain, no key, no account, refreshed daily.

```
https://registry.faa.gov/database/ReleasableAircraft.zip
```

Roughly 70 MB compressed. `MASTER.txt` is one row per registered airframe keyed on the N-number
without its leading N; `ACFTREF.txt` is the type reference. Because it is a government work it may
be **republished in full**, and this is the one pass where mirroring the data onto the site is the
right thing to do rather than a licence problem.

Pulled **24 August 2026** by `apis/government/code/faa_registry.js`. All ten N-registered tails this
investigation tracks were found. The extracted records are committed at
`apis/government/data/faa_registry/tracked_tails_faa_master.json`; the 70 MB archive is not, because
it is re-downloadable in a minute.

### And immediately it contradicted the community databases

This is why the pass exists. For several tails, **the registrant the FAA actually records is not the
owner name circulating in the tracking threads.** Both columns below were retrieved on the same day.

|---|---|---|
| N102DZ | ELDERBERRY CHESTNUT LLC — Cheyenne, WY | Aviation Enterprises Inc |
| N888KG | N888KG LLC — Lehi, UT | Fair Wind Air Charter |
| N582MM | DOJA CONTRACTING LLC — Mesa, AZ | Wildhorse LLC |
| N40JD | NUP AVIATION LLC — Princeton, IL | JDT Aviation LLC |
| N59906 | ALPHA WHISKEY 2 LLC — Brandon, MS | Marc Inc |
| N560TW | T W LEWIS-AVIATION LLC — Scottsdale, AZ | Pinnacle Aviation |
| N872RA | A&E HAWKER LP — Santa Ana, CA | Regency Air |
| N1098L | LASAI AVIATION II LLC — Manassas, VA | LASAI Aviation II LLC |
| N2100L | LASAI AVIATION II LLC — Manassas, VA | LASAI Aviation II LLC |
| N55906 | OPOLINER LARRY — Winter Park, FL | (individual) |

**The counterargument, and it is a strong one:** these are not necessarily errors. The FAA file is
current registration; a community database may be carrying a former owner, an operator rather than
an owner, or a management company rather than a registrant. Aircraft change hands. Both can have
been true at different times.

But that is exactly the point. **When a claim on X names the owner of an aircraft, it is very often
quoting a community database, not the registry** — and the two do not agree. Where this site names a
registrant, it names the FAA one, and says the date it was retrieved.

Note also that a registrant is frequently a holding LLC. **A holding company is not a person, and
this site does not go behind one to name private individuals.**

### The hole in the middle of this pass

**Every one of the five Egyptian tails at the centre of the following claim — SU-BTT, SU-BND,
SU-BTU, SU-BTV, SU-BGM — appears in none of it.** The FAA registry covers N-registered aircraft.
Egypt publishes no equivalent public download.

So the best free government record available to us describes the aircraft nobody is arguing about,
and is silent on the aircraft everybody is arguing about. That asymmetry is not a conspiracy — most
national registries are not published — but it does mean the foreign side of this case **cannot be
resolved by a download.** It can only be resolved by a request.

### Airport and facility data

Federal airport and facility data, and the public-domain OurAirports dataset used in the open-source
pass, supply runway lengths, elevation, ownership class and whether a field has scheduled service.
These are the numbers behind the argument that a particular field is an odd place for a foreign
government jet. **Use the dataset, not an adjective.**

## What has to be requested — and from whom

Matching the question to the right custodian is where most records requests die.

| The question | Who actually holds it | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns this airframe | Federal aviation registry | Free download, above |
| Air traffic handling, flight strips, radar | Federal air traffic agency | Federal FOIA |
| Customs processing of an international arrival | Federal border agency | Federal FOIA |
| **Landing fees, fuel tickets, ramp assignment, hangar, badge access** | **The airport itself** | **State public-records law** |
| Diplomatic clearance for a foreign state aircraft | Federal foreign-affairs department | Federal FOIA |

**The fourth row is the one that matters most here and is the least used.** Most of the fields in
this case are city-owned or run by a public airport authority. That makes them subject to **state**
public-records law, which is generally faster, cheaper and harder to stonewall than federal FOIA.
The ramp records — who parked where, who bought fuel, who was issued a badge — sit with the airport,
not with Washington.

There is a second point buried in the open-source data. **US customs processing for the 2025
Provo-bound arrivals did not happen at Provo.** The aircraft entered the United States at a northern
field first and hopped to Utah afterwards. Every published claim about passengers, contractors and
manifests has been aimed at Provo. **The customs records are somewhere else, and as far as this repo
can tell nobody has asked for them.**

## What we got so far

* **24 August 2026** — FAA Releasable Aircraft Database downloaded in full, HTTP 200, 73.1 MB.
  Ten of ten tracked N-tails extracted with registrant, address, serial, Mode S hex, certificate
  issue date and expiry. Saved to `apis/government/data/faa_registry/`.
* **24 August 2026** — FAA-recorded Mode S hex codes confirmed for all ten N-tails, and they agree
  with the hex codes the community databases give. **The hex codes agree; the owner names do not.**
* Airport reference facts for the fields named in this case are held in
  `Planes/following/airports.csv` and are cross-checked against the public-domain dataset.
* **The FAA pull still stands, re-checked 24 August 2026.** Nothing in the later flight-data review
  changed it: ten of ten tracked N-tails present, registrant and Mode S hex unchanged, the extract
  still committed at `apis/government/data/faa_registry/tracked_tails_faa_master.json`. It is the one
  result on this site that has needed no correction, which is what a **record** looks like next to a
  reading.
* **The public-domain OurAirports dataset is now held in the repo** at
  `apis/public_open_source/data/ourairports/airports.csv` (CC0), and is what resolves a trace position
  to a named field. **That resolution is geometry, not a landing record** — it measures distance to a
  set of published coordinates and knows nothing about ramps, tenancy or who got out, which is why
  every distance on this site is published in kilometres.

**Still not pulled, and both are free downloads:**

* **The federal NFDC airport and facility subscription files.** Runway surfaces and dimensions,
  lighting, ownership class, based-aircraft counts, FBO and fuel availability, customs status. These
  are the numbers behind "an odd field for a foreign state jet", and we are currently leaning on the
  community dataset for facts a government file publishes.
* **Federal operations counts by airport.** These would show **itinerant foreign operations by
  field**, which is the closest thing to a population baseline this investigation could have. Without
  it, "unusual" is an adjective. With it, "unusual" is a number with a denominator. **This is the
  cheapest unfired shot in this pass** — it is a free download, not a request.

## What we did NOT get

This is the work list, and it is the most useful section on this page.

### The thing this pass exists for, and it has still not been asked for

**An ADS-B record can locate an aircraft. It can never locate a passenger. Not once, not ever, not
under any conditions.**

That is not a limitation of our tooling. It is what the technology is. A transponder broadcasts an
airframe's identity and position. It broadcasts nothing about who is aboard, who chartered it, who
authorised it or why it went.

Take the strongest primary-data result this investigation has produced. Every row of the 85-row
overlap register was tested against primary position data from two independent archives. **29 rows
could be decided. 25 of them are corroborated** — the claimed aircraft really was at the claimed
field, some to within tens of metres and to the second, with both archives agreeing.

**Every one of those 25 confirmations is about an airframe.** Not one of them puts Charlie Kirk or
Erika Kirk anywhere. The Kirk half of every pairing is unchanged: thin for Charlie, and close to
empty for Erika, whose flight logs are reported erased — see
[Erika Kirk's flight logs reported erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased). **A confirmed aircraft
is not a confirmed overlap**, and no amount of additional ADS-B will ever close that gap, because
ADS-B is the wrong instrument for the question.

**What would close it is a facility record.** Specifically:

| Record | Custodian | Route | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Provo airport badge access list** — reported updated 11 September 2025 | The airport | **State public-records law** | **No request filed** |
| **The Duncan Aviation FBO rental-car log** | The FBO / the airport | **State public-records law** | **No request filed** |
| **FBO ramp assignment and hangar records** — who parked where | The airport | **State public-records law** | **No request filed** |
| **Fuel tickets and landing-fee invoices** — who paid, for what airframe | The airport | **State public-records law** | **No request filed** |
| **Customs manifests** for the international arrivals | Federal border agency | Federal FOIA | **No request filed** |

**Every one of those says No request filed. That is the loudest fact on this page and it should stay
loud until it changes.**

Most of the fields in this case are **city-owned or run by a public airport authority**, which puts
them under **state** public-records law rather than federal FOIA — generally faster, cheaper and much
harder to stonewall. **It remains the most under-used route in the entire investigation**, and it is
the only route that produces the kind of record that could put a named human being on a named ramp on
a named date.

**The counterargument, and it is a real one.** A badge log, a fuel ticket and a rental-car record may
come back showing exactly what an ordinary maintenance visit looks like — Duncan Aviation has plants
at Provo and at Lincoln, and an Egyptian-registered aircraft in a Duncan hangar is a mundane
explanation that fits the observed positions perfectly. **That is a reason to file the request, not a
reason not to.** A request that comes back innocent is still a record, and this site would publish it
the same way it publishes everything else.

* **No Egyptian registry.** No public download exists for SU- registrations. Owner, operator and
  airworthiness for the five tails at the centre of the claim are **unverified from any primary
  registry**, and everything published about them traces back to tracker readings.
* **No records request has been filed yet by this project.** Not one. The docket is empty. That is a
  gap in our work, not a refusal by anyone, and it is recorded here so it stops being invisible.
* **No customs records** for the northern-field entries, from any agency.
* **No ramp, fuel, landing-fee or badge records** from any of the airports in this case — including
  Provo, where a badge-access list is reported to have been updated the day after the assassination.
  That report is a claim on social media. **A public-records request is how it stops being a claim.**
* **No diplomatic-clearance records** for any foreign state aircraft movement in the window.
* **No air traffic data** — no strips, no radar, no recordings.

## The rule this pass runs on

Ask for **records**, never for conclusions. Name the exact aircraft by registration *and* by ICAO
hex, name the exact date range, name the record types, cite the free ADS-B trace as the reason the
date is specific. A narrow request that gets answered beats a broad one refused as burdensome.

**Never name a living private individual in a request.** Ask about an aircraft and a date.

And when an agency misses its statutory deadline, **the silence is a result and it gets published**
with the dates — as does any refusal, quoted with the exemption it cites.
