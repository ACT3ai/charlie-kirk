# Inversion cross-tab — foreign-fleet ground-visit days vs all 139 TPUSA/Kirk events

Generated by `inversion.py` / `pairs.py` / `crosstab.py` in this scratchpad. Read-only analysis;
nothing under `site/docs/Planes/following` was modified.

## What a row is

One row = one **(tail, UTC day, airport)** the aircraft was actually recorded ON THE GROUND at,
parsed out of `trace_visit_index.json`. 350 ground-visit records across the six foreign tails collapse
to **135 North-American ground-visit records → 56 unique (tail, day, airport) presence records → 51
unique tail-days at 12 distinct airports.**

Distances are great-circle miles from the **airport the plane was on the ground at** to (a) the event
city centroid (US Census 2024 gazetteer, via each `speaking/<slug>.yaml` `event.location.geocode`) and
(b) the event's inferred arrival airport (OurAirports coordinates). Gap is signed days: **negative =
plane was there BEFORE the event, positive = after**; 0 = plane present inside the event's own date span.
Combined score = `dist_mi/100 + |gap_days|/3` — 1.00 is the corner of the 100 mi / 3 day box, lower is better.

**5 of the 139 events have no location at all** (`UNKNOWN` / `AMBIGUOUS` city: three 2022 TPUSA Debate
Nights, one 2024 University of Nevada entry, one 2025 student town hall). They cannot be scored
spatially and are excluded — 134 events participate.

## Bucket definitions used

| Bucket | Test (applied in this precedence order) |
|---|---|
| SAME_AIRPORT | ground airport == the event's own inferred arrival airport, event within ±3 days |
| SAME_METRO | different airport, but same metro string OR the airport is inside that event's own 40-mile radius list, event within ±3 days |
| NEARBY_AIRPORT | different airport, ≤100 mi from the event city, event within ±3 days |
| KNOWN_TRANSIT | no event within 100 mi / ±3 days, and the field is a documented US customs port |
| DISTANT | no event within 100 mi / ±3 days, and not a customs port |

## 7. Coverage / selection-bias note

The archive pull that produced these traces was **event-centred**: 213 of the 284 foreign-fleet trace-days
we hold sit inside some event's ±2-day window, 30 more are the 1st-of-month ADSBX free samples, and only
52 are neither. The search was therefore *biased toward* finding the fleet near events — and it still
found 13 pairs at one metro. The negative result is stronger than the raw counts suggest.

Conversely, a day the fleet spent parked 300 miles from an event will usually only be in this dataset if
an event happened somewhere in the country that week. Absolute visit counts per field are therefore NOT a
usage census for these aircraft.

## 8. The six "new" pairs are NOT new overlaps — they are days inside one parked stay

Six (tail, date) combinations in section 6 are absent from `overlaps.csv`:
**SU-BND at KPVU on 2025-09-05, -06, -07, -08, -09 and -12.**

They must not be published as newly discovered overlaps. `overlaps.csv` row **OWENS-042** already records
that SU-BND **"leaves after 113 days"** on 2025-09-13, and **OWENS-035 / OWENS-065** record its arrival at
Provo on **2025-05-23**. 2025-05-23 → 2025-09-13 is exactly 113 days. The recovered traces confirm both ends:
KPVU ground contact on 2025-05-23, KPVU ground contact through 2025-09-12, and the aircraft at **LFPB (Paris
Le Bourget)** on 2025-09-14.

So the aircraft never went anywhere between those dates. The reason the traces cluster on 5–12 September is
that the free-archive pull was **event-centred (±2 days around each of the 139 events)** — 20250910_orem sits
in that window and June, July and most of August do not. **The "six consecutive days ending on the day Charlie
Kirk was killed" is a sampling artifact of where we looked, not a behaviour of the aircraft.** Stated correctly:
a Falcon was parked at a Duncan Aviation MRO seven miles from UVU for 113 continuous days that happened to
span 10 September 2025.

Genuinely new visit↔event pairs produced by the inversion: **zero.**

## 11. What this analysis cannot say

* An absence here is not a finding. We hold ADS-B ground contact for **51 tail-days across 3.5 years**. The
  fleet was in North America on days we hold nothing for; parked-with-transponder-off, out-of-receiver-coverage
  and a wrong claimed date all look identical from this seat.
* Every arrival airport for an event is an **inference**, not a record, so "same airport" is a statement about
  our inferred field, not about where anyone actually landed.
* **A trace proves presence, never purpose and never occupancy.** Nothing here places any person aboard any
  aircraft, and the traces cannot show why an aircraft was anywhere.
* We never assert intent. What is shown is where transponders were, and what fields those are.
* 5 of the 139 events have no location and are excluded from all distance work.
* The 2022 rows lean on `adsbexchange-samples`, which publishes **one day per month (the 1st)** and therefore
  can never test a claim about a specific mid-month date.
