# Wilmington (KILG) — November 2022 to October 2025

**Wilmington, Delaware appears more often in the Egyptian fleet's flight record than any other airport in the world outside Egypt and France.** Every US rotation by [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT), [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU) and [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV) from 2022 to 2025 ends here before crossing the Atlantic.

**And in three years, not one of those rotations ever begins here.** Wilmington appears on the **outbound leg only**, every single time, without exception. That asymmetry is what this page exists to document, because **"Delaware" is one of the five states in the geographic clustering offered as evidence of the shadowing pattern** — and a state that only ever appears on the way out of the country is not a place anyone was being followed to.

## The airport

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Wilmington Airport / New Castle Airport |
| **Identifiers** | **KILG** (ICAO) / **ILG** (IATA) |
| **City / State** | Wilmington, New Castle County, Delaware |
| **Class** | Public-use commercial/general aviation airport with a Delaware Air National Guard presence |
| **Role in business aviation** | A well-established **East Coast customs and fuel stop** for transatlantic corporate flights, in the Philadelphia–Baltimore–Washington corridor but without the slot pressure of the major hubs |
| **Not to be confused with** | **Wilmington, North Carolina — ILM.** See the correction section below. |

### How unusual is a foreign government jet here

Unremarkable. Wilmington's function in transatlantic business aviation is precisely this: a **last-stop-before-the-ocean** field where an aircraft clears US customs outbound, takes fuel, and departs east. Its convenience is that it is close to the Northeast corridor without being inside it.

**Twenty-one appearances. Every single one is "arrived from somewhere in the United States, departed for Cairo or Giza."** Not once in three years does an aircraft in this fleet arrive at Wilmington from outside the United States.

### What the asymmetry means

An airport that only ever appears on the way out is doing one job. **Wilmington is where these aircraft clear US customs outbound and take fuel for the Atlantic crossing.** The ground times — overwhelmingly one to three days — are consistent with a crew rest plus a departure slot, not with a visit.

That has a direct consequence for the overlap counts. **Delaware is one of the five states in the clustering** offered as evidence that the fleet was following the Kirks. But a state the aircraft only ever touch on their way home is not a destination. Any "Delaware overlap" is an overlap with the aircraft's exit procedure.

## The September 10 departure is different, and it is different in a specific way

The **10 September 2025** row is the one everybody knows about, and it looks like all the others in the table — Provo → Wilmington → Cairo, overnight, exactly the pattern of the twenty other rotations.

**What is not routine about it is the timing at the front end.** SU-BTT departed Provo at **07:14 am local**, roughly five hours before Charlie Kirk was shot. Every other rotation's US departure leg happened at a time nobody had any reason to note.

Two readings, both stated:

* **The pattern reading:** this is the twenty-first identical rotation. The aircraft finished its six-day Provo stay, flew to its usual customs stop, overnighted, and went home. The date is what makes it notable, not the flight.
* **The timing reading:** the aircraft left the city on the morning of the event, and the section of the overlap sheet covering this date is one of the few the [independent audit](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika) confirms — it marks the leg accurate while correcting the sheet's stated origin from "Paris" to Provo.

**The one thing the record does establish is that the destination was not chosen for this trip.** Wilmington had been the outbound stop on twenty previous rotations going back to November 2022.

## The Wilmington building claim, and what this page can and cannot say about it

A claim circulating on X ties the Wilmington destination to a specific address — **920 North King Street, One Rodney Square, Suite 404** — publicly associated over the years with federal agency offices, and describes an alleged foreign-dignitary transfer there on the evening of 10 September. That claim is set out in full on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT), where it belongs.

What **this** page adds is context that the claim's authors do not supply: **the aircraft went to Wilmington on every rotation for three years.** An address downtown does not explain twenty-one visits stretching back to 2022, most of which nobody has ever alleged anything about. If the September stop was operationally significant, the significance is in that stop specifically, not in the choice of city — which was fixed long before.

## The Wilmington DE / Wilmington NC correction

The most-circulated overlap timeline graphic, watermarked **@AlsaceTX**, contains a two-letter error that moves a claimed destination five hundred miles.

* Its footnote reads: *"Stops in Wilmington, **NC** for one day, leaves Wilmington for Cairo on 8/24."*
* Its September row reads **"PVU-ILM"** and **"ILM-CAI."**

**ILM is Wilmington, North Carolina. ILG is Wilmington, Delaware.** The Flightradar24 flight-history exports for all three aircraft show **ILG** on every rotation, including the 23–24 August one that footnote describes and the 10–11 September one.

Anyone auditing that graphic should read **ILG** and **Delaware** throughout. It is a small error and an easy one, but the two cities are in different states with different federal footprints, and the downtown-Wilmington claims above are claims about Delaware.

## The counterargument

* **Wilmington is a standard East Coast customs and fuel stop** for transatlantic business aviation. Its use is what the airport is for.
* **The asymmetry proves the function.** Twenty-one appearances, all outbound, none inbound, over three years. A destination would appear in both directions.
* **The ground times are one to three days** — crew rest and a slot, not a visit.
* **The pattern predates everything.** The first Wilmington stop in this record is **17 November 2022**.
* **No Kirk-side pairing has ever been offered for any Wilmington date**, including the September one.
* **The Gulfstreams do not use it at all.** [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND) and [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM) route out through **Goose Bay, Newfoundland** instead — a range difference, not a mission difference. The Falcon 7X has roughly 5,900 nm of range and can reach Cairo from Wilmington; the older Gulfstream IVs need the North Atlantic tech stop.

## What we do not know

1. **What happened at Wilmington during any of the twenty-one stops.** Ground handling records, FBO trip sheets and outbound CBP records exist for all of them and none is public.
2. **Whether anyone joined or left the aircraft here.** The overnight stops are exactly long enough for it, and nothing in the record says either way.
3. **The 10–15 December 2024 five-day stop** is far longer than the others. Nobody has explained it, and nobody has asked.
4. **Whether the 10 September stop differed from the other twenty.** That is the only genuinely open question on this page, and answering it requires the FBO record for that night.
5. **Nothing links any named individual to any of these stops.** The claims that do so are set out and assessed on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT).
