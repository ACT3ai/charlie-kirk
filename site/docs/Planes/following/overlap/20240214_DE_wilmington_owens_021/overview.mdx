## Counterargument

* **Duncan Aviation has held the Egyptian Air Force's aircraft maintenance account since 1999**, under a relationship documented in the company's own 2015 press release. Its headquarters plant is at **Lincoln, Nebraska**; it has a second facility at **Provo, Utah**. Landings at Lincoln, at Omaha (a fifteen-minute hop from Lincoln), at Wichita (Yingling Aviation, an FAA Part 145 **Dassault Falcon** service centre) and at Provo are all what that relationship produces.
* **The rotation never varies.** Cairo → Paris-Le Bourget → a US entry field → a short repositioning hop → sit five to eight days → [Wilmington](/Planes/following/Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) → Cairo. Three airframes flew it identically for three years. **A tasking that followed a person would vary with that person's schedule. A shop turnaround does not.**
* **Wilmington appears on the outbound leg only, twenty-one times, never inbound.** It is a customs and fuel stop being scored as a destination.
* **ADS-B has holes and they cut both ways.** A track gap is not proof of a covert leg; a clean track is not proof of intent.
* **The rotations continued after Charlie Kirk's death.** SU-BTV flew the identical Lincoln rotation 5–11 October 2025. If the flights were tasked to follow a man, they would have stopped when he died.

## What we do not know

* **Who was aboard.** No manifest, FBO log, crew list, or airport badge record has been obtained for any leg of any of these flights, at any location, by anyone.
* **Whether the aircraft entered a maintenance hangar.** No Duncan Aviation or Yingling work order or invoice has been produced either — **the maintenance explanation is as unevidenced by primary records as the shadowing one.**
* **Where Erika Kirk actually was.** No dated itinerary for her has ever been published, by the compilers or by anyone else.
* **What time of day any event ran.** Without event times, "same day" cannot be narrowed into a sequence on any date except 10 September 2025.
* **Which aircraft the Kirk party used.** The Kirk side of nearly every row here is a claimed location, not a tail number.

## We checked this row against primary ADS-B data

Run 24 August 2026. Every claimed row of the sheet was tested the same way, this one included.

**Confirmed.** The claimed aircraft really was at the claimed field. SU-BTT closed to **0.82 km** of **KILG** at **16:38:55 UTC on 14 February 2024**, still airborne on approach, and both archives hold the same trace. The day's track runs **Lincoln Airport, Lincoln NE** (KLNK/LNK, 1.04 km) to **Wilmington Airport, Wilmington DE** (KILG/ILG, 0.82 km).

**This confirms the aircraft half of the pairing and nothing else. It does not place Charlie Kirk or Erika Kirk anywhere.** An overlap needs both halves. The Kirk half of this row is exactly what it was before we ran the check.

| Date | adsb.lol | airplanes.live | SU-BTT heard? | Closest approach to KILG |

| 13 Feb | 404 | 404 | no | no position to measure |
| **14 Feb** | 200 | 200 | yes | **0.82 km** at 16:38:55 UTC |
| 15 Feb | 404 | 404 | no | no position to measure |

The **bold** row is the date the sheet claims. The other two are the day either side, which is the window the compilation itself used.

**Method, so a reader can repeat it.** Both free ADS-B history archives — adsb.lol globe history and airplanes.live — were queried for **SU-BTT** on **14 February 2024** and the day either side, with positions resolved to the nearest field in the OurAirports public-domain gazetteer. Positions on the ground or below 4,000 ft were measured against the claimed field's coordinates: within **15 km** counts as at the field, within **80 km** as the same metro area — and nearest-airport resolution is geometry, not a landing record.

**Our verdict disagrees with the audit verdict already recorded for this row, and we are not resolving it silently.** `overlaps.csv` records this row as **audited inaccurate** — the paid-archive audit put the aircraft somewhere else. Primary position data from two free archives puts it at **KILG**. Both readings stay on this page. A row the earlier audit called inaccurate that primary data now confirms is a finding in both directions: it means the sheet was right here, and it means the audit that corrected the sheet was not right everywhere either.

Here the free archives and the paid audit part company: both open networks put [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) 0.82 km from [Wilmington](/Planes/following/Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) on 14 February 2024, arriving from [Lincoln](/Planes/following/Lincoln_KLNK_2024-02-09_to_2025-10-11/overview), while the audit summarised in [the 73 overlaps](/Planes/following/73_overlaps) recorded the row as inaccurate — and the [overlap index](/Planes/following/overlap/overview) publishes both rather than resolving it quietly. A confirmed airframe is not a confirmed overlap in any case, because no archive anywhere places a person in a seat, and the document that would — a manifest or an itinerary — is exactly what is missing from the [Erika Kirk side of this claim](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased), from the [withheld foreign flight records](/CoverUp/Foreign_Flight_Records) and from the [foreign-nexus review Joe Kent says was stopped](/US_Intelligence/joe-kent). For the pattern this arrival belongs to, read [Paris-Le Bourget](/Planes/following/Paris_LFPB_2022-10-05_to_2025-10-05/overview): eighteen recorded stops, one overnight each, every US-bound leg staged through the same French field for three years.
