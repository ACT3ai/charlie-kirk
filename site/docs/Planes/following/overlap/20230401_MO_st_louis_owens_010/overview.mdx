## Counterargument

* **Duncan Aviation has held the Egyptian Air Force's aircraft maintenance account since 1999**, under a relationship documented in the company's own 2015 press release. Its headquarters plant is at **Lincoln, Nebraska**; it has a second facility at **Provo, Utah**. Landings at Lincoln, at Omaha (a fifteen-minute hop from Lincoln), at Wichita (Yingling Aviation, an FAA Part 145 **Dassault Falcon** service centre) and at Provo are all what that relationship produces.
* **The rotation never varies.** Cairo → Paris-Le Bourget → a US entry field → a short repositioning hop → sit five to eight days → [Wilmington](/Planes/following/Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) → Cairo. Three airframes flew it identically for three years. **A tasking that followed a person would vary with that person's schedule. A shop turnaround does not.**
* **Wilmington appears on the outbound leg only, twenty-one times, never inbound.** It is a customs and fuel stop being scored as a destination.
* **ADS-B has holes and they cut both ways.** A track gap is not proof of a covert leg; a clean track is not proof of intent.
* **The rotations continued after Charlie Kirk's death.** SU-BTV flew the identical Lincoln rotation 5–11 October 2025. If the flights were tasked to follow a man, they would have stopped when he died.

## What we do not know

* **What time of day any event ran.** Without event times, "same day" cannot be narrowed into a sequence on any date except 10 September 2025.

## We checked this row against primary ADS-B data

Run 24 August 2026. Every claimed row of the sheet was tested the same way, this one included.

**Confirmed to the St. Louis metro — but the aircraft landed at a different airport than the row names.**

:::caution[Downgraded 26 August 2026 — 0.48 km at 3,357 ft is an overflight, not a landing]

This row was published as **confirmed at the claimed field** on the strength of a 0.48 km closest
approach to Lambert (KSTL). **That 0.48 km happened at 3,975 ft barometric — 3,357 ft above the
field.** The aircraft was passing overhead.

The trace then continues **away** from Lambert and ends on final approach to a **different airport**:
**2.2 km from KSUS (Spirit of St. Louis, Chesterfield MO) at 600 ft, descending**, by which point it
was **24 km from Lambert**. Four independent sources — adsb.lol, airplanes.live, the adsb.lol GitHub
backup and the ADS-B Exchange monthly sample — agree fix for fix.

**Corrected reading: corroborated in the air, not on the ground.** The row keeps its *at the claimed
airport* verdict — 0.4 km is 0.4 km — but the page must no longer imply a landing at Lambert. The
aircraft reached the metro the row names, passed over the field the row names at 3,357 ft, and put
its wheels down 24 km away at Spirit of St. Louis. **Distance without altitude is not a landing
record** — that is now a standing rule on this site, and this row is the reason. See [the correction](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Overlap-Recovery).

This is not a one-way correction: the same re-read **reversed three published refutations into
confirmations**. It moves in whichever direction the fixes point.

:::

SU-BTT closed to **0.48 km** of **KSTL** at **15:55:15 UTC on 1 April 2023, at 3,357 ft above the
field**, and both archives hold the same trace. The day's track runs **Mouy Airfield, FR** (FR-0503,
10.99 km) to **Spirit of St Louis Airport, St Louis MO** (KSUS/SUS, 2.2 km on final at 600 ft).

**This confirms the aircraft half of the pairing and nothing else. It does not place Charlie Kirk or Erika Kirk anywhere.** An overlap needs both halves. The Kirk half of this row is exactly what it was before we ran the check.

**Method, so a reader can repeat it.** Both free ADS-B history archives — adsb.lol globe history and airplanes.live — were queried for **SU-BTT** on **1 April 2023** and the day either side, with positions resolved to the nearest field in the OurAirports public-domain gazetteer. Positions on the ground or below 4,000 ft were measured against the claimed field's coordinates: within **15 km** counts as at the field, within **80 km** as the same metro area — and nearest-airport resolution is geometry, not a landing record.

**Against the earlier audit.** `overlaps.csv` records this row as **audited accurate**, and the primary-data verdict agrees with it. Two independent methods reaching the same answer is worth more than either on its own.
