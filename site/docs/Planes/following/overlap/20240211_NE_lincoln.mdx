# 11 February 2024 — Lincoln, Nebraska

## The pairing, in one sentence

**Turning Point USA opened a chapter in Lincoln, Nebraska on 11 February 2024 while [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT) was parked at Lincoln Airport, on day three of a five-day stay — the same airport, the same city, the same days.**

**And it is the same airport that has held the Egyptian Air Force's aircraft maintenance account since 1999.** This page is the clearest example in the whole directory of a genuine same-airport co-location that has an ordinary explanation sitting right on top of it.

## Why this is the strongest Lincoln pairing and still not strong

Three things weaken it, and they should be read before the co-location is:

* **A chapter opening is not a Kirk appearance.** Nothing places Charlie Kirk in Lincoln on 11 February 2024.
* **The aircraft was there before the event and after it.** It arrived on the 9th and left on the 14th. It did not arrive for the event and did not leave with it.
* **The 2023 counter-case is stronger than the 2024 case.** On **25 August 2023, Charlie Kirk actually spoke in Lincoln, Nebraska** — a named, in-person appearance in this exact city. The independent audit places the aircraft **in Egypt** that day. **The one time Charlie Kirk is documented in Lincoln, the plane was on another continent.**

## The counterargument — and it is a document, not an opinion

**Duncan Aviation's main plant is at Lincoln, and Duncan Aviation has held the Egyptian Air Force's aircraft maintenance account since 1999.**

A Duncan Aviation press release dated **29 October 2015**, carried by the trade publication *DOM — Director of Maintenance*, headlined **"Duncan Aviation Signs a Five-Year Contract With the Egyptian Air Force"**:

> …announces the company has entered into a five-year direct contract agreement with the Egyptian Air Force (EAF) for aircraft maintenance services. **Duncan Aviation has been managing the maintenance and repair of the EAF fleet of Falcon 20 aircraft since 1999.**
>
> …the fact that **the EAF has chosen to fly over closer maintenance companies and bring their aircraft to Duncan Aviation** "reveals a lot about our relationship with them."
>
> **The first EAF aircraft arrived at Duncan Aviation's Lincoln, NE, facility on October 20 for a Z-check. The next will arrive in November for a C-check, Major Corrosion Inspection and complete paint.**

Four consequences land on this date:

* **The relationship predates Charlie Kirk's public career by fifteen years.**
* **The destination named in the release is Lincoln, Nebraska** — this airport.
* **The company itself anticipates the objection**: the EAF flies *past* closer facilities to get there. The long ferry from Cairo is this customer's documented norm.
* **Z-checks, C-checks, corrosion inspections and full repaints are multi-day to multi-week events.** A five-day dwell is at the light end.

And the rotation itself argues the same way. Across nine Lincoln visits by three aircraft over twenty months, **the dwell is five or six days every single time**, the Omaha-to-Lincoln hop is **fifteen to nineteen minutes** in seven of nine, and Wilmington appears on the **outbound leg only**. A tasking that followed a person would vary with that person's schedule. A shop turnaround does not.

## What we do not know

* **What day and time the chapter opening actually happened**, beyond the date on the researcher timeline. No TPUSA press release for it has been located.
* **Whether the aircraft entered a Duncan hangar** on this visit. No work order, invoice, or FBO record has been obtained for any visit in this record.
* **Who was aboard.** No manifest exists in the public record.
* **Why the sheet recorded the outbound leg wrongly** — the audit found the route was Lincoln → Wilmington, not Omaha → Wilmington. That is a data-quality signal about the tally, not about the flight.
