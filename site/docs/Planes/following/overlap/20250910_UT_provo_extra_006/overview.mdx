## Counterargument

* **Duncan Aviation has held the Egyptian Air Force's aircraft maintenance account since 1999**, under a relationship documented in the company's own 2015 press release. Its headquarters plant is at **Lincoln, Nebraska**; it has a second facility at **Provo, Utah**. Landings at Lincoln, at Omaha (a fifteen-minute hop from Lincoln), at Wichita (Yingling Aviation, an FAA Part 145 **Dassault Falcon** service centre) and at Provo are all what that relationship produces.
* **The rotation never varies.** Cairo → Paris-Le Bourget → a US entry field → a short repositioning hop → sit five to eight days → [Wilmington](/Planes/following/Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) → Cairo. Three airframes flew it identically for three years. **A tasking that followed a person would vary with that person's schedule. A shop turnaround does not.**
* **Wilmington appears on the outbound leg only, twenty-one times, never inbound.** It is a customs and fuel stop being scored as a destination.
* **ADS-B has holes and they cut both ways.** A track gap is not proof of a covert leg; a clean track is not proof of intent.
* **The rotations continued after Charlie Kirk's death.** SU-BTV flew the identical Lincoln rotation 5–11 October 2025. If the flights were tasked to follow a man, they would have stopped when he died.

## What we do not know

* **Who was aboard.** No manifest, FBO log, crew list, or airport badge record has been obtained for any leg of any of these flights, at any location, by anyone.
* **Whether the aircraft entered a maintenance hangar.** No Duncan Aviation or Yingling work order or invoice has been produced either — **the maintenance explanation is as unevidenced by primary records as the shadowing one.**
* **What time of day any event ran.** Without event times, "same day" cannot be narrowed into a sequence on any date except 10 September 2025.
* **Which aircraft the Kirk party used.** The Kirk side of nearly every row here is a claimed location, not a tail number.

## We checked this row against primary ADS-B data

**Confirmed.** Both free archives hold SU-BND on 9 and 10 September 2025, they agree with each other, and they place the aircraft **on the ground at Provo Municipal Airport**. The closest approach to the claimed field is **1.29 km at 20:03:33 UTC on 9 September 2025** and **1.29 km at 16:05:35 UTC on 10 September 2025** — on the ground on both fixes.

| Date | Tail | adsb.lol | airplanes.live | Heard | Closest approach to KPVU |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-09-09 | **SU-BND** | 200 | 200 | **yes — both agree** | **1.29 km, 20:03:33 UTC, on the ground** (131 fixes) |
| 2025-09-10 | **SU-BND** | 200 | 200 | **yes — both agree** | **1.29 km, 16:05:35 UTC, on the ground** (257 fixes) |
| 2025-09-11 | SU-BND | 404 | 404 | no — archives cover the date | not measurable |

**Method.** Both free ADS-B history archives — adsb.lol globe history and the airplanes.live mirror — were queried for the claimed tail on the claimed date and on the day either side, and every position fix was resolved to the nearest field in the OurAirports public-domain gazetteer. **Nearest-airport resolution is geometry, not a landing record**, which is why the distance in kilometres is published beside every result rather than a bare airport name.

**Every fix on both days resolves to Provo and to nothing else.** The extract's airport list for each day contains one entry — `KPVU` — and the first and last fixes of each day are the same field. **The primary data is consistent with an aircraft that sat on that ramp and did not fly.**

**This confirms the AIRCRAFT half and nothing else.** Position data locates an airframe. It does not put Charlie Kirk or Erika Kirk anywhere, it does not establish that anyone aboard knew where either of them was, and it does not establish who tasked the flight. The Kirk half of this pairing is exactly as strong, or as weak, as it was before we pulled the data.

**What it does not settle: the transponder timings.** The archives show that this airframe was *heard* on both days, which is what a live transponder produces. **They are not a switch-on and switch-off log**, and nothing in this pull corroborates or contradicts the specific cycle times published for 10 September, whose time base was never stated. That dispute is unchanged.

**The counterargument, which this confirmation does not touch.** A parked aircraft transmitting from a maintenance ramp is what a parked aircraft does. Duncan Aviation has a Provo facility and has held the Egyptian Air Force account since 1999. **Confirming that SU-BND was on that ramp confirms the least contested fact on this page** — nobody ever disputed the aircraft was there. What is disputed is what it meant, and position data cannot reach that.

**This agrees with the row's existing status.** `overlaps.csv` records it **audited accurate**, and the independent audit reached the same conclusion from paid archives. Two independent readings and one primary-data pull now agree on the aircraft's position.

This row is one of **85 checked in a single run on 24 August 2026** — 24 confirmed at the claimed field, 12 refuted, 22 not heard, 20 with no archive coverage, 2 naming no aircraft, and 5 naming no date. The whole result is on [Overlap dates](/Planes/following/overlap/overview).

## Where this page fits
