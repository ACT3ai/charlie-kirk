## Counterargument

* **Duncan Aviation has held the Egyptian Air Force's aircraft maintenance account since 1999**, under a relationship documented in the company's own 2015 press release. Its headquarters plant is at **Lincoln, Nebraska**; it has a second facility at **Provo, Utah**. Landings at Lincoln, at Omaha (a fifteen-minute hop from Lincoln), at Wichita (Yingling Aviation, an FAA Part 145 **Dassault Falcon** service centre) and at Provo are all what that relationship produces.
* **The rotation never varies.** Cairo → Paris-Le Bourget → a US entry field → a short repositioning hop → sit five to eight days → [Wilmington](/Planes/following/Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) → Cairo. Three airframes flew it identically for three years. **A tasking that followed a person would vary with that person's schedule. A shop turnaround does not.**
* **Wilmington appears on the outbound leg only, twenty-one times, never inbound.** It is a customs and fuel stop being scored as a destination.
* **ADS-B has holes and they cut both ways.** A track gap is not proof of a covert leg; a clean track is not proof of intent.
* **The rotations continued after Charlie Kirk's death.** SU-BTV flew the identical Lincoln rotation 5–11 October 2025. If the flights were tasked to follow a man, they would have stopped when he died.

## What we do not know

* **Who was aboard.** No manifest, FBO log, crew list, or airport badge record has been obtained for any leg of any of these flights, at any location, by anyone.
* **Whether the aircraft entered a maintenance hangar.** No Duncan Aviation or Yingling work order or invoice has been produced either — **the maintenance explanation is as unevidenced by primary records as the shadowing one.**
* **What time of day any event ran.** Without event times, "same day" cannot be narrowed into a sequence on any date except 10 September 2025.
* **Which aircraft the Kirk party used.** The Kirk side of nearly every row here is a claimed location, not a tail number.

## We checked this row against primary ADS-B data

**There is nothing to query.** The source row for 9 April 2024 names Omaha, names Charlie Kirk, and names **no aircraft at all** — its tail cell is empty. A flight archive is indexed by airframe, so a row with no airframe cannot be checked against one. **That is a fact about the claim, not about the data.**

| Date | Claimed tail | adsb.lol | airplanes.live | Heard | Closest approach to KOMA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024-04-08 | **none named** | not queried | not queried | — | not measurable |
| 2024-04-09 | **none named** | not queried | not queried | — | not measurable |
| 2024-04-10 | **none named** | not queried | not queried | — | not measurable |

**Method.** Both free ADS-B history archives — adsb.lol globe history and the airplanes.live mirror — were queried for the claimed tail on the claimed date and on the day either side, and every position fix was resolved to the nearest field in the OurAirports public-domain gazetteer. **Nearest-airport resolution is geometry, not a landing record**, which is why the distance in kilometres is published beside every result rather than a bare airport name.

**This agrees with the row's existing status, which is that it is a clean non-overlap.** The note recorded against it says no aircraft was in Nebraska on either candidate date. Nothing in the primary-data pass changes that, because there was never an airframe named to look for. **A row with a documented Kirk appearance and no aircraft is a documented Kirk appearance, not an overlap.**

**The counterargument, and it cuts both ways.** The absence of a named tail is not evidence that a jet was there and was hidden, and it is not evidence that the compilation was careless. It means the row is **untestable as written**. Publishing it is the point: a log that holds only the hits is a case, not a log.

This row is one of **85 checked against primary ADS-B, first on 24 August 2026 and again — fix by fix — on 26 August 2026**. The corrected result: **25 corroborated at the claimed field, 1 same-metro-wrong-field, 3 refuted**, 37 asked-and-empty, 10 with no archive coverage, 4 naming no aircraft, and 5 naming no date. The first pass reported 12 refutations; **9 were retracted** on the re-read — six had been scored against the wrong calendar day, and two against the shorter of two archives while the longer one put the aircraft at the claimed field.
