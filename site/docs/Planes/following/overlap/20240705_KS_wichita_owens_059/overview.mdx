## Counterargument

* **Duncan Aviation has held the Egyptian Air Force's aircraft maintenance account since 1999**, under a relationship documented in the company's own 2015 press release. Its headquarters plant is at **Lincoln, Nebraska**; it has a second facility at **Provo, Utah**. Landings at Lincoln, at Omaha (a fifteen-minute hop from Lincoln), at Wichita (Yingling Aviation, an FAA Part 145 **Dassault Falcon** service centre) and at Provo are all what that relationship produces.
* **The rotation never varies.** Cairo → Paris-Le Bourget → a US entry field → a short repositioning hop → sit five to eight days → [Wilmington](/Planes/following/Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) → Cairo. Three airframes flew it identically for three years. **A tasking that followed a person would vary with that person's schedule. A shop turnaround does not.**
* **Wilmington appears on the outbound leg only, twenty-one times, never inbound.** It is a customs and fuel stop being scored as a destination.
* **ADS-B has holes and they cut both ways.** A track gap is not proof of a covert leg; a clean track is not proof of intent.
* **The rotations continued after Charlie Kirk's death.** SU-BTV flew the identical Lincoln rotation 5–11 October 2025. If the flights were tasked to follow a man, they would have stopped when he died.

* **What time of day any event ran.** Without event times, "same day" cannot be narrowed into a sequence on any date except 10 September 2025.

## We checked this row against primary ADS-B data

**Untested. The archives cover this date and they hold no trace of this airframe.**

**This is not a refutation, and it must not be read as one.** Both free archives were queried for SU-BTT on 5 July 2024 and the day either side, and both returned nothing. The fleet does not fly every day, and volunteer receiver coverage over Egypt, over North Africa and across the mid-Atlantic is thin — an aircraft parked, or flying outside coverage, produces exactly this result. So does an aircraft that was never there. **The check cannot tell those apart.**

Coverage for these exact dates was verified separately with a control basket of unrelated aircraft: the archives were serving normally, so this is a silent airframe and not a silent archive.

**The method, so a reader can repeat it.** Both free ADS-B history archives — adsb.lol globe history and airplanes.live — were queried for SU-BTT on 5 July 2024 and on the day either side, and every returned position that was on the ground or below 4,000 ft was measured against the published coordinates of KICT. Within 15 km counts as at the field and within 80 km as the same metro; **nearest-airport resolution is geometry, not a landing record**, which is why the distance in kilometres is printed beside every field name rather than the name alone.

**The files.** This row's extract is `site/docs/Planes/following/overlap/20240705_KS_wichita_owens_059/data/adsb_verification.json`; the full 85-row run, its method string and its tally are in `site/docs/Planes/following/apis/public_open_source/data/overlap_verification/overlap_verification.json`. Both were written 2026-08-24T17:36:21.953Z.

**Against the audit verdict already on this page.** The audit recorded on this page marks the row **inaccurate**. **Our result does not corroborate that**, because it cannot: no trace means no position, and no position means no refutation. The audit had paid archives this check does not, so its reading stands on its own evidence rather than on ours.

**What this check cannot do.** It locates an airframe. It does not identify who was aboard, it does not establish why the aircraft was where it was, and it does not distinguish a shadowing flight from the maintenance rotation set out in the counterargument above. **Absence in an archive is not proof of a covert leg, and presence is not proof of intent.**
