## Counterargument

* **Duncan Aviation has held the Egyptian Air Force's aircraft maintenance account since 1999**, under a relationship documented in the company's own 2015 press release. Its headquarters plant is at **Lincoln, Nebraska**; it has a second facility at **Provo, Utah**. Landings at Lincoln, at Omaha (a fifteen-minute hop from Lincoln), at Wichita (Yingling Aviation, an FAA Part 145 **Dassault Falcon** service centre) and at Provo are all what that relationship produces.
* **The rotation never varies.** Cairo → Paris-Le Bourget → a US entry field → a short repositioning hop → sit five to eight days → [Wilmington](/Planes/following/Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) → Cairo. Three airframes flew it identically for three years. **A tasking that followed a person would vary with that person's schedule. A shop turnaround does not.**
* **Wilmington appears on the outbound leg only, twenty-one times, never inbound.** It is a customs and fuel stop being scored as a destination.
* **ADS-B has holes and they cut both ways.** A track gap is not proof of a covert leg; a clean track is not proof of intent.
* **The rotations continued after Charlie Kirk's death.** SU-BTV flew the identical Lincoln rotation 5–11 October 2025. If the flights were tasked to follow a man, they would have stopped when he died.

## What we do not know

* **Who was aboard.** No manifest, FBO log, crew list, or airport badge record has been obtained for any leg of any of these flights, at any location, by anyone.
* **Whether the aircraft entered a maintenance hangar.** No Duncan Aviation or Yingling work order or invoice has been produced either — **the maintenance explanation is as unevidenced by primary records as the shadowing one.**
* **What time of day any event ran.** Without event times, "same day" cannot be narrowed into a sequence on any date except 10 September 2025.
* **Which aircraft the Kirk party used.** The Kirk side of nearly every row here is a claimed location, not a tail number.

## We checked this row against primary ADS-B data

Run 24 August 2026. Every claimed row of the sheet was tested the same way, this one included.

**Confirmed as a departure from the claimed field.**

:::danger[This page said "Refuted" until 26 August 2026. It was wrong, and the aircraft was at Wichita.]

The published verdict was *"closest approach to KICT was 1,852.96 km. Both archives agree."*
**Both archives do not agree — they hold different amounts of that day**, and the 1,853 km figure is
the closest approach of the **shorter** trace only.

The **airplanes.live** trace for 3 April 2024 opens with SU-BTT **climbing out of Wichita**:

| Time (UTC) | Baro alt | Distance to KICT |
|---|---:|---:|
| **13:43:30** | 1,675 ft — **342 ft above the field** | **1.4 km** |
| 13:44:04 | 3,525 ft | 4.4 km |
| 13:46:34 | 15,350 ft | over KAAO |
| … FL410 across the country … | | |
| 16:05:35 | 450 ft | 1.1 km from KILG |

The **adsb.lol** trace does not begin until **14:15:16Z**, already at FL410 over Missouri — *after the
aircraft had left Kansas*. An earlier pass read that shorter trace as the whole day.

**The row was right and this site called it false.** SU-BTT departed Wichita on the morning of
3 April 2024 and flew nonstop at FL410 to a landing at Wilmington, Delaware at 16:05 UTC. The
neighbouring page [3 April 2024 — Lawrence](/Planes/following/overlap/20240403_KS_lawrence/overview)
already said *"the jet really did leave the state the morning he spoke."* **The trace proves that page
right and this one wrong.** See [the correction](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Overlap-Recovery).

:::

**And the limit, unchanged.** A departure roll at Wichita locates **the aircraft**. It does not place
Charlie Kirk, Erika Kirk or anyone else aboard it, and Wichita is a Dassault Falcon service centre.

| Date | adsb.lol | airplanes.live | SU-BTT heard? | Closest approach to KICT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 Apr | 404 | 404 | no | no position to measure |
| **3 Apr** | 200 | 200 | yes | **1,852.96 km** at 15:54:45 UTC |
| 4 Apr | 404 | 404 | no | no position to measure |

The **bold** row is the date the sheet claims. The other two are the day either side, which is the window the compilation itself used.

**Method, so a reader can repeat it.** Both free ADS-B history archives — adsb.lol globe history and airplanes.live — were queried for **SU-BTT** on **3 April 2024** and the day either side, with positions resolved to the nearest field in the OurAirports public-domain gazetteer. Positions on the ground or below 4,000 ft were measured against the claimed field's coordinates: within **15 km** counts as at the field, within **80 km** as the same metro area — and nearest-airport resolution is geometry, not a landing record.

**Against the earlier audit.** `overlaps.csv` records this row as **partial**. Primary data refutes the aircraft half outright, which is stronger than partial in the other direction.
