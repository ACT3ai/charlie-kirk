## Counterargument

* **Duncan Aviation has held the Egyptian Air Force's aircraft maintenance account since 1999**, under a relationship documented in the company's own 2015 press release. Its headquarters plant is at **Lincoln, Nebraska**; it has a second facility at **Provo, Utah**. Landings at Lincoln, at Omaha (a fifteen-minute hop from Lincoln), at Wichita (Yingling Aviation, an FAA Part 145 **Dassault Falcon** service centre) and at Provo are all what that relationship produces.
* **The rotation never varies.** Cairo → Paris-Le Bourget → a US entry field → a short repositioning hop → sit five to eight days → [Wilmington](/Planes/following/Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) → Cairo. Three airframes flew it identically for three years. **A tasking that followed a person would vary with that person's schedule. A shop turnaround does not.**
* **Wilmington appears on the outbound leg only, twenty-one times, never inbound.** It is a customs and fuel stop being scored as a destination.
* **ADS-B has holes and they cut both ways.** A track gap is not proof of a covert leg; a clean track is not proof of intent.
* **The rotations continued after Charlie Kirk's death.** SU-BTV flew the identical Lincoln rotation 5–11 October 2025. If the flights were tasked to follow a man, they would have stopped when he died.

* **What time of day any event ran.** Without event times, "same day" cannot be narrowed into a sequence on any date except 10 September 2025.

## We checked this row against primary ADS-B data

**Not tested — this row's "Refuted" verdict was withdrawn on 26 August 2026.**

:::warning[Retracted]

This page previously read **Refuted**. It was not refuted. The trace it rested on is for **18 August 2024**, not for the claimed **17 August 2024**, and *a claim about day D can only be refuted by day D*.

On 18 August 2024 the aircraft was tracked from Cairo International (4.81 km) to a field west of Giza (12.74 km), the first fix at 09:36Z, on airplanes.live only — adsb.lol returns nothing for that date either — **10,402 km** from the claimed field. Against a straight-line cruise at 800 km/h that leaves **33.6 hours available against 13.0 hours needed**, before any fuel stop. **The claim is physically compatible with the trace.** The adjacent-day observation is kept below as context, not as a refutation.

Neither free archive holds the claimed day itself, so this row is **untested** — which is a different fact from false, and a different fact again from nobody having looked. See [the correction](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Overlap-Recovery).

:::

**This is one of the four refutations in the whole run that the recovery work made possible.** Without the backup network this row would have been left untested rather than refuted.

| Date | adsb.lol | airplanes.live | SU-BTT heard? | Closest approach to KOMA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 Aug | 404 | 404 | no | no position to measure |

The **bold** row is the date the sheet claims. The other two are the day either side, which is the window the compilation itself used.

**Method, so a reader can repeat it.** Both free ADS-B history archives — adsb.lol globe history and airplanes.live — were queried for **SU-BTT** on **17 August 2024** and the day either side, with positions resolved to the nearest field in the OurAirports public-domain gazetteer. Positions on the ground or below 4,000 ft were measured against the claimed field's coordinates: within **15 km** counts as at the field, within **80 km** as the same metro area — and nearest-airport resolution is geometry, not a landing record.

**Against the earlier audit.** `overlaps.csv` records this row as **audited inaccurate**, and the primary-data verdict agrees with it. Two independent methods reaching the same answer is worth more than either on its own.
