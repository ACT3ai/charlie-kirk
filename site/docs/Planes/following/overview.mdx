# Planes That Followed Them

Trackers on X, led in public by [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens), say Egyptian-registered VIP jets repeatedly landed at the same airports Charlie Kirk, Erika Kirk, or a [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview) event used — the same day, the day before, or the day after — and they count that coincidence in the high sixties to the high seventies. Every figure on this page is a **researcher tally read from public ADS-B history**, not a confirmed record. The reconstructed list behind those numbers — every city, date and tail the sheet, the broadcasts, or X actually named — is on **[The 73 overlaps](./73_overlaps)**. The full X discussion, one topic at a time, is on **[X Discussions of the Following Planes](./x_discussions)**.

The claim is not one dramatic flight. It is the **repetition**: [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) (the "yellow plane"), [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) (the "blue plane"), and the rest of the Israel/Egyptian armada showing up at the same fields the Kirks and TPUSA used, often small regional or Army airfields, across a window researchers date from **2022 through September 2025**. The existing hub for that allegation is **[Planes Following Charlie or Erika](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika)**. This directory is the public location-by-location log of that same claim; the first page in it is the X-discussion dump, because the counts, the dates, and the disputes all started as posts.

The published tallies do not agree, and the disagreement is itself the finding. Owens's team and amplifiers have used **73 Erika / about 23 Charlie**, **72**, **"70+"**, **68 Erika of which 29 also hit Charlie**, **65–75**, and **77**. [Liz Wheeler](/People/liz-wheeler) says that under a same-airport, same-day rule the overlap **dwindles to one** — Utah on September 10. [KanekoaTheGreat](https://x.com/KanekoaTheGreat) says **66 percent** of the spreadsheet locations are wrong and **60 percent** put the jets on the wrong continent. Those numbers are recorded side by side on the [X discussions page](./x_discussions); they are not averaged.

What to read next: start with the [X discussions page](./x_discussions) if you want every sourced post, then the [hub](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika) for the compressed claim, then the tail-number pages — [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview), [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) — and **[Erika Kirk's flights](./Erika_Kirk_Flights)**, the first dedicated page for her alleged itinerary, plus [Erika's erased flight logs](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased), because the Erika side of every tally is the weak side. **The location pages are now live** — Provo, Lincoln, Omaha, Wichita, Wilmington, St. Louis, Minot, Detroit, and the two non-US fields Cairo and Sharm el-Sheikh, each with its own date range, arrival log, overlap table and counterargument. They are indexed in the table below.

**This does not make the pattern go away, and it is important to say why.** Foreign-government jets did land at these small fields, repeatedly, and the aircraft side of the record is checkable — **25 of the 85 rows are corroborated by raw ADS-B traces this investigation recovered and stored**, and only 3 are refuted by them. What collapses is the *attribution to Erika*, which is the larger part of the count. What is left standing is smaller, and Charlie-shaped, and harder to dismiss: of the **six dates** that survive a same-day test at a shared field, **three involve Charlie Kirk** — 3 April 2024 Wichita, 23 April 2024 Provo, 10 September 2025 Provo — and **three are a Turning Point event with no Kirk at it**. **Two of the three Charlie dates are in Utah.** The Wilmington row of 10 September is an Erika claim that fails this rule, and it falls on a date already counted among the three.

## The four source layers

Every figure on this page came out of a broadcast, spread as posts, was then written up by outlets, and is now restated by AI assistants to anyone who asks. **Those are four different kinds of evidence and they are held on four different pages, because collapsing them is how one source starts to look like twelve.**

**[The Candace Owens broadcasts](./Candace_Owens_Broadcasts)** are the origin. That page is the show-by-show record of what was actually said on air: the **68 overlaps that became 73 in the same breath**, the **29 that also included Charlie** — which means, on the show's own arithmetic, that roughly forty of them did not — the **0.000000001% probability** offered without a model, the **second-level [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) transponder timings for 10 September** (on at 09:05:36, off at 10:34:05, back on at 12:40:53, off at 13:29:40, aircraft never moved), the **contractor and FBO rental-car claims**, and the **920 N King Street** allegation. It also carries the ledger of what none of it established — no manifest, no work order, no stated time base, no published model.

**[X Discussions of the Following Planes](./x_discussions)** is the spread: every post the topic queries returned, from [@BasedSamParker](https://x.com/BasedSamParker)'s Inshas Airbase thread through the Erika-not-Charlie pivot to the [Liz Wheeler](/People/liz-wheeler) and [KanekoaTheGreat](https://x.com/KanekoaTheGreat) audits that cut the count apart — and a seventh section for the posts a later pass surfaced, including the longest and least sourced version of the whole theory.

**[Press coverage](./Press_Coverage)** is the write-up layer, and it is the one that should change how you read the numbers. **Sportskeeda, IBTimes, Factually, the Economic Times, the Times of India, the Hindustan Times, Document, and the Daily Wire are all reporting the same single broadcast.** Not one outlet in that record pulled the flight history and published its own dated list. Twelve bylines, one source — and the count arriving rounded as "nearly 70" or "around 70" on the way through. That page also carries **Erika Kirk's dispute of the location claims**, including her CBS town-hall response, which matters more than a denial usually does: she is the only person who could produce the itinerary the 73-count was measured against, and the count was assembled without it.

**[AI assistant answers](./AI_Assistant_Answers)** is the fourth layer and the newest one: what Google Gemini, Grok and the rest return when a member of the public asks the question directly — which for most readers is now the first answer and often the only one. That page carries one such answer verbatim and audits it. **Twelve of its assertions carry the same citation, and that citation is a single public note page** — no ADS-B pulled, no analyst named, no document quoted, and **not one YouTube or podcast source returned even though the question asked for them by name**. It also holds the one factual sentence in that answer this site's own primary-record page contradicts outright: that the [N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview) HADES jet was "nowhere near Utah" on 10 September, when the El Paso-to-Kalispell leg it cites as the alibi **is the leg that carried it over Orem**. What the assistants get right — the 66 percent spreadsheet audit, the time-zone correction, the unpublished Erika itinerary — is on the page too, with the attribution they stripped restored.

**And two locations in the index below are not overlap locations at all.** [Cairo](./Cairo_HECA_2022-11-13_to_2025-10-12/overview) is the home field where every rotation begins and ends — which is also, precisely, what the auditors mean when they say a majority of the spreadsheet rows put the aircraft on **the wrong continent**. It is where the [Inshas Airbase and El Sa'ka Forces argument](./Cairo_HECA_2022-11-13_to_2025-10-12/overview) belongs, and where the reasons that argument does not hold are set out. [Sharm el-Sheikh](./SharmElSheikh_HESH_2025-10-13_to_2025-10-13/overview) is the 13 October 2025 tarmac shot of SU-BTT beside **Air Force One** — **no Kirk presence, no overlap, and not a 74th row on anybody's tally.** What it does is remove "some charter operator sent a jet" from the list of explanations for Provo. That is a real narrowing, and it is the only thing it narrows.

Every verdict below is reproducible by anyone from files under `site/docs/Planes/<TAIL>/data/recovered/`, whatever any tracking site later decides to serve. The per-row working is on [the overlap index](./overlap/overview); the archive map and the pulls behind it are on [Flight Data Recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview).

### Why the numbers moved

Three changes, and each one makes the test stricter or better fed:

1. **The recovered corpus grew by an order of magnitude.** The 24 August pass ran against roughly 1,600 trace files. This one runs against more than 20,000, including days that had never been asked for at all. Nine claim dates sat at *never queried* — not tested, not testable, simply unasked — and closing them was the first thing this run did.
2. **The test is now strictly same-day.** The earlier pass accepted a position from the day before or after as evidence about the claimed date. That is unsound in both directions: this aircraft crosses an ocean overnight, so where it was on Tuesday refutes nothing about Monday. Five rows previously marked *elsewhere* were refuted only by an adjacent-day position and are now honestly recorded as *not heard*.
3. **Verdicts are measured off the raw trace points, not off a summary.** Closest approach is computed point-by-point against the named field, with on-ground positions ranked above airborne ones, so an overflight can no longer score the same as a landing.

**Two rows this page previously published as refutations are corroborations, and the correction belongs here at full size.** **OWENS-024 and SITE-003**, claimed as Wichita on 3 April 2024, were called refuted on the strength of an adsb.lol trace that begins at 41,000 ft over Missouri. The airplanes.live trace for the same day — recovered afterwards — puts SU-BTT **1.43 km from Wichita Eisenhower at 1,675 ft** at 08:43 local, before the Wilmington leg. Both legs happened. The earlier reading had half the day.

**One published distance was simply wrong.** This page previously gave OWENS-012, claimed as Wichita on 14 April 2023, as "7,411 km from Wichita at closest approach" — a figure that contradicted its own sentence, since the same sentence put the aircraft in Topeka, 200 km away. The recovered airplanes.live trace puts SU-BTT **27.6 km from Wichita at 5,050 ft**. It is now recorded as *right area, wrong field*: inside the source sheet's own 50-mile tolerance, outside a same-airport test, and refuting nothing.

**The three refutations that survive**, all on the Erika side, all clean:

* **OWENS-015**, claimed as Goose Bay on 9 May 2023 — the recovered trace puts the aircraft **1,063 km** away.
* **OWENS-050**, claimed as St. Louis on 12 May 2023 — **7,034 km** away.
* **OWENS-036**, claimed as Omaha on 18 June 2025 — **10,140 km** away.

**Duplicate rows are confirmed by the position data, and there are two different kinds.** Four pairs are mechanical double-counts *inside the source sheet*: OWENS-035/OWENS-065 are the same SU-BND arrival at Provo on 23 May 2025 and return an identical 0.03 km; OWENS-037/OWENS-066 are the same 7 July 2025 Provo claim; OWENS-031/OWENS-061 and OWENS-033/OWENS-063 are the same claimed Wichita dates and return identical *not heard* results. Index 56 is a fifth, flagged in the register as the same St. Louis claim advanced a year from index 14. The earlier audit caught three of those (31/61, 33/63, 35/65); position data confirms them and adds 37/66. **A duplicated row in a sheet whose headline is a count is a finding about the count.**

**One further pair is ours, not theirs, and it should not be counted against the compilers.** OWENS-024 and SITE-003 are the same 3 April 2024 Wichita event and return an identical 1.43 km, but SITE-003 exists because this site reconstructed it from an overlap page that carried no row in the file. That is a double-count in *our* register. It stays, labelled, rather than being deleted — every row in this file has a live page and no row has ever been removed from it.

**The limit is stated here at the same size as the number, because it is the same size.** A verdict of *at the claimed airport* confirms **the aircraft half** of a pairing and nothing else. **Delaware, Kansas and Missouri are the three.** Utah and Nebraska are not corroborated by any 2022 sample we hold. And the same data adds a detail that has to be reported as what it is rather than dressed up: the 1 June 2022 SU-BTT leg ends at **Almaza Air Force Base**, an Egyptian military field, not Cairo International.

**The sampling caveat travels with every one of these figures, every time they are cited.** This is **one day per month, the first**. Two of three sampled 2022 days put SU-BTT in the United States — but three sampled days cannot support a rate, a frequency, or a pattern. It establishes that the aircraft was in the United States in 2022, at those fields, on those dates. **It does not establish how often, and it must never be quoted as though it did.** Nor does it move the Kirk half: no 2022 sample places either Kirk anywhere.

**And the clustering omits the two busiest places the fleet actually went.** [Paris–Le Bourget](./Paris_LFPB_2022-10-05_to_2025-10-05/overview) takes **18 recorded stops** — more than any American field except [Wilmington](./Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) — and [Goose Bay](./GooseBay_CYYR_2023-05-09_to_2025-09-13/overview) takes the Gulfstreams every time they cross. Neither is in France or Labrador because of anything either Kirk did. **A geography claim that leaves out the two most-visited nodes is describing where Turning Point had activity, not where the aircraft went.**

There is one geographic fact the clustering misses in the other direction, and it is the one worth chasing: **every Utah-bound rotation entered the United States through North Dakota** — [Williston](./Williston_KXWA_2024-04-23_to_2024-04-23/overview) in 2024, [Minot](./Minot_KMOT_2025-04-08_to_2025-09-04/overview) four times in 2025 — while every other rotation used a conventional hub.

The clustering also omits what does not fit. **[Detroit](./Detroit_KDTW_2025-02-03_to_2025-03-30/overview) is not in it** — the fleet used it twice as an entry point, and nothing was happening in Michigan. That is what you would expect if the itinerary were driven by maintenance and the cluster were an artefact of where TPUSA happened to have activity.

## The route template, which never varies

Every US rotation by every Falcon in this fleet, across eleven months and three aircraft:

> **Cairo → Paris-Le Bourget → a US entry field → a 15–20 minute repositioning hop → sit six to eight days → [Wilmington](./Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) → Cairo.**

* **The dwell is fixed at five to eight days**, at every location, regardless of city. A tasking following a person would produce dwells tracking that person's schedule.
* **The repositioning hops are 15 to 19 minutes** — Omaha→Lincoln seven times, St. Louis Lambert→Downtown once. **No surveillance purpose is served by flying a Falcon 7X a quarter of an hour between two Nebraska cities.** It is an aircraft moving itself to a shop.
* **The two Gulfstreams do not use Wilmington at all.** [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) and [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview) route through **[Goose Bay, Labrador](./GooseBay_CYYR_2023-05-09_to_2025-09-13/overview)** instead — a range split, not a mission split. The Falcon 7X can reach Cairo from Wilmington; the older Gulfstream IVs need the North Atlantic tech stop.
* **[Paris–Le Bourget](./Paris_LFPB_2022-10-05_to_2025-10-05/overview) is westbound only, and always an overnight.** Sixteen recorded Cairo–Paris crossings all fall between **3:56 and 4:22**; every one is followed by a morning departure to a US field the next day. Going home, all three Falcons fly [Wilmington](./Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview) → Cairo nonstop and skip Paris entirely. **That asymmetry is the jet stream, not a mission** — and it has to be conceded before the September window can be argued honestly.
* **Every Utah-bound rotation entered the United States through North Dakota.** [Williston](./Williston_KXWA_2024-04-23_to_2024-04-23/overview) in April 2024, [Minot](./Minot_KMOT_2025-04-08_to_2025-09-04/overview) four times in 2025. Five flights, two years, no exceptions — while the Nebraska, Michigan and Missouri rotations used ordinary hubs. **Nobody has explained it, and the customs records for all five are in a state nobody has contacted.**

## What the audit did and did not dissolve

The [full row-by-row audit is on the hub](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika). Its structural findings, which apply to every page in this directory:

* **The source list contains duplicated rows that are counted.** Index 61 duplicates 31; 63 duplicates 33; 65 duplicates 35; and **56 is index 14 re-entered with the year advanced** — the same claimed event, counted twice, with the second instance describing an aircraft 1,200 miles from where the sheet places it.
* **The matching window is ±3 days on every single row**, per the sheet's own `Overlap Window` column. That is a seven-day window, conceded in a column of its own.
* **The sheet's own `Charlie Present` column reads "No" on 32 of 35 visible rows.** On its own data, these aircraft were overwhelmingly not overlapping with Charlie Kirk.
* **Erika Kirk's location is sourced to "Instagram confirms presence"** in the `Notes` column — undated, unlinked, unarchived.
* **The `Event/Reason` column names no actual events.** Every entry is a category label — "TPUSA appearances / media," "TPUSA / campus engagement" — with no venue, city, or date a reader could check.

**And the September core survives all of it.** The auditor marks the **4 September Provo arrival accurate**, the **13 September SU-BND departure accurate**, the **23 May SU-BND Provo landing accurate**, and confirms the **10 September departure** while correcting its stated origin from "Paris" to Provo. **The multi-year pattern does not survive. The September window does.**

The investigation is better served by the narrow surviving claim than by the wide one. A seven-day-window spreadsheet with duplicate rows is what a hostile reviewer reaches for first, and reaching for it lets them dismiss the September facts along with it.

## What we do not know

Named, so the holes are visible rather than papered over:

1. **No manifest for any leg has ever been published**, by anyone, for any of these aircraft, at any location.
2. **The customs records are in North Dakota, not Utah.** Every Provo arrival came in on a domestic leg from **[Minot](./Minot_KMOT_2025-04-08_to_2025-09-04/overview)**. Whoever was aboard was processed there — and **nobody has asked North Dakota for anything.** This is the largest unexploited lead in the aircraft angle of the case.
3. **The Kirk-side event dates are largely uncited**, coming from a researcher timeline circulated on X.
4. **The 27 May 2025 Provo arrival is attributed to two different aircraft** — [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview) in the flight-history export, [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) in the Owens account. A named-passenger inference rests on which is right. **SU-BTV is hex 0101D1, SU-BTU is hex 0101D0**; the raw archive would settle it in minutes.
5. **The window itself is unresolved, but it has moved.** The earliest overlap that survives the spreadsheet audit is **13 November 2022** (Wichita), and the auditor noted the archive "doesn't go back this far" for some 2022 rows. Primary position data now reaches 2022 through the monthly globe samples — SU-BTT on the ground at Wilmington on 1 June 2022 and at Wichita on 1 September 2022 — so **the aircraft's 2022 presence in the United States is no longer a screenshot claim**. What is still unknown is the **rate**: one sampled day a month cannot tell us how many rotations happened between the samples, and no sample places a Kirk anywhere. The start of the *following* window, as opposed to the start of the *flying* window, remains unknown in both directions.
6. **No Duncan Aviation work order, invoice, or shop record has been produced** for any of these visits. A single confirmation of which airframes were in the shop on which dates would close most of this directory.
7. **The 0.000000001% probability claim has no published model** — no assumptions, no denominator, no list of compared locations. It is quoted on the [broadcast page](./Candace_Owens_Broadcasts) because it was said, not because it computes.
8. **The 10 September transponder timings have no stated time base.** Local Mountain Time or UTC changes their meaning entirely — and a UTC-versus-local confusion is exactly the error that produced the disputed SU-BTT departure time on the same morning. A second tracker's reading of the same cycles puts the power-up **21 minutes before** the shot rather than 17 minutes after.
9. **No outlet independently pulled the flight data.** Every write-up in the [press record](./Press_Coverage) is summarising one broadcast. The volume of coverage is not corroboration.
10. **The 68→73 revision was never reconciled** into a single sourced list, and the compilation it rests on — credited to [@alsacetx](https://x.com/alsacetx) — is a set of ADS-B reads, not a primary record.
11. **Forty-two of the 85 claimed rows cannot be decided by any free archive.** Twenty-two fall on dates the archives cover with no trace of that airframe; twenty fall on dates no free archive reaches at all. **Those are holes, not refutations, and they are not confirmations either.** They are the single largest reason no honest total can be given for this claim.
12. **We still cannot place a Kirk at any confirmed field.** Twenty-four aircraft positions are now confirmed from primary data. **Zero Kirk positions are.** Erika Kirk's itinerary — the document the 73-count was measured against — has never been published by her, by the compilers, or by anyone else, and the aircraft-side recovery does not produce it. This is the gap the whole claim turns on and it has not narrowed by a day.

**What moved, and what did not.** Three items above are materially better than they were: the 2022 window now has position data behind it, the departure time on 10 September is settled from two independent archives, and 36 of the 85 claimed rows now have a primary-data verdict instead of a spreadsheet opinion. **Nothing on the Kirk side moved at all.** The list is longer than it was, not shorter, and that is the honest state of it.

## Where this page fits

This is the door into the location-by-location log of the shadowing claim, sitting directly under [the Planes index](/Planes/overview) and beside [the hub page for the same claim](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika) — the counts it carries are trackers' tallies read off public ADS-B history, they disagree with each other at 73, 72, 70-plus, 68 and 29, and this section shows the disagreement rather than averaging it. The whole reason the pattern is argued about is what it would mean if it held — [foreign intelligence involvement](/Proof_Intel_Services/overview), [the Israel main-suspect case](/Israel_Main_Suspect/overview), [the halted NCTC foreign-nexus review](/US_Intelligence/nctc) and [Law 2, which would force Congress to explain these tail numbers on the record](/laws/US_Intel/Law_2_US_Intel) — and none of it is established by an aircraft sitting on a ramp. Start with [Provo](/Planes/following/Provo_KPVU_2024-04-19_to_2025-09-13/overview), the one location that survives the sceptics' own line-by-line audit, then read [Wilmington](/Planes/following/Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview), where twelve departures and zero arrivals dismantle Delaware as a clustering state — and note throughout that Erika Kirk's half of every pairing rests on a claimed location because [her flight logs are reported erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased).
