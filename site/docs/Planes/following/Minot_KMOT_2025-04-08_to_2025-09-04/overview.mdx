# Minot (KMOT) — April to September 2025

**Every single Egyptian-registered aircraft that reached Provo in 2025 entered the United States at Minot, North Dakota.** Four aircraft, four arrivals, four immediate onward hops to Utah.

This page exists because that fact changes where the records are. **US customs processing for everyone aboard those flights happened at Minot** — not at Provo, where every published claim about passengers, contractors and manifests has been directed. **Nobody has asked North Dakota for anything.**

It also exists because Minot is a genuinely odd choice, and the oddness has not been examined by anyone.

## The airport

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Minot International Airport |
| **Identifiers** | **KMOT** (ICAO) / **MOT** (IATA) |
| **City / State** | Minot, Ward County, North Dakota |
| **Class** | Commercial-service **international** airport — small, with customs facilities |
| **Nearby** | **Minot Air Force Base** — a separate facility about 13 miles north, home to the 5th Bomb Wing (B-52H) and the 91st Missile Wing (Minuteman III). **It is one of the few installations hosting two legs of the US nuclear triad.** |
| **Distance to Provo (KPVU)** | ~900 miles; the logged hops run **1:35 to 1:55** |

### How unusual is a foreign government jet here

**Very.** And this is the one place in this section where the anomaly reading has real force.

Minot is a small northern-plains airport. It has customs, which is why it is technically eligible, but it is nobody's natural transatlantic entry point. A Cairo–Utah trip has obvious alternatives: the East Coast fields this same fleet uses on the way *out* ([Wilmington](../Wilmington_KILG_2022-11-17_to_2025-10-12/overview)), the Canadian tech stops the Gulfstreams use (Goose Bay), or any of the western international airports — Denver, Salt Lake City, Las Vegas.

**This same operator used Omaha, Detroit and St. Louis as entry points for its Nebraska, Michigan and Missouri rotations.** All three are conventional. **Every Utah-bound flight went to Minot instead.** That is a consistent, deliberate-looking choice across four flights and four months, and no published account explains it.

## The critical distinction: MOT is not the Air Force base

Claims circulating on X state that these aircraft **"arrived via Minot Air Force Base"** and treat that as placing foreign state aircraft on a nuclear-mission installation. One analysis quoted on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) puts it directly: *"both of them arrived via Minot Air Force Base. This is America's only nuclear-based that holds 2 out of the 3 US nuclear triad."*

**The flight-history exports do not show that.** They show **"Minot (MOT)"** — the identifier for **Minot International Airport**, the civil field. Minot Air Force Base is a separate installation with its own identifier (**KMIB / MIB**) about 13 miles away.

This distinction is not pedantry. It is the difference between two very different claims:

* **What the record supports:** foreign state aircraft repeatedly cleared US customs at a small civil airport in North Dakota, thirteen miles from a nuclear-weapons base, before flying on to Utah.
* **What the record does not support:** foreign state aircraft landing *on* that base.

**The first claim is odd enough on its own and it is the one that should be pressed.** The second, stated without evidence, is the kind of overreach that lets the first be dismissed alongside it.

### The 4 September sequence, minute by minute

The best-documented of the four, and the one that matters:

| Time (local) | Event |
|---|---|
| 3 Sep 2025 | Cairo → Paris–Le Bourget, 4:03 |
| 4 Sep 2025, **11:03 am** | **Lands Minot (MOT)** after an 8:52 transatlantic leg |
| 4 Sep 2025, **~11:45 am** | Departs Minot |
| 4 Sep 2025, **12:46 pm** | **Lands Provo (PVU)**, 1:39 en route |

**About one hour on the ground at Minot** — enough to clear customs and take fuel, and not much more.

**Six days later, this aircraft departed Provo at 07:14 am on 10 September.**

## Why this relocates the entire passenger question

Every claim on this site about who was aboard these aircraft — the alleged subcontractors, the "DoD Liaison" badges, the counter-UAS technicians, the named passenger, the missing manifests, the Provo "no-scan priority clearance" — is a claim about **Provo**.

**But Provo was a domestic arrival.** By the time these aircraft touched down in Utah, everyone aboard had already been admitted to the United States, hours earlier and 900 miles away.

That means:

* **The CBP entry records are Minot records.** Passport data, admission records, crew lists and any I-94 processing for every person aboard all four flights exist at **Minot International Airport, North Dakota**.
* **The general declaration (gen dec) — the arriving-crew-and-passenger form filed on international arrival — was filed at Minot.**
* **Any customs inspection of the aircraft or its cargo happened at Minot.**
* **A Provo FBO record shows a domestic arrival**, which is a far thinner document.

**No published request, FOIA, or inquiry has been directed at Minot by anyone in this investigation.** Given that a substantial part of the SU-BTT page consists of unverified claims about who was aboard, and that the records capable of answering them sit at a single small airport in North Dakota, **this is the largest unexploited lead in the aircraft angle of the case.**

## A related North Dakota entry

The 2024 record has one more North Dakota data point that fits the pattern: **[SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview)'s 23 April 2024 arrival at Provo routed Cairo → Paris → Williston, North Dakota (XWA) → Provo**, landing at 12:13 pm. The [independent audit](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika) marks it accurate, noting *"Acc 4/22-23. Williston code XWA."*

**Williston is a different North Dakota field** — Williston Basin International, in the Bakken oil region, about 120 miles west of Minot. So across two years the fleet used **two different small North Dakota airports** as its Utah entry point, never the same one twice in a row and never a conventional western international airport.

That strengthens rather than weakens the observation. **A single odd routing choice is a fluke. Five Utah-bound flights over eighteen months, all entering through small northern-plains fields, is a pattern with a reason behind it — and nobody has established what the reason is.**

The mundane candidates are real and should be stated: great-circle routing from Paris to Utah passes over the northern plains, so Minot and Williston are close to the direct line; both have customs; both are uncongested and cheap; and a fuel stop after eight and a half hours across the Atlantic is ordinary. **That may be the whole answer.** It has simply never been checked against the alternative — that a small field with minimal traffic and minimal scrutiny was chosen for a reason.

## The counterargument

* **Minot International is a civil airport with customs facilities.** Clearing there is lawful and ordinary.
* **The great-circle route from Paris to Utah crosses the northern plains.** Minot is near the direct line; it is not a detour.
* **Ground times of about an hour** are consistent with customs and fuel, nothing more.
* **The Air Force base is a separate installation.** Nothing in the flight record places any of these aircraft on it.
* **No Kirk or TPUSA pairing is claimed here by anyone**, including by the people making the strongest claims elsewhere.
* **The pattern includes Williston in 2024**, so it is not specific to Minot or to 2025.

## What we do not know

1. **The CBP records at Minot for all four 2025 arrivals** — and the Williston record for April 2024. **These exist and have never been requested.** They would answer the passenger question directly.
2. **Why Minot and Williston rather than a western international airport.** No published account addresses it.
3. **Whether anyone joined or left the aircraft at Minot.** The one-hour ground times permit it.
4. **Whether the aircraft or its cargo were inspected.** A customs inspection record would exist if so.
5. **Whether the 27 May arrival was [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview) or [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview)** — the tail dispute set out on the [Provo page](../Provo_KPVU_2024-04-19_to_2025-09-13/overview) applies to this arrival too, since the Minot leg and the Provo leg are the same flight.
