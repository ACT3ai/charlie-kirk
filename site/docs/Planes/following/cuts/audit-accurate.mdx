# Accurate — the rows the tracking-site audit upheld

**Where this came from.** On [the Following overview](/Planes/following/overview), the audit table's first line reads **"Accurate — the jet really was there that day."** This page is that cell, opened up. It is every row the enriched overlap register carries an **accurate** audit label on — **21 rows** — with the airframe, the field, the claimed date, the logged ground stay where one exists, and the independent ADS-B verdict printed on the same line so the two methods can be read against each other.

**Read the count carefully.** The overview prints **18** in this cell and says plainly that its audit figures are stated against **the 78 rows the auditor worked from**, not the 85 this register now holds. Counted out of the register, the accurate label sits on 21 rows. The gap is explained in full at the bottom of this page.

## What this slice is

The filter is the audit verdict column, and nothing else. An independent line-by-line check of the source sheet was made against paid flight-history archives, re-reading tracking-site pages date by date. A row lands here when that check concluded the aircraft really was at the field on the date claimed — as opposed to *partially accurate* (right aircraft and date, wrong route or duration), *inaccurate*, *not assessable*, *never published*, or *not reached*. The verdict is about the **aircraft's position only**. It is not a finding about who was aboard, and the auditor did not claim it was. An investigator can reproduce this list by filtering `overlaps.csv` on the audit verdict.

## What the specifics show

**The two methods mostly agree here, and where they do not, the disagreement is the interesting part.** 15 of these 21 rows are also corroborated by recovered ADS-B position data — the aircraft measured inside 15 km of the named field on the claimed date — and 8 of those are ground confirmations, with the on-ground flag set. One row goes the other way: **`OWENS-015`, 9 May 2023, Goose Bay (CYYR)**, which the audit called accurate and the recovered trace puts **1,063 km away** at closest approach. That single line is the cleanest example on this site of two honest methods reaching opposite answers, and it is left standing in both directions rather than reconciled.

**One more row sits between the two verdicts.** `OWENS-012`, 14 April 2023, claimed as Wichita (KICT), measures 27.58 km from the field — inside the source sheet's own 50-mile tolerance, outside any same-airport test. It is recorded as *right area, wrong field* and it refutes nothing.

**The 2022 rows in this list have no primary data behind them at all.** All three — `OWENS-004` (13 November 2022, Wichita), `OWENS-006` (20 December 2022, St. Louis) and `OWENS-007` (27 December 2022, Bangor) — come back **No archive coverage**: no free archive reaches those dates for any airframe probed. `OWENS-004` is noted in the register as the first US rotation that survives the audit, and it survives on the audit alone. A fourth row, `SITE-001` (2 April 2023, the three St. Louis fields), comes back **Not heard**.

**Geographically this list leans the same way the whole register does.** Provo (KPVU) holds 8 of the 21 rows and Utah 8 of the states, with Missouri next at 5. Three dates carry two rows each — 23 April 2024, 23 May 2025 and 10 September 2025 — which is how 21 rows resolve to 18 dates.

**Where this list is weakest.** 11 of the 21 rows still rest on Erika Kirk alone. Only 4 fall on a day this site can independently source a Kirk or TPUSA appearance: `SITE-001` (2 April 2023, the TPUSA Faith tour launch in Jefferson City, with neither Kirk at it), `SITE-004` (23 April 2024, Provo, where the register's own note records the Kirk-side event as uncited), and `EXTRA-006` and `SITE-006` (10 September 2025, Provo). An accurate audit verdict upgrades the *flight*. It leaves the *following* claim exactly where it was.

## What this list cannot tell you

* **A trace, and an audit, locate an aircraft. Neither places a person aboard.** Every verdict in this list is about where a jet was. Nothing here identifies a passenger, and the auditor did not claim otherwise.
* **Erika Kirk's presence is established on zero of these rows,** as it is on zero rows anywhere in the register. 11 of the 21 rest on her claim alone and 5 more pair her with Charlie Kirk. Her flight logs are [reported erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased) and no dated itinerary has ever been published, so an accurate flight verdict cannot be carried across to her side of the pairing.
* **"No archive coverage" and "Not heard" are archive limits, not deletions.** They appear on 4 of these 21 rows. They mean the free archives reach no data for that date, or hold no trace of that airframe on a date they otherwise serve. Neither is a refutation, neither is a confirmation, and neither is evidence that anything was removed.
* **An accurate verdict is not a finding about purpose.** The register's own counterarguments record that Wichita's Yingling Aviation is an FAA Part 145 Dassault Falcon service centre and that Duncan Aviation has held the Egyptian VIP Falcon account since 1999, with Lincoln as the main plant and Provo as a second facility. Those contracts produce landings at exactly these fields. This list cannot separate a maintenance rotation from a following pattern.
* **Every label here is attributed.** The claims are the compilers'; the accurate verdicts are the tracking-site auditor's; the ADS-B verdicts are this site's measurements against stored traces. No claim on this page asserts that any living person committed a crime.
* **Where this page differs from the overview — and it does.** The overview prints **18** in this cell, against the 78 rows the auditor worked from, alongside Partially accurate 5 and Not reached 4. This register carries the accurate label on **21** rows out of 85. One of the 21, `EXTRA-006`, is among the seven rows added later that the auditor never saw. That still leaves **20 accurate rows among the same 78** the overview counts 18 in, so the difference is not only the later additions: two of the auditor's own rows carry an accurate label in `overlaps.csv` that the overview's table does not count. The two figures are not reconciled here. **18 is the auditor's number; 21 is the register's; take the auditor's as the audit result and this page as the register's contents.**
