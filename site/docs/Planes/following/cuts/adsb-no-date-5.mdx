# The five rows that name no date

**This page opens the number 5 — rows with no date, no city, no airport, and a tail cell that reads “SU-BTT or SU-BND”.** They are the difference between the sheet's 67 published rows and the count of 72 quoted on air. **Nobody can check them, including the person who counted them** — and they are carried here rather than dropped, because they are still inside the number being broadcast.

**Where this came from.** On [the Following overview](/Planes/following/overview), the ADS-B verdict table — the one that re-tests all 85 rows against recovered position traces — ends with the line **"No date named | 5 | The five never-published rows. Uncheckable by anyone, including their author."** This page is those five rows. The count here, **5**, agrees exactly with the figure printed on the overview.

## What this slice is

A row is in this list if the register holds **no date for it at all**. Reproduce it from `overlaps.csv` by taking every row whose date field is empty: five rows, indexed `UNPUB-001` through `UNPUB-005`. They exist because the compiler stated a higher total than she itemised — Candace Owens said on 17 November 2025 that the count had gone from 68 overlaps to 73 — and this register carries the five unitemised claims as explicit placeholder rows rather than absorbing them silently into a headline number.

These are the same five rows that appear under [never published](/Planes/following/cuts/never-published-5) in the audit table and under ["SU-BTT or SU-BND"](/Planes/following/cuts/tail-either-btt-or-bnd-5) in the aircraft table. One set of five rows, failing three different tests for the same reason: nothing about them was ever published.

## What the specifics show

**These five rows are identical in every field the register holds.** `UNPUB-001` through `UNPUB-005` carry the same source, the same source post, the same attendee claim (Erika Kirk, Charlie not claimed), the same unresolved aircraft pair, and the same three blanks — no date, no airport, no city. They are not five separate observations. They are one statement that five more overlaps exist, written out five times so the register's row count matches the total that was claimed in public.

**Nothing here can be checked by anyone, including the person who made the claim.** A date is the key every archive is queried by. Without one there is no aircraft-day to look up, no tracking-site page to read, and no window to compare against a speaking schedule. The audit could not assess them, the position-data pass could not assess them, and neither outcome tells you anything about whether the underlying claim is true.

**They still count toward the headline.** The difference between "68 overlaps" and "73 overlaps" is exactly these five. Every published figure that uses the larger number is carrying five entries that name no date, no place and no definite aircraft — **5 of the 85 rows in this register, roughly one row in seventeen**, resting on an assertion alone.

**The weakest thing about this list is also the most useful thing about it.** Keeping the five as visible rows is what makes the gap between the claimed total and the itemised total measurable. Folding them into the count without a row would hide it.

## What this list cannot tell you

* **A trace locates an aircraft. It never places a person aboard.** No trace exists for these rows in any case — but the rule holds for the corroborated rows elsewhere in this register too, and it is why none of them establishes who was flying.
* **Erika Kirk's presence is not established on any of these five rows.** All five name her, and none supports her presence with anything. Her flight logs are [reported erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased) and no dated itinerary has ever been published. Site-wide, the number of rows on which her presence is actually established is [zero](/Planes/following/cuts/erika-established-0).
* **These five are not an archive gap and must not be counted as one.** They are not "not heard" and they are not "no archive coverage" — both of those are limits of what an archive holds. These five were never testable in the first place, because the claim itself withholds the date. Nothing has been deleted, hidden, or suppressed here.
* **An untestable claim is not a false claim.** Five undated assertions are unverifiable, not disproved. The correct reading is that they carry no evidentiary weight in either direction.
* **Every claim on this page is the compiler's.** The rows record what Candace Owens stated in *Operation Mocking-Plane* on 17 November 2025. This site asserts no crime by any person named here and no purpose behind any flight.
* **On the count, this page and the overview agree** — both give 5. Note the wider register: the audit that produced the verdicts on the overview worked from a 78-row version of the sheet, while this register holds 85 rows; these five are recorded as "never published" rather than as anything the audit weighed.
