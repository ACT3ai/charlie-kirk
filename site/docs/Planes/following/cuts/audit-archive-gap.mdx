# Rows the audit could not assess

**This page opens the number 2 — the rows even the paid flight archives could not reach.** Both are 2022, both name SU-BTT, and **the free archives cannot reach them either.** Two rows nobody on either side of this dispute can currently test.

**Where this came from.** [The Following overview](/Planes/following/overview) carries a table of the independent line-by-line audit of the overlap sheet against paid flight-history archives. The cell reading **"Not assessable — the flight archive does not reach back that far — 2"** links here. This page is those two rows in full. The count on this page and the count on the overview agree at two.

## What this slice is

A row lands here when `audit_verdict` in `overlaps.csv` reads `archive_gap` — the auditor examined the claim, found the archive he was working from did not extend to that date, and recorded that he could not rule on it. It is not a verdict of accurate, inaccurate or partially accurate; it is a statement about the archive's reach. **It is not a finding that anything was removed.** Both rows also carry the independent ADS-B verdict `NO_ARCHIVE_COVERAGE`, meaning the free archives this investigation queried afterwards likewise hold nothing for that date, for any airframe probed — again an archive limit, not suppression. Rows the audit simply never reached, and rows an archive covers but does not hear, are different categories and live on their own pages.

## What the specifics show

**This is the smallest cell on the overview, and it is small for a boring reason: the archives run out in 2022.** Both rows are from 2022 — `OWENS-043` on 25 November and `OWENS-044` on 5 December — and 2022 is the year the free and paid flight histories used in this investigation thin out or stop. Ten days apart, both naming SU-BTT, both claiming a Paris arrival and a Cairo departure, both attributed by the compilation to Erika Kirk alone.

**Neither pass could rule, and they failed for the same reason.** The auditor's own note on both rows is "Data doesn't go back this far", and the independent ADS-B pass afterwards returned `NO_ARCHIVE_COVERAGE` — no free archive holds that date for any airframe probed. That is the wording the register itself uses, and it is the reason the two passes agree: both ran out of archive rather than out of aircraft. When a control aircraft with no connection to this case fails the same way on the same date, the failure belongs to the archive and not to the airframe. **That is why these two rows must never be described as removals.**

**The auditor did not leave it there, and what he added cuts against the claim.** On both rows he records his own belief about where the aircraft actually was: on `OWENS-043`, "In Cairo; closest flight was 11/20/22, Wilmington to Cairo", and on `OWENS-044`, "Still in Cairo; flew back into Cairo 20 Nov, out to Paris then St. Louis 20 Dec." That is the auditor's reading, offered without the archive coverage that would settle it, and it is recorded here as his statement rather than as a finding.

**Nothing on either row can be paired with an appearance.** Table B shows no sourced Kirk or Turning Point appearance within three days of either date, anywhere in the country — not merely none in Delaware or Illinois. Neither row has a logged ground stay in this site's flight records, and neither has a ground confirmation. Whatever these two rows are, they are not evidence of anyone being followed to an event.

## What this list cannot tell you

* **A trace locates an aircraft. It never places a person aboard.** On these two rows there is no trace at all, so the point is doubly true: nothing here locates the aircraft, and nothing here locates a person.
* **This is an archive limit, not a deletion.** `NO_ARCHIVE_COVERAGE` and the auditor's `archive_gap` both mean the record was never reachable from these sources for these dates. No page on this site should read them as suppression, and this one does not.
* **Erika Kirk's presence is established on zero rows** — on these two and across the whole register. Both rows are attributed to her alone by the compilation. Her flight logs are [reported erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased) and no itinerary was ever published, so there is no document to check her against on any date.
* **An absence in an archive is not evidence of absence.** Nothing here shows SU-BTT was at Wilmington or Chicago, and nothing here shows it was not. Both claims remain exactly as unverified as they were when they were published.
* **This cell is stated against 78 rows, not 85.** The audit table on the overview reports the auditor's figures against the **78 rows he worked from**, not the 85 this register now holds; the seven later additions were reconstructed by this site from overlap pages he never saw. Both rows on this page are Owens sheet indices 43 and 44, so both are inside the 78 and the count of two is unaffected.
