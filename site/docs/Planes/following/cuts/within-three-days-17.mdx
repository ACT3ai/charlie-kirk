# Rows within three days of a sourced Kirk or TPUSA appearance in the same state

**This page opens the number 17 — the rows that fall within three days of a Kirk or TPUSA appearance this site can independently source in the same state.** Seventeen out of 85: **one claimed overlap in five clears even the loosest proximity test this project applies**, and that is with the window at three days and the match at state level rather than airport.

**Where this came from.** On [the Following overview](/Planes/following/overview), the row that
reads *"fall within three days of a Turning Point or Charlie Kirk appearance this site can
independently source in the same state"* carries the count **17**. This page is those 17 rows,
every column of each one. They come out of a register of **85** claimed overlaps, so this is the
one row in five that clears even the loosest proximity test this project applies.

## What this slice is

Every claimed overlap in `overlaps.csv` carries a `nearest_event_gap_days` column: the number of
days between the date claimed on that row and the closest Kirk or Turning Point USA appearance this
site can source independently — from a tour press release, a venue listing, or contemporaneous
news — in the same state. A row lands here when that column holds a number and that number is
**3 or less**. Rows whose column is blank, or holds something that is not a plain number, are out.
Note what the rule does *not* require: it does not require the aircraft to have been at the
airport, it does not require anyone to have been aboard, and a gap of 3 is still three days. It is
a proximity filter, not a proof, and 10 of the 17 are same-day while the other 7 sit one to three
days away.

## What the specifics show

This is the strongest-looking list in the whole register, and it is small. Thirteen of the 17 rows
come back **At the claimed airport — corroborated**, the other four are **Not heard**, and nothing
here is refuted or falls in an archive-coverage hole. Six rows carry a transmitted on-ground
position rather than an overflight: OWENS-013 (20 April 2023, KILG, 0.56 km), OWENS-026 and
SITE-004 (both 23 April 2024, KPVU, 0.00 km), and EXTRA-006, OWENS-041 and SITE-006 (all
10 September 2025, KPVU, 1.29 km and 1.23 km). The 10 September 2025 cluster is four of the 17
rows on one day, all of them tied to the UVU appearance in Orem.

The concentration is by date, not by breadth. Seventeen rows sit on only 12 distinct dates, and
three dates carry eight of them: 3 April 2024 (OWENS-024 and SITE-003, Wichita against the
University of Kansas event in Lawrence), 23 April 2024 (OWENS-026 and SITE-004, Provo against the
University of Utah event in Salt Lake City), and 10 September 2025 (EXTRA-006, OWENS-041, SITE-005
and SITE-006). Utah supplies 5 rows and Kansas 4. Whole years of the register produce nothing that
reaches this page.

The list is weakest at its edges, and in two specific ways worth naming. First, **the "same state"
half of the label does not hold on three rows.** OWENS-013 (20 April 2023, Wilmington, Delaware)
is matched to an Erika Kirk Instagram post in Arizona and a Charlie Kirk event in Texas. OWENS-021
(14 February 2024, Wilmington, Delaware) is matched to a Lincoln, Nebraska chapter opening.
SITE-005 (10 September 2025, Wilmington, Delaware) says so on its own row: *"none sourced in
Delaware; the sourced Kirk event that day is UVU, Orem UT."* All three are Delaware rows matched to
out-of-state events, and Delaware is where the jet's own home-base pattern already puts it.

Second, **two of this site's own files disagree about three rows.** The slice was cut on the
`nearest_event_gap_days` column in `overlaps.csv`. Rebuilding the same match directly against
`tpusa_events.csv` — which is what Table B below prints — finds **no** sourced Kirk or Turning
Point appearance within three days anywhere for OWENS-020 (9 February 2024), SITE-002 (11 February
2024) and OWENS-021 (14 February 2024). The event those three rest on is a TPUSA chapter opening at
Lincoln on 11 February 2024, recorded in the sheet's own nearest-event note but carrying no row in
the events catalog. Table B therefore reads *"None. No sourced Kirk or TPUSA appearance within
three days, anywhere"* on all three, and the sheet's note contradicts it. We publish both rather
than pick one: until an independent source for the Lincoln chapter date is filed in
`tpusa_events.csv`, those three rows should be read as unsupported by this site's own event
catalog. On the same rebuilt basis the same-day count is 10 rather than the 9 the column gives:
SITE-001 (2 April 2023) and EXTRA-003 (23 April 2023) match same-day events the column scored at 1
and 3 days, and SITE-002 (11 February 2024), which the column scores 0, matches nothing at all on
the rebuilt basis. 9 + 2 - 1 = 10.

One further caution about EXTRA-003 (23 April 2023, Topeka): its nearest-event note reads *"this
row"*. The overlap and the event are the same entry, so the proximity is definitional rather than
independent, and the audit never reached that row at all.

Finally, the attendee split cuts against the headline the list invites. On 6 of the 17 rows the
sheet claims **neither Kirk** was present — the match is to a Turning Point event only. Charlie
Kirk alone accounts for 3 rows and both Kirks together for 4. Four rows rest on Erika Kirk alone,
and her presence is established on none of them.

## What this list cannot tell you

* **A trace locates an aircraft. It never places a person aboard.** The six ground confirmations
  prove a transponder was transmitting from a ramp at a named field on a named day. No row here
  carries a manifest, a boarding record, or any document naming a passenger.
* **Erika Kirk's presence is established on zero rows.** Four rows in this slice rest on her alone
  and eight name her, but her flight logs are [reported erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased)
  and no itinerary was ever published, so there is nothing to check any of it against. Where the
  sheet gives her location it is sometimes contradicted by her own account — several 10 September
  2025 rows record the sheet saying Nebraska/Delaware and Erika Kirk saying Arizona.
* **Proximity is not following.** Three days is a wide window, and 7 of these 17 rows use all or
  part of it. An Egyptian-registered jet on a US ramp within three days of a Turning Point event in
  the same state is a coincidence this page can measure; it is not, on its own, evidence that the
  aircraft was there because of the event. The control question — how often the same jets sit near
  cities with no Turning Point event at all — is answered elsewhere on this site, not by this list.
* **An absence in an archive is not evidence of absence.** The four **Not heard** rows mean the
  volunteer receiver networks served other airframes that day and hold no trace for this one.
  Parked with the transponder off, outside receiver coverage, and a wrong claimed date all look
  identical from the outside. That is an archive limit, not a deletion and not a finding.
* **The two counts on this page are both real.** The overview cell says 17 and so does this page.
  Inside it, the same-day figure is 9 by the sheet's own column and 10 by rebuilding against
  `tpusa_events.csv`; the At a glance table prints 10 because that is what the rebuilt match
  returns. More broadly, the tracking-site audit worked from 78 rows while this register holds 85,
  which is why one row here is marked **Not reached by the audit** rather than judged.
* **No living person is accused of anything.** The claims here are the compilers' and the
  auditors'. This page reports what their rows say, what the recovered position data confirms, and
  where the two disagree.
