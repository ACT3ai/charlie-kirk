# Every row naming SU-BTT, the yellow plane

**Where this came from.** On [the Following overview](/Planes/following/overview), the aircraft
table has a row reading *"Overlaps where the aircraft was SU-BTT, the yellow plane"* with the count
**57**. This page is those 57 rows in full. They are 57 of the register's 85 claimed overlaps — two
rows in three — which makes SU-BTT the airframe the entire following claim rests on.

## What this slice is

A row lands here when the aircraft named on it in `overlaps.csv` is **SU-BTT and only SU-BTT** — the
Egyptian-registered Dassault Falcon 7X, ICAO hex `0101D3`, the one the compilers call the yellow
plane. Rows naming SU-BND on its own are not here (there are 16 of those), and neither are the
**3** rows that name both tails at once, which are counted separately so no row is double-counted.
Nine further rows name no resolvable aircraft at all. 57 + 16 + 3 + 9 = 85, the whole register. If
you are looking for the 10 September 2025 rows that name both jets side by side, they sit in the
both-tails slice, not here — this page carries the two SU-BTT-alone rows for that date, OWENS-041
at Provo and SITE-005 at Wilmington.

## What the specifics show

The geography is the most concentrated thing in this list. Three fields hold 37 of the 57 rows —
Wichita (KICT, 17), Wilmington (KILG, 10) and Omaha (KOMA, 10) — and three states hold 43 of them:
Kansas 18, Nebraska 15, Delaware 10. Seven more airports appear exactly once each, scattered from
Bangor to Sacramento. Whatever this jet was doing, it did it repeatedly in three places and rarely
anywhere else. Utah, the state the case turns on, carries just 3 rows in this whole list, all three
at Provo: OWENS-026 (23 April 2024) and OWENS-040 (4 September 2025), both logged as Utah, plus
OWENS-041 (10 September 2025), logged as Utah/Delaware because the row spans a departure from Provo
and an arrival at Wilmington.

The position data can speak on 19 of the 57 rows, and on 17 of those it agrees with the sheet. Four
rows are stronger than the rest because the aircraft transmitted an on-ground position rather than
an overflight: OWENS-013 (20 April 2023, KILG, 0.56 km), OWENS-026 (23 April 2024, KPVU, 0.00 km),
OWENS-040 (4 September 2025, KPVU, 0.16 km) and OWENS-041 (10 September 2025, KPVU, 1.23 km). Those
four are the hard core of this list: on those four days, at those four fields, the airframe was on
the ramp and said so.

Publish the two that go the other way as plainly as the 17. OWENS-036 (18 June 2025, claimed
Omaha) is **refuted** — the airframe was tracked that day and its closest recovered position is
**10,140 km** from KOMA. It was not in Nebraska. OWENS-012 (14 April 2023, claimed Wichita) comes
back **right area, wrong field**: the closest recovered position is 28 km from KICT, inside the
source sheet's own 50-to-100-mile tolerance but outside a same-airport test, so it is neither
confirmed nor refuted.

The tracking-site audit is harsher than the position data. It called **34 of 57** rows
**Inaccurate** against 12 accurate and 8 partially accurate. Read Table C carefully before treating
that as a contradiction: on several rows the two verdicts disagree because they test different
things. OWENS-021 (14 February 2024, Wilmington) is marked Inaccurate by the audit for naming the
wrong origin city, while the ADS-B trace still puts the jet 0.8 km from KILG that day. An audit
verdict of Inaccurate can mean the whole row is wrong, or it can mean one cell in the row is wrong.
Two rows — OWENS-043 (25 November 2022) and OWENS-044 (5 December 2022) — the audit could not
assess at all, and one, EXTRA-003 (23 April 2023, Topeka), it never reached.

The oldest end of the list is the emptiest, and the reason is an archive boundary rather than
anything about the aircraft. All **10** rows marked **No archive coverage** sit inside one block of
dates at the front of the record — 5 October 2022 (OWENS-001) to 15 January 2023 (OWENS-008) — a
window that also takes in OWENS-043, OWENS-044 and OWENS-045. The block is not quite unbroken: one
row inside it, OWENS-003 (1 November 2022), was reached and came back **Not heard** instead. No free
archive this project can reach holds any position data for those ten dates at all. That is a retention
limit on the archives, not a removal, and not a statement about where the jet was.

The last number is the one that most weakens the following claim, and it is the biggest. On **42 of
the 57 rows** the only person the sheet claims was being followed is **Erika Kirk alone** — 74% of
this list. Charlie Kirk alone accounts for 6 rows, both Kirks together for 4, and a Turning Point
event with neither Kirk named for 5. Cross the two columns and the Erika-alone rows are exactly
where the evidence is thinnest: of those 42, **21** are Not heard and **10** are in the
no-archive-coverage window, so 31 of 42 were never testable; 9 come back corroborated, 1 is right
area / wrong field, and 1 is the refuted Omaha row. Only 10 of the 57 rows fall on a day this site
can independently source a Kirk or Turning Point appearance at all.

## What this list cannot tell you

* **A trace locates an aircraft. It never places a person aboard.** The four ground confirmations
  above establish that a transponder registered to SU-BTT transmitted from a ramp at a named field
  on a named day. Not one of the 57 rows carries a manifest, a boarding record, a customs
  declaration, or any document naming a passenger.
* **Erika Kirk's presence is established on zero rows.** Forty-two of these 57 rows rest on her
  alone. Her flight logs are [reported erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased) and no itinerary
  was ever published, so on 42 rows the person side of the pairing has no supporting document of
  any kind. Where the sheet gives her location it is sometimes contradicted by her own account:
  several 10 September 2025 rows record the sheet saying Nebraska/Delaware and Erika Kirk saying
  Arizona. Nothing on this page resolves that, and nothing on this page should be read as placing
  her on any aircraft.
* **An absence in an archive is not evidence of absence, and it is not a deletion.** The 28 rows
  marked **Not heard** mean the volunteer receiver networks served other airframes that day and
  hold no trace for this one. The 10 marked **No archive coverage** mean no free archive reaches
  those dates. Both are limits of the archives. Parked with the transponder off, outside receiver
  coverage, and a wrong claimed date all look identical from the outside, and none of the three can
  be told apart here.
* **Presence at a field is not following.** SU-BTT is an Egyptian-registered business jet with a
  documented pattern of long international legs, and this list contains many rows where the sheet
  claims no Kirk at all. Concentration at Wichita, Wilmington and Omaha is a fact about the
  aircraft's habits before it is a fact about anybody's calendar. The question of how often the
  same airframe sat near cities with no Turning Point event is a control question, answered
  elsewhere on this site rather than by this list.
* **Where the count here differs from the overview page.** This register holds 85 rows; the
  tracking-site audit worked from 78. That is why one row here is marked **Not reached by the
  audit** rather than judged, and why an audit-side total quoted elsewhere may be smaller than the
  totals on this page. Where the two disagree, the JSON behind this page is the count of record for
  this slice.
* **No living person is accused of anything.** The claims summarised here are the compilers' and
  the auditors'. This page reports what their rows say, what the recovered position data does and
  does not confirm, and where the two disagree.
