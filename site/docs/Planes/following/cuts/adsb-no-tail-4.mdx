# The four rows that name no aircraft

**Where this came from.** On [the Following overview](/Planes/following/overview), the table that
breaks the register down by what the recovered position data says carries a line reading **"No
aircraft named"**. That cell is the count **4**. These are those four rows — every row of the
register that gives a date and a place but never names the airframe.

## What this slice is

A row lands here when the claim as published carries no tail number. There is no registration, no
ICAO hex, and therefore nothing to look up in any archive. This is not a row that was tested and
came back empty; it is a row that cannot be tested at all in its published form. To reproduce the
list from `overlaps.csv`, take every row whose date is present but whose following-tail field is
blank or recorded as UNKNOWN — two rows are blank and two are logged UNKNOWN, and all four resolve
to the same ADS-B verdict.

## What the specifics show

All four rows are Nebraska, split evenly between Lincoln (KLNK) and Omaha (KOMA), and all four
carry the `EXTRA-` prefix — none appears on the Candace Owens spreadsheet that supplies most of the
register. Their sourcing is the thinnest on the site: `EXTRA-002` rests on an Owens episode naming
an Omaha date alongside an Associated Press report of the rally; `EXTRA-001` rests on a single X
reply from @RealCandaceO stating that Charlie Kirk was in Lincoln on 14 September 2024; and
`EXTRA-007` and `EXTRA-004` both rest on accounts that amplified a Stew Peters post rather than on
any flight record. Because none names a tail number, the tracking-site audit never reached any of
them, which is why the audit column reads the same on all four.

Three of the four sit on a day this site can independently source a Charlie Kirk or Turning Point
appearance — but on two of those three the appearance is nowhere near the airport on the row.
`EXTRA-004` pairs an Omaha, Nebraska airport claim with a sourced Kirk appearance at the Tampa
Convention Center in Florida the same day, and `EXTRA-007` pairs Lincoln with a TPUSA student town
hall whose location is not recorded at all. Only `EXTRA-002` puts the claimed airport and the
sourced venue in the same city, Omaha, four miles apart. The remaining row, `EXTRA-001`, has no
sourced appearance that day; the nearest is one day earlier in Manhattan, Kansas. That makes this
the weakest four rows in the register on every axis at once: no airframe, no audit, and in three
of four cases no local event either.

## What this list cannot tell you

* These four rows are **untestable as published**, not disproven. No archive can be asked about an
  aircraft nobody named. If a tail number is ever attached to one of these dates, the row becomes
  testable and should be tested.
* Equally, nothing here shows any aircraft was present. A row with no tail is not evidence of a
  flight; it is a claim about a place and a date.
* A trace proves an aircraft was there. It never proves who was aboard — and on these four rows
  there is not even a trace, so the question of occupancy does not arise.
* **Erika Kirk's presence is established on zero rows of the register**, this slice included. One
  row here, `EXTRA-004`, is logged as Erika Kirk alone; her flight logs are
  [reported erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased) and no itinerary was ever published, so that
  cell records the compilers' assertion and nothing more.
* "Not reached by the audit" is a limit on the audit's scope, not a verdict against the row.
* Nothing here establishes that any person committed any offence. The sources named in Table C are
  the accounts that made the claims, attributed as they made them.
* Counts on this page come from the 85-row register. Where the overview page prints a figure drawn
  from the tracking-site audit instead, that audit worked from 78 rows and never covered these
  four, so the two totals are not directly comparable.
