# Rows the tracking-site audit called inaccurate

**Where this came from.** On [the Following overview](/Planes/following/overview), the audit table reports what an independent line-by-line check of the overlap sheet against paid flight-history archives found. Its largest cell reads **"Inaccurate — the jet was somewhere else, usually another continent — 44"**. This page is that cell, opened up: all 44 rows, with the audit's verdict and the recovered-position verdict printed side by side on every one of them.

## What this slice is

A row is in this list if the audit verdict recorded for it in `overlaps.csv` is *inaccurate* — the auditor re-read the tracking-site history for the named tail on the named date and reported the aircraft was not at the claimed field. That is a judgement about a tracking-site page, made by a person, using a paid subscription archive. It is not the same test as the ADS-B column beside it, which measures stored position traces this site pulled and kept. Reproducing the list takes one filter on `overlaps.csv`: keep every row whose audit verdict is `inaccurate`.

## What the specifics show

The most important thing on this page is that the second test does not confirm the first. Of the 44 rows the audit called inaccurate, recovered position data reaches a located answer on only **5**. It **agrees** on 2 of them — OWENS-050 on 12 May 2023, whose nearest recovered position is 7,034 km from KSTL, and OWENS-036 on 18 June 2025, at 10,140 km from KOMA. It **disagrees** on 3: OWENS-021 on 14 February 2024, corroborated in the air 0.8 km from KILG; OWENS-025 on 19 April 2024, corroborated **on the ground** 0.1 km from KPVU; and OWENS-041 on 10 September 2025, corroborated **on the ground** 1.2 km from KPVU. Both verdict columns stay on the page and neither is reconciled into the other.

OWENS-041 is the row a reader will care about most, and it is a disagreement rather than a confirmation. The audit marked the 10 September 2025 Provo row inaccurate; the trace this site recovered puts SU-BTT on the ground 1.2 km from KPVU on that date, and it is one of only two ground-position confirmations anywhere in this 44. It is also one of only two rows here that fall on a day this site can independently source a Kirk or Turning Point appearance nearby — the other is EXTRA-005, 25 August 2023, a sourced Charlie Kirk speaking appearance in Lincoln, Nebraska, on a row the position data records as *not heard*.

The remaining 39 rows return nothing that speaks to the audit either way: 34 are marked *Not heard* and 5 have *No archive coverage*. That is the honest state of this list — the audit's conclusion stands unchallenged on 39 of its 44 rows, and independently tested on 5.

The list is concentrated in the middle of the window and in five states. Kansas holds 12 rows and Wichita alone holds 12; Nebraska 10, Delaware 6, Utah 6 and Missouri 4 account for 38 of the 44 between them, with the remaining 6 scattered one apiece across California, Georgia, Illinois, Massachusetts, New York and one row logged across two states. 2024 is the heaviest year at 15 rows and 2022 the lightest at 4. Three dates carry two rows each — 12 January 2025, 15 March 2025 and 7 July 2025. Every row here names an aircraft: 34 SU-BTT and 10 SU-BND, with no unresolved tails.

The weakest part of this list, as with the register as a whole, is the person side: 38 of the 44 rest on Erika Kirk alone, 4 claim both Kirks, 1 claims Charlie Kirk alone, and 1 is matched to a Turning Point event with neither Kirk claimed present.

## What this list cannot tell you

* **A trace proves an aircraft was there. It never proves who was aboard.** That applies to the three rows on this page where position data contradicts the audit, including the two with the on-ground flag. They locate an airframe on a ramp and nothing more.
* **Erika Kirk's presence is established on zero rows.** Her flight logs are [reported erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased) and no itinerary for her was ever published. The 38 rows here that rest on her alone were never documented on her side, before or after the audit.
* **An absence in an archive is not evidence of absence, and it is not a confirmation of the audit either.** The 34 rows marked *Not heard* mean a volunteer receiver network recorded nothing for that airframe on that date. The 5 marked *No archive coverage* mean no free archive reaches that date at all. Both are limits of the archive — not deletions, not suppression, and not independent support for the audit's verdict.
* **"Inaccurate" is a verdict about a claim, not an accusation against a person.** The audit says a row misstated where an aircraft was. Nothing on this page establishes that anyone acted unlawfully, and no such conclusion should be drawn from it.
* **The denominators differ, and the overview says so.** The audit's own totals were stated against the **78** rows its author worked from; this register holds **85**. Of those 85, 5 were never published in any form and 5 more were added later and were never reached by the audit, leaving 75 rows carrying an audit verdict. The 44 counted here match the auditor's published figure but sit inside a larger register — the seven rows added on 24 August 2026 were reconstructed by this site and the auditor never saw them.
