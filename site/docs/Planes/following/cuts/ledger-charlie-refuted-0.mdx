# The Charlie/TPUSA rows position data refutes — there are none

**Where this came from.** On [the Following overview](/Planes/following/overview), the Charlie
Kirk and TPUSA ledger splits its 24 rows three ways by what recovered ADS-B position data says:
corroborated, refuted, and the rows the archives cannot answer. This page is the **refuted**
cell. It holds **0 rows**. The other two cells hold 13 and 11, and 13 + 0 + 11 = 24, so nothing
is missing here — the cell really is empty.

## What this slice is

A row joins the Charlie Kirk and TPUSA ledger when the compilers' sheet places Charlie Kirk on it,
places both Kirks on it, or ties it to a Turning Point USA event with neither Kirk named — 9, 9 and
6 rows respectively. A ledger row lands on **this** page only if the recovered position data for
the named aircraft, on the claimed date, puts that aircraft somewhere other than the claimed
airport — the verdict this project writes as `ELSEWHERE`. Across all 24 ledger rows that verdict
is returned **zero times**. To reproduce the list from `overlaps.csv`: take the rows whose
attendee class is `CHARLIE_ONLY`, `CHARLIE_AND_ERIKA` or `TPUSA_NO_KIRK`, then keep only those
whose ADS-B verdict is `ELSEWHERE`. Nothing survives.

## What the specifics show

The three tables above are empty on purpose. Every column heading is left in place so a reader can
see exactly what a row in this cell would have had to contain, and see that no row does.

An empty refuted cell is a real result, and it points in the direction the compilers would want.
On the Charlie Kirk and TPUSA side of the sheet, the position data we recovered never once caught
the named jet somewhere else on the claimed day. Where it could speak at all it agreed: 13 of the
24 ledger rows come back **At the claimed airport — corroborated**, and 8 of the 24 carry a
transmitted on-ground position rather than an overflight. Those 24 rows fall on 18 distinct dates
and, counted by row rather than by date, split 5 in 2023, 9 in 2024 and 10 in 2025, across seven
airport entries, most heavily KPVU (8 rows), KICT (7) and KLNK (4).

The result is much weaker than it first sounds, and the reason is arithmetic rather than argument.
Only 13 of the 24 rows got an answer at all. Eight came back **Not heard** and three name no
aircraft, so 11 of 24 — nearly half the ledger — were never in a position to be refuted. A test
that cannot fail on 11 rows has not passed on those 11 rows; it was not run. The honest sentence
is: *of the 13 Charlie/TPUSA rows the archives could actually adjudicate, 13 were corroborated and
0 were refuted.* That is a clean 13-for-13 on a small sample, not a 24-for-24 on the whole ledger.

The absence also cannot be read as the aircraft being somewhere else and hidden. `Not heard` and
`No archive coverage` are limits of the volunteer receiver networks and of what the free archives
retain — not deletions, and not evidence the aircraft was elsewhere.

## What this list cannot tell you

* **A trace locates an aircraft. It never places a person aboard.** Not one row in the parent
  ledger, and therefore not one row that could ever have appeared here, carries a passenger
  manifest, a boarding record, or any document naming who was on the jet.
* **Erika Kirk's presence is established on zero rows** anywhere in this register, including every
  row of the Charlie/TPUSA ledger that names her alongside Charlie. Her flight logs are
  [reported erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased) and no itinerary was ever published, so
  there is no document against which any of it can be checked.
* **An empty cell is not a verdict of innocence and not a verdict of guilt.** It records that one
  specific test — "does the position data put the jet somewhere else that day?" — returned nothing
  on this side of the sheet. It says nothing about intent, ownership, or why an Egyptian-registered
  jet was on a US ramp on any given date.
* **An absence in an archive is not evidence of absence.** The 11 undecided rows are undecided.
  Parked with the transponder off, outside receiver coverage, and a wrong claimed date all look
  identical from the outside.
* **Where the count here differs from the overview page**, this register is the newer of the two.
  The tracking-site audit worked from 78 rows; this register holds 85, of which 24 form the
  Charlie/TPUSA ledger. Four of those 24 were never reached by that audit at all. Where a figure
  on the overview and a figure here disagree, the JSON behind this page is the count of record and
  the difference is the audit's smaller starting set, not a changed finding.
* **No living person is accused of anything here.** The claims summarised are the compilers' and
  the auditors'; this page reports what their rows say and what the recovered position data does
  and does not confirm.
