# N102DZ Kirk's Plane

Tail number **N102DZ** is reported in public flight records as the aircraft associated with **[Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk)'s** travel into Utah on the morning of September 10, 2025. Reporting describes it as a larger private aircraft (15–16 passenger capacity) that flew from **Scottsdale, Arizona** into the Salt Lake City area ahead of his Utah Valley University appearance. This is the Kirk family's own aircraft, and it appears in the investigation mainly because it anchors the timeline of where Kirk and his party were in the hours before the event. What X accounts alleged about **Erika Kirk's use of this tail**, her on-camera invitation to check it, and the reported May 2026 FlightRadar24 removal is collected on **[Erika Kirk's flights](/Planes/following/Erika_Kirk_Flights)**.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** N102DZ
* **Type:** **Gulfstream Aerospace G-V (Gulfstream V)** — confirmed in the FAA Civil Aviation Registry. Commentary describing it as "a Gulfstream G5" was correct; commentary calling that characterization unconfirmed is now out of date.
* **Year built:** 1998 · **Serial number:** 555 · **Seats:** 20 in the FAA type record (commentary citing 15–16 passenger capacity was in the right range)
* **Registered owner:** Elderberry Chestnut LLC, Cheyenne, Wyoming — also listed on the registration: Sunny Blue Sky LLC and UST Enterprises LLC. **Naming a registered owner is not naming a wrongdoer**, and nothing here asserts anything improper by any of them.
* **Registration dates:** current certificate issued **16 October 2025**, last registry action **23 December 2025** — both after the assassination. Certificate reissues are ordinary events (sale, transfer between owner LLCs, renewal, address change) and are **not evidence of anything by themselves**; the dates are recorded because they fall inside the window in which researchers separately allege the aircraft's public flight history was removed.
* **Association:** Reported as Charlie Kirk's plane for the September 10 travel into Utah, and tied in reporting to Charlie Kirk, Erika Kirk, and [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview) travel logistics

## Flight history relevant to the case

Public flight records and reporting describe the day's movements in two sets that cover different legs:

* **Morning inbound (Sept 10):** Kirk's plane (tail **N102DZ**) is reported to have landed at **Salt Lake City International (SLC) around 9:30 a.m. MDT**, after departing **Scottsdale, Arizona**, for a scheduled interview before the UVU event.
* **Afternoon movements (Sept 10):** A separate set of records circulated by investigators lists N102DZ arriving into **Scottsdale at ~1:13 p.m. local**, departing again **immediately at ~1:13 p.m.**, and **landing into Provo (PVU) at ~3:31 p.m. local** — i.e., later movements on the same day after the shooting. **The "departed immediately at 1:13" half of that reading has since been shown to be a misread column** — see [the correction below](#the-recovered-flightradar24-record).
* **Provo ground movements (Sept 10):** According to investigators tracking the records, after the **~3:30 p.m.** Provo landing the aircraft is described as having **waited roughly 30 minutes at the gate** and then **taxied to Duncan Aviation**, the FBO on the field. They note that an Egyptian-registered jet, **[SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview)**, is reported to have still been on the Duncan tarmac with its power on at that time. This account is reproduced as reported; it has not been independently confirmed here.
* **Later California flights:** Some reporting describes N102DZ making subsequent flights to **Camarillo, California (CMA)**. Per user reports, portions of the day's flight data were later said to be harder to find on some public trackers.

The two record sets describe different legs of the day and are reproduced here as reported. **They have now been reconciled against a recovered primary record**, set out immediately below.

## The recovered FlightRadar24 record

Researchers reported that this tail number's FlightRadar24 history **was removed in May 2026**, after which no member of the public could pull it. **This site previously reported the HTTP 403 our scripts got back as documenting that removal, and that reading is retracted.** Opened in an ordinary browser on **24 August 2026** the FlightRadar24 page for N102DZ returns **HTTP 200** and renders its full identity block — **Gulfstream V**, type code **GLF5**, Mode S **A00C85**. The page itself has not been taken down. The **HTTP 403** our scripted probe recorded is a robot block, not a removal: the identical script got the identical 403 for **seven unrelated control aircraft** — among them a Ryanair 737, a Lufthansa A380 and Nike's corporate Gulfstream — for **all twelve of the other aircraft tails in this investigation**, and for **FlightRadar24's own home page and aircraft index**. Every one of those responses was the same body of roughly **5,877 bytes**. A response that is identical for the front page of the site cannot tell you anything about one aircraft. The flight table is empty for a different and equally ordinary reason — FlightRadar24 shows a logged-out visitor the **last seven days only** and paywalls everything older, and a private jet that has not flown in a week therefore shows nothing. Nike's Gulfstream, a control with no connection to this case, printed the same empty table on the same day. See [Flight Data Recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview) and [What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means).

**What that leaves the May 2026 removal claim as: REFUTED, with one narrow thing still out of reach.** The page is present and complete, and **an independent second browser session on the same day reproduced that result** across six probes — HTTP 200 every one. **"N102DZ was removed from FlightRadar24" is refuted; nothing was taken down.** The empty table is the free tier's seven-day window, and the control settles it: the unrelated aircraft **N628TS prints the identical "could not find data for specified flight" message at 53,318 bytes against N102DZ's 53,310**, while SU-BTT, SU-BND and a Ryanair control all render history because they had flown recently. What a page loading today **cannot** do is testify about what its table held in May 2026 — so **a narrower claim that history was specifically purged and later restored is beyond this method in either direction**, and this site does not assert it. A logged-out free tier is also the weakest view FlightRadar24 offers; a paid tier shows 90 days to 3 years and we have not looked through one. What the sweep does establish is set out further down: the **underlying position record was not destroyed**, whatever FlightRadar24 was or was not showing.

**The table it used to carry has been recovered.** The Internet Archive holds a copy of
`flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n102dz` captured at **11 September 2025, 05:57 UTC** — roughly
twenty hours after the assassination. It contains
FlightRadar24's flight-history table intact: **eleven rows covering 4–10 September 2025**, with
scheduled and actual times. The full recovery, the method, and the other aircraft it was run against
are on **[Flight Data Recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview)**.

The five September 10 legs, as FlightRadar24 published them. Times are **UTC** as the archived page
gave them; local times are computed alongside (Scottsdale MST = UTC−7, Utah MDT = UTC−6, California
PDT = UTC−7). For reference, the shooting occurred at approximately **12:20 MDT / 18:20 UTC**.

| From | To | Duration | Actual off (UTC) | Actual on (UTC) | Local |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scottsdale (SCF) | Salt Lake City (SLC) | 1:11 | 14:12 | 15:23 | 07:12 MST → **09:23 MDT** |
| Salt Lake City (SLC) | Scottsdale (SCF) | 1:13 | 19:00 | 20:13 | **13:00 MDT** → 13:13 MST |
| Scottsdale (SCF) | Provo (PVU) | 0:59 | 20:31 | 21:31 | 13:31 MST → **15:31 MDT** |
| Provo (PVU) | Camarillo (QTC) | 1:38 | 22:10 | 23:48 | 16:10 MDT → 16:48 PDT |
| Camarillo (QTC) | Provo (PVU) | 1:11 | 00:21 (11th) | 01:32 (11th) | 17:21 PDT → **19:32 MDT** |

**What this confirms.** The **~9:30 a.m. MDT arrival into Salt Lake City** is right — the recovered
record puts it at 09:23. The **~3:30 p.m. Provo landing** is right — 15:31. The **Camarillo,
California** legs are real, and there were two of them, out and back, on the evening of the 10th.

**What this corrects.** The reported *"arriving into Scottsdale at ~1:13 p.m., departing again
immediately at ~1:13 p.m."* is **a misreading of FlightRadar24's duration column.** The Salt Lake
City → Scottsdale leg has a flight **duration** of `1:13` and it **landed** at 13:13 Scottsdale
local — the same figure by coincidence. The aircraft's actual next departure was **13:31 MST**, an
**eighteen-minute turn**. A fast turnaround, and worth noting as one; not the zero-minute
turnaround that has been described.

**What it does not show.** It says where an airframe was. It says nothing about who was aboard any
leg, who arranged it, or why. The free FlightRadar24 page displayed only **seven days** of history,
so even this recovered copy never held the multi-year record the overlap spreadsheets were built
from.

The archived page also carries FlightRadar24's own identity fields — **Gulfstream V**, type code
**GLF5**, Mode S **A00C85**, operator "Private owner", page title `N102DZ - Gulfstream V [555]`.
That supports the "Gulfstream G5" characterization noted above, though it is a tracking-site
database entry rather than a registry record, and the FAA registry still outranks it.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

These are presented as open questions and reported allegations raised by independent investigators, not as conclusions. N102DZ is the Kirk family's own aircraft, and nothing here asserts wrongdoing by any person aboard.

* **The SLC-versus-Provo diversion.** On her November 17, 2025 podcast episode ("Operation Mocking-Plane: The Charlie Kirk Plot Thickens"), [**Candace Owens**](/People/candace-owens) flagged it as strange that Kirk landed at SLC — roughly 45 miles north of UVU — instead of the much closer **Provo Municipal Airport (PVU)**, about 7 miles from the campus. By Owens's account, Provo was also the airport where the Egyptian-registered jet [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) had been based. Owens frames this as a question about why Kirk's morning routing was longer than the obvious option, not as proof of anything.
* **Timeline-and-logistics scrutiny.** Independent investigators on podcasts and videos have built detailed timelines of the Kirk family's movements on and around September 10, raising questions about the aircraft's parking, runway exits, and how its movements may relate to Erika Kirk's reported activities after the event. They say their timelines are cross-referenced with public statements and flight-tracking data; some posted Flightradar24 screenshots that, per user reports, were later harder to find in some apps.
* **The Duncan Aviation link-up question.** Researcher **[@AnaEscobarShow](/Influencers/x/anaescobarshow)** has posed the Provo ground movements as an open question: *"Why did Charlie's plane (N102DZ) land in Provo at 3:30pm, wait for 30 mins at the gate and THEN make its way to Duncan Aviation to link up with the Egyptian plane (SU-BND) which was still there on and running? Was this the wait for Andrew?"* She credits the work of **[@baroncoleman](/Influencers/podcasts-baron)**, writing *"Barons timeline has helped me sm. I am going to go John-Cullen-level autist on the flight records"* — a reference to investigator [John Cullen](/Influencers/john-cullen)'s detailed flight-record method. These are framed by the researchers as questions about parking choices (the main terminal versus Duncan Aviation) and the alignment of the aircraft's arrival with the official timeline, not as findings of coordination by any person.

In response to such claims, Erika Kirk has reportedly provided her own flight logs and accounts of her movements as alibis. No public evidence ties the routing choices to any plan, and ordinary scheduling, airport-handling, and crew availability are equally consistent with the records. These remain open lines of inquiry.

## Flightradar24 screenshots and reported scrubbing

Much of the public timeline work on this aircraft rests on flight-tracking screenshots shared by X users. According to posts attributed to **[@hurtfeelingzday](/Influencers/x/hurtfeelingzday)**, extensive screenshots of N102DZ's flight data — captured from **Flightradar24** — were shared on X, including the **Scottsdale** and **Provo (PVU)** movements described above. These screenshots are the basis for several of the timestamps that independent investigators have cited.

Per user reports, portions of that flight data were later said to have been **scrubbed or made harder to find** on some public trackers and apps. This claim is reproduced as reported by X users and has not been independently confirmed here. A common, non-suspicious explanation is that commercial trackers honor the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list (see the tracking note at the bottom of this page), which can remove flights an owner or the government has asked to hide; investigators raise the disappearance of the data as an open question rather than as proof of anything.

Two parts of that question have now been separated, because they have different answers. **The page is still there** — FlightRadar24 serves it, HTTP 200, in a browser, in two independent sessions on 24 August 2026 — so **the removal claim is refuted** and **the archived copy of the table it used to carry has been recovered in full**. What no method here can reach is **what that page displayed in May 2026**, in either direction. The scripted 403 that this site once cited as proof of a removal turns out to be site-wide bot protection that returns the same body for FlightRadar24's own front page. See [the recovered record above](#the-recovered-flightradar24-record), the control test in [the primary-data sweep](#the-24-august-2026-primary-data-sweep), and the status block below.

## The 24 August 2026 primary-data sweep

Everything above this line rests on screenshots, archived tracking pages and reporting about them.
This section is different: it is what the aircraft's own **position data** says. It was pulled on
**24 August 2026** from free public archives that anyone can query without an account, and every raw
response is saved in this repo with the source named in the filename.

The short version: **the record was not destroyed, and the page was not removed.** Our *scripted*
method returned no answer at all on the removal question — and this site previously said it did — but
a real browser session, run twice and independently, **refutes the removal**. What remains beyond
reach is what that page displayed in May 2026, in either direction.

### The control test, and the correction it forced

The reasoning this site published was: the Internet Archive holds a populated copy of the
FlightRadar24 page, the same URL returns HTTP 403 to the public today, therefore something was
removed. That reasoning required a control, and the control was run.

| Probed on 24 August 2026 | Result |
|---|---|
| `flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n102dz` | **HTTP 403** |
| The twelve other aircraft tails tracked in this investigation | **HTTP 403, every one** |
| Seven unrelated control aircraft with no connection to this case | **HTTP 403, every one** |
| `flightradar24.com` — FlightRadar24's own home page | **HTTP 403** |
| `flightradar24.com/data/aircraft` — the aircraft index | **HTTP 403** |

Every one of those responses is the same body of roughly **5,877 bytes**. FlightRadar24 returns 403
to scripted requests across its entire site, its own front page included. That is bot protection. It
is not about any aircraft, and it cannot tell a removed record from a present one.

**So the scripted probe answers nothing — neither confirming nor refuting.** Our script simply cannot
see the question, and a control test is what showed us that. Deciding it needed a real browser
session.

**That session was run, and then re-run independently, and the removal claim is REFUTED.** In a real
logged-out browser on 24 August 2026 the FlightRadar24 page for N102DZ returns **HTTP 200** with page
title `N102DZ - Gulfstream V [555] - Flightradar24`, MODE S **A00C85**, TYPE CODE **GLF5**, and
**nothing on it says removed, blocked, restricted or unavailable.** Sixteen case tails and four
unrelated controls all returned 200 in the first run; a second, independent session the same day
reproduced it across six more probes. **The page was not taken down.** Its empty history table is the
**seven-day free-tier window**, proved by the control: the unrelated aircraft **N628TS prints the
identical "could not find data for specified flight" message at 53,318 bytes against N102DZ's
53,310**, while SU-BTT, SU-BND and a Ryanair control render history because they had flown recently.
Evidence: `site/docs/Planes/following/apis/browser_capture/captures/fr24_page_availability_2026-08-24.tsv`
and `.../fr24_n102dz_removal_test_2026-08-24b.tsv`.

**The limit, stated with the refutation every time.** A page that loads today cannot testify about
what its table held in **May 2026**. **"The page was taken down" is refuted; a narrower
purge-and-restore claim is beyond this method in either direction** and this site does not assert it.
A logged-out free tier is also the weakest view FlightRadar24 offers — a paid tier shows 90 days to
3 years, and we have not looked through one.

This site has published that no 2022 date could be tested. That is now out of date. The ADS-B
Exchange **monthly sample** publishes one free full day of global traces for the **first of every
month back to 2016**, and it holds three of them for this tail. Each trace self-identifies:
registration `N102DZ`, ICAO hex `A00C85`, type `GLF5`, "GULFSTREAM 5", year 1998 — matching the FAA
record quoted at the top of this page.

| Date sampled | What the trace shows | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **1 May 2022** | DeKalb-Peachtree, Atlanta (**KPDK**, 0.82 km) outbound toward Great Harbour Cay, Bahamas; the last fix is **35.43 km short of the field**, so the sample does not contain the landing. Max altitude 40,025 ft. | 377 |
| **1 June 2022** | DeKalb-Peachtree, Atlanta (**KPDK**, 1.03 km) → **Teterboro, New Jersey** (KTEB, 0.68 km). | 583 |
| **1 November 2022** | **Van Nuys, California** (KVNY, 17.84 km at first fix) → **Palo Alto** (KPAO, 2.40 km). | 766 |

Two caveats belong with every citation of these rows. **This is one day per month, always the 1st** —
three sampled days establish that the aircraft was flying these routes in 2022; they cannot support a
rate, a frequency, or a pattern. And **the distances are geometry, not landing records**: 0.82 km

That is why the kilometre figure is printed every time.

One further detail worth recording plainly. The 2022 traces carry a community-database owner string
of **"WILMINGTON TRUST CO TRUSTEE"**, not the FAA registrant named at the top of this page. Community
databases routinely carry a former owner, a trustee, or a management company; **the FAA registry is
the record and it outranks them**. Owner trusts and holding companies are ordinary in business
aviation, and this site does not go behind one to name private individuals.

### The counterargument, and the limit on all of it

The innocent reading of this entire section is the straightforward one, and it is well supported.
FlightRadar24 blocks scripts as a matter of commercial policy and paywalls history beyond seven days
as a matter of business model; an owner may lawfully enrol an aircraft in the FAA's
[LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) programme; volunteer receiver networks have coverage holes
that come and go; archives change what date ranges they serve. **Every observation on this page is
consistent with a private aircraft flying an ordinary schedule while commercial trackers behave
commercially.** Nothing here establishes that anything was suppressed, and the one removal claim that
would have mattered is the one we could not test.

And the limit that governs the whole section: **this data locates an airframe. It does not locate a
person.** A position report says a Gulfstream V with hex A00C85 was at a set of coordinates at a
timestamp. It says nothing about who was aboard, who booked the leg, who paid for it, or why. Charlie
Kirk, Erika Kirk, and every crew member and passenger are absent from this record entirely. Where

## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — N102DZ](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n102dz) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.
* [ADS-B Exchange — N102DZ](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/?reg=N102DZ) — volunteer-fed, unfiltered tracker that does **not** honor LADD, so it often shows blocked aircraft and tracks the filtered sites leave out.

## Dates we looked for and did not find

Not every claimed N102DZ flight could be checked against a primary ADS-B trace. **Every date we queried and got nothing back for is listed — with the exact HTTP status and the reason we looked — on [ADS-B gaps for N102DZ](/Planes/N102DZ/data/adsb/MISSING_DATA).** That page is published for the same reason the recovered traces are: a gap that is written down can be argued with, and a gap that is quietly dropped cannot.

**An absence there is not a finding.** A 404 means a volunteer receiver network heard nothing on that UTC day — parked and silent, outside coverage, or a wrong claimed date all come first, and [what a 403 means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means) sets out why a refusal and a deletion look identical from outside. The four free archives that did produce data, and the control test that separates a retention boundary from a removal, are on [Flight data recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview) and [per-aircraft recovery status](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Per-Aircraft-Status).
