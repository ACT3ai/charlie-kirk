# SU-BND Blue Plane

An Egyptian-registered **Gulfstream** (tail number SU-BND) — referred to on [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens)'s podcast as **"the blue plane"** — is reported to have flown into Provo, Utah in spring 2025 and stayed for nearly four months, still parked there in the window around [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk)'s September 10, 2025 assassination. According to the notes circulating in public coverage, its **transponder was switched ON but it did not take off** at the key moment. It is the companion aircraft to [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) ("the yellow plane") in what X researchers call the **"Israel/Egyptian Plane Armada"**. SU-BND is the strongest-documented of the secondary Israel/Egyptian jets — but it is also the tail for which the **innocent maintenance explanation is strongest**, and that counterpoint is laid out prominently below. Every claim here comes from independent open-source researchers working from public ADS-B data; little of it has been independently verified.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** SU-BND
* **Type:** Gulfstream (described in coverage as a **Gulfstream G550**; some notes also reference Gulfstream-class / Gulfstream IV airframes within the broader Israel/Egyptian fleet — one compilation specifically identifies it as a **Gulfstream IV-SP, serial number 1332, built 2014**, a designation reported by trackers and not independently confirmed)
* **Registry / operator:** Egyptian-registered — described as Israel/Egyptian Government / Israel/Egyptian Air Force use
* **Nickname in coverage:** "The blue plane" (Candace Owens's color-coded shorthand; SU-BTT was "the yellow plane")
* **Base in Utah window:** Provo Municipal Airport (PVU), ~7 miles from Utah Valley University, where Duncan Aviation runs a full-service maintenance hangar
* **Part of a larger fleet:** Trackers say SU-BND is one of **at least five Israel/Egyptian Air Force jets** (alongside SU-BTT, [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview), [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview), and [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview)) that they allege cycled in and out of Provo around the event — the cluster X researchers call the "Israel/Egyptian Plane Armada". The fleet count is the trackers' claim, not an official tally.

## Flight history relevant to the case

The dated bullets below reflect the published timelines of independent trackers, amplified by Candace Owens. They have not been independently confirmed and are reported here as claims:

* **May 23–25, 2025:** SU-BND is reported to have flown into Provo and then **stayed there for nearly four months** — trackers describe local "Provo locals" test flights around May 25.
* **Through summer 2025:** The aircraft reportedly remained parked at Provo across the entire window leading up to September 10.
* **September 10, 2025 (day of the assassination):** Per the notes, SU-BND's **transponder was turned ON but the aircraft did not take off** at the key moment, in contrast to SU-BTT, which departed. One account describes this "second Egyptian plane" as having **left roughly three hours after the assassination**, while other logs place its outbound legs days later.
* **September 11, 2025:** A separate compilation states that SU-BND sat on the ground at Provo **with power and transponder on the entire day of September 10** and then **departed on September 11** — the day after the assassination.
* **The N102DZ "link-up" claim:** Some trackers allege that **[N102DZ](/Planes/N102DZ/overview) — a jet associated in coverage with the Kirk party — reportedly "linked up" with SU-BND at Duncan Aviation** after N102DZ's own landing at Provo at roughly **3:30 PM** on the day in question. The claim is attributed on X to **[@AnaEscobarShow](/Influencers/x/anaescobarshow)**, who is quoted describing SU-BND as **"still there on and running"** when N102DZ arrived. This is an unverified OSINT reading of ground positions and aircraft states at a shared maintenance ramp; two aircraft parked at the same MRO facility is exactly what a busy service hub looks like, and no record ties the two tails together operationally.
* **September 13, 2025:** Reported departure Provo → **Goose Bay → Paris → Cairo** (described by trackers as the "lag plane" that stayed for months).

These accounts of when SU-BND left — "three hours after," a September 11 departure, and a September 13 departure — are not consistent with one another in the source material, and all are reported here as competing claims rather than settled fact.

## The "transponder ON only three times" screenshot claim

The most heavily shared SU-BND item is a single screenshot circulated on X with the caption that **"this Egyptian plane doesn't move for days and the transponder ONLY goes on THESE 3 TIMES."** The screenshot poster lines those three transponder pings up against three arrivals on September 10:

* **10:05 AM** — when **Erika Kirk** allegedly lands at Provo.
* **1:40 PM** — when a man the screenshot names only as **"Andrew"** allegedly lands at Scottsdale, Arizona, in what the poster captions as a pickup of Erika Kirk's cell phone.
* **2:29 PM** — when **[Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson)** allegedly lands at Provo.

This is an **unverified screenshot claim**, not an established sequence of events, and it should be read with strong caution. The "Andrew picks up Erika Kirk's phone in Scottsdale" element in particular is an accusatory assertion about living private individuals that no source has corroborated; it is reported here only as the content of a referenced screenshot, and no wrongdoing by any named person is established or implied. Several skeptical counterpoints apply directly:

* The "transponder only on three times" framing assumes the pings are meaningful signals. A parked aircraft on ground power, or one being repositioned on the ramp by maintenance crews, can produce intermittent transponder activity for entirely mundane reasons — the same innocent reading that applies to the broader "transponder ON, no takeoff" detail.
* The timestamps mix arrivals at two different airports (Provo and Scottsdale) and depend on the screenshot's own time labels, which other reviewers have flagged across the Israel/Egyptian-plane material as prone to **UTC-versus-local-time errors**.
* No public record ties SU-BND's ground activity to any phone, person, or vehicle; the linkage exists only in the screenshot's captions.

## Liz Wheeler's "narrowed parameters" rebuttal

Commentator **[Liz Wheeler](/People/liz-wheeler)** has publicly challenged the overlap math behind SU-BND and SU-BTT, arguing the tallies rely on **loose criteria** — counting a "match" when an Israel/Egyptian jet was within a broad region (tens of miles) and within a few days of one of Erika Kirk's locations, rather than the same airport on the same day. Per Wheeler, **"when you narrow the parameters to Egyptian planes landing on the same day in the same location, the overlap dwindles to one — on 9/10 in Utah."** Her rebuttal is reported here for balance; it disputes the methodology behind the 68- and 73-overlap counts above rather than the raw ADS-B data itself.

## Long-term tracking pattern (2022–2025)

Beyond the September window, Owens and independent trackers group SU-BND with SU-BTT as **"two Egyptian Air Force intelligence jets"** that they allege shadowed the Kirk family for years:

* Their tallies claim the two jets logged roughly **73 documented overlaps with Erika Kirk** across the United States — Omaha, Wichita, Provo, plus domestic and international legs.
* A separate **dual-plane count** circulated on X claims the blue (SU-BND) and yellow (SU-BTT) jets *together* overlapped with Erika Kirk's locations **68 times** between 2022 and September 2025 — including overseas trips — and that **29** of those overlaps also coincided with Charlie Kirk's location. This 68/29 figure differs from the 73-overlap tally above; both are independent researchers' claims, not reconciled numbers.
* Trackers also note that the Israel/Egyptian jets **routinely fly in and out of Israel**, which they fold into theories of the fleet monitoring Erika Kirk, the wider family, or [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview) events. The Israel-routing claim rests on the same public ADS-B records and is reported here as an allegation.
* Trackers point to public ADS-B records they say show the aircraft appearing near the family's locations with unusual frequency over **2022 through 2025**.

As with SU-BTT, these claims rest entirely on public ADS-B screenshots circulated by independent researchers and have not been independently confirmed.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

Researchers covering SU-BND have floated several interpretations, framed here as open theories rather than conclusions:

* **Foreign surveillance / "intel vacuum"** — that a foreign-government aircraft may have been monitoring Charlie Kirk, Erika Kirk, or TPUSA events. Owens's framing describes the Israel/Egyptian jets as "Deep State vacuums" harvesting phones, faces, and plates at rallies.
* **A staged or support role on Sept 10** — built on the detail that the transponder was powered up but the aircraft never left, which some readers interpret as a standby or signaling role and others as simple ground-power during maintenance.
* **Drone association** — the counter-UAS testing claims attached to [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) have been extended by some posters to the fleet generally, SU-BND included. Nothing tail-specific has been published to support that extension, and no drone claim on this site rests on this aircraft.

## Counterpoints and innocent explanations

Several facts cut against the sinister reading of SU-BND specifically:

* **Provo is a legitimate jet-service hub.** Duncan Aviation operates a full-service MRO facility at Provo Municipal Airport — airframe, engine, avionics, paint, and interior work. A foreign VIP Gulfstream parking there for an extended service is not inherently suspicious. (Duncan's local connections have been noted in coverage, including reporting that a former Duncan Aviation VP, Tom Burt, is tied by family relationship to figures discussed in the case — a detail offered without any established wrongdoing.)
* **The four-month stay fits maintenance, not an op.** The companion "yellow plane" SU-BTT moved in and out quickly; SU-BND did the opposite, which is the hallmark of a heavy-maintenance or storage booking.
* **A skeptic's audit disputed the overlap counts.** At least one reviewer who pulled a year of FlightRadar history said **"5 of the 8" overlaps Owens cited were wrong** — claiming the plane "hadn't even left Egypt" on some of the listed dates — and noted that **Erika Kirk herself said she stayed home in Arizona** on Sept 10 rather than traveling. That reviewer found that the SU-BND-specific entries on May 23 and one other date were accurate, but could not source one of the July dates at all.
* **Erika Kirk has disputed the overlap claims.** In later interviews and public statements, Erika Kirk reportedly pushed back on the narrative that the Israel/Egyptian jets shadowed her movements, including saying she stayed home in Arizona on Sept 10. Her denial is reported here for fairness; the overlap tallies remain the trackers' claims and have not been confirmed.
* **Transponder ON, no takeoff** is exactly what a parked aircraft running ground power or being moved on the ramp would show — a mundane reading at least as plausible as a standby-signal interpretation.

### Where that leaves this aircraft

Set out from the record rather than from either narrative, three things are established and one is not, and it is worth separating them before the reader goes any further down the page.

**Established.** SU-BND was on the Provo ramp continuously from **23 May to 13 September 2025** — 113 days, two Provo→Provo sorties in the whole of it — and the [primary traces recovered for this tail](#the-primary-flight-data-we-hold-on-this-aircraft-and-where-it-came-from) put it at Provo on **19 April 2024, 23 May 2025 and 9–13 September 2025**. Provo is where its operator's aircraft go: **Duncan Aviation has held an Egyptian Air Force maintenance account since 1999**, a contract documented with sources on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview). And the airframe was **27 years old** (MSN 1332, 1998 build), which is the age at which a Gulfstream IV goes in for a heavy check.

**Established, and pointing the other way.** On **10 September 2025** this aircraft's transponder was up, seven miles from Utah Valley University, and it never left the ground — and the page's [departure sequence](#the-departure-sequence-and-the-19-hour-question) section shows the public accounts of when it finally did leave do not agree with one another. Neither the maintenance record nor the flight record explains that day, and this page does not pretend either does.

**Not established, by anyone.** No dated Duncan work order, invoice or release-to-service for SU-BND has ever been published. No crew list or passenger record exists for the 113 days. No authorisation for a foreign air force's recurring use of a non-international US municipal airport has been produced. The gap in the record is not between two competing readings — it is between what ADS-B can see and what only documents can settle, and the documents are exactly what nobody outside the operator, the shop and the US government can obtain. That is the finding this page actually supports, and it is the reason the [Charlie Kirk Investigation Laws](/Fix/overview) matter more here than any further argument about the trace.

## Open questions

1. Why did SU-BND remain parked at Provo for nearly four months in 2025, and what work order or contract covered the stay?
2. Who were the crew and any passengers associated with the aircraft during the Provo period?
3. Which departure account is correct — a departure roughly three hours after the assassination, a September 11 departure, or the September 13 Provo → Goose Bay → Paris → Cairo routing?
4. Do the "73 overlaps" with Erika Kirk survive a documentary audit, given the skeptic's claim that several cited dates are wrong?
5. Do the 113 days on the Provo ramp reconcile with **dated** Duncan Aviation work orders and release-to-service records — and do those records cover the two Provo→Provo sorties and the 10 September transponder activity, or is either outside them?

| [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview) | Gulfstream IV | 010070 | 1048 | Egypt Goverment *(sic)* | *(none)* | — |

Two things follow, and they pull in opposite directions:

* **SU-BND is not listed as a government or air-force aircraft at all.** Flightradar24's database records it as a **private owner** with no operator entry. Everything on this page and elsewhere that describes it as "an Egyptian military plane" is using the **SU-** registration prefix (Egypt's civil registration block) plus its company in the Provo cluster as the basis for that description, not a registry entry. That is a meaningful weakening of the "foreign military aircraft" framing as applied to this specific airframe.
* **But its hex code sits inside the same block.** 01003E, 010070, 0101D0, 0101D1 and 0101D3 are all in Egypt's allocated ICAO 24-bit range, and the three Falcon 7Xs are consecutive serials (239, 243, 246) delivered 2013–2015 — a fleet purchase. SU-BND and SU-BGM are the older Gulfstreams. So the five are plainly related as a fleet even though the database describes two of them differently.

The honest formulation is that **SU-BND is an Egyptian-registered Gulfstream IV-SP of 1998 vintage whose public database entry does not identify a government operator.** A 27-year-old Gulfstream is also, bluntly, the kind of aircraft that generates long maintenance downtime — which matters for what follows.

## The full Provo stay: 113 days, and only three flights in all of it

The same export gives SU-BND's complete movements around its Utah stay. This is the record that the "four months at a regional airport" claim rests on, and it is more striking in full than in summary:

| Date | From | To | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 22 May 2025 | Cairo (CAI) | Paris–Le Bourget (LBG) | 4:18 |
| 23 May 2025 | Paris (LBG) | Goose Bay (YYR) | 5:48 |
| 23 May 2025 | Goose Bay (YYR) | Minot (MOT) | 3:44 |
| 23 May 2025 | Minot (MOT) | **Provo (PVU)** | 1:52 |
| **10 Jun 2025** | **Provo (PVU)** | **Provo (PVU)** | *(no duration logged)* |
| **05 Sep 2025** | **Provo (PVU)** | **Provo (PVU)** | **1:08** |
| 13 Sep 2025 | **Provo (PVU)** | Goose Bay (YYR) | 5:10 |
| 13 Sep 2025 | Goose Bay (YYR) | Paris (LBG) | 5:09 |
| 15 Sep 2025 | Paris (LBG) | Cairo (CAI) | 4:08 |

**Arrival 23 May, departure 13 September: 113 days on the ground at a regional airport in Utah.** In that entire span the aircraft logged exactly **two** flights, both of them Provo→Provo round-robins that landed where they started — one on **10 June** and one on **5 September**.

That record is the strongest single fact on this page, and it is genuinely double-edged:

* **It argues hard for maintenance.** A jet that sits for nearly four months and flies twice is not conducting operations. It is being worked on, or waiting for parts, or in storage. A 1998 Gulfstream IV in for a heavy check plus paint plus an avionics upgrade would look exactly like this. Duncan Aviation's Provo facility does precisely that work.
* **But the 5 September flight is 1 hour 8 minutes long and lands back at Provo.** That is not a five-minute post-maintenance run-up. It is a substantial sortie flown five days before the assassination by an aircraft that had not moved in nearly three months, and it is the flight described elsewhere on this page as a possible corridor run north of Provo. A maintenance test flight after a heavy check is entirely normal and often runs about an hour. So is a survey pattern. The duration alone distinguishes nothing — but the fact that this was one of only two flights in 113 days is why it draws attention at all.

The 10 June round-robin is logged with **no duration recorded**, meaning the tracker captured a departure and an arrival without a continuous track — the sort of gap that produces both innocent explanations (poor low-altitude coverage in the valley) and less innocent ones. It is unresolved.

## The departure sequence, and the 19-hour question

The export's timings for the September 13 departure are worth setting out because they are the source of the "19-hour delay" claim:

| Leg | Scheduled (STD) | Actual (ATD) | Arrival (STA) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Provo → Goose Bay | 6:07 PM | **12:57 PM** | 9:07 PM | Landed 9:07 PM |
| Goose Bay → Paris | 9:10 PM | 9:57 PM | 7:06 AM | Landed 8:05 AM |
| Paris → Cairo (15 Sep) | 2:26 PM | 10:18 AM | 2:26 PM | Landed 2:26 PM |

Note that the tracker's scheduled and actual columns are not necessarily in the same time reference, which is a well-known reading hazard on these exports and the source of more than one erroneous claim in this investigation. Taken at face value the Provo departure is *early* against its scheduled time rather than late. Any assertion built on a delay at this airport should be checked against the raw track before it is relied on.

Note too that the export dates the departure to **13 September**, whereas the independent analysis quoted elsewhere on this page gives **14 September**. One of the two is wrong by a day, and neither has been reconciled against the raw ADS-B archive.

## Why "the plane that stayed" matters more than the plane that left

Public attention went to [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) because it departed on the morning of the assassination. The record above suggests the more interesting aircraft may be this one, for a reason that has nothing to do with drama:

**SU-BND was on the ground at Provo for the entire period in which every other event on this site occurred.** It arrived nearly four months before the assassination and left three days after it. Every arrival and departure of every other Egyptian jet in the cluster — [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview) through 16 April, [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) in April, [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview) in late May, SU-BTT in September — happened at an airport where this aircraft was already parked.

That makes SU-BND the fixed point of the pattern rather than a participant in it, and it raises a question the shadowing framing does not ask: **if a foreign fleet were rotating aircraft through Provo for maintenance, one airframe sitting there long-term is exactly what you would expect to see, and it would draw the others in.** A four-month heavy check on one aircraft is a reason for the operator's other aircraft to use the same facility. The alternative reading — that a long-term parked aircraft was a standing platform of some kind — requires evidence about what was aboard it, and no such evidence has been published.


## The 113-day Provo stay, tested against the TPUSA event calendar

SU-BND is the hardest tail to test this way, because it did not move. It landed at Provo on **23 May 2025** and left on **13 September 2025** — one continuous 113-day ground window with two local sorties in the middle of it. Any co-location test therefore asks a different question for this aircraft than for the Falcons: not *did it follow him*, but *what was happening while it sat there*.

The calendar used is `site/docs/Planes/following/tpusa_events.csv` — **139 dated rows, 4 January 2022 to 29 October 2025**, built from TPUSA postings, campus press and event listings, each row flagged `EVENT_DATE`, `PODCAST_PROXY`, `SCHEDULED_NOT_HELD` or `AFTER_DEATH`. It is the spine of the [Following section](/Planes/following/overview).

### The one ground window

| On the ground | Airport | Nearest TPUSA event **in Utah**, any year | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **23 May – 13 Sep 2025** (113 days) | **Provo (PVU), UT** | **10 Sep 2025 — Charlie, Orem, UT — The American Comeback Tour, Utah Valley University** | **0 days** |

The gap is zero — but only because the window is four months long. **The Utah event fell on day 111 of 113.** Read the other way, SU-BND was parked seven miles from Utah Valley University for **110 days during which no TPUSA event took place anywhere in Utah**, and it was still parked there on the day one did.

**Eleven TPUSA events in eleven weeks, in Texas, Florida, California, South Carolina, Korea and Japan — and the aircraft did not move for any of them.** Including the one event in the whole window that this repository can place **Erika Kirk** at: the June Young Women's Leadership Summit in Grapevine, Texas. SU-BND was on the ground in Utah, 1,000 miles away, for all three days of it.

This is the single most important thing on this page for anyone assessing the shadowing claim, and it points in an uncomfortable direction for that claim:

* **A stationary aircraft cannot follow anyone.** For 113 days SU-BND had exactly one location. Every "overlap" credited to it in that period is an artifact of Erika Kirk being alleged to have passed through Utah, not of the aircraft going anywhere.
* **It is also why the maintenance reading is strongest here.** A jet that sits for four months and flies twice — both times Provo→Provo — is in a hangar, not on a mission. The [SU-BND registry panel](#the-registry-panel--and-why-this-aircraft-is-different-from-the-other-four) puts the airframe at **27 years old** (MSN 1332, 1998 build), which is exactly the age at which a Gulfstream IV goes in for a heavy check, paint and an avionics refit.
* **And it is why the one date still matters.** Whatever explains the other 110 days, on **10 September 2025** this aircraft's transponder came on, seven miles from UVU, and it did not take off. That is on this page's [departure sequence](#the-departure-sequence-and-the-19-hour-question) section, and this table does not explain it away.

### The limits of this test

* **This tests Charlie's side, not Erika's.** Of 139 rows, exactly **one** places Erika Kirk at a TPUSA event before 10 September 2025. The 68-and-73-overlap tallies were measured against *her* claimed locations, and her flight logs are reported erased — see [Erika's Flight Logs Erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased). This table cannot refute those tallies. It can only report that **no public source reproduces them**.
* **The calendar records public events only.** Private travel is invisible to it.
* **The window is the aircraft's, not a person's.** A 113-day window will collide with almost anything; the honest number here is not "one overlap" but "one overlap out of 111 possible days before it happened."

Where it confirms a claim above, it says so. Where it corrects one — including where it corrects this site — it says so just as plainly.

### The 2022 traces — and a correction to this site

**This site has published, in more than one place, that "the archive does not reach 2022; every 2022 claim rests on a screenshot." That is no longer true, and we are correcting it here.**

`samples.adsbexchange.com/traces/` publishes a free full-day globe sample for the **first of every month back to 2016**, with no account and no key. It has produced two pre-2023 days for SU-BND, each from a trace carrying its own registration in the payload:

| Sampled day | Points | What the trace shows |
|---|---|---|
| **2022-09-01** | 989 | **At 41,000 ft over Bremen, Indiana at 00:00:18 UTC** — crossing the United States eastbound — then on the ground at **Paris-Le Bourget at about 09:30 UTC** |
| **2022-01-01** | 35 | Egypt only — Capital International, New Cairo, to Ras Sedr |

What that does and does not do:

* **It puts SU-BND over the continental United States on 1 September 2022, eastbound**, and then across the Atlantic to Paris the same day. The trackers' window for this pattern starts around 2022. For this tail, a 2022 United States transit is now on record from primary position data rather than from a screenshot.
* **It is an overflight, not a stop.** 41,000 feet over Indiana is a nearest-airport label attached to cruise altitude. Nobody was picked up or dropped off at Birkey Private Airport, Bremen. The trace establishes that the aircraft was crossing the US that night and nothing more, and it should never be cited as a landing.
* **The 1 January 2022 day is Egypt only** — 35 points, a domestic hop. It is included because a day that shows nothing is part of the record too.

**The sampling caveat is not optional and must travel with both dates.** This is **one day per month, the 1st, and nothing else.** One of the two sampled 2022 days puts SU-BND over the United States. **Two sampled days cannot support a rate.** They establish that the aircraft transited US airspace in 2022. They say nothing about how often.

### The September 10, 2025 transponder windows, from primary data

The most-shared item on this page is [the screenshot claiming the transponder "ONLY goes on THESE 3 TIMES"](#the-transponder-on-only-three-times-screenshot-claim) on 10 September 2025, at **10:05 AM**, **1:40 PM** and **2:29 PM**. That section says it is an unverified screenshot claim and should be read with strong caution. The raw track for that day lets the timing half of it be checked directly, and the result is precise.

Both archives hold the day and record **the same 257-to-328 position samples in two blocks**, with nothing at all in between:

| Block | UTC | Local (MDT) | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First transponder window** | 16:05:35 → 17:34:09 | **10:05:35 → 11:34:09 AM** | 1 h 28 min |
| *(nothing recorded)* | 17:34 → 19:40 | 11:34 AM → 1:40 PM | 2 h 06 min of silence |
| **Second transponder window** | 19:40:53 → 20:29:38 | **1:40:53 → 2:29:38 PM** | 48 min |

**The screenshot's three times are confirmed to the minute, and one thing about them is corrected.** 10:05 AM is the start of the first window, 1:40 PM is the start of the second, and 2:29 PM is **the end of the second** — not a third ping. So the day contains **two transponder windows, not three events**. Two independently operated archives record identical block boundaries.

**And the aircraft did not take off.** Every fix in both windows is tagged **on the ground** except a single sample at 20:29:19 UTC reading 4,525 ft barometric — which is essentially Provo's own field elevation of 4,497 ft, i.e. one sample where the ground bit dropped out, not a departure. Both archives carry that same anomalous sample, which is a useful reminder that these two networks share upstream feeders and are not fully independent of one another.

**What that settles, and what it emphatically does not.** It settles that SU-BND's transponder was live on the Provo ramp in two windows on 10 September 2025, one of which began at 10:05 AM local and one of which ended at 2:29 PM local, and that the aircraft never moved. It settles nothing about the screenshot's *interpretation* of those times — the claim that they line up with Erika Kirk landing, with a named private individual collecting a phone in Scottsdale, or with [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) landing. **No public record ties this aircraft's ground activity to any person, phone or vehicle**, that linkage exists only in the screenshot's captions, and the accusatory element about living private individuals remains uncorroborated by anything. Tyler Robinson is charged, not convicted.

The innocent reading of the two windows is unchanged and is at least as strong: a parked aircraft on ground power, or one being repositioned on a maintenance ramp, produces exactly this pattern.

Files: `SU-BND_2025-09-10_adsb-lol_trace_full.json` and `SU-BND_2025-09-10_airplanes-live_trace_full.json`.

### Two more page questions the primary data answers

**The 5 September sortie was 1 hour 8 minutes and it reached 45,025 feet.** The export above logs a Provo→Provo round-robin on 5 September with a block time of **1:08**, and this page describes it as possibly a corridor run north of Provo. The raw track gives it exactly: **airborne 18:06:12 UTC, back on the ground 19:14:00 UTC — 1 hour 8 minutes, matching the export precisely — with a maximum recorded altitude of 45,025 ft.** That is the certified ceiling of a Gulfstream IV-SP. A flight that climbs straight to the aircraft's ceiling and comes home is the classic shape of a **post-maintenance certification check**, and it is a poor fit for a low-level survey pattern. This does not close the question — an aircraft can do anything at altitude — but the "corridor run" reading now has a specific number to answer.

**The departure was 13 September, not 14 September.** [The departure-sequence section](#the-departure-sequence-and-the-19-hour-question) records that the export dates the outbound leg to 13 September while an independent analysis quoted on this page gives 14 September, and notes that neither had been reconciled against the raw archive. It can be reconciled now. On **12 September** the aircraft is on the ground at Provo all day (13:37 to 22:17 UTC, no airborne fix). On **13 September** the track begins at **18:57:40 UTC already climbing through 4,500 ft**, 0.62 km from Provo, and ends at **41,000 ft at 21:34:06 UTC** on an eastbound track; by **14 September** it is inbound to Paris-Le Bourget. **The 13 September date is correct and the 14 September date is wrong by a day.**

### The counterargument

**Duncan Aviation has held an Egyptian Air Force maintenance contract since 1999, with plants at Lincoln, Nebraska and Provo, Utah.** That contract is documented at length on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) and is referenced rather than restated here, because it does not need strengthening — it needs applying. **Every confirmed position in the table above is at Provo**, which is where that contract puts this operator's aircraft. Confirming SU-BND on the Provo ramp on 19 April 2024, 23 May 2025 and 9–13 September 2025 confirms it was at its operator's contracted maintenance facility, and it fits what this page's own [113-day test](#the-113-day-provo-stay-tested-against-the-tpusa-event-calendar) found independently: a 27-year-old Gulfstream IV-SP that sits for 113 days and flies twice, once straight to its certified ceiling, has the profile of an airframe in and out of a heavy check.

**That is a fact about where the airframe belonged, not a clearance for the days in question.** A contracted field explains why the aircraft was at Provo at all; it does not say what any particular day was for, and no dated work order covering this stay has ever been published. It is also silent on the one day the page cannot get past — 10 September 2025, transponder up, seven miles from UVU, no takeoff.

For the **2022 United States transit**, the innocent explanation is the ordinary one: a state-operated aircraft crossing the Atlantic. An overflight of Indiana at 41,000 feet is not an event.

And the counterargument runs both ways here, which is why it is worth stating. This page previously read **five refuted rows out of nineteen** and called that a real error rate in the source document. **Four of those five were withdrawn on 26 August 2026** — three had been scored against the day before the claim, and one was a two-leg row whose first leg the trace confirms. **One refutation out of eight decidable rows is what the primary data actually supports.** The symmetry still holds and still binds: a reader who takes the seven confirmations as vindication has to take OWENS-050 at the same strength, and has to accept that **eight of nineteen rows on this tail can be decided at all.**

### What none of this establishes

**All of it locates the aircraft. None of it locates a Kirk.**

Every confirmation above confirms one half of a pairing. An overlap needs two halves — an aircraft at a place and a person at that place — and the person half is unchanged by anything in this section. For this tail that gap is at its widest, because **a stationary aircraft cannot follow anyone**: for 113 days SU-BND had exactly one location while [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview) held eleven events across Texas, Florida, California, South Carolina, Korea and Japan.

**Erika Kirk's side is the weak side and this page must keep saying so.** Her flight logs are reported erased — see [Erika's Flight Logs Erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased) — and in the 139 dated rows this repository can source, exactly one places her at a TPUSA event before 10 September 2025. **The 68- and 73-overlap tallies were measured against her claimed locations, and this repository cannot reproduce the list they were measured against.** That is not a refutation of those tallies. It is the more honest and more damaging statement that they are untestable with anything public.

The counts remain in dispute and are not reconciled here: **73 / about 23**, **72**, **"70+"**, and the separate dual-plane tally of **68 / 29**. Nobody has reconciled them, and averaging them would invent a figure nobody published.

Nothing in this section establishes who was aboard SU-BND on any date, who tasked it, or why. No public record establishes tasking in either direction, and this page does not assert one.

## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — SU-BND](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/su-bnd) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.
* [ADS-B Exchange — SU-BND](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/?reg=SU-BND) — volunteer-fed, unfiltered tracker that does **not** honor LADD, so it often shows blocked aircraft and tracks the filtered sites leave out.

## Note on Registration & Control

This aircraft carries an Egyptian registration — the **SU-** prefix on its tail number is Egypt's national civil registry, so the Egyptian registration itself is real and not in dispute. What is *not* settled is **who ultimately controlled the jet.** If an Egyptian front company was registered as the aircraft's operator, that registration — Egyptian on paper — may have been arranged on behalf of an intelligence service from another country. That open possibility is why this page labels the aircraft **"Israel/Egyptian"**: the label flags the unresolved question of control. It is **not** a claim that the jet was Israeli-registered or Israeli-owned — the public record does not establish that, and nothing here asserts as fact that any government or person controlled it.

## Dates we looked for and did not find

Not every claimed SU-BND flight could be checked against a primary ADS-B trace. **Every date we queried and got nothing back for is listed — with the exact HTTP status and the reason we looked — on [ADS-B gaps for SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/data/adsb/MISSING_DATA).** That page is published for the same reason the recovered traces are: a gap that is written down can be argued with, and a gap that is quietly dropped cannot.

**An absence there is not a finding.** A 404 means a volunteer receiver network heard nothing on that UTC day — parked and silent, outside coverage, or a wrong claimed date all come first, and [what a 403 means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means) sets out why a refusal and a deletion look identical from outside. ## Where this page fits

The blue plane is the tail that stayed: SU-BND sat at Provo for close to four months and, per the notes, had power and transponder up on 10 September without ever leaving — the opposite behaviour to [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), which departed that morning, and a shape that reads as a heavy-maintenance booking at least as easily as anything else — which is the hardest fact on this page for the [following-planes claim](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika), because an aircraft that does not move for 113 days cannot follow anybody, and eleven TPUSA events across Texas, Florida, California, Korea and Japan passed while it sat there.
