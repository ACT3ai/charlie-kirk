# N708JH — The DOJ Gulfstream

**N708JH** entered this investigation through a claim posted on X about a government aircraft and a flight-tracking block. Most claims of that kind cannot be checked. This one has a checkable half, and the FAA registry confirms it.

## Aircraft identification

From the **FAA Civil Aviation Registry**:

* **Tail number:** N708JH
* **Type:** **Gulfstream Aerospace GV-SP (G550)** — a 20-seat ultra-long-range business jet
* **Year built:** 2019 · **Serial number:** 5599
* **Registered owner:** **UNITED STATES GOVERNMENT** — with **DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE** listed on the registration
* **Registered address:** Washington, DC
* **Certificate issued:** 14 January 2020
* **Cost to buy new today:** **~$58.8 million** — the G550 ended production in 2021; Gulfstream's in-production aircraft in that cabin and range class is the **G600** at roughly $58.8 million list. See [Aircraft Costs](/Planes/Aircraft-Costs/overview).

## What is confirmed and what is not

**Confirmed:** this is a federal law-enforcement aircraft. It is not registered to a shell company or a trust — the registrant line reads *United States Government*, with the Department of Justice named. A near-new G550 held directly by DOJ is a senior-official transport, not a surveillance platform.

**Not confirmed:** everything said about where it flew, who was aboard, and whether its tracking was deliberately suppressed around the Charlie Kirk case. Those claims circulate on X and are catalogued on the [influencer coverage pages](/Influencers/x). This site has not verified any of them.

## On the tracking-block question

Claims that a government aircraft "went dark" or "was blocked" usually rest on the aircraft not appearing on a commercial tracker. There is a mundane mechanism for that, and it applies to this aircraft by default:

* Federal law-enforcement and government aircraft are routinely excluded from public flight-tracking feeds as a matter of standing policy, not case-specific decision.
* The FAA's **[LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd)** programme lets owners request removal from the data feeds that commercial trackers consume.
* An aircraft registered openly to the Department of Justice being absent from FlightRadar24 is the **expected** condition, not an anomaly requiring explanation.

That is not a reason to stop asking where it went. It is a reason not to treat its absence from a tracker as a finding.

## Open questions

1. Did N708JH operate into Utah at any point in September 2025?
2. Which DOJ component operates the aircraft, and under what tasking authority?
3. Would its movements be releasable under the [proposed disclosure laws](/Fix/overview), which are aimed squarely at exactly this category of record?

## Where this page fits

A federal law-enforcement jet registered openly to the United States Government is the cleanest case in this section for a rule the rest of the [Planes investigation](/Planes/overview) depends on — that absence from a public tracker is the expected condition for a government airframe, not an anomaly — and it belongs beside the other government tails, [SAM 99-0404](/Planes/SAM-99-0404/overview), the [Air Force Two casket flight](/Planes/Casket-Flight) and [Joint Base Andrews](/Planes/Airport-Joint-Base-Andrews). Whether it ever operated into Utah is exactly the sort of record that only comes out by statute, which is what the [four proposed disclosure laws](/Fix/overview) exist for — [Law 1 aims squarely at the DoJ and FBI](/laws/DoJ_FBI/Law_1_DoJ_FBI), and [the FBI's own handling of this case](/FBI/overview) is why anyone thought to ask. The precedent behind those laws is worth reading before dismissing them: [Law 1](/laws/DoJ_FBI/Law_1_DoJ_FBI) is modelled on the Epstein Files Transparency Act, and its congressional findings cite roughly twelve foreign-registered phones reportedly detected at the shooting site and foreign military aircraft on the ground nearby as the stated grounds for compelling disclosure.
