# N55906 Cullen Flight

Tail number **N55906** is an aircraft that independent investigator **[John Cullen](/Influencers/john-cullen)** (@I_am_JohnCullen) has repeatedly flagged as a second flight of interest in the [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) case, naming it directly alongside the **[N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview)** [HADES](/US_Intelligence/US_Army_HADES) surveillance plane. In Cullen's own framing, "HADES N1098L is the elephant in the room. N55906 is the gorilla in the room." Public reporting on N55906 is thin, and it is recorded here as an open lead worth pulling records on — not as an established finding.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** N55906
* **Type:** Not confirmed in public reporting
* **Registry / operator / owner:** Not confirmed in public reporting
* **Flagged by:** John Cullen (@I_am_JohnCullen), who paired it with N1098L using the "elephant in the room" / "gorilla in the room" framing

## Flight history relevant to the case

The public record contains no confirmed route, timestamps, or altitude data for N55906 in connection with September 10, 2025. What exists is Cullen's call to examine the tail number as part of the broader array of aircraft activity scrutinized around **Utah Valley University**. No dated flight log, ADS-B track, or arrival/departure pairing has been published that this page can responsibly cite. That absence is itself the point: investigators are asking for the records precisely because they are not yet public.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

These are open, attributed theories — not conclusions.

* **A paired surveillance asset (Cullen).** John Cullen has publicly listed N55906 as an "interesting flight" worth examining alongside N1098L, the aircraft he describes as a HADES-class surveillance platform. The implication he raises is that more than one aircraft of interest may have been aloft in the relevant window. Cullen has not published a confirmed route or operator for N55906, so this remains a request to investigate rather than a documented finding.
* **A transcription or data-entry artifact.** Because N55906 differs from the better-documented **[N59906](/Planes/N59906)** by a single digit, one open possibility is that some references to "N55906" are a misreading of N59906, the MARC Inc. survey Piper. This page treats the two as distinct (see below), but does not rule out that the lead resolves to N59906 once records are pulled.

## A note on the similar tail number

The investigation also tracks **[N59906](/Planes/N59906)** — the **MARC Inc.** Piper PA-31 Navajo Chieftain reported making four low-altitude passes over the UVU campus. N55906 and N59906 differ by a single digit and should not be assumed to be the same aircraft. The investigation's aircraft list treats them as **two distinct tail numbers**; confirming whether N55906 is a genuinely separate flight or a transcription of N59906 is itself one of the open questions below.

## OSINT tracking on X

N55906 surfaces almost entirely through open-source intelligence (OSINT) work on X, not through conventional reporting. **John Cullen** (@I_am_JohnCullen) and **similar accounts on X** have flagged the tail number and folded it into the **extensive plane-array entries** the investigation has been compiling — a running list of aircraft scrutinized for **timing and patterns near the event**. Per Cullen and these accounts, N55906 belongs in that array as one more flight worth pulling records on around **September 10, 2025**.

The tail number is **highlighted in deep-dive threads on X**, where it is discussed alongside N1098L and other aircraft of interest. All of this is alleged OSINT analysis: no operator, route, or wrongdoing is established here, and the framing reflects what these accounts have asked investigators to examine rather than any confirmed finding.

## Tracked trip in the array

The investigation's plane array records N55906 as a flagged entry rather than a logged point-to-point flight:

* **Route:** Listed as **"Various"** with the note that it fits **patterns near the event area** — no single confirmed origin or destination is published.
* **Timing:** Tied to **September 10, 2025 context** and the broader event timeframe.
* **Operators:** Recorded only as **"operators per OSINT"** — no confirmed owner or operator in public reporting.
* **Why it is listed:** Per John Cullen and other X accounts, it is alleged to be **part of broader suspicious aerial activity noted on X** in the relevant window. This is an open lead, not a conclusion.

## Open questions

1. Is N55906 a distinct aircraft, or a misreading of N59906?
2. What was N55906's route, and when was it near Utah relative to the September 10, 2025 event?
3. Who is the registered owner and operator, and what is the aircraft type?
4. What specifically prompted John Cullen to flag it alongside N1098L?


## Why the digit-transposition risk is now higher, not lower

The case for keeping these two tails rigidly separate got stronger once the primary records for [N59906](/Planes/N59906) were published. That aircraft now has a full public identity attached to it — **Mode S hex A7C14D**, **serial number 31-7552027**, a **1974 Piper PA-31**, operator **MARC INC**, squawking a discrete **4323** on a **2-hour-39-minute Provo→Provo** sortie on September 10, 2025.

None of those identifiers belongs to N55906. When a post cites "the survey plane," "the Piper," "MARC Inc.," "the five passes over UVU," "the 19,000-foot mapping grid," or the ADS-B Exchange capture stamped **2025-09-10 18:06:10 Z**, it is describing **N59906**, whatever tail number the post itself prints.

The practical rule for anyone auditing a claim about either aircraft: **match on the hex code or the serial number, never on the printed registration.** Registrations get retyped by hand and one digit slips; Mode S hex codes and MSNs are copied from tracker output and do not.

## What a reader should do with a source that names this tail

Because so much of the circulating material transposes the digits, a claim naming N55906 falls into one of three buckets, and the third is the only one that belongs on this page:

1. **It repeats a detail that is documented for N59906** — the MARC Inc. operator, the survey payload, the September 10 morning passes, the Provo round trip. That is a mistyped reference to the other aircraft, and it should be read against [N59906](/Planes/N59906) instead.
2. **It repeats a detail that is documented for neither aircraft.** Several of the anomalous-aircraft catalogs circulating on X list tail numbers with no supporting track, screenshot, or timestamp behind them. Those entries are unusable until someone produces the underlying record.
3. **It is genuinely about N55906 and carries its own evidence** — its own tracker capture, its own hex code, its own timestamps. That is the only category worth building on, and it is by far the smallest.

As of this writing, no publicly circulated ADS-B capture, Flightradar24 playback card, or FAA registry screenshot has been produced that shows **N55906** operating in the Utah Valley area on September 10, 2025. That absence is not proof the aircraft was elsewhere — it is proof that the claim has not been evidenced, which is a different and more useful thing to say.

## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — N55906](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n55906) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.
* [ADS-B Exchange — N55906](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/?reg=N55906) — volunteer-fed, unfiltered tracker that does **not** honor LADD, so it often shows blocked aircraft and tracks the filtered sites leave out.
