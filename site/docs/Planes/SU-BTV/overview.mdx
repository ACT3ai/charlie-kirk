# SU-BTV Israel/Egyptian Jet

**SU-BTV** is an Egyptian-registered VIP jet — described in flight logs as a **Dassault Falcon 7X** — that appears as one of the five tail numbers in what X researchers call the **"Israel/Egyptian Plane Armada"**: a cluster of Israel/Egyptian government Falcons and Gulfstreams ([SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview), [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview), SU-BTV, [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview)) that trackers say routed through Provo, Utah during 2025.

Be honest about the sourcing: **standalone reporting on SU-BTV specifically is thin.** It is rarely discussed on its own; its significance is almost entirely as a member of the fleet pattern. The bulk of the analysis — the timeline, the [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview)-overlap claims, the maintenance counterpoints — lives on the hub page, **Israel/Egyptian Plane Armada**. This page collects only what public sources say about SU-BTV by name and frames every claim as an open, unverified allegation.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** SU-BTV
* **Type:** Dassault Falcon 7X (per the flight-log screenshots circulated on X; "Egyptian-registered" in other notes)
* **Registry / operator:** Egyptian-registered — described as Israel/Egyptian Government / Israel/Egyptian Air Force use
* **Nickname in coverage:** None. [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens) color-coded SU-BTT ("yellow plane") and SU-BND ("blue plane"); SU-BTV carries no color label and is listed only in flight-overlap discussions.
* **Reported destination airport in Utah:** Provo Municipal Airport (PVU), roughly 7 miles from Utah Valley University.

## Flight history relevant to the case

SU-BTV surfaced publicly when raw flight-log screenshots — attributed to the account [@WeAre_TheElite](/Influencers/x/weare-theelite) and amplified by [@ProjectConstitu](/Influencers/podcasts-project-constitution) (Project Constitution) — were used to expand the "two Israel/Egyptian planes" story into a "five-plane armada." The dated legs below are those researchers' published readings of the logs, not independently verified records:

* **March 2, 2025:** Provo (PVU) activity logged.
* **July 2, 2025:** Provo local flight.
* **October 2, 2025:** Provo activity — which one thread speculated, with a question mark, could be "post-assassination cleanup." This is rhetorical speculation, not evidence.
* **October 29, 2025:** Cairo local flight.

Researchers characterize the pattern as **"Provo spikes pre-September,"** grouping SU-BTV with the other four tails as recurring 2025 visitors to a non-international US municipal airport. No passenger manifest, tasking, or operator statement for any SU-BTV leg has been made public.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

All of the following are **open theories** advanced by independent commentators, not established facts:

* **Fleet-pattern surveillance** — that the five Israel/Egyptian tails collectively shadowed TPUSA events and the Kirk family's movements (the "armada" framing). SU-BTV is cited as a data point in that pattern rather than as an individually documented surveillance flight.
* **Foreign orchestration** — the [@ProjectConstitu](/Influencers/podcasts-project-constitution) thread floated "[Mossad](/intelligence/Mossad) proxies via Egypt" and alleged "no-scan DoD badges" dropping "experts" for drone/laser operations. These are unverified assertions made about the fleet as a whole, with no specific evidence tied to SU-BTV.
* **Routine VIP and maintenance operations** — that SU-BTV's US stops are ordinary movements of a government VIP jet, most of them into shops its operator has a documented account with. This is the reading the rotation record on this page most closely fits, and the section below sets out both why and where it runs out.

## The maintenance geography, and where it stops explaining

Provo Municipal Airport hosts **Duncan Aviation**, a full-service maintenance, repair, and overhaul facility — airframe, engine, avionics, paint, interior — and **Duncan has held an Egyptian Air Force maintenance account since 1999**, with plants at Provo and at **Lincoln, Nebraska**. That contract is a matter of record and is documented at length, with sources, on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview). **Wichita** carries the same weight for this type: **Yingling Aviation** there is an FAA Part 145 facility that services Dassault Falcons.

On SU-BTV that geography is not an argument — it is what the rotation table further down the page actually contains. Lincoln three times, Wichita three times, Provo once, Wilmington on the outbound leg of every rotation. Two of this aircraft's three US destinations are the two best-documented Falcon maintenance centres in North America, and its operator has a publicly announced, decades-old account at one of them. That is the strongest evidentiary reason on any page in this fleet to read SU-BTV's US movements as shop visits, and it is derived here from the flight record rather than borrowed from anyone's opinion.

**It stops short in three specific places, and the page should say so rather than round up.** No dated Duncan or Yingling work order, invoice or release-to-service for SU-BTV has ever been published, so a contract explains the destinations and not the individual legs. A shop visit accounts for the airframe and never for the cabin — no manifest, crew list or passenger record exists for any of these arrivals. And nothing here touches the **September 2025 Provo window**, which belongs to [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) and [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) and rests on a different evidence file entirely.

Separately, and on their own footing rather than under the maintenance heading, two further cautions bear on the fleet-level claim: a sceptic's audit of the Owens overlap counts reportedly found **several of the cited dates wrong**, and transponder gaps and tracker artifacts can mimic "stealth" behaviour without anything having been concealed.

### On this tail the destinations are a shop rotation

Eight rotations in eleven months, all identical in shape, and the **destination cities are a maintenance itinerary rather than a political one**:

* **Lincoln, Nebraska — three visits** (Feb, Mar, Oct). Duncan Aviation's main plant, holder of the Egyptian Air Force maintenance account since 1999 (see [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview)).
* **Wichita, Kansas — three visits** (Dec, Mar–Apr, Jul–Aug). The "Air Capital of the World," and home to **Yingling Aviation**, an FAA Part 145 facility that services **Dassault Falcon** aircraft.
* **Detroit (DTW) — two arrivals**, both used as the transatlantic entry point before hopping onward (to Lincoln in February, to Wichita in March).
* **Provo, Utah — one visit**, 27 May to 2 June 2025.
* **Wilmington (ILG) on the outbound leg of all eight rotations, and only ever outbound.**

Two of the three US destinations this aircraft visits are the two best-documented **Falcon maintenance centres in North America**, and the operator has a publicly-announced, decades-old contract with one of them. A tally that scores each of these arrivals as a "location overlap" with a Kirk or TPUSA event in Nebraska or Kansas is scoring a maintenance rotation.

### The 27 May – 2 June Provo stay, and why it matters beyond this page

**SU-BTV's Provo window — arrival 27 May, departure 2 June 2025 — matches, to the day, the visit that Candace Owens attributes to [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview)** in her August 2026 segment, and on which she builds the "purple plane" passenger link across aircraft. The same export puts SU-BTU at Provo six weeks earlier, on 8–14 April.

If the export is right, the aircraft in Provo in late May was this one. The full comparison and what rides on it is set out on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview). It is unresolved and it is checkable: **SU-BTV is hex 0101D1, SU-BTU is hex 0101D0**, and the raw ADS-B archive for 27 May 2025 would settle it in minutes.

This page takes no position on which is correct. It records that the question is open, that it has not been asked publicly before, and that a chain of inference about a named individual currently rests on the answer.

### The Minot approach, again

Like [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) in April and [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) in September, SU-BTV entered Utah via **Minot (MOT)**, North Dakota — Paris → Minot (8:21), then Minot → Provo (1:35), both on 27 May. Three separate aircraft used the same unusual two-stop approach into Provo across three separate months.

Note that **MOT is the civil Minot International Airport identifier.** Claims that these aircraft routed through **Minot Air Force Base** are a step beyond what the export shows; the base is a distinct facility. What the export does support is that a small North Dakota field was repeatedly used as the North American entry point for Utah-bound legs by an operator whose other US trips entered through Omaha, Detroit, or St. Louis. Why Minot for the Provo runs and not for the Lincoln ones is an open question with no published answer.


## All seven US rotations, tested against the TPUSA event calendar

SU-BTV has the fullest US record of any of the five Egyptian tails — seven complete rotations between December 2024 and October 2025, covering Kansas, Nebraska, Michigan, Utah and Delaware. That breadth makes it the best available test of whether the fleet's American movements track TPUSA's calendar.

The calendar used is `site/docs/Planes/following/tpusa_events.csv` — **139 dated rows, 4 January 2022 to 29 October 2025**, built from TPUSA postings, campus press, event listings and the Charlie Kirk Show archive, each row flagged `EVENT_DATE` (118 rows), `PODCAST_PROXY` (8), `SCHEDULED_NOT_HELD` (4) or `AFTER_DEATH` (9). It is the spine of the [Following section](/Planes/following/overview).

The test is deliberately generous: a window counts as an overlap if **any** event happened **in the same US state** within **three days either side**. That is roughly the criterion [Liz Wheeler](/People/liz-wheeler) says the original overlap spreadsheet applied.

**Zero out of fourteen.** Across ten months and five states, not one SU-BTV ground window falls within three days of a TPUSA event in the same state. The closest is 86 days.

Three specifics are worth pulling out because they are routinely cited the other way:

* **Kansas.** Kansas is one of the five states in the clustering claim, and SU-BTV visited Wichita three times. The only Kansas event in the entire 139-row calendar is a September 2024 stop at **Kansas State University in Manhattan** — a different city, 130 miles away, and 86 days before the earliest of those visits. Wichita is a major Falcon service and aerospace hub; that is what the three visits track.
* **Detroit.** SU-BTV entered the US via Detroit twice (3 February and 30 March 2025), and Turning Point Action's People's Convention was held in Detroit — in **June 2024**, eight and nine months earlier respectively. No source connects them.
* **The 27 May – 2 June Provo stay.** This is the visit [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens) attributes to [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) and to a named passenger. The flight logs put **SU-BTV** on those exact dates instead — the attribution conflict is set out in full on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview). Either way, the nearest Utah event is 100 days later, and the calendar shows **no TPUSA event anywhere in the world** on 27 May – 2 June 2025.

### What this table means for the SU-BTV case

* **The pattern it fits is Duncan Aviation's, not TPUSA's.** Lincoln, Nebraska three times; Wichita three times; Wilmington on the outbound leg of all seven rotations. Those are maintenance and customs geography. As noted [above](#on-this-tail-the-destinations-are-a-shop-rotation), SU-BTV's destinations track shops rather than events — and this table is why. It is a statement about this tail's rotation record, not a verdict on the fleet.
* **SU-BTV is not in the 68/73 tally.** It was added by [@WeAre_TheElite](/Influencers/x/weare-theelite) to build the five-plane framing. This test is the reason it should be carried as fleet context rather than as evidence of shadowing.
* **The October rotation is after the assassination.** The 5–12 October 2025 window sits three-and-a-half weeks after 10 September, and the same Omaha→Lincoln→Wilmington shape repeats unchanged. A rotation that continues identically after the event is evidence the rotation was never about the event.

### The limits of this test

* **It tests Charlie's side, not Erika's.** Of 139 rows, exactly **one** places Erika Kirk at a TPUSA event before 10 September 2025. The overlap tallies were counted against *her* claimed locations, and her flight logs are reported erased — see [Erika's Flight Logs Erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased). Fourteen clean misses here do not refute those tallies; they establish that **no public source reproduces them for this tail**.
* **The calendar records public events only.**
* **The flight record is a tracker export, not a manifest.**

That has a direct consequence for how the fleet is written about. **Any page — on this site or anywhere else — that presents all five Egyptian tails as equally documented is overstating three of them.** The evidence base under SU-BTT is more than four times deeper than the one under SU-BTV. That is the difference between a claim that can be checked and one that cannot, and it is the reason this page has always described the standalone record for this tail as thin. The primary data confirms that description rather than repairing it.

Note the direction of the imbalance, because it is the opposite of what the page's flight-export section might suggest. **SU-BTV has the fullest US record of the five Egyptian tails in the tracker export, and the thinnest-but-one record in primary position data.** Those two facts are not in conflict: a tracker export is a table someone published, and a trace is a signal a receiver heard. The export is far larger than anything we can independently verify.

### The 2022 trace, and a correction to this site

`samples.adsbexchange.com/traces/` publishes a free full-day global sample for **the first of every month, back to 2016** — no account, no key. It reaches this tail once before 2023.

**2022-10-01.** The trace opens **1.15 km from Almaza Air Force Base, Cairo (HEAZ)** and closes **3.44 km from the same field** — an Egyptian domestic round trip, 269 position points, nothing outside Egypt. The file self-identifies as `"r": "SU-BTV"`, `"t": "FA7X"`. It is `site/docs/Planes/SU-BTV/data/recovered/SU-BTV_2022-10-01_adsbexchange-samples_trace_full.json`.

**Say plainly what that is worth: this trace supports nothing whatsoever about the US following pattern.** It is a military-field round trip in Egypt on a day nobody has claimed anything about. It confirms the airframe existed, was flying, and was operating out of an Egyptian Air Force base in October 2022. That is all.

Two of the sibling tails did produce US content on their sampled 2022 days — [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) at Wilmington, Delaware in June and Wichita, Kansas in September, and [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) on approach to St Louis, Missouri in December. **SU-BTV produced none.** Reporting the sibling results without this one would be picking the sample.

**The trace does correct a standing statement on this site.** We have published that "the archive does not reach 2022; every 2022 claim rests on a screenshot." **That is no longer true** — primary 2022 position data now exists for this fleet, including for this tail, from a free public dataset that was available the whole time.

**The sampling caveat has to travel with this fact every time it is cited.** The sample is **one day per month, the 1st**. For SU-BTV that is one day in the whole of 2022. **It cannot support a rate, a frequency or a pattern**, and it cannot be read as evidence that the aircraft stayed in Egypt — only that it was in Egypt on the one day we can see.

### The Internet Archive never crawled this aircraft's pages

For SU-BTV the archived-page recovery that produced 153 flight legs across the fleet produced **nothing**, because there is nothing to recover. **The Internet Archive holds no snapshot of this tail's FlightRadar24, FlightAware, RadarBox or Planespotters pages. Not one, on any date.**

**That is absence of archival interest, not removal.** Nobody ever asked the Archive to crawl those URLs. Our re-run of the Archive query on 24 August 2026 succeeded on all 30 URLs it asked about, and **every CDX row it returned, across every URL in the dataset, carries `statuscode=200`. There is no 200-to-404 transition anywhere in it.** A page that was never captured cannot have been taken down, and we do not report it as though it had been.

Three snapshots do exist of the ADS-B Exchange globe page for hex `0101D1`, spanning **May to December 2022** — and they contain **no aircraft data**. The Archive's own content digest for them, `AJQHJML54QE6X2IE4TMCM24E3DPGKFD6`, is **byte-identical across SU-BGM, SU-BTV and SU-BTU**. Three different hex codes cannot produce three identical files if the files hold per-aircraft data. The Archive captured the **empty JavaScript application frame** that loads before the map fetches anything. **These snapshots must never be cited as though they held evidence about this aircraft.**

The live FlightRadar24 page for this tail loads normally in a browser and returns HTTP 403 to a script. That is bot protection, not a removal: the identical 403 comes back for unrelated control aircraft and for FlightRadar24's own home page — see [What A 403 Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means).

### The overlap register does not name this aircraft at all

We tested all **85 rows** of `site/docs/Planes/following/overlaps.csv` against both free archives, on the claimed date and the day either side, measuring the aircraft's closest approach in kilometres to the claimed field. Across the whole register: **24 rows put the claimed aircraft at the claimed field, 12 put it somewhere else entirely**, 22 were not heard, 20 fall outside any free archive's coverage, and 7 name no tail or no date.

**None of those 85 rows names SU-BTV.** Nor [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview), nor [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview). The register is 57 rows naming [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), 16 naming [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview), 3 naming both, 5 naming "SU-BTT or SU-BND", and 4 naming no aircraft at all.

That is worth saying out loud on a page this long: **this site gives SU-BTV considerable space, and the overlap claim itself does not rest on this aircraft in any way.** There is nothing here to verify and correspondingly nothing here that has been verified. The disputed tallies — 73 and about 23, or 72, or "70-plus", or 68 and 29 — were never counted against this tail. Those figures conflict with one another, and this site reports the conflict rather than reconciling it.

The one SU-BTV question that primary data could settle is the **27 May – 2 June 2025 Provo window** and the attribution conflict with [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) set out above. **We cannot settle it yet.** Neither free archive holds 27 May 2025 for hex `0101D0` or `0101D1`, and the monthly sample does not fall on that date. The question stays open, it stays checkable, and the hex codes remain the way to check it.

### What the primary data settles on this tail

The primary record does not contradict what the rotation table already showed, and the agreement should be stated as plainly as any disagreement would be.

This aircraft's US record is **Lincoln three times, Wichita three times, Provo once, and Wilmington on the outbound leg of every rotation** — two of its three destinations being the two best-documented Falcon maintenance centres in North America, at one of which its operator has a publicly announced, decades-old account. **Fourteen ground windows produced zero calendar overlaps** under a deliberately generous test. Nothing in the nine days of primary trace we hold cuts against any of that, and the single corroborated day — **11 October 2025, repositioning out of Lincoln** — sits inside the same shape.

What that settles is bounded, and the boundary matters more than the result. It settles that **SU-BTV is not the tail the following claim runs on**: no row of the overlap register names it, no ground window it flew lands near a TPUSA date, and the destinations track shops rather than events. It settles nothing about the two tails that were at Provo in September 2025, and it produces no document — no work order, no manifest, no authorisation for foreign-military use of a US municipal field — that would close the question for any of the five. On this tail the record is unusually legible; on the fleet it is not, and this page is not the evidence for that one.

The counterargument to an empty archive is equally plain: **an empty archive means the archive had nothing.** It does not mean the aircraft was hidden, and it does not mean a transponder was off. These are volunteer receiver networks, not registries — they hold whatever a feeder happened to hear.

### What all of this locates, and what it does not

**Every fact in this section locates an aircraft. Not one of them locates a Kirk.**

A confirmed airframe is one half of an overlap. The other half — where Charlie or Erika Kirk actually was — is unchanged by anything here: sourced but incomplete for Charlie, and close to empty for Erika. Of the 139 dated rows in `tpusa_events.csv`, exactly **one** places Erika Kirk at a TPUSA event before 10 September 2025, and **her flight logs are reported erased** — see [Erika's Flight Logs Erased](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased). Any overlap tally counted against her claimed locations cannot be reproduced from public data, for this tail or any other.

## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — SU-BTV](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/su-btv) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.
* [ADS-B Exchange — SU-BTV](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/?reg=SU-BTV) — volunteer-fed, unfiltered tracker that does **not** honor LADD, so it often shows blocked aircraft and tracks the filtered sites leave out.

## Note on Registration & Control

This aircraft carries an Egyptian registration — the **SU-** prefix on its tail number is Egypt's national civil registry, so the Egyptian registration itself is real and not in dispute. What is *not* settled is **who ultimately controlled the jet.** If an Egyptian front company was registered as the aircraft's operator, that registration — Egyptian on paper — may have been arranged on behalf of an intelligence service from another country. That open possibility is why this page labels the aircraft **"Israel/Egyptian"**: the label flags the unresolved question of control. It is **not** a claim that the jet was Israeli-registered or Israeli-owned — the public record does not establish that, and nothing here asserts as fact that any government or person controlled it.
