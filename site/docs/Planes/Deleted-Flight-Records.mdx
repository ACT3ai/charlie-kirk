# Which Flight Records Were Deleted — And Whose

**This investigation holds 1,831 aircraft-days of primary position data on the aircraft in this
case — 3,619 trace files, 308 MB, pulled from four independent archives, covering 15 aircraft, and
including dates the live tracking APIs now refuse to serve.** All of it came from free, open,
no-account sources. The full count is the next thing on this page.

**What is gone, and cannot be got back, is also countable:** everything before **6 February 2023**,
including all of 2022; **seventeen dates in late 2025** that one archive answers HTTP 403 for; **six
whole days** two archives lost outright; **15,721 days an archive was asked about and held nothing**;
and, above all, **a dated itinerary for Erika Kirk, which nobody on any side of this argument has
ever published.** Those gaps are set out date range by date range in
[What is missing](#what-is-missing-date-range-by-date-range).

**One records change did happen in the disputed window, and it is not a flight record.**
[N102DZ](/Planes/N102DZ/overview)'s **FAA registration was re-issued on 16 October 2025 —
thirty-six days after the assassination** — to a Cheyenne, Wyoming holding company, with a further
registry action on **23 December 2025**. It is the **only** case-central aircraft whose registration
moved after 10 September 2025. Full registry table:
[the one thing that genuinely changed](#the-one-thing-that-genuinely-changed-in-the-window).

**And here is the result that runs against this investigation's own working assumption, stated
before anything else so it cannot be read as buried: no flight record has been shown to be removed
from anywhere.** Four suppression hypotheses were written down in advance and tested — by aircraft
nationality, by named person, by date, and by tracking company. None survived. Every one of those
tests, every NOT SUPPORTED verdict, the control aircraft that produced them, and the three claims
this site published as removals and then had to retract, are set out in full further down, under
[how each hypothesis was tested](#how-each-hypothesis-was-tested-and-what-the-controls-showed).
They are not softened and not shortened. They are simply no longer the first thing on the page.

### What was actually removed

**Zero flight records have been shown to be removed from anywhere.** That is the finding of this
page and the controls are what make it sayable. It is not a comfortable answer and it is not the
answer this investigation started with — three separate "removals" were published on this site
before an unrelated airliner was asked the same question and failed in exactly the same place.

There is one number on the other side of the ledger, and it is large:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Days an archive was asked about and held nothing** | **15,721** |

**That is a coverage fact, not a deletion count, and the distinction is the whole page.** An
asked-and-empty day means a volunteer receiver network heard nothing. Transponder off, parked at a
home stand in Cairo with no receiver within range, or a claimed date that was simply wrong — all
three produce the identical record. **A record that was never captured was never deleted.**

Each of those 15,721 days is written to disk as its own `.miss.json.meta.json` file, with the exact
URL, the HTTP status and the retrieval time, precisely so that *"we asked and the archive was
empty"* can never be confused with *"nobody has looked."* Those are different facts and only the
first one belongs near a published sentence.

### The one thing that genuinely changed

Not a flight record. **N102DZ's FAA registration** was re-issued on **16 October 2025** — thirty-six
days after the assassination — with a further registry action on **23 December 2025**. It is the only
case-central aircraft whose registration moved after 10 September 2025. It is documented below.

### And the correction that came out of the recovered data

The recovered files did overturn something — but it was **this site's own analysis**, not a tracking
company's archive. A first pass over the 85-row overlap register reported **12 refutations**. Reading
every position fix in every one of the 3,619 recovered trace files retracts **9 of the 12**: six had
been scored against the wrong calendar day, and two had been scored off the shorter of two archives
while the longer archive showed the aircraft **climbing out of the very airport it was said to be
1,853 km from**. The corrected result is **25 corroborated, 1 same-metro and 3 refuted** of the 29 rows
primary data can decide. Full working on
[Overlap Recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Overlap-Recovery).

**That is what the recovery was for.** Not to prove a deletion — it did not find one — but to replace
somebody's reading of a website with a position report, and then to correct the readings that turned
out to be wrong, in whichever direction they turned out to be wrong.

**N102DZ is the only case-central aircraft whose registration moved after 10 September 2025.** The
certificate was issued **thirty-six days after the assassination**, to a Wyoming holding company, and
a further registry action followed on **23 December 2025** — months before the reported May 2026

**State what this is and what it is not.** It is a public federal record of a registration change, in

Aircraft are re-registered constantly; ownership passes; a registrant is frequently a holding LLC
and a holding company is not a person, so this site does not go behind one to name individuals. It is
also worth saying that the community tracking databases still list the *previous* name for this tail
— which is one prosaic way a researcher could look at two sources, see two different owners, and
reach for the word "erased".

What makes it worth publishing is that it is the **only** dated change anywhere in the fleet that
falls in the disputed window at all. Every other candidate on this page dissolved into archive
mechanics. This one is a real record, and it is left standing as a question rather than an answer.

## What is still genuinely gone

Sorting the gaps did not make them go away. Four things remain unrecoverable, and the honest version
of this page has to lead with them as prominently as the refutations.

**1. All of 2022, from the daily archives.** The following-planes claim opens with alleged overlaps
in **October, November and December 2022** — New York, Chicago, Boston, Wichita, Wilmington, St
Louis. **Neither free daily archive can be asked about any of them.** The only free route into 2022
is the ADS-B Exchange monthly sample, which holds **one day per month, the 1st**. It can fix a
position on 1 October 2022. It can say nothing whatever about 5 October 2022. **Twenty of the
eighty-five alleged overlaps sit under this floor and cannot be tested by anyone, for or against.**

**2. Whose feet were on the aircraft.** This is the one that matters most and the one no archive
anywhere will ever fix. **A trace proves presence. It never proves purpose and it never proves
occupancy.** Twenty-six days of N102DZ tracks in May 2026 do not place any named person on any leg.
**Erika Kirk's itinerary is the missing document, and no backup produces it** — which is precisely
why she was able to issue the invitation, and precisely why taking her up on it does not settle
anything either way.

**3. Twenty-two alleged overlap dates the archives simply never heard.** Inside the covered period,
twenty-two aircraft-and-date pairs return nothing from either network after the GitHub off-site
backup was also asked and also came back empty. **That is a coverage fact, not a finding.** Parked
with the transponder off, outside receiver coverage, and *the claimed date is simply wrong* all look
identical from here — and several of these rows are already marked inaccurate in the spreadsheet's
own audit column.

**4. What any of these pages displayed in May 2026.** A page that loads today cannot testify about a
table's contents eight months ago. **"The page was taken down" is refuted.** A narrower claim — that
history was specifically purged and later restored — is beyond every method used here, in either
direction, and **no page on this site may assert it.**

## How each hypothesis was tested, and what the controls showed

Everything from here down is the method, the control test, and the verdicts — including four
**NOT SUPPORTED** results, two **REFUTED** results, and the retractions this site had to publish
against itself. None of it is softened. It sits below the counts rather than above them because a
reader wants the record first and the machinery second, not because the machinery matters less.


**The question this page answers.** Researchers on X say the public flight history for the aircraft
at the centre of this case was pulled down — most loudly that **N102DZ**, the jet
[Erika Kirk invited the public to check](/Planes/Erika-Flight-Logs-Erased), was **"ERASED in May
2026 from flightradar24 so now NOBODY can check them."** If records really were removed, the
removals should show a *shape*: they should cluster on one nationality of aircraft, or one person's
travel dates, or one window of time, or one tracking company. Find the shape and you have found who
did it and what they were protecting.

So the shape was hunted for directly. Every aircraft-day this investigation has ever asked an
archive about was collected — **15,131 logged queries across sixteen case aircraft, two unrelated
control aircraft, four independent archives and 917 distinct dates**, snapshot **26 August 2026,
17:42 UTC** — and the gaps were sorted four ways: **by aircraft nationality, by named person, by
date, and by tracking site.**

**None of the four produced a shape.** What produced a shape every single time was the archive's own
mechanics — and the controls prove it, because two airliners with no connection to this case fail in
exactly the same places.

## The five things that looked deleted, and what each one actually is

Every candidate deletion in this investigation collapsed into one of five archive mechanics. Naming
them matters, because from the outside all five look identical to suppression, and three of them
were published on this site as suppression before the controls were run.

**1. The archive floor — 2022 does not exist.** The oldest track either free daily archive holds for
any aircraft is **6 February 2023** (airplanes.live) and **24 February 2023** (adsb.lol). Ask either
of them about 2022 and you get HTTP 404 forever. This is the largest single "gap" in the
investigation and it swallows the entire opening year of the following-planes claim.

**2. The 403 band — a date range, not an aircraft list.** adsb.lol answers HTTP 403 for a run of
dates in late 2025. It is the same date range for every aircraft, case or control.

**3. Whole-day holes — and both archives have them.** airplanes.live lost **2023-09-11** completely;
adsb.lol lost **2023-02-06 through 2023-02-10**. On each of those days the archive returns nothing
for *anything*, including daily-flying airliners.

**4. The free-tier retention window — 7 days, then 14 days.** FlightRadar24 shows a logged-out
visitor **seven days** of history and paywalls the rest. FlightAware shows **fourteen**. A table that
was full on Thursday and empty the next Thursday has not been purged; it has rolled.

**5. Coverage, not concealment.** A volunteer receiver network only holds what somebody's aerial
actually heard. A jet parked at its home stand in Cairo is invisible to a network whose nearest
receiver is in another country. The same jet at cruise altitude over the same city is heard from
hundreds of kilometres away — which is why the Egyptian tails appear in these archives *airborne
over Egypt* on most of their tracked days and almost never *on the ground at Cairo*.

**None of the five is a person, an agency, or a motive. All five are geometry, retention policy, and
volunteer hardware.**

## How any of this can be said at all: the control test

**Nothing on this page may be read as a removal unless an aircraft with no connection to this case
failed the same way.** That is the whole method, and it is the reason the four pattern tests below
can be trusted in either direction.

Two airframes were picked precisely because nobody would suppress them: a **Ryanair 737** (hex
`4ca7b5`) and a **Lufthansa A380** (hex `3c6444`). They were asked the same questions, on the same
dates, through the same endpoints, by the same code.

| Test | Case aircraft | Ryanair + Lufthansa controls | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Days asked before 6 Feb 2023 | 1,461 asked → **0 tracks** | 28 asked → **0 tracks** | Archive floor, not removal |
| Days asked from 6 Feb 2023 on | 5,749 asked → 1,516 tracks (26.4%) | 112 asked → 104 tracks (92.9%) | Archives healthy; the gap is about the aircraft |
| The 17 HTTP 403 dates | 403 on all 17, all 12 tails | 403 on the 3 of those dates they were asked | Date-scoped, aircraft-blind |
| 2023-09-11 on airplanes.live | 404 for all 14 tails | **404 for both airliners** | Whole-day archive hole |
| Tracking-site pages, in a browser | HTTP 200, 16 of 16 | HTTP 200, 4 of 4 | No page removed from anyone |

The control gap in the third row is the one that needs stating honestly: **the case aircraft really
are much sparser in these archives than an airliner is — 26.4% against 92.9%.** That gap is not
evidence of hiding. An airliner flies every day through the densest receiver coverage on earth. A
government VIP jet flies a few days a month, much of it outside it. The four tests below exist
because that raw gap, on its own, proves nothing at all — the question is whether the *shape* of the
missing days points at anybody.

## Test 1 — Is it the Egyptian aircraft?

This was the leading hypothesis, and it is the one the raw numbers most look like they support.

| Fleet | Aircraft-days asked, 2023-02-06 onward | Days with a primary track | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egyptian **SU-BGM, SU-BND, SU-BTT, SU-BTU, SU-BTV** | 2,424 | 278 | **11.5%** |
| US **N102DZ, N40JD, N560TW, N582MM, N872RA, N888KG** | 2,763 | 1,047 | **37.9%** |
| San Marino **T7-ELL** | 461 | 90 | 19.5% |
| Controls | 112 | 104 | 92.9% |

The Egyptian fleet is three times sparser than the American one. **Three separate checks say that is
coverage, not suppression.**

**First, the tracking sites serve the Egyptian jets openly, today.** Probed from a real logged-out
browser session on 24 August 2026, FlightRadar24 returns full history for the Egyptian tails and
does not hide their operator:

| Tail | HTTP | History rows rendered | Operator as FR24 labels it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SU-BTT** | 200 | **56** | Egypt Government / Egypt - Air Force |
| **SU-BTU** | 200 | **56** | Egypt - Air Force |
| **SU-BTV** | 200 | **54** | Egypt - Air Force |
| **SU-BND** | 200 | **14** | Private owner |
| **SU-BGM** | 200 | 0 — has not flown in 7 days | Egypt Goverment *(sic, as printed)* |
| **N102DZ** | 200 | **0** — has not flown in 7 days | Private owner |
| **N888KG** | 200 | **0** — has not flown in 7 days | Private owner |
| **N1KE** *(control, Nike Gulfstream)* | 200 | 0 — has not flown in 7 days | Private owner |

Read that table the way it actually falls. **The aircraft showing zero rows are the American ones
and an unrelated Nike jet. The aircraft showing full history are Egyptian Air Force.** Whatever is
happening, nobody is hiding Egyptian government aviation from the public — a site that wanted to
would not print "Egypt - Air Force" in the operator field.

**Second, FlightAware is publishing their current movements.** Its activity log on 24 August 2026
held **eleven SU-BTT legs between 11 and 19 August 2026** — Cairo to Mersa Matruh and back, ordinary
domestic Egyptian government flying, with times and coordinates.

**Third, where the Egyptian jets ARE heard says the missing days are physics.** Pulling the position
data out of every recovered track and bucketing it by region:

| Tail | Days with a track | Includes positions over Egypt | Over Europe | Over North America |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SU-BTT | 92 | **70** | 30 | 26 |
| SU-BND | 73 | **51** | 15 | 18 |
| SU-BTV | 45 | **37** | 12 | 12 |
| SU-BTU | 55 | **32** | 23 | 23 |
| SU-BGM | 34 | **28** | 5 | 4 |

Egypt is not a hole in the map. These aircraft are heard *over* Egypt on the clear majority of the
days we hold anything for. **What is nearly absent is ground presence at their home base.** Across
all five tails the recovered ground stops are **Wilmington, Provo, Paris, Wichita, Lincoln, Minot,
Bangor, Farnborough, Corfu, Preveza, Nice, Sofia, Berlin, Rome, Salt Lake City** — and, in Egypt,
only **El Arish, El Nozha and Sudr Cape**, once to three times each. **Not one recovered ground stop
at Cairo International**, the field these aircraft live at.

That is exactly the fingerprint of line-of-sight radio. A transponder at 41,000 feet over Cairo
reaches a volunteer aerial in Cyprus, Israel or Greece. The same transponder on the ramp at Cairo
reaches nothing. **The Egyptian gap is a gap in what a hobbyist antenna can hear, and it lands hard
on the days these jets never left home.**

**Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED.** The pattern that looked like Egyptian suppression is the pattern of
where receivers physically are.

## Test 2 — Is it a person? Erika, or Charlie?

This is the sharpest version of the question, because it is the one the erasure claim actually
makes. The [73-overlap spreadsheet](/Planes/following/73_overlaps) records, for each alleged
following-flight, whether **Erika Kirk** is claimed present and whether **Charlie Kirk** is claimed
present. If dates were being scrubbed to protect a person, the dates naming that person would come
back empty more often than the dates that do not.

They do not.

| Split | Dates where the aircraft was heard | Dates where it was not | Recovery rate | Fisher exact, two-tailed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Erika Kirk claimed present** | 28 | 21 | 57.1% | **p = 0.133** |
| Erika Kirk not claimed | 8 | 1 | 88.9% | — |
| **Charlie Kirk claimed present** | 9 | 3 | **75.0%** | **p = 0.329** |
| Charlie Kirk not claimed | 25 | 19 | 56.8% | — |
| **SU-BTT** (the lead tail) | 22 | 17 | 56.4% | **p = 0.547** |
| **SU-BND** (the second tail) | 11 | 5 | 68.8% | — |

**All three are null results, and two of them run backwards.** The Charlie-present dates come back
*better* than the rest, not worse. The Erika-present dates do come back worse — but the comparison
group is nine rows, the test does not clear significance at any conventional threshold, and the
imbalance has an ordinary cause: the not-claimed rows are concentrated in later years the archives
cover better.

**Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED for either person.** There is no person-shaped hole in this dataset.

## Test 3 — Is it the dates? The event windows?

If somebody were removing the days that matter, the day of a TPUSA or Charlie Kirk speaking
engagement would be the day that goes missing. The sweep queried a **±7-day window around 131
speaking events**, which makes this directly measurable: line every queried day up by its offset
from the nearest event and read the recovery rate down the column.

| Offset from event | Egyptian SU-* | Kirk / TPUSA N-* | All subject tails |
|---|---|---|---|
| −3 days | 19.6% | 65.2% | 37.2% |
| −2 days | 8.3% | 25.8% | 17.7% |
| −1 day | 9.1% | 27.0% | 18.7% |
| **Day of the event** | **7.9%** | **32.6%** | **21.5%** |
| +1 day | 6.6% | 31.7% | 20.3% |
| +2 days | 7.5% | 26.7% | 18.1% |
| +3 days | 6.2% | 24.4% | 14.6% |

**The event day is the peak on the Kirk side and flat on the Egyptian side.** Charlie Kirk's own
aircraft are *best* documented on the days he was speaking, which is what you would expect of jets
that actually flew him there. Nothing dips at zero.

Note the second thing that table says, which cuts against this site's own thesis and belongs here
anyway: **the Egyptian tails show no elevated presence on event days either — 7.9% on the day
against 9.1% and 6.6% on the days either side.** That is a fact about the following claim, not about
deletion, and it is reported in full on [Overlap Recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Overlap-Recovery).

**Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED.** No date-shaped hole.

## Test 4 — Is it a tracking company?

Five tracking sites, sixteen case aircraft, four unrelated controls. **Eighty page checks.**

Asked by a script, three of the five sites answer HTTP 403 — FlightRadar24, RadarBox and
Planespotters — for **every aircraft, subject and control alike, including FlightRadar24's own home
page.** That 403 is a bot filter. This site published it as a documented removal and had to retract
it; the full account is on [What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means).

Asked from a real logged-out browser, the picture is unanimous:

| Site | Subject aircraft served | Control aircraft served | Any removal found |
|---|---|---|---|
| FlightRadar24 | **16 / 16** (HTTP 200, identity block intact) | 4 / 4 | None |
| FlightAware | 16 / 16 | 5 / 5 | None |
| ADS-B Exchange | 16 / 16 | 5 / 5 | None |
| RadarBox | Script-blocked — tells us nothing either way | Script-blocked | Untestable |
| Planespotters | Script-blocked — tells us nothing either way | Script-blocked | Untestable |

**Not one page in this investigation has been removed from any tracking site.** The per-aircraft
provenance record records `removed_from: []` for all sixteen tails.

**Verdict: REFUTED.** No company-shaped hole.

## What the empty tables actually are: the retention window, measured

The strongest-looking evidence for deletion was never a 404. It was watching a page that **used to
show flights** stop showing them. That happened, it is on record in the Internet Archive, and it has
an exact explanation.

**N888KG** is the clean case, because the Internet Archive happened to capture its FlightRadar24
page fourteen times across five months:

| Snapshot date | Flight rows | Oldest flight shown | Age of that flight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 Sep 2025 *(×3 captures)* | **2** | 10 Sep 2025 | 1 day |
| 12 Sep 2025 | **2** | 10 Sep 2025 | 2 days |
| 13 Sep 2025 | **2** | 10 Sep 2025 | 3 days |
| **18 Sep 2025** *(×4 captures)* | **0** | — | *10 Sep is now **8 days** old* |
| 19–20 Sep 2025 | 0 | — | — |
| 2 Nov 2025 | 0 | — | — |
| 28 Jan 2026 | 0 | — | — |

**The rows vanish on the first capture after the 10 September flight crosses seven days old.** Not
on the day of the killing. Not on the day the story broke. On day eight.

Test that against every FlightRadar24 capture on disk, across five aircraft and six years:

> **Oldest flight visible in any archived FlightRadar24 free page: 7 days. Zero exceptions in 22
> captures spanning 2020 to 2026, Egyptian and American tails alike.**
>
> **Oldest flight visible in any archived FlightAware free page: 14 days. Zero exceptions in 16
> captures spanning 2011 to 2016.**

Two different companies, two different hard ceilings, never once exceeded. FlightRadar24's own page
prints the reason next to the empty table: *"More than 7 days of history is available with an
upgrade to Silver (90 days)…"* — and **paywall=1 on every one of the twenty rows probed, subject and
control alike.**

**This is the finding that dissolves the headline claim.** A free FlightRadar24 page for N102DZ
**never held more than seven days of history at any point in its existence.** There was no
multi-year log on that page in April 2026 for anybody to erase in May 2026. The multi-year tally the
73-overlap spreadsheet was built from did not come from that page and could not have.

## What would actually prove a deletion

So that this page is falsifiable rather than merely reassuring, here is what would overturn it. Any
one of these would be a finding:

* **A tracking-site page that loads for a control aircraft and 404s for a case aircraft, in a real
  browser, on the same day.** Eighty checks so far; zero.
* **A dated Internet Archive capture showing rows, and a later capture of the same page showing
  fewer rows for flights still inside that site's retention window.** The N888KG series looked like
  this until the seven-day arithmetic was done.
* **An aircraft-day present in one volunteer archive and absent from another, where the same
  archive holds a daily-flying control on that date.** Ten such cases exist; every one is explained
  by a whole-day archive hole that hit the controls too.
* **An FAA LADD or PIA privacy filing for one of these tails.** FlightRadar24 still prints
  N102DZ's real Mode S address `A00C85`, matching the FAA record exactly, so no PIA substitution is
  in force. A LADD blocking request would be the ordinary, lawful, documented way an owner removes a
  tail from public tracking, and it would be visible.
* **A paid-tier record for the disputed window.** FlightRadar24 Silver shows 90 days, Gold and
  Business more. **This whole investigation ran on free sources.** A single paid subscription would
  reach into windows no method here can, and that is the cheapest open lead on the page.

---

## Counterpoints

**"You only tested free tiers, so of course you found retention limits."** True, and stated. A paid
FlightRadar24 or FlightAware tier sees further back and might show something this method cannot. What
the free-tier finding *does* settle is narrower and still decisive: the specific claim was that a
*public* page was erased so the *public* could not check it, and that public page never held more
than seven days to begin with.

**"Two volunteer networks that both use the same software could both be missing the same thing."**
Fair, and it is why the controls carry the weight rather than the agreement between the two archives.
It is also why the archives were cross-checked: on the aircraft-days both hold, the two independent
networks agree to the second on timestamps and to four decimal places on position.

**"An absence of evidence for suppression is not evidence of absence."** Correct, and this page does
not claim otherwise. It claims something smaller and checkable: **four specific suppression
hypotheses were stated in advance, tested against controls, and none of them survived.** Somebody may
yet find the shape. It is not in these 15,131 queries.

**"The sweep is still running, so these numbers will move."** They will. An automated job was still
fetching event windows when this snapshot was taken at **17:42 UTC on 26 August 2026**. The counts
will grow; the four null results are structural and are not expected to.

## Where this page fits

This is the page that says which flight records were actually removed, and the answer is that after 15,131 archive queries and eighty tracking-site checks, none were — the gaps sort into an archive floor, a paywall window and volunteer receiver coverage, and every one of them fails the same way for a Ryanair 737. That matters beyond aviation, because the same discipline decides which of this investigation's other suppression claims hold: [the 403 that was published as a removal and retracted](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means) is the cautionary case, while [a named witness saying agents phoned back to confirm he had erased his own 4K footage](/CoverUp/FBI_Asked_Delete_Video) is a human on both ends of the line and survives every control a status code cannot. Between those two poles sit [warrants sealed into 2026](/Legal/Evidence-Sealing-2026), [the ATF fragment that could neither be identified nor excluded](/Proof_Not_Tyler/ATF_Inconclusive_Ballistics), and [the Egyptian-registered aircraft themselves](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika) — which are still openly published by every tracking site on earth, and are therefore an argument that has to be won on where they flew rather than on who hid them.
