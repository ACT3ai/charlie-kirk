# N55906

Tail number **N55906** circulated in open-source flight threads around **September 10, 2025** in the [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) case. **No confirmed route, timestamps, altitude figures or ADS-B track for it has ever been published**, and three independent flight archives hold zero days for the tail.

The tail number itself is not a mystery. The **FAA Civil Aviation Registry** identifies N55906 as a **1973 Piper PA-28R-200 Cherokee Arrow** — a four-seat piston single owned by a private individual in Florida. It is not a survey aircraft and not a surveillance platform, and nothing in the public record places it in Utah on any date that matters to this case.

**The most likely resolution is a one-digit misreading.** N55906 differs from **[N59906](/Planes/N59906/overview)** — the MARC Inc. survey Piper that *is* documented over the UVU campus that morning, with primary ADS-B behind it — by a single character. Every checkable detail that circulates attached to "N55906" turns out to be documented under N59906 instead. This page exists to record that, and to keep the two tails from being merged by anyone reading a screenshotted registration.

## Aircraft identification

From the **FAA Civil Aviation Registry**:

* **Tail number:** N55906
* **Type:** **Piper PA-28R-200 — a Cherokee Arrow**
* **Year built:** **1973** · **Serial number:** 28R-7335308 · **Seats:** 4
* **Mode S / ICAO hex:** **A72351** — the identifier to audit on, because registrations get retyped by hand and hex codes are copied from tracker output
* **Registered owner:** a **private individual** in Winter Park, Florida (not named here; this site does not publish the identifying details of private individuals with no established role in the case)
* **Registration status:** valid, certificate issued 2005

A PA-28R-200 is a **single-engine, four-seat piston aircraft** — a 1970s trainer and personal touring aeroplane. It is the kind of aircraft flown by private pilots on weekends. It cannot loiter at altitude, it carries no sensor capability, and it is one of the most common light aircraft types in the United States. Whatever else is true of the tail number, the airframe behind it is an ordinary light single.

## Flight history relevant to the case

The public record contains **no confirmed route, timestamps, or altitude data for N55906** in connection with September 10, 2025. No dated flight log, ADS-B track, or arrival/departure pairing has been published that this page can responsibly cite, and the recovery sweep set out further down found nothing for the tail in any free archive on any of 67 queried dates.

That absence is not proof the aircraft was elsewhere, and for a 1973 piston single it is the ordinary condition rather than an anomaly — see [the recovery result](#flight-data-recovery) below. What it does mean is that there is no evidenced claim here to build on.

## The one-digit-away tail number: N59906

The investigation also tracks **[N59906](/Planes/N59906/overview)** — the **MARC Inc.** Piper PA-31 Navajo Chieftain that flew five survey-pattern passes over the UVU campus at roughly 19,000 ft MSL on the morning of September 10, confirmed against primary ADS-B to the minute.

N55906 and N59906 differ by a single character, and they are **two genuinely distinct registrations on two genuinely distinct airframes** — a four-seat 1973 Cherokee Arrow on one side, a twin-engine survey Navajo on the other. The registry keeps them apart. What the evidence does not keep apart is the *sources*: the leading explanation for how "N55906" entered the flight threads at all is that it is a transcription of N59906, the tail that actually has a documented track.

This page therefore treats N55906 as a real but unremarkable aircraft, and treats most references to it in the case material as pointing at N59906.

## OSINT tracking on X

N55906 surfaces almost entirely through open-source threads on X, not through conventional reporting. Accounts compiling **running lists of aircraft near the event** folded the tail number into those lists, alongside other registrations scrutinised for **timing and patterns around September 10, 2025**.

None of those posts published an operator, a route, a tracker capture, or a timestamp for this tail. A registration appearing in a list is not evidence of anything by itself, and this one has never been backed by a record.

## Tracked trip in the array

The investigation's plane array records N55906 as a flagged entry rather than a logged point-to-point flight:

* **Route:** Listed as **"Various"** with the note that it fits **patterns near the event area** — no single confirmed origin or destination is published.
* **Timing:** Tied to **September 10, 2025 context** and the broader event timeframe.
* **Operators:** Recorded only as **"operators per OSINT"** — no confirmed owner or operator in public reporting.
* **Why it is listed:** it appears in open-source compilations of aircraft flagged near the event. **No supporting track, screenshot, or timestamp has been published behind the entry.**

## Open questions

1. Is any given reference to "N55906" in the case material about this aircraft, or a misreading of [N59906](/Planes/N59906/overview)?
2. Has anyone ever produced a dated flight record — a tracker capture, a hex-matched trace, a timestamp — placing this specific airframe near Utah on or around September 10, 2025?
3. Does this 1973 Cherokee Arrow carry ADS-B Out at all? That would settle whether the archive blank is an equipment fact or a coverage fact.


## Why the digit-transposition risk is now higher, not lower

The case for keeping these two tails rigidly separate got stronger once the primary records for [N59906](/Planes/N59906/overview) were published. That aircraft now has a full public identity attached to it — **Mode S hex A7C14D**, **serial number 31-7552027**, a **1974 Piper PA-31**, operator **MARC INC**, squawking a discrete **4323** on a **2-hour-39-minute Provo→Provo** sortie on September 10, 2025.

None of those identifiers belongs to N55906. When a post cites "the survey plane," "the Piper," "MARC Inc.," "the five passes over UVU," "the 19,000-foot mapping grid," or the ADS-B Exchange capture stamped **2025-09-10 18:06:10 Z**, it is describing **N59906**, whatever tail number the post itself prints.

The practical rule for anyone auditing a claim about either aircraft: **match on the hex code or the serial number, never on the printed registration.** Registrations get retyped by hand and one digit slips; Mode S hex codes and MSNs are copied from tracker output and do not.

## What a reader should do with a source that names this tail

Because so much of the circulating material transposes the digits, a claim naming N55906 falls into one of three buckets, and the third is the only one that belongs on this page:

1. **It repeats a detail that is documented for N59906** — the MARC Inc. operator, the survey payload, the September 10 morning passes, the Provo round trip. That is a mistyped reference to the other aircraft, and it should be read against [N59906](/Planes/N59906/overview) instead.
2. **It repeats a detail that is documented for neither aircraft.** Several of the anomalous-aircraft catalogs circulating on X list tail numbers with no supporting track, screenshot, or timestamp behind them. Those entries are unusable until someone produces the underlying record.
3. **It is genuinely about N55906 and carries its own evidence** — its own tracker capture, its own hex code, its own timestamps. That is the only category worth building on, and it is by far the smallest.

As of this writing, no publicly circulated ADS-B capture, Flightradar24 playback card, or FAA registry screenshot has been produced that shows **N55906** operating in the Utah Valley area on September 10, 2025. That absence is not proof the aircraft was elsewhere — it is proof that the claim has not been evidenced, which is a different and more useful thing to say.

## Flight data recovery

The recovery sweep found **no flight data for this tail in any source.** Three independent ADS-B
archives hold zero days for it, and its FlightRadar24 page loads but prints *"Sorry, but we could
not find data for specified flight"*. The reading that fits the aircraft is about equipment, not
about erasure: N55906 is a 1973 four-seat piston single (Mode S **A72351**), and a light single of
that vintage either is not fitted with ADS-B Out or is not being received by the volunteer network.
**Nothing can be removed from an archive that never recorded it.**

|---|---|
| adsb.lol `globe_history` | **nothing** — 0 days |
| globe.airplanes.live | **nothing** — 0 days |
| ADS-B Exchange monthly sample | **nothing** — 0 days |
| FlightRadar24 page | **HTTP 200 in a browser on 24 August 2026**, identity block intact — *P28R · Mode S A72351 · private owner*. **0 history rows**; the page prints the no-data message. |
| Internet Archive | **1 page snapshot** held — a FlightAware page captured **4 March 2010**; **0 flight rows** recovered off it. |

**Removed from: nothing.** No tracking site was found to have removed this aircraft's page. Our
scripted probe recorded HTTP 403 from FlightRadar24, RadarBox and Planespotters, and those 403s are
robot blocks rather than removals — five unrelated control aircraft got the identical 403 from the
identical script, and all of them load normally in a browser. See
[What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means).

It is worth being precise about what the sweep does and does not say. It does not show that anything
was taken down, and it cannot show where the aircraft was on any given day. The one observation it
supports is a narrow and sourceable one: a Piper Cherokee Arrow is a four-seat piston single, not a
survey or ISR platform, and an ADS-B blank in the volunteer archives is the ordinary condition for
that class of aircraft.

## The primary flight data we hold on this aircraft, and where it came from

**None. Not one position fix, on any date, from any free archive.**

Every other aircraft page in this investigation carries a table of aircraft-days and archives. This
one carries the opposite, and the empty result is the finding rather than an apology for the absence
of one.

**The thread marker first.** N55906 surfaced inside the **September 10 aircraft-activity** line of
inquiry. That is a **different claim** from the eighteen-month pattern of foreign-registered aircraft
alleged to have shadowed Charlie and Erika Kirk, which lives at
[Following Planes](/Planes/following/overview). This site links the two threads and never merges
them.

### What we looked for, and where

Pulled **24 August 2026**, matching on the Mode S hex **`a72351`** rather than on the printed
registration, because hex codes are copied from tracker output and registrations are retyped by
hand:

| Source | Queried | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **adsb.lol** globe history | **67 dates** across all three case windows | HTTP 404 or 403 on every one — **0 days** |
| **globe.airplanes.live** | the **same 67 dates** | HTTP 404 on every one — **0 days** |
| **ADS-B Exchange monthly sample** | **56 months** back to 2022 | **0 months found** |
| **FlightAware activity log** | live, 24 Aug 2026 | **HTTP 200 — 0 legs** |
| Internet Archive | all tracking-site URLs for this tail | **1 snapshot**, a FlightAware page from **4 March 2010**; 0 flight rows |

The three case windows are **1–16 September 2025** (the fortnight the case turns on), **8–16 October
2025**, and **25 April – 5 June 2026**. Every date in all three was asked of both archives
independently. Every one came back empty. The verdict recorded against all 67 rows in
`site/docs/Planes/following/apis/public_open_source/data/recovery/recovery_index.json` is the same
word: `NEITHER_HAS_IT`. The list of the September dates and their HTTP codes is on disk at
`site/docs/Planes/N55906/data/adsb/MISSING_DATA.md`.

**The FlightAware line is the one that carries the most weight, because it is a control rather than
an absence.** The same script, the same endpoint, the same afternoon: **N1098L returned 37 legs,
N2100L returned 37 legs, N59906 returned 13 legs. N55906 returned zero.** The method works. It found
flights for the other three aircraft on this thread. It found none for this one.

### Both readings, side by side, and we do not choose between them

**Reading one — the aircraft.** N55906 is a **1973 Piper PA-28R-200 Cherokee Arrow**, a four-seat
piston single. A light aircraft of that vintage may carry no ADS-B Out transmitter at all, and even
where one is fitted, a low-and-slow single operating from small fields is exactly the class of
aircraft the volunteer receiver networks hear worst — coverage thins out sharply below a few thousand
feet away from the big metropolitan areas. An ADS-B blank is the **ordinary condition** for this
class of aircraft, not an anomaly.

**Reading two — the limit of the method.** **An absence of a track is not evidence of anything being
hidden.** It is not evidence that the aircraft was grounded, that a transponder was switched off, or
that a record was removed. **Nothing can be removed from an archive that never recorded it.** These
archives serve what volunteer receivers happened to hear; silence in them is silence, and it does not
travel in either direction as proof.

Both of those are true at once and neither is chosen over the other here.

### What the empty result does do — it strengthens the transposition warning

This page already warns, at length, that **N55906 and [N59906](/Planes/N59906/overview) differ by a
single digit** and that the digit-transposition risk is high. **The empty result makes that warning
stronger, and the two findings should be read together.**

Hold the two tails side by side as the primary data actually returns them:

| | N55906 | [N59906](/Planes/N59906/overview) |
|---|---|---|
| Mode S hex | `a72351` | `a7c14d` |
| Type | Piper PA-28R-200, four-seat piston single | Piper PA-31 Navajo Chieftain, survey twin |
| Days of primary trace held | **0** | **48**, spanning 2022–2026 |
| Trace on 10 September 2025 | **none** | **held by both archives**, 1,289 and 1,579 points |
| Utah Valley on 10 Sep 2025 | **nothing** | **five passes over UVU at 19,000 ft, 11:01–11:35 MDT**, matching the published timeline to the minute |
| FlightAware legs, 24 Aug 2026 | **0** | 13 |

Every checkable detail that circulates attached to "N55906" — the survey passes, the mapping grid,
the Provo round trip, the MARC Inc. operator, the ADS-B Exchange capture stamped
**2025-09-10 18:06:10 Z** — now has **primary position data behind it under the other tail number**,
and **none at all under this one**. On this evidence, **a source naming N55906 has most likely meant
N59906.**

**The rule that follows is a practical one: match on the hex code, never on the printed
registration.** `a7c14d` is the survey Piper. `a72351` is a fifty-three-year-old four-seat single
that two independent archives have never heard, on any of the 67 dates that matter to this case.

### The counterargument

The counterargument to an empty result is the simplest one on this site: **the aircraft was not
there, or it was there and nobody heard it, and our method cannot tell those apart.** It also cannot
tell you whether some unpublished record exists that nobody has produced. What it does
establish is narrower and firmer than any of that: **we looked, in two independent archives, on 67
dates, plus 56 monthly full-day samples and a live activity log that returned flights for the other
three aircraft on this thread, and we found no trace of this airframe.** Our data locates aircraft;
it places no person anywhere, and it establishes nothing about tasking in either direction. **The
burden sits on the claim.**

## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — N55906](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n55906) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.
* [ADS-B Exchange — N55906](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/?reg=N55906) — volunteer-fed, unfiltered tracker that does **not** honor LADD, so it often shows blocked aircraft and tracks the filtered sites leave out.

## Dates we looked for and did not find

Not every claimed N55906 flight could be checked against a primary ADS-B trace. **Every date we queried and got nothing back for is listed — with the exact HTTP status and the reason we looked — on [ADS-B gaps for N55906](/Planes/N55906/data/adsb/MISSING_DATA).** That page is published for the same reason the recovered traces are: a gap that is written down can be argued with, and a gap that is quietly dropped cannot.

**An absence there is not a finding.** A 404 means a volunteer receiver network heard nothing on that UTC day — parked and silent, outside coverage, or a wrong claimed date all come first, and [what a 403 means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means) sets out why a refusal and a deletion look identical from outside. The four free archives that did produce data, and the control test that separates a retention boundary from a removal, are on [Flight data recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview) and [per-aircraft recovery status](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Per-Aircraft-Status).

## Where this page fits

A tail number in a list is a different thing from a finding, and this page is what happens when the records are actually pulled: N55906 resolves in the FAA registry to a 1973 four-seat Cherokee Arrow with no track in any archive on any date that matters, sitting one digit away from the [MARC survey aircraft N59906](/Planes/N59906/overview) that demonstrably did fly over campus and has the primary ADS-B to prove it. The transposition risk is not academic — it decides whether a widely repeated claim points at a surveillance flight or at a mapping contractor, and the same care is owed to everything in the [intelligence-services case](/Proof_Intel_Services/overview), the [drone counts over campus](/Topics3/Drones/overview) and every [independent researcher's thread](/Influencers/overview) built on a screenshotted tail number. The claim in this area that survives scrutiny best is not a tail number at all: [Joe Kent's foreign inquiry shutdown](/Proof_Intel_Services/Joe_Kent_Foreign_Inquiry_Shutdown) has a sitting intelligence director saying on the record that his center was told to stop once a suspect was in custody, with leads still open, and [Law 2](/laws/US_Intel/Law_2_US_Intel) is the attempt to force those leads back into daylight.
