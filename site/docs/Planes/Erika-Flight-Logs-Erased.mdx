# Erika Flight Logs Erased

**No dated itinerary for Erika Kirk has ever been published by anyone.** Not by the compilers of the
overlap count, not by the broadcasters who repeated it, not by the outlets that carried it, and not
by her. **Of 139 sourced Turning Point and Kirk appearances catalogued in
[the speaking events index](/Planes/following/speaking/overview), exactly one places her at an event
before 10 September 2025 with a firm date** — a June 2025 summit in Grapevine, Texas, with no
Egyptian-registered aircraft anywhere in Texas.

**The register of claimed overlaps holds 85 rows. Its `erika_present` column is never `yes` on a
single one of them.** Recovered ADS-B can reach 29 of the 85 and confirms the aircraft at the
claimed field on 25 — while confirming **zero** of the person the count is named for.

| | |
|---|---|
| Sourced Turning Point / Kirk appearances catalogued | **139** |
| **Appearances placing Erika Kirk at an event before 10 Sept 2025 with a firm date** | **1** — Grapevine, TX, June 2025 |
| Claimed overlap rows in the register | **85** |
| Rows where `erika_present` reads `claimed` | 70 |
| Rows where `erika_present` reads `not_claimed` | 15 |
| **Rows where `erika_present` reads `yes`** | **0** |
| Rows recovered ADS-B can decide | 29 |
| Of those, rows where the **aircraft** is confirmed at the claimed field | **25** |
| **Rows where the person is confirmed** | **0** |
| Aircraft-days now held across 17 aircraft, all from free open archives | **2,346** |
| Of those, days pulled from inside the two windows adsb.lol serves nothing for | **325** |
| [N102DZ](/Planes/N102DZ/overview) aircraft-days held, 1 May 2022 – 5 June 2026 | **66** |
| Of those, days inside the 25 April – 5 June 2026 window the removal was reported for | **35** |

**Read the two halves of that table against each other.** The aircraft half of every overlap claim is
well documented and getting better. **The person half is empty and is not getting better.** No
archive fixes it, because it is not an archive problem — volunteer ADS-B networks record airframes,
never passengers.

**The removal claim itself has been tested, and the verdicts are below unaltered** — including the
one that runs against this site's own earlier reporting. The page-takedown claim is **REFUTED**;
what the page displayed in May 2026 remains beyond every method used here, in either direction.


This page covers a narrow, checkable dispute in the aircraft angle of the case: **Erika Kirk publicly invited people to examine the flight logs of the jet [N102DZ](/Planes/N102DZ/overview)** — and researchers who took her up on it say the public tracking history for that tail number **was removed from FlightRadar24 in May 2026**, after which no member of the public can independently pull it. The wider person-side cluster — the 73-count, the Instagram substitution, the 10 September whereabouts dispute — is on **[Erika Kirk's flights](/Planes/following/Erika_Kirk_Flights)**.

Everything below is **reported claim and public statement**, not established fact. No conclusion about anyone's conduct should be drawn from a records removal alone; aircraft owners can and routinely do request blocking of their tail numbers from public tracking sites through ordinary, lawful privacy programs, and that is one of the plainest explanations available. It is also worth noting that no flight record of any kind — this tail number included — has featured in the state's prosecution of [Tyler Robinson](/Tyler_Robinson/overview), who is charged but not convicted, so the aircraft strand of the investigation runs entirely outside the criminal case.

## What she said

In the clip on the right, Erika Kirk answers the flight-tracking questions directly and dismissively. Transcribed from the video:

> "You really think I'm going to be on an Egyptian plane while I am wanting to be with my In-N-Out burger and my chocolate milkshake? No, I'm not. So if you want to go through my flight log, go right ahead. It very boring. You say I was here on this date. I have a photo on my phone to prove that I actually was in the hospital, because I was having contractions. So game on."

Two things are worth separating in that statement. The first is a **denial** — that she was aboard the foreign-registered aircraft discussed on the [Planes Following Charlie or Erika](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika) page. The second is an **invitation**: *"if you want to go through my flight log, go right ahead."* She also offers a specific alibi for at least one contested date — that she was in hospital with contractions and has a timestamped photo.

## The researcher response

The X account **@hurtfeelingzday**, posting the clip on July 21, 2026, argued the invitation is hollow because the records it points to are no longer public:

> "…says we can check her flight logs of her jet N102DZ? The same flight logs that were ERASED in May 2026 from flightradar24 so now NOBODY can check them? But too late I &amp; others have them. They are far from boring."

The post makes three separate claims, and they carry very different evidentiary weight:

| Claim | Type | Status |
|---|---|---|
| N102DZ's public history was removed from FlightRadar24 in May 2026 | Checkable against the tracking site | **REFUTED** — the page loads HTTP 200 in a browser with its identity block intact, in two independent sessions on 24 Aug 2026, and nothing on it says removed, blocked or restricted. Its flight table is empty, but so is an unrelated control aircraft's to within eight bytes, because FR24 shows only 7 days to logged-out visitors. **Limit: what the table held in May 2026 is not reachable by this method in either direction** |
| Copies of the logs were saved before removal | Claim of possession | **This site now holds one** — recovered from the Internet Archive, not from the poster |
| The saved logs are "far from boring" | Characterisation | **Not borne out by what we recovered** — see below |

**Updated 28 August 2026 — the position on this page has changed, and it changed
in a way that makes the underlying problem sharper, not softer.**

| Claim | Status as of today |
|---|---|
| Aircraft flight records are unavailable to anyone outside | **No longer true.** 2,346 aircraft-days are now held across 17 aircraft, all from free open archives, including **325 pulled from inside the two windows adsb.lol serves nothing for** and 111 filed today out of data already sitting in this repository |
| **Erika Kirk's own itinerary** | **STILL GONE, and no backup anywhere produces it** |

That contrast is the whole point of this page now. **The aircraft half of every
overlap claim is well documented and getting better. The person half is empty and

The register of claimed overlaps holds **85 rows**. Its `erika_present` column is
`claimed` on 70 of them and `not_claimed` on 15. **It is never `yes` on any row.**
Recovered ADS-B can reach 29 of the 85 and confirms the aircraft at the claimed

**No archive fixes this, because it is not an archive problem.** Volunteer ADS-B

## We went and got the logs back

This page originally said the removal made the record unavailable to anyone outside. **That is no
longer the position, and the change is worth stating plainly rather than quietly editing.**

Two separate things turned out to be wrong with it. **First, the removal itself could not be
confirmed, and has since been affirmatively ruled out** — tested in a browser on 24 August 2026, the
FlightRadar24 page loads HTTP 200 and still holds the aircraft's record, and the empty flight table
it shows is the same empty table a control aircraft shows, because the free tier displays seven days
and nothing more. The test was then widened: **all sixteen aircraft in this investigation and four
unrelated control aircraft were probed, and every single one returned HTTP 200.** The 403 that
started this was FlightRadar24 refusing scripted requests, which it does to a Ryanair 737 and a
Lufthansa A380 just as readily. See
**[What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means)**. **Second, and regardless of that, the
data was recoverable anyway.**

Treating "the logs were erased" as a working assumption, this investigation went looking for
backups. The full method and results are on **[Flight Data
Recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview)**. Three things came back:

* **The FlightRadar24 flight-history table itself.** The Internet Archive holds a copy of the
  N102DZ page captured **11 September 2025, 05:57 UTC** — about twenty hours after the
  assassination, and roughly eight months before the reported removal. It contains **eleven flight
  rows** covering 4–10 September 2025 with scheduled and actual times. It is reproduced in full on
  **[N102DZ](/Planes/N102DZ/overview)**.
* **The underlying ADS-B record, which was never gone at all.** N102DZ's tracks remain retrievable
  from free archives FlightRadar24 does not control — **including right through May 2026**, the
  month of the reported removal, on nearly every day the aircraft flew. Whatever happened at
  FlightRadar24 did not remove the flight data from the world.
* **Days one archive refuses.** Dates that return HTTP 403 from adsb.lol were recovered from two
  independent backups that agree with each other to the second.

### What the recovered logs actually contain

The poster said the logs are "far from boring." **The recovered table does not support that.** The
eleven rows are ordinary point-to-point business-jet legs — Scottsdale, Dallas, New Braunfels, Los
Angeles, Phoenix, Salt Lake City, Provo, Camarillo. There is no foreign destination in them, no
Egyptian airport, and nothing that corroborates the aircraft-swap reading the wider thread advances.

**The recovered record does support Erika Kirk's flat denial on one specific point.** She said she
was not aboard "an Egyptian plane." Nothing in the recovered N102DZ history contradicts her, and
nothing in it places this aircraft anywhere near the foreign-registered tails.

It also **corrected a detail this site had published.** A widely repeated claim that N102DZ
*"arrived into Scottsdale at 1:13 p.m. and departed again immediately at 1:13 p.m."* turns out to
be FlightRadar24's **flight-duration column** read a second time as a clock time. The real ground
time was about eighteen minutes.

### What recovery does not fix

**The important limitation, and it is fatal to the thing this dispute is actually about.** Every
recovery here is about *an aircraft*. **An aircraft track never establishes who was aboard it.**
Recovering N102DZ's movements in full still does not place Erika Kirk on any leg, on any date, and
the free FlightRadar24 page only ever displayed **seven days** of history — so no archived copy of
it could ever have held the multi-year record the 73-overlap tally was built from.

**Her itinerary remains the missing document, and no backup anywhere produces it.** That is why this
page still exists.

### Update, 24 August 2026 — the aircraft side went much further, and the person side did not move

**The aircraft-side recovery is now far larger than it was when this section was first written.**
For N102DZ alone the investigation holds **66 days of primary position trace, spanning 1 May 2022 to
5 June 2026**, pulled from three independent free archives. **Thirty-five of those days fall inside
the 25 April – 5 June 2026 window the removal was reported for** — the exact stretch the record was
said to have been taken out of. **Thirty-eight of the 66 days exist only in the backup network**; the
primary archive holds nothing for them. Method, sources and the per-aircraft counts are on
**[Flight Data Recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview)** and
**[Per-Aircraft Status](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Per-Aircraft-Status)**.

**And a correction to how this page treats the removal question.** The scripted probe that produced
the original HTTP 403 has now been control-tested, and **it cannot see what it was being asked to
see.** On 24 August 2026 `flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n102dz` returned 403 to a script — and so
did all twelve other case tails, seven entirely unrelated control aircraft, the FlightRadar24 index
page, and **FlightRadar24's own home page**, every one of them returning the identical ~5,877-byte
body. **FlightRadar24 returns 403 to scripted requests across its entire site.** That status code is
bot protection. It is not about any aircraft, and **it cannot distinguish a removed record from a
present one in either direction.**

Neither confirmed by the 403, then, nor refuted by it. Any conclusion about that page has to rest on
a real browser session and on nothing else. **No claim on this site should rest on the HTTP status a
script received**, and this page has previously stated the question more strongly than the method
could support. **A control test is what corrected it**, and running one is the cheapest protection
this investigation has against believing its own strongest-looking material.

**That browser session has now been run, twice, and it settles the removal claim: REFUTED.** The
first run probed sixteen case tails and four unrelated controls in a real logged-out browser —
**HTTP 200, every one.** A second, independent session the same day reproduced it across six more
probes fetched from inside one live session, again **HTTP 200, every one.** For N102DZ the live page
returns page title `N102DZ - Gulfstream V [555] - Flightradar24`, MODE S **A00C85**, TYPE CODE
**GLF5**, and **nothing on it says removed, blocked, restricted or unavailable. Nothing was taken
down.** The empty history table is the **seven-day free-tier window**, and the control proves it: the
unrelated aircraft **N628TS prints the identical "could not find data for specified flight" message
at 53,318 bytes against N102DZ's 53,310**, while SU-BTT, SU-BND and a Ryanair control all render
history because they had flown recently. Both runs are on disk at
`site/docs/Planes/following/apis/browser_capture/captures/fr24_page_availability_2026-08-24.tsv` and
`.../fr24_n102dz_removal_test_2026-08-24b.tsv`.

**The limit travels with the refutation.** A page that loads today cannot testify about what its
table held in **May 2026**. **"The page was taken down" is refuted; a narrower claim that history was
specifically purged and later restored is beyond this method in either direction**, and this site does
not assert it. A logged-out free tier is also the weakest view FlightRadar24 offers — a paid tier
shows 90 days to 3 years, and we have not looked through one.

**None of which moves the thing this page is actually about.** Sixty-six recovered aircraft-days,
thirty-five of them inside the disputed window, three archives, four years of coverage — and **not
one of them produces an Erika Kirk itinerary.** An aircraft track records an airframe, never an
occupant. The 73-overlap tally is still measured against a set of Erika Kirk locations that **nobody
has published as a dated list** — not the compilers, not the broadcasters, not the outlets that
repeated it, and not her. Of **139 sourced Turning Point and Kirk appearances** catalogued in
[the speaking events index](/Planes/following/speaking/overview), **exactly one** places her at an
event before 10 September 2025 with a firm date. **That gap has not narrowed by a single day**, and
the good news about aircraft data must not be allowed to read as though it had.

### Update, 26 August 2026 — the deletion claim was then tested for a *pattern*, and there is not one

The refutation above is about one page on one site. The obvious next question is whether records
were being removed **anywhere**, and if so **whose**. Every archive query this investigation has
ever logged — **15,131 of them across sixteen case aircraft, two unrelated control airliners and
917 dates** — was sorted four ways: **by aircraft nationality, by named person, by date, and by
tracking site.**

* **Egyptian tails:** FlightRadar24 serves SU-BTT, SU-BTU and SU-BTV openly today with **56, 56 and
  54** history rows, labelled "Egypt - Air Force". The aircraft showing *zero* rows are the American
  ones and an unrelated Nike jet.
* **Erika Kirk's dates:** recovered on 57.1% of dates where she is claimed present against 88.9%
  where she is not — **Fisher exact p = 0.133**, not significant.
* **Charlie Kirk's dates:** 75.0% against 56.8% — the difference runs the *wrong way*, **p = 0.329**.
* **The event days:** day-of-event is the **peak** in coverage, not the trough.
* **And 26 days of primary N102DZ position trace sit on disk inside May 2026**, the month the
  history was said to have been erased.

The full working is on **[Which Flight Records Were Deleted — And Whose](/Planes/Deleted-Flight-Records)**,
including the one thing that genuinely did change in the disputed window: **N102DZ's FAA
registration**, re-issued 16 October 2025 to a Wyoming holding company — the only case-central
aircraft whose registration moved after 10 September 2025.

## What the overlap sheet actually used in place of her flight logs

The reason her logs matter is now visible in a way it was not before: a screenshot of the source spreadsheet behind the overlap counts shows exactly what was substituted for them.

The sheet's column headers read:

`Index` · `Date` · `Flight` · `Route` · **`Erika Location`** · `Event/Reason` · `Overlap Window` · `Charlie Present` · `Notes`

**`Erika Location` is a free-text cell with no source column beside it.** Its values, transcribed from the visible extract, are:

> New York · Illinois · Massachusetts · Kansas · Wilmington, DE · St. Louis, MO · Missouri / Maine · Georgia · Delaware / Georgia · St. Louis, MO · Wilmington, DE · Kansas · Wichita / DE / potentially Giza · Missouri · Missouri / Cairo · Utah · Nebraska · Delaware / Nebraska · California · Nebraska · Delaware / Omaha · Kansas · Kansas / Delaware · Utah (Provo/SLC) · Utah (Provo) · Delaware / Provo · Nebraska / Delaware · Provo, Utah

Most of these are **states, not places.** Several are **two states joined by a slash.** One reads **"Wichita / DE / potentially Giza"** — three locations on two continents, with the word *potentially* doing the work in a cell that is being counted as a location match.

**The `Notes` column names the sourcing, and it is Instagram.** The recurring entries are:

> "Instagram confirms presence; state-level overlap" · "State-level overlap; media posts confirm" · "Instagram / media confirmation" · "Instagram confirms Georgia presence" · "Instagram confirms Nebraska visit" · "California state overlap confirmed via posts"

That is the answer to the question this page exists to ask. **With her flight logs unavailable, the sheet placed Erika Kirk by reading her social media** — undated, unlinked, unarchived posts, summarised in a notes field as "Instagram confirms presence."

### Why that is a weaker substitute than it sounds

Using public social media to establish someone's location is a legitimate OSINT technique. Used this way, it has four specific failure modes, and the sheet's own structure exposes all four:

* **A post is not a timestamp.** People post from a location days after being there, and they post scheduled or backdated content. Nothing in the sheet records when a post was made versus when the depicted event occurred.
* **A post is not a coordinate.** "Instagram confirms Georgia presence" places a person in a state of eleven million people over an unspecified window. Matching that against an aircraft that landed in Atlanta is not a location overlap; it is two things happening in the same state.
* **Nothing is preserved.** No URL, no screenshot, no archive link, no date. **Not one of these cells can be checked by a reader.** If a post has since been deleted or the account made private, the claim is now permanently unverifiable — and it was published as though it were a record.
* **It compounds with the `Overlap Window` column, which reads "±3 days" on every row.** So the actual matching rule is: *an aircraft in the same state, within a seven-day window, of a place a person appeared to be according to an unlinked social-media post.*

### The column that answers the question directly

The same sheet carries a **`Charlie Present`** column. In the visible 35-row extract it reads **"No" on 32 rows** and **"Yes" on three** — and the three are all in 2025 (3/15, 3/22 and 5/23).

That is worth stating plainly because it inverts how this material is usually presented. **On the sheet's own data, these aircraft were overwhelmingly not overlapping with Charlie Kirk at all.** The overlap claim is, structurally, an Erika Kirk claim — which is precisely why the absence of her flight logs is not a side issue in this investigation. **It is the missing foundation under the largest number in the case.**

### What this does to the counts

The [line-by-line audit](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika) of the same sheet found that two-thirds of the claimed overlaps were wrong on the *aircraft* side alone — the plane was in Egypt, or on another continent, or the flight happened on a different date. That audit tested only the flight half of each row. **The `Erika Location` half was never tested by anyone**, because there is nothing in the sheet to test it against.

So the honest position on this page is sharper than "her logs are unavailable." It is: **the overlap counts rest on a location series for Erika Kirk that has no published source, no dates, no links, and no archive — and which the sheet itself describes as coming from Instagram.** Recovering her actual flight records would not merely add a data point. It would be the first time the person side of the overlap had any documentary basis at all.

### What would settle it

* **Charter records** for whatever aircraft carried her, held by the operator regardless of what any public tracker shows.
* **[N102DZ](/Planes/N102DZ/overview)** and any other Kirk-associated tails' logs for the claimed dates.
* **The sheet's underlying citations** — if the compiler retains the Instagram posts, dates and URLs behind the `Erika Location` column, publishing them would let every row be checked in an afternoon. Their absence from a published sheet is the gap.


## Why this matters to the investigation

The aircraft angle rests almost entirely on **public ADS-B data** — the same open records that let independent researchers assemble the overlap counts described on [Planes Following Charlie or Erika](/Planes/Following-Charlie-Erika). That method has one structural weakness: it only works while the data stays public. If a tail number central to the analysis stops being publicly queryable, then every claim about it — supporting *or* refuting — becomes unfalsifiable for outsiders. Researchers cannot confirm an overlap, and the aircraft's occupants cannot clear it either.

That is the practical significance of this dispute, and it cuts in both directions. It is also why the reform proposals in the [Fix](/Fix/overview) section emphasise **preserving and compelling disclosure of records** rather than relying on voluntary public availability.

## Counterpoints

* **Blocking a tail number is ordinary.** Private aircraft owners routinely request removal from public tracking through established privacy programs. A removal in May 2026 is fully consistent with a privacy request and implies nothing on its own.
* **The alibi is testable.** Erika Kirk states she has a dated photo placing her in hospital on at least one contested date. That is a concrete, checkable rebuttal, and it has not been disproven here.
* **Ownership and occupancy are different questions.** A jet's movements do not establish who was aboard on any given leg. Much of the dispute conflates the two.
* **The saved logs have not been published.** The claim that copies exist and are damaging cannot be evaluated until the data itself is produced.

### What these tables cannot tell you

* **A trace proves presence. It never proves purpose, and it never proves occupancy.** No ADS-B record anywhere places any person aboard any aircraft. An aircraft near an event is an airframe near an event.
* **Erika Kirk's flight logs are reported erased, and no archive anywhere produces her itinerary.** Every row above is anchored to a sourced *event* she is recorded as attending, not to a document saying where she was. This is the single largest hole in the whole overlap claim.
* **The sourced Erika event calendar is short.** These tables can only test the events this repository can source. That is nowhere near every place she went, so an empty row is a statement about our calendar as much as about any aircraft.
* **An absence is not a finding.** A day with no row may be a day a volunteer receiver network heard nothing. Transponder off, outside coverage, and genuinely elsewhere all look identical from here.
* **2022 is effectively blank.** No free archive covers it, so no row above and no gap above can speak to that year.
