# N40JD Provo Arrival

A **Beechcraft Premier 1** (6-seat light business jet) registered **N40JD** is listed in flight records circulated by independent investigators as one of several private aircraft arriving at **Provo Municipal Airport (PVU)** on September 10, 2025, the day [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) was assassinated at Utah Valley University in nearby Orem. It is on the investigation's aircraft list for one narrow reason: to build a complete picture of who flew into Provo around the event so that no traveler is overlooked. Nothing in the public record ties this aircraft, its crew, or its passengers to the shooting.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** N40JD
* **Type:** Beechcraft Premier 1 — light business jet, reported 6 seats
* **Registry / operator / owner:** Not publicly identified in the investigation record; FAA registration would name the registrant.
* **Program / callsign:** None reported. Tracked by its civil registration N40JD.
* **Role on the list:** A private aircraft logged arriving at Provo (PVU) on the afternoon of September 10, 2025.

## Flight history relevant to the case

As reproduced from flight-record screenshots circulated by independent investigators:

* **Departed** Scottsdale, Arizona at **~12:27 p.m. local**.
* **Arrived** into Provo (PVU) at **~2:29 p.m. MDT**.

This is an afternoon arrival, after the shooting (the shot was fired shortly after noon MDT). The same set of records groups N40JD with the other same-day inbound private jets — Charlie Kirk's own aircraft [N102DZ](/Planes/N102DZ/overview), which reached Provo at ~3:31 p.m., and [N872RA](/Planes/N872RA/overview), which arrived from Santa Barbara at ~4:36 p.m. The morning flight that first drew [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens)' attention, [N560TW](/Planes/N560TW/overview), is a separate aircraft on the same Provo list. No public reporting establishes a connection among these flights beyond their shared destination and date.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

The flight's public tracking has been **referenced in podcasts and independent investigations** as part of broader arrival/departure pattern analysis around UVU and Provo. Some of those investigator discussions have, **according to commentary circulated on X**, connected the flight to figures such as [**Tyler Bowyer**](/People/tyler-bowyer), a [Turning Point USA](/TPUSA/overview) figure.

That association is **reported by commentators and unproven**. There is no evidence in the public record tying Mr. Bowyer, the aircraft, its crew, or its passengers to the event, and no wrongdoing by him is alleged or established. It is noted here only to explain why the flight has drawn discussion. The page treats N40JD as an open item on a manifest-review list — not as a theory of involvement.

The 2:29 p.m. Provo arrival time has also been swept into the broader **Israel/Egyptian-plane theory** circulating on X. An unverified screenshot, shared in those threads, claims that a parked Egyptian-registered jet ([SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview)) sitting on the ground at Provo on September 10 powered its transponder on **only three times that day**, and labels the third of those moments as a **2:29 p.m. Provo landing** — the same minute these records log N40JD touching down. That is the entire connection: a matching clock time on a screenshot, presented by its poster as significant.

This is an **unverified online claim**, not established fact, and it should be read with the same skepticism the rest of the Israel/Egyptian-plane material draws. Critics such as [**Liz Wheeler**](/People/liz-wheeler) argue these overlap claims rely on loose criteria and that several rest on **UTC versus local time-zone errors** in the underlying logs, which can make unrelated events appear synchronized. Grok and other reviewers note that public ADS-B data shows aircraft activity but establishes **no proven surveillance or assassination link**, and the official finding remains a lone shooter. Nothing about the shared 2:29 p.m. timestamp identifies who was aboard N40JD or ties the flight to any plan.

## Why this flight is scrutinized

N40JD earns its place on the investigation list because, **per some discussions and X OSINT**, its arrival into Provo has been flagged as **mysterious or scrutinized** within the event window — described by some posters as occurring **pre- or around** the September 10 timeframe rather than cleanly after it. The aircraft is **flagged in citizen timelines** that catalog every plane logged at PVU around the assassination.

* Treated as part of a **broader Provo/UVU arrival and departure pattern analysis** circulating on X, where investigators map who flew in and out around the event.
* **Tracked in podcasts and independent investigations** rather than mainstream reporting; public ADS-B history is the underlying data.
* Tied, **per some discussions and unconfirmed**, to **private operators with possible links to TPUSA figures** such as **Tyler Bowyer** — an association that is **alleged, unverified, and not established as fact**. No wrongdoing by Mr. Bowyer is alleged or proven, and nothing connects him, the crew, or the passengers to the shooting.
* **Public details remain limited** — the operator, owner, and occupants are not named in the public record, which is part of why the flight stays an open item.

These points reflect **online commentary and citizen-timeline framing**, not confirmed findings. They are recorded here to explain the scrutiny, not to assert that the flight had any role in the event.

## Aircraft identification, confirmed

The FAA Civil Aviation Registry confirms the reported type and corrects the seat count. N40JD is a **Raytheon Aircraft Company 390** — a **Premier I** light jet — serial **RB-35**, **8 seats** in the type record (not 6), registered to **NUP Aviation LLC** of Princeton, Illinois.

## Flight data recovery

Coverage for this tail is thin rather than absent — **10, 12 and 11 days** across the three
independent archives, and airplanes.live stops carrying it after **16 October 2025**. Thin coverage
is what a lightly-flown private aircraft over a sparse volunteer receiver network looks like; it is
not evidence of anything having been taken away. The FlightRadar24 page loads in a browser and
served **56 history rows** on 24 August 2026.

| Source | What it holds for N40JD |
|---|---|
| adsb.lol `globe_history` | 10 days, 2 Sep 2025 – 16 Sep 2025 |
| globe.airplanes.live | 12 days, 2 Sep 2025 – 16 Oct 2025 |
| ADS-B Exchange monthly sample | 11 days, 1 Jun 2022 – 1 Apr 2026 |
| FlightRadar24 page | HTTP 200 in a browser on 24 Aug 2026; 56 history rows on the free seven-day tier; older history paywalled |
| Internet Archive | 1 page snapshot held (FlightRadar24, 22 May 2022); 0 flight rows recovered off it — that capture's flight table was present but empty |

**Nothing here was found to have been removed.** No tracking site was found to have taken this
aircraft's page down. Our scripted probe recorded HTTP 403 from FlightRadar24, RadarBox and
Planespotters — but the identical script got the identical 403 for five unrelated control aircraft
chosen for having nothing to do with this case, so a 403 is a robot block and says nothing about
this tail. See [What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means).

Where a day is absent from an archive, that means a volunteer receiver network heard nothing on that
day. It does not establish that the aircraft did not fly, and it does not establish that a
transponder was switched off. Both free daily archives begin in 2023, so every 2022 date is
untestable by construction rather than missing.

The raw files are on disk at `site/docs/Planes/N40JD/data/recovered/` — the source is in every
filename, and each file carries a `.meta.json` sidecar recording the source, the URL, the HTTP
status, the byte count and the date, so any line above can be checked without trusting this page.

This is the thinnest record of the four Kirk-side tails, and the reason is visible in the numbers:
**ten of the twelve days the backup network holds are also held by adsb.lol.** The two archives
agree almost completely, and only two days needed the second source. A lightly-flown light jet
simply generates fewer aircraft-days than a charter airframe does. **Thin coverage of a
lightly-flown aircraft is what a volunteer receiver network looks like. It is not evidence that
anything was taken away.**

### The 2022 trace, on this site for the first time

`samples.adsbexchange.com` publishes a free full-day sample for **the first of every month**, back
to 2016, with no account and no key. One of those sampled days falls in 2022 for N40JD.

| Sampled day | First fix | Last fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 June 2022 | Northwest Florida Beaches Intl, Panama City Beach, FL (KECP, 0.45 km) | a private strip near Indianola, Mississippi (7.69 km) |

Positions are the nearest airport and the distance to it — **geometry, not a landing record**, and
7.69 km is a long way from a runway. The strip is a privately owned field, and we name the place
rather than the property. The sampling caveat travels with the row: **this is one day per month.**
One sampled day establishes where the aircraft was on 1 June 2022 and supports nothing about any
other date in that year.

Read against the 23-day record, it points the same way the rest of the data does. **Scottsdale is
this aircraft's base** — 31 touches, six times more than any other field — and Panama City Beach,
the origin of the 2022 sampled day, is the second most touched at 5. The 10 September 2025 leg this
page is built around **departed Scottsdale**. A Scottsdale-based light jet departing Scottsdale is
the ordinary case, not the anomaly.

### The one archived page, and the trap it avoids

The Internet Archive holds exactly **one capture** of an N40JD tracking page: FlightRadar24, **22
May 2022**, HTTP 200, 56,801 bytes. Its flight-history table is present and **empty**. Our sidecar
labels it `PRESENT_BUT_EMPTY`, and the temptation with a capture like that is obvious — an empty
history table on an archived tracking page looks like a record that was pulled.

It is not, and this capture proves it out of its own text. The archived page carries FlightRadar24's
own message:

> "More than 7 days of N40JD history is available with an upgrade to a Silver (90 days), Gold (1
> year), or Business (3 years) subscription."

**That is the free tier's seven-day history window, not a removal.** The aircraft had not flown in
the seven days before 22 May 2022, so the free page had nothing to show. This is the identical
mechanism that dissolved the strongest-looking removal claim in this whole investigation, on
[N888KG](/Planes/N888KG/overview), where a table that emptied out between 13 and 18 September 2025
turned out to be the same seven-day window rolling forward. **The evidence for that explanation is
sitting in this tail's own archived file**, at
`site/docs/Planes/N40JD/data/recovered/N40JD_20220522070338_wayback_flightradar24.html`.

### This tail is not in the overlap sheet

None of the 85 claimed overlap rows in `site/docs/Planes/following/overlaps.csv` names N40JD. The
sheet names SU-BTT, SU-BND, or no aircraft at all. N40JD belongs to the **Kirk-side** aircraft —
tails that bear on where people were, not tails alleged to have followed anyone. **Do not merge
those threads.** The 18-month following pattern, the 10 September day-of timeline, the
[N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview) thread and the [N888KG](/Planes/N888KG/overview) Wendover
departure are four separate claims about four separate aircraft sets. That matters especially on
this page, which already records an attempt to sweep a 2:29 p.m. clock reading into the
Egyptian-plane thread on the strength of a matching minute.

The limit that governs everything above: **position data locates an aircraft, not a person.** Twenty-three
days of track establish nothing about who was aboard N40JD on 10 September 2025 or on any other
date, and nothing here supports or undercuts the unverified commentary associating the flight with
any named individual. No wrongdoing by anyone is alleged or established. Private manifests are not
public — which is what the [disclosure laws](/Fix/overview) are proposed to change.

### The counterargument

The plain reading of all of it is that N40JD is a lightly-flown Scottsdale-based light jet with a
sparse but entirely ordinary public flight record, and that a Scottsdale-to-Provo leg on the
afternoon of 10 September 2025 — after the shooting — is one more unremarkable trip. Nothing we
pulled contradicts that. The empty 2022 archived page, which is the only thing on this tail that
could be mistaken for a removal, explains itself in its own text. **On this evidence there is
nothing anomalous about this aircraft at all**, and its place on the arrival list is completeness,
not suspicion.

### Why pulling this data matters at all

The claim this section answers is not about N40JD. It is the broader claim that this category of
record has been erased. **We pulled nearly four years of ordinary flying for this aircraft, on
demand, from free archives.** What came back is unremarkable business aviation, and the one artefact
that looked like a deletion turned out to be a paywall. That is a finding: a record that
reconstructs this easily has not been scrubbed, and reporting a recovery honestly means reporting
what it actually shows rather than what would have been more interesting.

## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — N40JD](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n40jd) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.
* [ADS-B Exchange — N40JD](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/?reg=N40JD) — volunteer-fed, unfiltered tracker that does **not** honor LADD, so it often shows blocked aircraft and tracks the filtered sites leave out.

## Dates we looked for and did not find

Not every claimed N40JD flight could be checked against a primary ADS-B trace. **Every date we queried and got nothing back for is listed — with the exact HTTP status and the reason we looked — on [ADS-B gaps for N40JD](/Planes/N40JD/data/adsb/MISSING_DATA).** That page is published for the same reason the recovered traces are: a gap that is written down can be argued with, and a gap that is quietly dropped cannot.

**An absence there is not a finding.** A 404 means a volunteer receiver network heard nothing on that UTC day — parked and silent, outside coverage, or a wrong claimed date all come first, and [what a 403 means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means) sets out why a refusal and a deletion look identical from outside. The four free archives that did produce data, and the control test that separates a retention boundary from a removal, are on [Flight data recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview) and [per-aircraft recovery status](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Per-Aircraft-Status).

## Where this page fits

This page closes a hole rather than opening one — a light business jet on the Provo arrival list for September 10, catalogued beside [N872RA](/Planes/N872RA/overview) and [N560TW](/Planes/N560TW/overview) so no traveller into [Provo](/Locations/Provo_Airport) that afternoon goes unlisted, with the day's movements assembled on the [September 10 flight timeline](/Planes/Sept10-Flight-Timeline). It also shows how a coincidence becomes a claim: one screenshot matched its 2:29 p.m. landing minute to an Egyptian jet's transponder, and that is the entire connection — the kind of reasoning the [foreign intelligence claims](/Theories/Foreign_Intelligence_Claims) and the [Egyptian operations at Provo](/intelligence/Egyptian_Foreign_Ops) have to be read against. For the strongest version of the following argument, read [Egyptian foreign operations at Provo](/intelligence/Egyptian_Foreign_Ops), which reports SU-BTT sitting on that field from September 4 and departing around 7:08 a.m. on the morning of the killing — then read [the overlap window definition](/Planes/following/Overlap_Window_Definition) to see how loosely the word overlap was defined before deciding what any of it is worth.
