# ISR Program Context

ISR — **Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance** — is the umbrella term for military and contracted aviation missions that collect intelligence in real time over a target area. This page is the **program-level hub** for the aircraft in this section that are tied to active US Army ISR work — chiefly the **LASAI Aviation** fleet of Bombardier Global 6500 business jets flown under the Army's **[HADES](/US_Intelligence/US_Army_HADES)** program. Two of those tail numbers are catalogued individually: the [N1098L spy plane](/Planes/N1098L/overview), documented descending over the Orem / Utah Valley University corridor on September 10, 2025, and its fleet sister ship [N2100L](/Planes/N2100L/overview). Read this page first to understand what ISR capability is and why an Army-tasked ISR jet in the UVU airspace window matters; then read the two aircraft pages for the specific flight data.

## What ISR aviation does

ISR-equipped aircraft typically combine some or all of the following capabilities:

* **Signals intelligence (SIGINT):** Intercepting radio, cellular, and other electronic emissions in the target area.
* **Imagery intelligence (IMINT):** Real-time electro-optical and infrared sensors with operator-grade resolution.
* **Synthetic aperture radar (SAR):** Through-cloud and through-foliage ground imaging.
* **Drone deployment and recovery:** Releasing and retrieving small unmanned systems for closer-in collection — a capability the Army has publicly said it wants to test from the Global 6500 HADES airframe (per *The War Zone* / TWZ reporting cited in the case notes).
* **Communications relay:** Acting as an airborne node for ground-team communications.

A platform with these sensors flying near an event does not, by itself, prove anything about that event. What it does establish is that **records may exist** — and that is the entire point of the questions below.

## The HADES program

**HADES — High Accuracy Detection & Exploitation System** — is the US Army's planned ISR aircraft program-of-record built on the **Bombardier Global 6500** airframe. It is intended to replace and consolidate several legacy ISR platforms (the Army's earlier ARTEMIS, ARES, and ATHENA prototype jets). According to the case notes, the HADES program is run out of **[Biggs Army Airfield](/Planes/Airport-Biggs-Army-Airfield) (KBIF)** in El Paso, Texas, and is associated with program lead [**Bradley Hansell**](/People/bradley-hansell).

The aircraft **N1098L** (ICAO hex **A0299E**, callsign **AXLE10** — also rendered **AXEL10** in some ADS-B logs) is publicly documented as part of this Global-6500 ISR ecosystem, operated by **LASAI Aviation II LLC**. Its fleet sister ship **N2100L** is named in the investigation as a "similar Global 6500" in the same LASAI reconnaissance fleet, tied in X analysis to the same **AXLE**-series effort and reported under callsigns **AXLE21 / AXEL21**. The whole LASAI Global 6500 fleet is described in citizen analysis as flying under a Leidos / LASAI **"training" cover** — the stated mission framing that the routine-vs-targeted debate below turns on. Both aircraft pages are being maintained in parallel:

* [**N1098L — HADES Spy Plane**](/Planes/N1098L/overview) — the documented September 10 low-altitude passes near UVU.
* [**N2100L — LASAI Sister Ship**](/Planes/N2100L/overview) — catalogued as a fleet-mate; not independently placed over UVU.

## The contractor structure that limits public disclosure

Most US Army ISR missions are flown by **defense contractors** rather than uniformed military crews. Per the case notes and FAA records, **LASAI Aviation II LLC** is a defense contractor specializing in ISR missions for the US Army, registered at **10660 Aviation Lane, Manassas, VA 20110**, and control of LASAI Aviation reportedly **fell under Leidos in 2021**. Industry reporting on the broader Army business-jet ISR competition has also named other large defense firms — among them Sierra Nevada Corporation and SAIC — as participants; the specific aircraft tracked in this case are the Leidos / LASAI Global 6500s.

The contracting structure means:

* Crew identities are typically protected as proprietary or classified.
* Mission tasking is typically not subject to public records requests.
* FAA-level flight tracking (ADS-B) is sometimes the only public visibility into the mission.
* Public NTSB databases will only show entries if there was an incident — and a search for N1098L returns no incidents.

This is why investigators of the September 10 N1098L flight have been **unable to publicly confirm the crew or the tasking** — the contracting layer is opaque by design. The case notes document that obtaining crew details would require formal FOIA requests to the FAA, direct requests to LASAI Aviation, or airport ramp-log requests at Biggs (KBIF) and Glacier Park (KGPI) — channels that are slow, often redacted, and rarely productive for a private observer. Two different and **unverified** crew lists for the September 10 flight have circulated on X, which underscores how little is actually confirmed.

## Routine training vs. targeted surveillance — the open debate

Online discussion of the LASAI / HADES flights divides into two camps, and this page presents both **even-handedly** because neither is settled:

* **Routine operations / training.** On this reading, the September 10 flight profile was ordinary. One account framed N1098L's Orem low passes as **drone drop-and-recovery training** — describing two maneuvers (reported around 600 ft AGL and 335 ft AGL) as "textbook drone drop/recovery simulations." Skeptics in this camp, such as X user **[@TJPHager](/Influencers/x/tjphager)**, have argued the aircraft **was not built out to launch drones at all**, which would weaken the surveillance reading entirely. This connects to a broader online dispute over whether the LASAI jets carry the full **ME-11B** sensor configuration: critics reportedly point out that the publicly visible equipment is **interior-only**, with **no visible exterior modifications** (radomes, antennas, or sensor blisters) of the kind a fully kitted-out ISR collection aircraft would normally show — an argument that, if correct, would cut against the most aggressive surveillance readings. Under this view, grouping N2100L with N1098L is guilt-by-fleet-association, not evidence.
* **Targeted surveillance.** Other citizen investigators on X — focused on the aircraft's surveillance capabilities, low-level operations, and possible real-time monitoring — argue that an Army ISR jet descending to low altitude near a campus hours before, and looping back after, a high-profile assassination is worth a full explanation regardless of the stated training purpose. The case notes also record that a contact at the **National Counterterrorism Center (NCTC)** said officials there were **not aware of N1098L's flight path** before or after the killing, and described the HADES plane's maneuvers as "too damning" when combined with the drone findings — an attributed, second-hand account, not a confirmed official finding. Some citizen theories in this camp have also raised **Fort Huachuca** — the US Army's intelligence base in southern Arizona — as a possible command or communications-relay node for the flights; this is presented as an online hypothesis, not an established fact.

Whether the September 10 profile reflects **routine training** or a **targeted use** of ISR assets has been debated extensively online and remains an open question, not a settled conclusion.

## Why the September 10 ISR question matters

If a US Army ISR-tasked aircraft was operating in or near the UVU airspace at the time of the assassination, then the federal government has — at minimum — the **possibility of sensor records** of the area from an airborne platform. Whether those records exist, who has accessed them, and whether they have been preserved are questions that fall to **congressional oversight**, because the contractor structure puts them beyond ordinary public-records reach.

The same logic applies to any **transponder-off** segment of a foreign-government aircraft (see [SU-BTT Israel/Egyptian Jet](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview)) or a private aircraft operating in the same airspace window.

## Open questions

1. Was N1098L tasked for ISR collection over UVU on September 10, or was the flight purely the repositioning / training run described in some accounts?
2. If sensor or imagery records exist for that flight, who has accessed them, and have they been preserved under any litigation hold?
3. Is N2100L actually ISR-equipped and was it airborne anywhere relevant on September 10–11, or is the "not equipped, not involved" view correct?
4. How many Global 6500s does LASAI Aviation operate for the Army, and do they share crews, basing, or AXLE-series callsigns?
5. Which congressional committee has jurisdiction to compel disclosure of HADES tasking and contractor records?


## The primary published source on air-launched drones from a Global Express

The HADES air-launched-drone claim that circulates in this investigation is not invented. It traces to a specific, checkable trade article, and quoting it precisely separates what is documented from what is inferred.

The article is **"Army Wants To Test Air-Launched Drones From A Global Express Business Jet,"** published by **The War Zone (TWZ)** under the section **AIR / AIRBORNE SENSORS**, by **Thomas Newdick**, **published January 9, 2025, 4:03 PM EST** (38 comments at capture). Its sub-headline:

> The planned trials tally with previous comments about launching the drones from the new Global 6500-based HADES surveillance jet.

The article's lead image is a photograph of a **matte-grey Bombardier Global Express in US Army markings**, tail number **24001**, with **"US ARMY"** on the rear upper fuselage. Visible modifications in the photograph: a **large canoe-shaped fairing along the upper fuselage behind the cockpit**, a **large ventral radar fairing under the fuselage**, and **multiple blade antennas** top and bottom. Landing gear extended, climbing against an overcast sky.

That establishes three things as documented fact rather than speculation:

1. **The US Army operates Global Express-family aircraft as ISR platforms**, in Army markings, with externally visible sensor fairings.
2. **HADES is Global 6500-based**, publicly acknowledged, and named in mainstream defence trade press.
3. **The Army has publicly stated an intent to test air-launched drones from this platform** — as a planned trial, as of January 2025, eight months before the assassination.

What the article does **not** do is place any such aircraft anywhere near Utah, or establish that the drone-launch capability was fielded rather than merely planned. The gap between "the Army wants to test" and "it happened over Orem on September 10" is the entire distance the OSINT threads are trying to cover, and the article covers none of it.

## The two Crowdsource The Truth posts that opened the ISR thread

**[@JG_CSTT](/Influencers/x/jg-cstt)** (Crowdsource The Truth) posted the article on **September 20, 2025**, with this framing:

> Maybe this is a coincidence but just last night [another account] and I were discussing the possibility of air launched drones at the Charlie Kirk assassination and this Bombardier HADES ISR jet was the exact platform we suspect they came from.

Note the honesty of the construction: *"Maybe this is a coincidence"* and *"the platform we suspect."* The poster is describing the article as matching a prior hypothesis, not as evidence for it.

The same post quotes an earlier one from the same account, seventeen hours before, which is the sharper question and the better one:

> What was an ISR aircraft doing loitering over Utah Lake near stall speed in striking distance of Utah Valley University during the Charlie Kirk assassination?

**"Loitering over Utah Lake near stall speed"** is a specific, falsifiable assertion about a flight profile, and it is the claim that everything else on this thread hangs from. It is testable against the raw ADS-B archive: a large business jet flying near stall speed at low altitude over a lake would show a distinctive and unmistakable groundspeed and altitude signature. As of this writing, no one has published the raw track that would confirm or refute it — only annotated screenshots of it.

## The @pennedpatriot Utah Lake telemetry claim

A separate post the same morning — **@pennedpatriot, 9:43 AM, September 20, 2025, 211 views** — attached a Google Earth 3D reconstruction of the Utah Valley looking west-southwest across Utah Lake, with an aircraft track overlaid as a chain of blue aircraft icons, each carrying its own UTC timestamp. The post text:

> Drones drop from 650' above and into the water ~3 hours prior to the incident on the initial decent.
>
> Drones are "picked up" from 300' above the water ~3 seconds after the incident on the second decent.

*("decent" is written that way in the original.)*

The timestamps legible on the reconstruction all carry the date **2025-09-10** and cluster in two groups:

* A **dense, overlapping chain** near the far shore — 15:06:05, 15:06:58, 15:07:04, 15:07:20 UTC, and later 15:22:54, 15:22:57, 15:23:55, 15:24:03 UTC.
* A **spaced-out line running horizontally across the middle of Utah Lake** — 15:15:08, 15:15:21, 15:15:34, 15:15:47, 15:16:01, 15:16:14, 15:16:28, 15:16:37, 15:16:44, 15:16:51 UTC.

A **bright yellow vertical line** is drawn dropping from the track down toward the ground near the **Clarke Building** on the UVU campus. Map labels visible on the reconstruction place the scene precisely: **Utah Lake**, **Provo River Delta**, **Powell Slough Waterfowl Management Area**, **UVU Museum at Lakemount**, **Clarke Building**, **Westmore Elementary School**, **Orem Community Park**, **Lakeside Sports Park**, **Utah Lake State Park**, with **Lake Mountains HP** and **Mosida** on the far ridge.

### Converting the timestamps — and what they do and do not support

Utah was on **Mountain Daylight Time (UTC−6)** on September 10, 2025. Converting:

| UTC as shown | Utah local (MDT) |
|---|---|
| 15:06 – 15:07 | **9:06 – 9:07 a.m.** |
| 15:15 – 15:17 | **9:15 – 9:17 a.m.** |
| 15:22 – 15:24 | **9:22 – 9:24 a.m.** |

Charlie Kirk was shot at approximately **12:23 p.m. MDT = 18:23 UTC**.

Two conclusions follow, and they cut in opposite directions.

**The first half of the claim is corroborated by an independent analyst.** The 15:15 UTC group converts to **9:15 a.m. local** — which is, to the minute, the time an independent flight-data analysis quoted on the [SU-BTT page](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) gives for the **first of two low passes by AXL10**, a Bombardier Global 6500 ISR aircraft, "about 3 hours before Charlie was shot." Two researchers working separately, one from a Google Earth track reconstruction and one from KML exports, put an aircraft over the same corridor at the same minute. That convergence is the strongest thing on this page. It also matches the post's own "~3 hours prior" framing: 9:15 a.m. to 12:23 p.m. is three hours and eight minutes.

**The second half of the claim is not supported by the evidence in the post.** Every timestamp visible on the reconstruction falls between **15:06 and 15:24 UTC — i.e. between 9:06 and 9:24 a.m. local.** The post asserts a second descent and a drone "pick-up" **"~3 seconds after the incident,"** which would be at approximately **18:23 UTC**. **No timestamp anywhere near 18:23 UTC appears in the image.** Whatever supports the second-descent claim, it is not in the graphic offered as its evidence. The independent AXL10 analysis does place a second pass at **12:48 p.m. local (18:48 UTC)** — twenty-five minutes after the shooting, not three seconds — which is a materially different claim.

**The altitude figures are unsourced.** "650 feet above and into the water" and "300 feet above the water" appear only in the post text. The reconstruction shows a track; it does not show altitudes. Note also that the AXL10 analysis derives its own low-altitude figure (~210 ft AGL) by subtracting terrain and lake elevation from barometric altitude — an arithmetic step that is highly sensitive to which elevation figure is used, and which no one has published the working for.

### How to weigh it

This post is a useful lead and a poor proof, and both halves of that should be said plainly. The 9:15 a.m. corroboration is real and independently arrived at. The drone drop-and-recovery narrative built on top of it is not evidenced by anything in the material — it is an interpretation of what an aircraft flying low over a lake *might* have been doing, presented in the grammar of a finding.

The way to settle it is the same as everywhere else on this page: **pull the raw ADS-B Exchange track for September 10, 2025 over the Utah Lake corridor and read the altitude and groundspeed columns directly.** That is a few minutes of work for anyone with archive access, and it would either establish a genuinely extraordinary flight profile or close the question.

## Track These Aircraft

## Where this page fits

Before you read what [N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview) did on 10 September, this page tells you what the aircraft type is for — signals intelligence, imagery, synthetic aperture radar, drone launch and recovery — and which [fleet](/Planes/LASAI-Fleet/overview) and [Army programme](/US_Intelligence/US_Army_HADES) it belongs to, with [the sister ship](/Planes/N2100L/overview) and [the Army field it launched from](/Planes/Airport-Biggs-Army-Airfield) catalogued separately. The capability is the point: an ISR platform near an event proves nothing about the event, but it does mean records exist — which is why [the drone-count claims](/Drones/overview), [the sixteen drones reported over campus](/Drones/Military_Drones_On_Video), [the campus and event camera threads](/technology_surveillance/overview) and [the reported halt of the FBI's foreign leads](/FBI/Foreign_Leads) all end at the same closed door. For the specific numbers, go to [N1098L Air-Launched Drones](/Drones/N1098L_Air_Launched_Drones): a reported 203-foot pass at 9:16 AM and a return at 12:48 PM, twenty-five minutes after Kirk died, with no Army statement explaining either.
