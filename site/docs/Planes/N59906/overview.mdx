# N59906 MARC Survey Plane

A **Piper PA-31 Navajo Chieftain** registered **N59906**, operated by aerial-survey contractor **MARC Inc.**, is reported in public OSINT to have flown **five** mapping-style passes over and around the **Utah Valley University** campus on the morning of **September 10, 2025**, hours before [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) was shot at roughly 12:23 p.m. MDT. Because the aircraft type is configured for high-resolution camera and LiDAR mapping, independent researchers flagged it as a possible aerial-surveillance flight rather than a drone. What it actually photographed — and who tasked it — remains unconfirmed.

This page is about tail **N59906** (MARC Inc. survey aircraft). Do not confuse it with the separately discussed tail **[N55906](/Planes/N55906/overview)**, a different aircraft raised in independent-investigator analysis. The two tail numbers differ by one digit and are easy to transpose.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** N59906
* **Type:** Piper PA-31 Navajo Chieftain
* **Operator / owner:** MARC Inc. — described in OSINT reporting as North America's largest provider of specialized contract aircraft for airborne GIS, geophysical surveys, mapping, and surveillance projects
* **Base of operation that day:** Provo Municipal Airport (PVU), about 4 miles south of the UVU campus
* **Mission profile:** contract aerial survey / mapping; not a scheduled or military flight

## Flight history relevant to the case

According to OSINT analysis attributed to researcher **@rosenwald_eric** and circulated by independent investigators, on the morning of September 10, 2025 the aircraft made **five** passes that originated and ended at Provo Airport, flying at roughly **19,000 feet MSL** — about **15,000 feet above ground level** given Provo's elevation. That is a standard high-altitude aerial-mapping band, not a treetop pass; the "low-altitude" label used in some early posts is misleading, and the figures are reproduced here as reported.

| Pass | Time (MDT) | Duration | Note |
|------|-----------|----------|------|
| 1 | 11:01 a.m. | ~5 min | First north-south pass over central campus |
| 2 | 11:09 a.m. | ~4 min | Second pass, offset slightly eastward |
| 3 | 11:18 a.m. | ~5 min | Third pass, reportedly focused on the event area |
| **4** | **11:26 a.m.** | ~5 min | **Fourth pass — see the correction note below** |
| 5 | 11:35 a.m. | ~6 min | Final pass; alleged shooter reportedly first seen on campus surveillance at 11:50 a.m. |

**Correction — there are five passes, not four.** Earlier versions of this page listed four, omitting the **11:26 a.m.** pass. The annotated adsbexchange graphic compiled by **Eric R Rosenwald** (EricRosenwaldPhotography.com), captured at `2025-09-10 18:06:10 Z`, lists the passes explicitly and there are five of them:

> *"Apparent Aerial Surveillance Contractor Flights over Utah Valley University Just Prior to the Charlie Kirk Shooting — 1st pass: 11:01 am · 2nd pass: 11:09 am · 3rd pass: 11:18 am · 4th pass: 11:26 am · 5th pass: 11:35 am · Alleged shooter first spotted on campus: 11:50 am · Charlie Kirk shot at 12:23 pm"*

The five passes are spaced **eight to nine minutes apart across a 34-minute block**, which is what a programmed survey grid looks like: parallel lines flown at a constant interval so the imagery overlaps. The graphic marks the approximate midpoint and approximate length of each pass. **That regularity is the point in both directions** — it is the signature of an automated mapping run, which is also the innocent explanation, and it is not what an aircraft loitering to watch a specific thing would fly.

The same graphic records the aircraft's identity from adsbexchange: **hex `A7C14D`**, a **1974 Piper PA-31-300/310/325/350/425**, type code **PA31**, registered to **MARC INC**, with the ADS-B position source shown as **TIS-B** rather than a direct ADS-B Out return.

The reporting states that open-source ADS-B flight tracking confirmed this pass pattern. One early post mislabeled the flight date as "Sep 15," but the same post places the passes "hours before" the September 10 attack; the September 10 morning timing is what the pass-by-pass log describes.

N59906 also appears in the broader "anomalous aircraft near UVU / Utah corridor" catalogs that independent investigators have assembled — informal lists cross-referencing aircraft reportedly operating around the campus and the surrounding Utah airspace in the hours and days around the event. Its inclusion there is reported as a flagged data point for follow-up, not as proof of any coordinated activity.

## Survey and surveillance payload

Public reporting describes MARC Inc.'s survey aircraft as equipped for high-resolution data capture. The payloads below are described in general for the operator's fleet; it is not independently confirmed which specific sensors were aboard N59906 on September 10.

* **Cameras:** Vexcel large-format digital, Leica, hyperspectral, and infrared scanners.
* **LiDAR:** Optech Orion / Gemini / 3100 systems for 3D mapping.
* **Storage:** Onboard data systems for multispectral and hyperspectral imaging.

MARC Inc. is reported to serve U.S. federal and state governments, foreign entities, private mapping firms, oil and gas companies, civil-engineering groups, and universities — applications ranging from aerial photography and geophysical surveys to pipeline monitoring and surveillance. Neither MARC Inc. nor the FBI has publicly confirmed the flight's purpose or client, and the airspace over the event reportedly remained unrestricted, which some commenters criticized given the timing.

## Alleged leaked imagery

Public reporting states that on **October 13, 2025**, a TikTok account (**[@universehop00](/Influencers/x/universehop00)**) posted images claimed to originate from the N59906 flight — reportedly one day after the same account's first post on the topic. The account is said to have had no other content, which some commenters read as a sign of a targeted leak from a contractor or insider. The images are described as high-resolution orthomosaic views of the UVU quad and stage area, with metadata said to align with the 11:35 a.m. pass, and one post alleged a figure near a rooftop access point about 45 minutes before the shooting. **These images are unverified.** Their authenticity, source, and metadata have not been independently confirmed, and they should be treated as an unproven claim rather than evidence.

## The Alpha Whiskey / Alexis Wilkins allegation (unproven)

A separate and far more contested claim circulated on X tied N59906's operator to a company linked to **Alexis Wilkins**, a country singer who is publicly reported to be the girlfriend of **[Kash Patel](/People/kash-patel)**, the current FBI Director. This section sets out the claim as it was posted and then the rebuttal. **None of the connection alleged below has been confirmed; it is an unproven allegation amplified on social media and is reproduced here only to document and assess it.**

### What the X accounts alleged

A thread shared by accounts including **[@ProjectConstitu](/Influencers/podcasts-project-constitution)**, **[@AnaEscobarShow](/Influencers/x/anaescobarshow)**, **[@leahfiles](/Influencers/x/leahfiles)**, **[@Mini_111111](/Influencers/x/mini-111111)**, and **@StefaniWilcox8** carried the headline claim, quoted here verbatim:

> "BOMBSHELL: The MARC INC Plane That Was Circling UVU Taking SURVEILLANCE Pictures During The Charlie Kirk Event Is Tied to a Company Where Kash Patel's GIRLFRIEND Alexis Wilkins Is the Registered Agent?"

The posts laid out the following alleged ownership chain:

* A **pending registration** reportedly names **Alpha Whiskey 2 LLC** in **Brandon, Mississippi** in connection with the aircraft's ownership/operation.
* A separately named entity, **Alpha Whiskey Inc** (Tennessee, reportedly active since **2020**), is said to list **Alexis Wilkins** as registered agent/principal, based in **Nashville**.
* The posts note that **"Kash Patel is currently the Director of the FBI,"** and frame the shared "Alpha Whiskey" name plus the timing of the flight as evidence of an intelligence connection — "The intelligence connections are impossible to ignore."

The accounts used these claims to push for legislation forcing disclosure of MARC Inc.'s flight records, who hired the aircraft, the passengers and crew aboard, and any transponder deactivation.

### The rebuttal — what the evidence actually shows

The allegation rests on a **name similarity plus timing**, and the rebuttal to it is prominent and substantive. Responses in the same threads (including from Grok) and from other commenters made the following points:

* **No public evidence** links the two LLCs — or Alexis Wilkins or Kash Patel — to the flight, to MARC Inc., or to the Charlie Kirk event.
* The inference is **speculation drawn from a shared company name and the timing** of the flight, not from any documented relationship.
* **High-resolution LiDAR and aerial-survey work is standard** for the kind of contractor MARC Inc. is, so the aircraft's capabilities are not by themselves anomalous.
* There are **no confirmed shared ownership, officers, communications, financial, or operational links** between the entities or the people named.

Because **Alexis Wilkins and Kash Patel are living people**, no statement here should be read as asserting that either is connected to the aircraft, the flight, or the assassination. They are not. The claim is an allegation made on social media, it is disputed in the same threads where it appeared, and as of now it is **unproven**.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

These are open theories raised by independent investigators, not established facts:

* **Routine survey work.** Provo sits on the Interstate-15 corridor, and survey contractors regularly fly mapping grids for infrastructure, utility, and engineering clients. Under this reading the timing is coincidental, and any imagery — if it exists — would simply document the area, per @rosenwald_eric's own framing that the photos could "debunk conspiracies."
* **Tasked pre-event imaging.** Other commenters argue the four-pass pattern in the two hours before the attack, by a contractor that lists "surveillance" among its services, is suspicious and worth subpoenaing. Under this reading the flight may have captured the stage area, rooftops, and approaches before the shooting.
* **Evidence-capture, intent unknown.** A middle position: the aircraft plausibly recorded high-resolution imagery of the campus that morning regardless of who tasked it, making the existence, custody, and content of that data the central question — whether it helps the [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) case or points elsewhere.

No theory here asserts criminal conduct by any named person or by MARC Inc. The operator's purpose and client remain officially unconfirmed.

The most-circulated N59906 artifact is a single annotated screen capture of **ADS-B Exchange**, stamped **Date: 2025-09-10, Time: 18:06:10 Z** and credited in a caption line along the bottom of the image to **"Compiled by Eric R Rosenwald — EricRosenwaldPhotography.com."** That capture is the origin of nearly every downstream post about this aircraft, so its own contents are worth reading directly rather than through summaries. Three details in that panel are worth pulling out, because they are checkable and they are not in the summaries:

* **The build year is 1974.** This is a fifty-one-year-old piston twin, not a modern turbine ISR platform. That cuts against the more dramatic readings of the flight and toward the survey-contractor reading.
* **The position 40.217°, −111.721° is the Provo Municipal Airport ramp**, and the capture shows the aircraft **on the ground at 11 knots** — i.e. taxiing — at the moment the screenshot was taken. The annotated flight track on the map is playback history, not a live position.
* **The data source is TIS-B, not ADS-B.** TIS-B (Traffic Information Service-Broadcast) is a ground-station rebroadcast of radar-derived tracks rather than the aircraft's own ADS-B Out. TIS-B positions are lower-fidelity and can lag, drop, or snap. Anyone attempting to re-derive the pass geometry or altitudes from this capture should account for that; it is one reason the altitude figures on this page are given as reported rather than as measured.

## Five passes, not four — and the timeline printed on the graphic

The annotated map carries its own timeline in white text in the upper-left of the frame. It lists **five** passes, not the four described in earlier posts, and it interleaves them with two case anchors:

| Line as printed on the graphic | Time |
|---|---|
| 1st pass | 11:01 am |
| 2nd pass | 11:09 am |
| 3rd pass | 11:18 am |
| **4th pass** | **11:26 am** |
| 5th pass *(highlighted yellow on the graphic)* | 11:35 am |
| Alleged shooter first spotted on campus *(highlighted yellow)* | 11:50 am |
| Charlie Kirk shot | 12:23 pm |

The **11:26 a.m. fourth pass** is missing from the four-row version of this table circulating elsewhere; the compiler's own graphic lists it, and the pass log higher on this page has been corrected to five rows to match. The passes are spaced **8, 9, 8 and 9 minutes apart** — a metronomic interval, which is exactly what a survey aircraft flying a photogrammetric grid produces and also exactly what a loiter orbit produces. The interval alone distinguishes nothing.

Two further annotations appear on the map itself: a green dotted line labelled **"approximate midpoints of each pass"** and red bars labelled **"approximate length of each pass."** Both are the compiler's own reconstruction, drawn on top of the track, not values the tracker output. The five red bars run **southwest to northeast** with arrows alternating direction — a back-and-forth boustrophedon, the standard mapping pattern. A light-blue star marks **Utah Valley University** beside the track.

Note also that the compiler chose to highlight the **5th pass and the shooter-sighting line in yellow** — a visual pairing that invites the reader to connect them. The 11:35 pass and the 11:50 sighting are 15 minutes apart, and nothing on the graphic establishes any relationship between them beyond adjacency in time.

Several of these are new to this page and each carries weight:

* **A 2-hour-39-minute round trip out of and back into Provo.** That is a long time aloft for five passes lasting four to six minutes each, which means the great majority of the sortie was spent somewhere other than directly over the UVU campus. The map in the same capture bears this out — it shows a **tight parallel-line grid over Utah Lake and the adjacent land**, plus a second, larger and more irregular loop running north toward Flat Top Mountain and south past Salem. That is a survey block, and UVU sits on its edge.
* **Squawk 4323** is a discrete transponder code assigned by ATC. It is not 1200 (VFR), not 7500/7600/7700 (emergency), and not a military or restricted block. A discrete code means the aircraft was in contact with and being worked by air traffic control for the flight — the opposite of a covert profile.
* **19,000 ft barometric at 212 kts.** Provo field elevation is roughly 4,500 ft, so 19,000 ft MSL is about **14,500 ft above ground level** — consistent with the high-altitude mapping band described elsewhere on this page and flatly inconsistent with the "low pass over the crowd" framing that some posts implied.
* **MSN 31-7552027** confirms the airframe identity independently of the registration, so the aircraft in the capture can be tied to a specific serial-numbered airframe rather than to a tail number alone.

## MARC Inc.'s published fleet and sensor list

A companion video compiled by **[@ProjectConstitu](/Influencers/podcasts-project-constitution)** (Project Constitution) walks through screenshots of MARC Inc.'s own website. The navigation bar shown reads **Home · Our Aircraft · Sensors · Our Pilots · Maintenance · Contact Us** — the site of a working survey operator, publicly advertising exactly the capability at issue. The slides captured in that video add two facts not previously on this page:

* **The fleet is described as a Piper Navajo alongside a Swearingen Merlin III** — a spec-comparison slide sets the two types side by side. The Merlin III is a pressurised turboprop; the Navajo is the piston twin flown on September 10.
* **The published client list names federal, state, and foreign governments** among its customers. That "foreign governments" line is the one investigators seized on. It is, however, unremarkable for an aerial-mapping contractor — foreign mapping ministries and resource agencies routinely buy survey flights — and it says nothing about who bought this one.

The same video's sensor slide shows a grid of aerial camera bodies and **LiDAR** units, consistent with the payload list already described on this page. The title card the compiler placed over the flight map reads: **"Apparent Aerial Surveillance Contractor Flights over Utah Valley University Just Prior to the Charlie Kirk Shooting."** Note the word *apparent* — the compiler's own framing is conditional, and this page carries it at that strength.

## Provenance of the aerial imagery in circulation

The high-altitude campus footage that circulates alongside the N59906 story — top-down views of the UVU amphitheatre with the crowd seated in curved rows, the plazas, the parking lots, and the adjacent residential streets — carries a persistent **TikTok watermark reading `@universehop00`** in the lower centre of frame. In a separate recorded conversation, a **Project Constitution** guest credits the release to **"Universe Hope 00 on TikTok and Eric Rosenwald on Twitter"** — placing the same two names behind both the imagery and the annotated flight map.

That matters for weighing the material: **the flight-track analysis and the aerial imagery are not independent corroboration of each other.** They come from the same two-person pipeline. A reader treating "the map plus the pictures" as two sources agreeing is double-counting one source.

The imagery itself has never been forensically tied to N59906. No metadata has been published, no chain of custody has been shown, and the visible content — a wide-area campus view in bright midday sun — is consistent with drone footage, with mapping-aircraft imagery, or with commercial satellite/3D-map renders. Some frames circulating in the same set are demonstrably **Google Earth-style 3D renders** rather than photographs at all: one shows a blue teardrop map pin dropped on the campus, and another shows a media-player scrub bar reading `1:13 / 1:48`. Those are screen recordings of mapping software, not aircraft imagery, and their presence in the same reel is a caution about the whole set.



## Registration change since the event

The FAA registry no longer shows this aircraft registered to MARC Inc. As of a certificate issued **4 April 2026**, N59906 is registered to **Alpha Whiskey 2 LLC of Brandon, Mississippi**.

Two things are worth noting and neither is an accusation. MARC Inc. remains a large operator — the registry shows it holding **32 aircraft** at Bolton, Mississippi, the majority of them PA-31-350 Navajo Chieftains, which is exactly the fleet profile of a working aerial-survey contractor and supports the survey explanation for the UVU passes rather than undercutting it. And MARC also holds an aircraft registered at **Brandon, Mississippi** — the same town as the new registrant — so the transfer may be an internal or local one rather than an arm's-length sale.

A single aircraft changing hands seven months after the event is unremarkable on its own. It is recorded here so the current registry state matches what this page says.

### The five passes are confirmed from primary data, to the minute

**This is the most significant result on this page and it did not come from us reading a
screenshot.** Both free daily archives independently hold the full 10 September 2025 trace —
adsb.lol returns 1,289 position points, globe.airplanes.live 1,579 of the same flight — and both
self-identify the airframe as **N59906, type PA31**.

Counting every point that comes within 5 km of the UVU campus, the survey block runs like this
(UTC, with MDT in brackets), set beside the times printed on the Eric Rosenwald graphic reproduced
higher on this page:

| Pass | Primary trace (UTC) | Primary trace (MDT) | Printed on the graphic |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 17:01:06Z | **11:01** | 11:01 am |
| 2 | 17:08:36Z | **11:08–11:09** | 11:09 am |
| 3 | 17:17:34Z | **11:17–11:18** | 11:18 am |
| 4 | 17:25:40Z | **11:25–11:26** | 11:26 am |
| 5 | 17:34:58Z | **11:34–11:35** | 11:35 am |

**Five passes. All five match the compiler's published times.** The compiler's timeline was
reconstructed by hand off a tracking website; ours is measured off the raw position points held by
two independent volunteer networks. **They agree.** That is as good a corroboration as citizen
open-source work gets, and the correction this page already made — five passes, not four — is
confirmed by primary data.

Everything else in the trace lines up as well:

* **Altitude: 19,000 ft barometric, held flat across the entire block.** The Flightradar24 playback
  card reproduced above reads 19,000 ft. The raw data reads 19,000 ft, with a whole-day maximum of
  19,150 ft. UVU sits at roughly 4,600 ft, so the passes were about **14,400 ft above the ground** —
  a mapping altitude. **The "low pass over the crowd" framing is flatly refuted by primary data.**
* **Closest approach to the campus: 0.79 km (airplanes.live) and 1.02 km (adsb.lol)** — directly
  overhead, at nearly three miles up.
* **Groundspeed alternates, leg by leg, between about 125 kt and about 210 kt.** That is what a
  back-and-forth grid flown into and out of the wind produces, and it is what the Flightradar24
  card's 212 kt cursor reading was catching. It is an ordinary photogrammetric survey signature.
* **Airborne time.** The trace has the aircraft on the ground at Provo at 15:08:45Z, rolling by
  15:28, and back on the ground at Provo by 18:08:35Z — **about 2 hours 40 minutes**, against the
  Flightradar24 card's stated **2:39**. Confirmed.
* **It landed before the shooting.** The final descent crosses within 5 km of campus at 18:03:30Z
  (**12:03:30 MDT**) at 5,725 ft, and the trace ends **on the ground at Provo Municipal, 0.26 km from
  the airport reference point, at 18:08:35Z (12:08 MDT)** — roughly **fifteen minutes before Charlie
  Kirk was shot at 12:23 MDT**. The much-circulated ADS-B Exchange capture stamped
  **2025-09-10 18:06:10 Z** showing the aircraft *on the ground at 11 knots* is therefore exactly
  right, and our data explains what it was: the aircraft taxiing in after the survey sortie.

**And the limit, stated as plainly as the confirmation.** All of this locates **the aircraft**. It
establishes that a survey-configured Piper flew a mapping grid over the Utah Valley at 19,000 ft on
the morning of 10 September 2025 and landed fifteen minutes before the shooting. **It says nothing
about who tasked it, what its sensors were set to record, whether anything was recorded at all, or
where that data is now.** Those remain the open questions on this page, and no free archive can
answer any of them.

### Six 2022 days, new to this site — and they support the mundane explanation

The site has published that "the archive does not reach 2022." **That is no longer true.** The
ADS-B Exchange public sample publishes one full day — the **first of every month** — back to 2016,
free and without an account, and for N59906 it has produced **six pre-2023 aircraft-days**:

| Date | What the trace shows |
|---|---|
| 2022-02-01 | Music City Executive, Gallatin **TN** (KM33) — out and back, local sortie, 1,112 points |
| 2022-03-01 | St Louis Lambert → Spirit of St Louis, **MO** (KSTL → KSUS), 3,044 points |
| 2022-05-01 | Kansas City International → Dodge City, **MO to KS** (KMCI → KDDC), 339 points |
| 2022-06-01 | Watsonville Municipal, **CA** (KWVI) — local, ending near Modesto, 2,957 points |
| 2022-09-01 | Bellingham International, **WA** (KBLI) — a single position point |
| 2022-12-01 | John Bell Williams → Freebird, **MS** (KM16 → 7MS7), 917 points |

**The sampling caveat travels with these every time they are cited, without exception.** This is
**one day in thirty** — the 1st of the month and nothing else. Six sampled days across a year cannot
support a rate, a frequency, or a pattern. They establish where the aircraft was on six specific
days and nothing more.

What they do show is a **flight profile**: short local sorties, a different state most months,
regional and municipal fields, often out and back from the same strip. **That is the working pattern
of a contract aerial-survey aircraft**, and it matches what MARC Inc.'s own published fleet and
sensor list — described higher on this page — says the company does. **This corroborates the mundane
explanation for the UVU flight, and it is reported here as corroboration because that is what it
is.**

### The backup network, and why the gaps are not about this aircraft

**Seventeen of N59906's forty-eight days exist only on globe.airplanes.live** — adsb.lol does not
hold them. That includes 14 October 2025 and the entire May 2026 run at Kenosha. It is not
suppression, and the control test is what settles it: adsb.lol returns **HTTP 403 for every
aircraft** from **exactly 12 October 2025** to about 30 December 2025, and **HTTP 404 for every
aircraft** from about 31 December 2025 until roughly 1 August 2026 — including unrelated control
airframes chosen for having nothing to do with this case. **Archive-wide conditions, not decisions
about N59906.**

The 10 September window is the reassuring case and deserves saying out loud: **for 1–16 September
2025 the two archives agree with each other on this aircraft on every day either of them holds.**
**There is no scrubbing in the fortnight the case turns on.**

### The four archived tracking pages, and a correction about what they prove

The Internet Archive holds four captures of this aircraft's pages — two of **FlightRadar24**
(29 and 30 August 2022) and two of **FlightAware** (30 August and 1 September 2022). **No flight
rows were recovered from any of them.**

Two corrections about how those are read:

* **"0 rows" is not the same as "the table was empty."** The FlightAware captures were never parsed
  into a row state at all; elsewhere in this fleet, modern FlightAware captures have been re-labelled
  `JAVASCRIPT_SHELL_NO_SERVER_RENDERED_ROWS` precisely because FlightAware is now a client-side
  application whose archived HTML holds page chrome and no server-rendered flight table. **An absence
  of parsing is not an absence of evidence**, and this site recorded 153 real flight legs across the
  fleet that had previously been written down as zeros.
* **The provenance record flags two of these pages as "public then, not public now" and that flag is
  wrong.** It rests on the archived captures returning HTTP 200 while the live FlightRadar24 URL
  returns HTTP 403 to a script. A control test settled it: **FlightRadar24 returns 403 to scripted
  requests across its entire site, including its own front page**, and a browser session returns
  **HTTP 200** for this aircraft's page with the identity block intact. **Nothing was taken down.**
  See [What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means).

### The counterargument

Read this section at its least flattering and it still holds. **Confirming the five passes confirms a
survey flight, and a survey flight is the innocent explanation.** Everything the primary data adds —
the 19,000 ft mapping band, the metronomic 8-to-9-minute spacing, the alternating into-wind and
downwind groundspeeds, the closed Provo-to-Provo loop, the 2022 profile of short local sorties across
six states — points at a contractor flying a photogrammetric grid, which is what MARC Inc. is in
business to do. The timing remains the only thing that makes it interesting, and **timing is not
tasking**. Nothing in a position record identifies a client, a sensor setting, or a frame of
imagery. **Our data locates the aircraft; it does not place Charlie Kirk, Erika Kirk or anyone else
anywhere, and it establishes no tasking in either direction.** The records that would — the contract,
the client, the imagery and its chain of custody — are exactly the ones the
[Charlie Kirk Investigation Laws](/Fix/overview) are written to force open.

## Where this page fits

Five low mapping passes over a campus on the morning of a killing is the kind of fact that sounds like a finding and is not, and this is where that gets tested — a contract survey aircraft confirmed from primary ADS-B to the minute, read against the [news helicopter that orbited the same campus that afternoon](/Planes/N155TV/overview), the [one-digit-away tail it is constantly confused with](/Planes/N55906/overview) and the [September 10 flight timeline](/Planes/Sept10-Flight-Timeline). What the aircraft photographed and who paid for it are the open questions, and they land in the same place as the rest of the imagery fight — [campus surveillance](/cameras/UVU_Surveillance), [the drone counts](/Topics3/Drones/overview) and the [government evidence never produced](/gov/overview). The recovery run put six 2022 survey days on this site for the first time and they support the boring explanation rather than the exciting one, a working mapping contractor flying grids, which is the standard the [flight data recovery hub](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview) and its [per-aircraft status table](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Per-Aircraft-Status) hold themselves to: publish the result that weakens a claim as loudly as the one that helps it.
