# N872RA Provo Arrival

A **Hawker Beechcraft** business jet (reported 7-seat) registered **N872RA** is listed in flight records circulated by independent investigators as one of several private aircraft arriving at **Provo Municipal Airport (PVU)** on September 10, 2025. Its value to the investigation is not a standalone smoking gun — sourcing on this specific tail is thin — but as part of the **Provo arrival cluster**, and because it shares a **Santa Barbara** origin with two other items researchers have flagged: the [N560TW](/Planes/N560TW/overview) jet, and the disputed "tarmac hug" timeline involving a plane said to have landed from Santa Barbara.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** N872RA
* **Type:** Hawker Beechcraft business jet (reported 7 seats)
* **Registry / operator / owner:** Not yet publicly identified in the investigation notes
* **Role on the list:** A private aircraft logged arriving at Provo (PVU) on September 10, 2025

## Flight history relevant to the case

As reproduced from flight-record screenshots circulated by independent investigators:

* **September 10, 2025, ~2:19 p.m. MT** — Departed **Santa Barbara, California**.
* **September 10, 2025, ~4:36 p.m. MT** — Arrived **Provo (PVU)**.

This is a late-afternoon arrival, hours after the shooting. No public reporting ties N872RA, its crew, or its passengers to the event. It is logged alongside the other day-of arrivals — [N40JD](/Planes/N40JD/overview) (Scottsdale → Provo, ~2:29 p.m.), [N102DZ](/Planes/N102DZ/overview) ([Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk)'s plane, into Provo ~3:31 p.m.), and N560TW — so its manifest can be reviewed in context.

## The Santa Barbara connection

Two separate threads in the investigation single out a **Santa Barbara** origin on September 10, which is why N872RA is flagged rather than treated as routine traffic:

* **Shared route with N560TW.** The [N560TW](/Planes/N560TW/overview) Cessna Citation — owned by T.W. Lewis Aviation LLC, tied to a major [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview) donor — is reported by [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens) to have flown Scottsdale → Provo (land 10:06 a.m.) → **Santa Barbara** (depart ~11:37 a.m.) → Scottsdale (~12:44 p.m.) that morning. Researchers ask whether the Santa Barbara leg of N560TW and the later Santa Barbara departure of N872RA are connected, or coincidental.
* **The contested "tarmac hug" timeline.** According to Candace Owens's reporting, TPUSA and Erika Kirk stated she met TPUSA spokesman [Andrew Kolvet](/People/andrew-kolvet) on the Provo runway when **his plane landed from Santa Barbara** — a plane Owens says did not land in Provo until about **4:40 p.m.** That arrival time and Santa Barbara origin closely match the N872RA record above (Santa Barbara → Provo, ~4:36 p.m.). The investigation notes do not explicitly confirm N872RA is the Kolvet aircraft; the overlap in origin and arrival time is noted as a possible identification to verify, not an established one.

## Citizen tracking and X OSINT

N872RA is one of several arrival flights **flagged in citizen tracking** of activity around the assassination timeframe, per the open-source intelligence (OSINT) circulating on X.

* **Provo airport movements on X.** The tail surfaced alongside other day-of **Provo airport movements** compiled and shared by independent trackers **on X**, not in any official briefing.
* **Part of the Provo arrival cluster.** Per X OSINT, N872RA is **part of the Provo arrival cluster** noted in OSINT and X posts — a set of private aircraft logged into PVU on September 10, 2025 that citizen researchers grouped together for review.
* **Arrival flagged in X OSINT.** The **arrival into Provo** itself is **flagged in X OSINT** around the event, which is why this otherwise thin tail entry was retained.
* **Private operators.** The flight is attributed to **private operators**; no commercial or government carrier is associated with the record.

These points are OSINT observations from X and citizen tracking, noted for context only. Nothing here asserts wrongdoing by any operator, crew, or passenger.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

These are open, unproven interpretations circulated by independent researchers — not conclusions, and not statements of fact about any person:

* **Routine arrival, no connection.** The simplest reading: a private charter that happened to land at Provo that afternoon with no tie to the case. Nothing in public reporting contradicts this.
* **The "tarmac hug" aircraft.** That N872RA may be the Santa Barbara plane referenced in the disputed Kolvet/Erika Kirk runway-meeting timeline. If so, its manifest and exact arrival time bear on the broader question Owens raised about when Erika Kirk reached the hospital. This is a possible identification to confirm, not a finding.
* **Part of a Santa Barbara movement.** That the two flagged Santa Barbara legs on September 10 (N560TW's and N872RA's) reflect coordinated movement of people through that airport. No evidence currently links the two flights beyond the shared airport.

## Aircraft identification, confirmed

The FAA Civil Aviation Registry resolves what earlier commentary left open. N872RA is a **Hawker Beechcraft 400A** (the Beechjet 400A / Hawker 400XP family), built **2008**, serial **RK-563**, 10 seats in the type record, registered to **A&E Hawker LP** of Santa Ana, California. Descriptions of it as an unspecified "Hawker Beechcraft business jet with a reported 7 seats" can be replaced with the specific type.

## Flight data recovery

This aircraft is a clean illustration that a page archive and a sensor archive are two completely
separate things. The Internet Archive holds **no snapshot of any N872RA tracking page at all** — its
crawler simply never visited one — and yet the flight record itself is intact: **48 days** of raw
track on airplanes.live and **24 monthly samples reaching back to April 2022**. Nothing was lost;
nothing was ever archived on the page side to lose. In a browser today the FlightRadar24 page loads
normally and shows **294 history rows in seven days** — under operator *Regency Air*, this airframe
flies constantly.

| Source | What it holds for N872RA |
|---|---|
| adsb.lol `globe_history` | 12 days, 1 Sep 2025 – 10 Oct 2025 |
| globe.airplanes.live | 48 days, 1 Sep 2025 – 5 Jun 2026 |
| ADS-B Exchange monthly sample | 24 days, 1 Apr 2022 – 1 Aug 2026 |
| FlightRadar24 page | HTTP 200 in a browser on 24 Aug 2026; 294 history rows on the free seven-day tier; older history paywalled |
| Internet Archive | 0 page snapshots held; 0 flight rows recovered off them |

**Nothing here was found to have been removed.** No tracking site was found to have taken this
aircraft's page down. Our scripted probe recorded HTTP 403 from FlightRadar24, RadarBox and
Planespotters — but the identical script got the identical 403 for five unrelated control aircraft
chosen for having nothing to do with this case, so a 403 is a robot block and says nothing about
this tail. See [What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means).

Where a day is absent from an archive, that means a volunteer receiver network heard nothing on that
day. It does not establish that the aircraft did not fly, and it does not establish that a
transponder was switched off. Both free daily archives begin in 2023, so every 2022 date is
untestable by construction rather than missing.

The raw files are on disk at `site/docs/Planes/N872RA/data/recovered/` — the source is in every
filename, and each file carries a `.meta.json` sidecar recording the source, the URL, the HTTP
status, the byte count and the date, so any line above can be checked without trusting this page.

## The primary flight data we hold on this aircraft, and where it came from

This page has described N872RA as a thin tail — a line on somebody's screenshot of a Provo arrival
list. On the position side it is not thin at all. **We hold 70 days of primary track for this
aircraft**, more than for any other Kirk-side tail in this investigation, pulled by us from free
public archives and saved to disk at `site/docs/Planes/N872RA/data/recovered/` with a `.meta.json`
sidecar beside every payload recording the source, the URL, the HTTP status, the byte count and the
retrieval time.

| Measure | N872RA |
|---|---|
| Days of primary track held | **70** |
| Span of those days | **1 April 2022 – 1 August 2026** |
| From adsb.lol `globe_history` | 12 days |
| From globe.airplanes.live (the backup network) | 48 days |
| From the ADS-B Exchange monthly sample | 24 days |
| Corroborated by **both** free daily archives | **12 days** |
| Recovered **only** from the backup network | **36 days** |
| Held **only** by the monthly sample | **22 days** |
| Days before 2023 | **2** |
| Fields touched most often | **John Wayne / Orange County (KSNA) — 45** · Salt Lake City (KSLC) 9 · Scottsdale (KSDL) 5 |

Counts from `site/docs/Planes/following/apis/public_open_source/data/provenance/tail_provenance.json`.

**Thirty-six of the seventy days — more than half the record — exist only because we asked a second
archive.** That is the single clearest demonstration on this page of why one source is not enough.
It is not evidence that anything was removed from the first archive: the two networks run on
different volunteer receivers with different antennas in different places, and a busy charter jet
flying constantly will be heard unevenly. But a check of this tail against adsb.lol alone would
have seen 12 days and concluded the record was thin. The record is not thin.

### The 2022 traces, on this site for the first time

`samples.adsbexchange.com` publishes a free full-day sample for **the first of every month**, back
to 2016, with no account and no key. Two of those sampled days fall in 2022 for N872RA.

| Sampled day | First fix | Last fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 April 2022 | Austin-Bergstrom, TX (KAUS, 1.41 km) | John Wayne / Orange County, CA (KSNA, 0.19 km) |
| 1 December 2022 | Scottsdale, AZ (KSDL, 0.65 km) | Palmdale, CA (KPMD, 3.82 km) |

Positions are the nearest airport and the distance to it — **geometry, not a landing record.** The
sampling caveat travels with both rows: **this is one day per month.** Two sampled days establish
where the aircraft was on two days in 2022. They support no claim about frequency, about any other
date, or about a pattern.

Read against the 70-day record, they say something modest and useful. **KSNA is where this aircraft
lives** — 45 touches, far more than any other field, and it matches the FAA registrant's own city:
A&E Hawker LP of Santa Ana, California, which is Orange County. A Southern California charter jet
that ends its day at John Wayne in 2022 is doing exactly what a Southern California charter jet
does. The **Santa Barbara** origin this page treats as a flag is an ordinary short hop inside that
aircraft's normal operating area.

**And the fact that cuts both ways, stated plainly: Salt Lake City is this tail's second most
touched field, at 9 days.** That does not make Utah suspicious for N872RA; it makes Utah routine
for N872RA. An arrival into the Provo area by an aircraft that regularly works the Salt Lake
corridor is weaker evidence of anything, not stronger.

### No archived page snapshots at all — and what that does and does not mean

The Internet Archive holds **zero captures of any N872RA tracking page**, on any site, ever. Our
re-query of the Archive's CDX index on 24 August 2026 succeeded and returned an empty result, which
is a different thing from a query that failed.

**Nobody ever asked the Archive to crawl these pages. That is absence of archival interest, not
removal.** There is no 200-to-404 transition here, because there was never a 200 to begin with.
Across the entire archive dataset for this investigation, every recorded CDX row on every URL is
`statuscode=200`, and there is no page in it that was archived and later disappeared. Any account
that treats an empty Archive result for this tail as a takedown is reading a gap in crawler
coverage as an act.

This also makes N872RA a clean illustration of a distinction that gets blurred constantly: **a page
archive and a sensor archive are two unrelated things.** The page archive holds nothing. The sensor
archive holds 70 days. Nothing was lost, because nothing was ever archived on the page side to
lose.

### This tail is not in the overlap sheet

None of the 85 claimed overlap rows in `site/docs/Planes/following/overlaps.csv` names N872RA. The
sheet names SU-BTT, SU-BND, or no aircraft at all. N872RA belongs to the **Kirk-side** aircraft —
tails that bear on where the Kirk party and its associates were, not tails alleged to have followed
them. **Do not merge those threads.** The 18-month following pattern, the 10 September day-of
timeline, the [N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview) thread and the
[N888KG](/Planes/N888KG/overview) Wendover departure are four separate claims about four separate
aircraft sets.

The limit applies here with particular force, because this page raises the "tarmac hug" question:
**position data locates an aircraft, not a person.** Nothing in 70 days of track establishes who was
aboard N872RA on 10 September 2025, and it therefore cannot confirm or refute the identification of
this airframe as the Santa Barbara aircraft in that account. Private manifests are not public.

### The counterargument

The plain reading is that N872RA is a hard-working Orange County charter jet — the FlightRadar24
page served 294 history rows in a single seven-day window — that flies Southern California, Utah
and Arizona constantly, and that a Santa Barbara-to-Provo leg on 10 September 2025 is one more
ordinary day of that. Nothing we pulled contradicts that reading. The Salt Lake City count and the
Santa Ana home base both actively support it. **On this evidence there is nothing anomalous about
this aircraft's presence at Provo**, and its place on the arrival list is a manifest-review item
rather than a lead.

### Why pulling this data matters at all

The claim this section answers is not about N872RA. It is the broader claim that this category of
record has been erased. **We pulled more than four years of ordinary flying for this aircraft, on
demand, from free archives — 70 days of it, for a tail whose tracking pages nobody ever bothered to
archive.** What came back is unremarkable business aviation. That is a finding: a record that
reconstructs this easily is not a record that has been scrubbed, and reporting the recovery honestly
means reporting what it shows.

## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — N872RA](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n872ra) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.
* [ADS-B Exchange — N872RA](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/?reg=N872RA) — volunteer-fed, unfiltered tracker that does **not** honor LADD, so it often shows blocked aircraft and tracks the filtered sites leave out.
