# N2100L LASAI Sister Ship

**N2100L** is named in the investigation as a **Bombardier Global 6500** belonging to the **LASAI Aviation** fleet — the same operator type and airframe as the [N1098L spy plane](/Planes/N1098L/overview), and reportedly tied to the same **Leidos**-associated U.S. Army reconnaissance work. According to the case notes, LASAI's fleet of Global 6500s supports U.S. Army reconnaissance, which is why crew and tasking details for these tail numbers are often classified or otherwise protected. This page exists so the sister ship is catalogued. **It has not been independently placed over UVU**, and whether it played any role at all is disputed among online researchers — both views are presented below.

## Why this aircraft is in the catalog

N2100L appears in the investigation as context for understanding [N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview): it is cited as an example of "similar Global 6500s" in the **same LASAI Aviation reconnaissance fleet**. The point researchers draw from it is structural — that N1098L was not a one-off charter but one airframe in a fleet of Army ISR-capable business jets, which helps explain why operator filings and crew manifests for these flights are hard to obtain. Separately, X posts (for example by **[@troofevades](/Influencers/x/troofevades)**) singled out N2100L for its movements the night after the September 10, 2025 shooting and offered flight-path video comparisons against N1098L — claims that other users have pushed back on.

## Aircraft identification

* **Tail number:** N2100L
* **Type:** Bombardier Global 6500 (G6500)
* **Operator/fleet:** LASAI Aviation (the same operator family behind N1098L's LASAI Aviation II LLC), reportedly Leidos-owned or Leidos-associated
* **Program/callsign:** Tied in X analysis to the U.S. Army / Leidos **AXLE**-series ISR effort. The callsign for this airframe is recorded in the case notes as **AXEL21** (also rendered **AXLE21** in some threads) — the same family of business-jet reconnaissance flights that N1098L flew under the **AXLE10** callsign
* **Reported role:** Supports U.S. Army reconnaissance, per the case notes — the same Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance (ISR) mission profile associated with the broader LASAI / [HADES program](/Planes/ISR-Operations)
* **Significance:** Cited as a fleet sister ship to N1098L, illustrating that LASAI operates multiple ISR-capable Global 6500s

## Flight history relevant to the case

The hard, sourced facts on this airframe are thin — keep that in mind when weighing the claims below.

* **Night of September 10–11, 2025** — X posts (e.g. [@troofevades](/Influencers/x/troofevades)) flag N2100L as active the night after the shooting and present ADS-B flight-path video for analysis. This is an OSINT claim circulated on social media, not a confirmed track in the court or FAA record.
* **Reported Sept 11 leg — Ontario to El Paso, with a St. George pass.** According to OSINT posts (timing referenced to a **[@BasedSamParker](/Influencers/x/based-sam-parker)** tweet), N2100L is said to have departed **Ontario, California (ONT)** at roughly **6:59 PM PDT on September 11, 2025**, on a route toward **El Paso, Texas**, and to have made a **low-altitude pass over St. George, Utah**. Researchers note this would place the aircraft over southern Utah around the **~8 PM MDT** window of [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson)'s reported surrender. This is a circulated social-media flight-path claim, not a confirmed track in the court or FAA record, and the inference that the two events are connected is the posters' own.
* **No documented UVU pass** — Unlike N1098L's daytime descent in the region, there is **no public ADS-B track in the case material placing N2100L over Orem, UVU, or the Utah corridor** on the day of the shooting. It is catalogued here as a fleet-mate, not as an aircraft independently observed at the scene.

## Theories about the aircraft's role

These are competing, unresolved interpretations of the same limited data. None is established.

* **"Not ISR-equipped, not involved."** Some X users argue N2100L (and the fleet flagging around it) is a false lead — that this particular airframe was **not ISR-equipped and was not involved**, and that grouping it with N1098L is guilt-by-fleet-association rather than evidence. Critics specifically contend that any sensor fit on these jets is **interior equipment only, with no external mods** visible to confirm an ISR role, and that the fleet is associated with the Army's **[HADES](/US_Intelligence/US_Army_HADES)** "training" program rather than an operational mission. On this reading, the only solidly documented aircraft of interest is N1098L.
* **Post-event surveillance suspicion.** Other researchers tie N2100L's reported night-after activity to **post-event positioning or monitoring**, arguing that an Army/Leidos ISR jet maneuvering in the hours after the assassination is worth explaining regardless of where it was at the moment of the shooting. Proponents point to the timing and the platform's surveillance capability.
* **Surrender-timing / coordination question.** A narrower version of the surveillance theory focuses on the reported **St. George pass coinciding with Tyler Robinson's ~8 PM MDT September 11 surrender window**, with some posters (citing **[@BasedSamParker](/Influencers/x/based-sam-parker)** and **[@tjphager](/Influencers/x/tjphager)**) raising the question of pre-knowledge or coordination. This is framed as an open question rather than a finding; the overlap is a claimed timing correlation only, and no record establishes any link between the flight and the surrender.
* **Structural / fleet argument.** A more cautious view uses N2100L only to make a structural point — that N1098L sits inside a **fleet** of Army ISR-capable Global 6500s, which is why operator filings and crew manifests are hard to obtain — and draws no conclusion about N2100L's own movements on September 10.

## Who is making these claims

The N2100L discussion lives almost entirely on X, where citizen investigators trade ADS-B screenshots and flight-path videos.

* **[@troofevades](/Influencers/x/troofevades)** — posted the flight-path video analysis of N2100L's reported night-after movements and the side-by-side comparison with N1098L.
* **[@BasedSamParker](/Influencers/x/based-sam-parker)** — referenced in threads for the timing of the reported September 11 Ontario-to-El Paso leg and the St. George pass.
* **[@tjphager](/Influencers/x/tjphager)** — cited alongside [@BasedSamParker](/Influencers/x/based-sam-parker) on the surrender-window timing question.
* **Grok responses** — Grok (the X AI assistant) was queried inside several of these threads, and its replies are circulated as part of the debate over whether N2100L is ISR-equipped and whether the fleet flagging holds up. Grok's answers are AI-generated summaries of public data, not independent confirmation — treat them as one more contested input, not a source of record.

All of the above is OSINT (open-source intelligence) circulating on social media. None of it is a confirmed track in the court or FAA record.

## What is and is not established

* **Established:** N2100L is named as a LASAI Global 6500 in the same reconnaissance-supporting fleet as N1098L, and is reportedly tied to Leidos / U.S. Army ISR work.
* **Reported / contested:** Social-media (ADS-B) analysis claims night-after activity around September 10–11, 2025; other users dispute that the aircraft was ISR-equipped or involved at all.
* **Not established:** No public track places N2100L over UVU or the Utah corridor on the day of the shooting, and no official record confirms a role in the case.

## Open questions

1. Was N2100L airborne anywhere relevant on or around September 10–11, 2025, and can the [@troofevades](/Influencers/x/troofevades) ADS-B claim — and the reported Ontario (ONT) departure, El Paso routing, and St. George low-altitude pass — be independently confirmed?
2. Is N2100L actually ISR-equipped, as the post-event-surveillance theory assumes, or is the "not equipped / not involved" view correct?
3. How many Global 6500s does LASAI Aviation operate, and which support Army ISR tasking?
4. Do N2100L and N1098L share crews, contracting authority, basing, or AXLE-series callsigns?

## Flight data recovery

N2100L is registered to the same owner as [N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview) — LASAI Aviation II LLC —
and its data situation is much the same: present, ordinary, and not withheld. It is also the busiest
aircraft of the pair right now. Its FlightRadar24 page loads in a browser and served **378 history
rows** on 24 August 2026, which on a free tier that shows only the last seven days means this
airframe has flown a great deal in the last week.

**Nothing here was found to have been removed.** No tracking site was found to have taken this
aircraft's page down. Our scripted probe recorded HTTP 403 from FlightRadar24, RadarBox and
Planespotters — but the identical script got the identical 403 for five unrelated control aircraft
chosen for having nothing to do with this case, so a 403 is a robot block and says nothing about
this tail. See [What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means).

Where a day is absent from an archive, that means a volunteer receiver network heard nothing on that
day. It does not establish that the aircraft did not fly, and it does not establish that a
transponder was switched off. Both free daily archives begin in 2023, so every 2022 date is
untestable by construction rather than missing.

This page opens by saying "the hard, sourced facts on this airframe are thin." That was true when it
was written. It is less true now, and the change came from pulling primary ADS-B position data rather
than reading anybody's screenshot of it. Everything below was pulled from free public archives on
**24 August 2026** and written to disk in this repository.

**The thread marker first.** N2100L belongs to the **ISR / HADES** line of inquiry — the LASAI
Aviation II Global 6500 fleet. That is a **different claim** from the eighteen-month pattern of
foreign-registered aircraft alleged to have shadowed Charlie and Erika Kirk, which lives at
[Following Planes](/Planes/following/overview). This site links the two and never merges them.

### The 10 September 2025 answer, from primary data

This page has said there is "no public ADS-B track in the case material placing N2100L over Orem,
UVU, or the Utah corridor" on the day of the shooting. **Both free archives now hold that day, and
they confirm it far more strongly than the original wording did.** On 10 September 2025 N2100L's
**closest approach to the UVU campus was 559 km** — it was working the Texas/New Mexico corridor and
then California. It was nowhere near Utah.

**That is a finding that cuts against the claim, and it is published here for exactly that reason.**

### The 11 September legs — the OSINT claim, checked against position data

The claim on this page, sourced to posts by [@troofevades](/Influencers/x/troofevades) and
[@BasedSamParker](/Influencers/x/based-sam-parker), is that N2100L departed **Ontario, California at
roughly 6:59 PM PDT on 11 September 2025**, routed toward **El Paso**, and made **a low pass over
St George, Utah**. Here is what the raw trace holds, from both archives, times UTC with local in
brackets:

| Time | Where | Baro alt | Groundspeed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00:39–01:59Z 12 Sep (17:39–18:59 PDT 11 Sep) | **on the ground at Ontario, California** | 925 ft | — |
| **01:59:34Z (18:59:34 PDT 11 Sep)** | **Ontario, 0.11 km — departure roll** | 1,175 ft | 159 kt |
| **03:01:10Z (21:01:10 MDT 11 Sep)** | **directly over St George, Utah, 0.10 km** | **4,975 ft** | **173 kt** |
| 03:41:36Z | western New Mexico, cruise | 37,000 ft | — |
| 20:09:21Z 12 Sep | **Biggs Army Airfield, El Paso, 2.10 km** | 3,900 ft | 124 kt |
| 23:27:42Z 12 Sep | on the ground, Austin-Bergstrom | ground | 91 kt |

**Every element of the posted claim is corroborated by primary position data.** The Ontario
departure is at **18:59:34 PDT** — the posted figure was "roughly 6:59 PM PDT", and it is right to
the minute. The St George pass is real: the aircraft is **directly overhead at 4,975 ft**, and
St George Regional sits at about 2,941 ft, so roughly **2,000 ft above field elevation at 173 knots**
— low and slow for a Global 6500, which cruises above 40,000 ft at more than three times that speed.
El Paso is reached the following afternoon.

**Two things must be said in the same breath, because both are true.**

* **St George is not on the way.** A direct line from Ontario to El Paso runs east-southeast.
  St George is well to the north-east. The aircraft tracked out to St George, descended over it, then
  turned south-east and climbed to 37,000 ft. **That is a deliberate detour and a deliberate
  descent**, and the data shows it plainly.
* **A descent and a slow pass over a public airport is also exactly what a practice approach looks
  like**, and St George Regional is an ordinary public field. Nothing in a position record says why
  an aircraft descended. **We can prove the manoeuvre. We cannot prove a reason, and we do not
  assert one.**

**The surrender-timing inference needs a correction.** This page carries a theory tying the St George
pass to [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson)'s reported surrender window of around 8 PM MDT on
11 September. The overflight is at **21:01 MDT — about an hour after** that window, not during it.
The timing correlation the posts drew is looser than the posts implied. Tyler Robinson is **charged,
not convicted**, and nothing here bears on that case in either direction.

**And the standing of the claim has changed, precisely.** It remains **not a court record and not an
FAA record**. It is now **confirmed against primary ADS-B position data held by two independent
volunteer archives** — which is a considerably better class of evidence than the social-media
screenshot this page previously described it as, and it should be described that way from here.

### N2100L was on the ground at Provo Municipal on 1 July 2025

New to this site, and it belongs on the record. The ADS-B Exchange monthly sample for **1 July 2025**
tracks N2100L from Miley Memorial Field, Big Piney, Wyoming, into the Utah Valley, passing
**4.17 km from the UVU campus at 4,925 ft on the approach**, touching down and sitting **on the
ground at Provo Municipal Airport, 0.05 km from the airport reference point, from 23:47:48Z
(17:47 MDT) to the end of the sampled day.**

**Read it carefully, because it is easy to over-read.**

* This is **1 July 2025**, ten weeks before the assassination. It is not a September date.
* **The 4.17 km figure is not a campus overflight.** [N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview)'s two Provo
  approaches on 10 September bottom out at **4.16 km** from the same point. **4.16–4.17 km from the
  UVU campus is what the Provo Municipal approach path measures** — it is a description of an
  airport, not of an aircraft looking at a university.
* A Global 6500 landing at a public regional airport with a full-service Duncan Aviation maintenance
  operation on the field is unremarkable in itself.

What it does establish is narrow and worth having: **a LASAI Aviation II Global 6500 was physically
on the ground at Provo Municipal in the summer of 2025**, which is a fact rather than an inference,
and it is the first such fact this site has held for this tail.

### Why there is nothing before November 2023

Neither this aircraft nor [N1098L](/Planes/N1098L/overview) has a single trace before 2023, and the
honest explanation is about the archives and about the airframes, not about concealment. The only
free source reaching back to 2022 is the **ADS-B Exchange monthly sample**, which publishes **one day
a month — the 1st.** An aircraft that did not happen to fly on the first of a month simply does not
appear in it. Beyond that, this airframe is **serial 60100, built 2023**: there is no 2022 flying to
find. **Say the mechanism; do not imply anything about the aircraft.**

### The backup network holds 21 of the 45 days, and that is not suppression

**Twenty-one of N2100L's forty-five days exist only on globe.airplanes.live.** adsb.lol does not have
them — including the El Paso leg of 12 September 2025, where adsb.lol's coverage puts the aircraft no
closer than 104 km to Biggs while the backup network has it 2.10 km out at 3,900 ft. **Without the
second archive that leg could not have been confirmed at all.**

The control result is what keeps this honest. adsb.lol returns **HTTP 403 for every aircraft** from
**exactly 12 October 2025** to about 30 December 2025, and **HTTP 404 for every aircraft** from about
31 December 2025 to roughly 1 August 2026 — including unrelated control airframes with no connection
to this case. **Those are archive-wide conditions. Nothing about N2100L was suppressed.** Two
volunteer networks with different feeders and different antennas hold different days, and that is
all this is.

### The archived pages hold nothing usable

Three Internet Archive captures of this aircraft's FlightAware page are held — **26 March 2023,
6 March 2025 and 20 September 2025** — and **no flight rows were recovered from any of them.** That
is not because the tables were empty. Modern FlightAware is a client-side application: the archived
HTML is page chrome with the flight table never rendered into it. On the tails where these captures
have been re-parsed they are labelled `JAVASCRIPT_SHELL_NO_SERVER_RENDERED_ROWS`, and **that label is
deliberately not "0 rows"** — an unparseable capture is not an empty one, and recording it as a zero
is how an absence of parsing gets mistaken for an absence of evidence.

### The counterargument

The strongest case against everything in this section is short. A Global 6500 registered to an Army
ISR contractor, based at an Army airfield, flying approaches at public regional airports and moving
between Texas, California and Utah, is **an aircraft doing its job**. Ontario, St George, Provo and
El Paso are all ordinary public fields. A descent over one of them is a manoeuvre, not a motive.
Position data shows where an aircraft was and how fast it was going and is **silent on who was
aboard, what was fitted, what was recorded and who ordered the flight**. And the thing that would
actually settle it — a tasking record — is not held by any archive in this section. **Our data
locates the aircraft. It does not place Charlie Kirk, Erika Kirk or anybody else anywhere, and it
establishes no tasking in either direction.**

## Track This Aircraft

Research this aircraft's flight history yourself on both major flight-tracking sites. They differ in one important way:

* [FlightRadar24 — N2100L](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/aircraft/n2100l) — large commercial tracker that honors the FAA's [LADD](https://www.faa.gov/pilots/ladd) block list, so flights an owner or the government has asked to hide can be missing.
* [ADS-B Exchange — N2100L](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/?reg=N2100L) — volunteer-fed, unfiltered tracker that does **not** honor LADD, so it often shows blocked aircraft and tracks the filtered sites leave out.
