# T7ELL & EJM36 (Flagged Tails)

When researchers expanded the Egypt story from **two** jets ([SU-BTT](../SU-BTT/overview),
[SU-BND](../SU-BND/overview)) to a **five-plane armada**, Project Constitution's post also flagged
**two additional foreign tails**: **T7ELL** and **EJM36**. Public route detail for these
two is **thin** compared to the SU-registered fleet — they appear in source notes as
*"more planes"* grouped for disclosure purposes.

Source: [@ProjectConstitu on X](https://x.com/ProjectConstitu/status/1999347020142710872) amplifying the five-plane Provo swarm narrative.

## What is documented publicly

| Tail | Public detail level |
|------|---------------------|
| **T7ELL** | Named in CK_FILE and the Egyptian Armada fleet list. **This is no longer the whole record:** a recovery sweep on 24 August 2026 returned **37 aircraft-days of primary ADS-B data, 1 Feb 2022 – 1 Aug 2026**, and the tail now has its own page — **[T7-ELL](/Planes/T7-ELL/overview)**. See *What changed*, below. |
| **EJM36** | Still exactly this — a callsign flagged for manifest review under proposed [Fix Laws](/Fix/overview), with **no hex code, no airframe, and no logged leg anywhere**. |

**T7ELL now has an independent per-tail page:** [T7-ELL](/Planes/T7-ELL/overview). It still has **no
verified Provo leg on September 10** — nothing in its 37 recovered days puts it in Utah at all,
which is a different and more useful answer than "unknown". EJM36 has no per-tail page because it is
a callsign rather than a registration, so there is no airframe to build one around. Both remain
catalogued so the fleet picture matches the research record and law drafts.

in *What changed*, below. Neither identifier is an established participant in the assassination, and
the correction on T7ELL points away from the fleet-list framing rather than toward it.

## What the two identifiers actually are

Both tails were carried into this investigation as if they were two more Egyptian airframes alongside the SU- fleet. Neither identifier supports that reading, and the difference matters enough to state before anything else on this page.

| | **T7ELL** | **EJM36** |
|---|---|---|
| Format | Aircraft **registration** | Aircraft **callsign** |
| Prefix / designator | **T7-** | **EJM** |
| What that prefix means | The civil registration prefix of the **Republic of San Marino** | The ICAO three-letter operator designator for **Executive Jet Management**, a United States operator (a NetJets company) |
| Is it Egyptian? | **No.** Egyptian civil registrations use **SU-** | **No.** It is a US operator's flight identifier |
| Published legs in the source threads | **None** | **None** |
| Primary ADS-B data held by this repo | **37 aircraft-days, 1 Feb 2022 – 1 Aug 2026** — see [T7-ELL](/Planes/T7-ELL/overview) | **None, and none is possible** — a callsign has no hex code to query |
| Airport, date or Kirk/TPUSA pairing ever published by any researcher | **None** | **None** |
| Rows in the 85-row `overlaps.csv` register | **0** | **0** |

Two consequences follow, and this site should state both rather than carry the fleet-list framing unexamined:

* **"Five Egyptian planes plus two more" is not what the identifiers say.** The five SU- tails ([SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview), [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview), [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview), [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview), [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview)) are Egyptian-registered. T7ELL is San Marino-registered. EJM36 is a US callsign that **may not be a distinct airframe at all** — a callsign can belong to an aircraft already on the list under a different identity, which is exactly the kind of double-count that inflates a fleet tally.
* **A fleet-list mention with no sourced leg is a placeholder, not an overlap.** Neither identifier contributes anything to the [following-pattern](/Planes/following/overview) claim, and neither appears in a single row of the 85-row `site/docs/Planes/following/overlaps.csv` register. For **EJM36** the original reason still holds exactly: no date, no airport, no movement anywhere in the public record. For **T7ELL** the reason has changed — **we now hold 37 aircraft-days of its movements**, and none of them is in Utah, at Provo, or anywhere near a Kirk or TPUSA location. It contributes nothing to the pattern because the data says it was somewhere else, which is a stronger statement than silence.

## The T7- prefix is not a dead end — it is a documented Egyptian intelligence pattern

The San Marino prefix would ordinarily end the inquiry. It does not, because there is a **separate, sourced, non-hypothetical case of an Egyptian intelligence aircraft carrying a T7- registration**, and it is worth setting out precisely — while being clear it is a *different tail*.

On **29 August 2024**, the aviation-tracking account **@Dinlas3** posted:

> #T7EGY Bombardier BD-700 w/ reg. 🇸🇲 **T7-EGY** utilized by The head of the 🇪🇬 **Egyptian General Intelligence, Abbas Kamel**, from Cairo, Egypt to 🇸🇩 Port Sudan on 8/23, and went to 🇨🇩 Brazzaville, Congo on 8/26 and 🇰🇪 Nairobi, Kenya before heading back to El-Alamein. #500215 @Borrowed7Time

The attached tracking panel shows a white-and-gold **Bombardier Global 7500**, "T7-EGY" on the rear fuselage, "7500" on the tail fin, sourced to Planespotters.net.

Two months later, on **21 October 2024**, the analyst **@doyaksec** (Donato Yaakov Secchi) posted on the same subject:

> #Egypt #Intelligence — Al #Sisi has removed the head of intelligence, Abbas #Kamel, appointed advisor to the pres and replaced by his deputy, General **Hassan Mahmoud Rashad**. #CIA and #Mossad have leaked #disturbance for the removal of Kamel. Come on! Al Sisi changes his strategies?

**What this establishes, and what it does not:**

* **It establishes that the T7- San Marino register is used to carry an Egyptian government intelligence aircraft.** San Marino has no meaningful domestic aviation industry; the T7- register is widely used as a flag of convenience for privately and state-operated business jets, and at least one aircraft on it was publicly identified as the ride of Egypt's General Intelligence Service chief.
* **It does not establish that T7ELL is such an aircraft.** T7ELL and T7-EGY are **different registrations**. *(Corrected 24 August 2026: this bullet previously read "no source has published a type, an operator, an owner, a flight, or a date for T7ELL." A type, an operator string and 37 days of dated flights now exist — see [T7-ELL](/Planes/T7-ELL/overview) — and they describe a Dubai-based commercial charter Global Express, not an intelligence airframe. The owner is still unpublished.)* Anyone reading this page should treat the T7-EGY case as *what makes the San Marino register worth a records request*, not as a finding about this tail.
* **It does not name anyone as a suspect.** Abbas Kamel and Hassan Mahmoud Rashad are named here only because they are publicly identified officeholders in reporting about the aircraft's use, and no wrongdoing by either is established or alleged.

## Tested against the TPUSA event calendar: nothing to test — corrected for T7ELL

Every other tail in this fleet gets a co-location test on its page against `site/docs/Planes/following/tpusa_events.csv` — the **139-row dated record** of Charlie Kirk and TPUSA appearances from 4 January 2022 to 29 October 2025 that is the spine of the [Following section](/Planes/following/overview).

| Tail | US ground windows in the public record | Windows tested | Overlaps with a TPUSA event in the same state within 3 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| [SU-BTT](/Planes/SU-BTT/overview) | 6 | 6 | **1** — Provo, 4–10 Sep 2025 |
| [SU-BND](/Planes/SU-BND/overview) | 1 (113 days) | 1 | 1, on day 111 of 113 |
| [SU-BTU](/Planes/SU-BTU/overview) | 10 | 10 | 0 |
| [SU-BTV](/Planes/SU-BTV/overview) | 14 | 14 | 0 |
| [SU-BGM](/Planes/SU-BGM/overview) | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| **T7ELL** | **0 published** — but **8 US days now recovered** from primary ADS-B, none of them in Utah | **8** | **0** |
| **EJM36** | **0** | **0** | **not testable** |

**The T7ELL row was "not testable" and is now tested.** Of its 37 recovered aircraft-days, eight
touch the United States — Arizona and California in 2022, Los Angeles again in 2023, Miami-Opa Locka
in 2024, and a Pennsylvania–Dulles–O'Hare sequence in early October 2025. **None is in Utah. None is
at Provo Municipal. None falls within three days of a dated Charlie Kirk or TPUSA appearance in the
same state.** The test now returns a real zero instead of a shrug, which is a better result for the
page than the gap it replaces.

**The EJM36 row stands exactly as written.** It cannot be shown to have followed anyone, because
**no source has ever published a single flight for it and it has no hex code to query.** Carrying
that gap openly is worth more than filling it.

Both zeros carry the same limit every co-location test on this site carries: 139 dated Kirk/TPUSA
appearances is every one this repo can source, and it is nowhere near every location the Kirks were
at. **A zero is a statement about what we can currently prove.**

## What would move either tail out of placeholder status

*(Item 2 has been met for T7ELL. It is kept here, struck through in words rather than deleted, so the record of what was asked for and what answered it stays visible.)*

1. **A type and owner for T7ELL** from the San Marino Civil Aviation Authority register — the register is small and the record is obtainable. **Partly met:** the ADS-B traces carry the type (GLEX, Bombardier Global Express) and two community sources give conflicting operators. **The owner is still unpublished, and the register is still the way to settle it.**
2. ~~**A single dated ADS-B leg** for T7ELL at any airport, from any tracker, with a screenshot.~~ **MET, 24 August 2026 — and far past the bar asked for.** Not one leg but **37 aircraft-days** of raw position traces, from three independent free archives, saved to this repo with their HTTP metadata. See [T7-ELL](/Planes/T7-ELL/overview).
3. **The airframe behind EJM36.** Executive Jet Management files hundreds of flights under EJM callsigns; resolving EJM36 to a specific N-number would establish whether it is a distinct aircraft or a duplicate of one already listed.
4. **An FBO or ramp record** at Provo Municipal, Lincoln, Wichita or Wilmington naming either identifier — the [Fix Laws](/Fix/overview) manifest-disclosure provision is written to compel exactly this.



## What this aircraft would cost to buy new today

**Not estimable.** No aircraft type has been established for this tail in any sourced record so no comparable can be selected. EJM36 is an operator callsign format rather than a tail number so no single airframe can be priced.

Every other aircraft in this investigation is priced on [Aircraft Costs](/Planes/Aircraft-Costs/overview). These two are the only entries on that table with no figure, and the reason is the same reason they have no published flight legs: there is not enough established about them to work with.

## Flight data recovery

The recovery sweep covered **T7-ELL** — EJM36 is an operator callsign, not a registration, so there
is no aircraft page anywhere to probe for it. T7-ELL turns out to be well recorded, and what the
archives hold argues against grouping it with the Egyptian SU- tails rather than for it.

| Source | What it holds for T7-ELL |
|---|---|
| adsb.lol `globe_history` | **5 days**, 3 Sep 2025 – 11 Oct 2025 |
| globe.airplanes.live | **26 days**, 3 Sep 2025 – 29 May 2026 |
| ADS-B Exchange monthly sample | **12 days**, 1 Feb 2022 – 1 Aug 2026 |
| FlightRadar24 page | **HTTP 200 in a browser on 24 August 2026**, identity block intact — *GLEX · Mode S 50018A*, operator **"Falcon Luxe" / "Falcon Luxe San Marino"**. **0 history rows** on the free seven-day tier, meaning it had not flown in that window; older history paywalled. |
| Internet Archive | **0 page snapshots** held; **0 flight rows** recovered. |

**An operator correction worth recording.** This repo's fleet file cites the community database
adsbdb giving the operator as *Solairus Aviation*. FlightRadar24's own page, read in a browser on
24 August 2026, gives **Falcon Luxe / Falcon Luxe San Marino**. Both are commercial charter
operators and neither is an Egyptian government entity, which is consistent with the recovered
traces — a Dubai- and Van Nuys-based global charter pattern, set out on
[Flight Data Recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview).

**Removed from: nothing.** No tracking site was found to have removed this aircraft's page. Our
scripted probe recorded HTTP 403 from FlightRadar24, RadarBox and Planespotters, and those 403s are
robot blocks rather than removals — five unrelated control aircraft got the identical 403 from the
identical script, and all of them load normally in a browser. See
[What A 403 Actually Means](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/What-A-403-Means).

Raw files: `site/docs/Planes/T7-ELL/data/recovered/` — the source is in every filename, and each
file has a `.meta.json` sidecar recording source, URL, HTTP status, bytes and date.

Fleet-wide: [Flight Data Recovery](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/overview) ·
[Per-Aircraft Recovery Status](/Planes/Flight-Data-Recovery/Per-Aircraft-Status)

## What changed: T7ELL is no longer a placeholder, EJM36 still is

This page was built around a single honest sentence — *nobody has ever published a flight for
either of these identifiers, so there is nothing to test.* **That sentence was true when it was
written and half of it is now false**, and the correction is worth more to a reader than the tidier
version of the page would be.

**What changed for T7ELL.** A recovery sweep of the free ADS-B archives on **24 August 2026** —
adsb.lol `globe_history`, globe.airplanes.live, and the ADS-B Exchange free monthly sample — returned
**37 aircraft-days of primary position data** for the aircraft with Mode S hex `50018a`, which every
trace self-identifies as `"r": "T7-ELL"`. The span runs **1 February 2022 to 1 August 2026**. Five of
those days are corroborated by both free daily archives; **21 exist only because the backup network
was checked**; 11 come only from the monthly sample. The raw files, with their `.meta.json`
provenance sidecars, are at `site/docs/Planes/T7-ELL/data/recovered/`.

**The tail now has its own page: [T7-ELL](/Planes/T7-ELL/overview).** In short, what the data shows:

| | |
|---|---|
| Type carried in the traces | GLEX — Bombardier BD-700 Global Express |
| Field touched most often | **Al Maktoum International, Dubai (OMDW) — 12 of 37 days** |
| 2022 legs recovered | Dateland AZ → **Tucson**; **Los Angeles → Tucson**; **Los Angeles → Farnborough, UK** |
| US days out of 37 | **8** — Arizona, California, Florida, Pennsylvania, Virginia, Illinois |
| Days in **Utah** | **0** |
| Days at **Provo Municipal (KPVU)** | **0** |
| Rows in the 85-row `overlaps.csv` | **0** |
| Where it was 3, 4 and 7 September 2025 | Dubai, Greece, **Paris-Le Bourget** — Europe and the Gulf, both archives agreeing |

**This corrects the fleet-list framing, and it corrects it against us.** T7ELL was carried on this
site as a sixth aircraft in an "Egyptian armada". The registration prefix already said San Marino;
the primary flight data now says a **US-operated and Gulf-based commercial charter Global Express**,
flying US domestic and transatlantic legs and a route map across five continents. That is exactly
what a San Marino flag of convenience with a commercial operator looks like. **It is not what an
Egyptian government fleet aircraft looks like, and we should stop implying otherwise.**

**What did NOT change, and must not be blurred:**

* **EJM36 is untouched by any of this.** `EJM` is the ICAO operator designator for **Executive Jet
  Management**, a United States operator. **EJM36 is a callsign, not a registration.** It has no hex
  code, so there is no aircraft to query in any archive; it has never had a single logged leg; and
  it may not be a distinct airframe at all. Every word this page says about EJM36 stands.
* **We hold no data for T7-ELL on 10 September 2025.** The archives jump from 7 September to
  1 October. Nothing here establishes where it was on the day itself.
* **Zero Utah days is not proof it was never in Utah.** 37 days out of roughly 1,640 in the span is
  thin coverage, and volunteer ADS-B networks have real holes. **Absence in an archive is not a
  refutation** — it is an absence.
* **The counterargument to all of it** is the same one this page already carries: a commercial
  charter aircraft doing charter work explains every fact above without any further theory, and a
  fleet-list mention was never evidence to begin with. The separately-sourced **T7-EGY** case above
  is about a **different registration** and does not transfer to this one.
