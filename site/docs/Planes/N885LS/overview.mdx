# N885LS — The Las Vegas Sands BBJ

**N885LS** appears in the case notes alongside the [99-0004](/Planes/99-0004-Vance/overview) entry — the US Air Force C-32A logged into Las Vegas on 27 May 2025. It is included here so the tail is documented rather than left as a bare N-number in a research file.

## Aircraft identification

From the **FAA Civil Aviation Registry**:

* **Tail number:** N885LS
* **Type:** **Boeing 737-7EL** — a Boeing Business Jet, the corporate conversion of the 737-700
* **Year built:** 2001 · **Serial number:** 32775
* **Registered owner:** **LAS VEGAS SANDS CORP**, Las Vegas, Nevada
* **Seats:** 149 in the FAA type record; a BBJ interior seats far fewer in a VIP configuration
* **Cost to buy new today:** **~$76.5 million** — the 737-700 BBJ is out of production; Boeing's in-production equivalent is the **BBJ MAX 7** at roughly $76.5 million factory list for a green airframe, before interior completion. See [Aircraft Costs](/Planes/Aircraft-Costs/overview).

That makes N885LS, together with the C-32A, one of the two most expensive airframes in this entire investigation.

## Why it is in the file

The connection is **co-location, and nothing more**: a corporate BBJ registered to a Las Vegas casino operator, noted in the same Las Vegas entry as a government VIP flight on 27 May 2025 — more than three months before the assassination.

This site takes no position on why either aircraft was in Las Vegas. Las Vegas Sands is headquartered there and its corporate jet being at its home base is unremarkable. **Presence at the same airport as another aircraft is not evidence of a connection between them**, and readers should weight this entry accordingly. It is documented for completeness of the tail list, not because a case has been made.

## Open questions

## Where this page fits

Co-location at an airport is not a connection, and this page says so about itself — a casino operator's Boeing Business Jet noted in the same Las Vegas entry as [the C-32A logged there on 27 May 2025](/Planes/99-0004-Vance/overview), documented so that a bare N-number in a research file does not quietly become a lead, and priced against every other airframe on [Aircraft Costs](/Planes/Aircraft-Costs/overview). With that C-32A it is one of the two most expensive airframes in the whole investigation, which is the one useful thing it contributes — a sense of who can afford to move people this way, set against the donor money running through the [political context](/political_context/overview), the [companies and organizations index](/Companies_Organizations/overview) and [Turning Point USA](/TPUSA/overview) itself. The entry it was noted against is the more substantial one: [the 99-0004 Vance flight](/Planes/99-0004-Vance/overview) documents a US Air Force C-32A reported as Air Force Two into Las Vegas on 27 May 2025 and states the open question about who was aboard — exactly the category of record the [proposed disclosure laws](/Fix/overview) would compel, and [Law 1](/laws/DoJ_FBI/Law_1_DoJ_FBI) is the draft that names the agencies holding it.
