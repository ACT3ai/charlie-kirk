# The Mic Mounted Under the Shirt, Not Over (Claims)

:::caution Attributed claims only
**The AV staffer is a living private individual charged with nothing.** We assume that every TPUSA employee and every audio-visual contractor working at UVU that day had no knowledge of any explosive that may have been in the mic, even if they handled the mic. Everything below is a reported observation, not a finding, and no one is accused.
:::

Where the microphone sat matters only because of what the majority of citizen investigators on X have
concluded about how Charlie Kirk was killed: not by the rifle in the government's account but by an
[explosive device at the microphone itself](/Tyler_Robinson_Not_Assassin/explosive-mic-killed-charlie).

## What is alleged

The file records the observation in one line and does not develop it further: the wireless mic was placed under Charlie Kirk's shirt when it already had a furry windscreen attached, while "numerous other outdoor debates show that the wireless mic with that windscreen was placed on the outside of Charlie's shirt." An AV staffer is described elsewhere in the file as an **AV team member who reportedly mic'ed up Charlie Kirk at the event**, part of an AV crew that included Terryl Farnsworth, and reportedly seen adjusting equipment during audio and feedback issues.

The mounting details matter because they are the physical foundation the exploding-mic theory is built on. The file describes a **RØDE Wireless PRO transmitter** measuring 44 × 45.3 × 18.5 mm, worn under Kirk's white "FREEDOM" shirt and held by a **MagClip GO** — a magnetic clasp where a clip sits outside the shirt and a neodymium magnet sandwiches the fabric from behind. The file states that multiple cameras reportedly captured a visible rectangular bulge on the upper right chest before the event, created by the 18.5 mm housing pushing the fabric outward, and that **after the event the bulge is reportedly gone** while the clasp remains visible on a shirt that lies nearly flat.

The "under, not over" observation is offered as the entry point to that argument: if the mic had been worn conventionally, on the outside, there would be no concealed object and no bulge to lose.

## Why the placement matters

Running a lavalier under the shirt is common broadcast practice, and nothing in the file suggests the AV staffer or anyone on the AV crew did anything but their job. Whoever mic'ed Kirk is not the question. The question is what the placement did physically.

Investigators point to three things. First, the change: other outdoor Kirk debates reportedly show the same mic and windscreen worn **outside** the shirt, so this event departed from his own pattern ([AV team mic setup](/Mic/AV_Team_Mic_Setup)). Second, the geometry: under the shirt, held by a MagClip GO, the transmitter sat directly against his chest and close to his neck, with fabric over it — exactly where the shirt is seen [expanding outward before the neck wound appears](/Mic/mic-explosion-video-analysis). Third, the aftermath: the reported pre-event bulge is gone afterward while the clasp remains, and a [burnt object](/Mic/burnt-object-rode-transmitter) in the SUV is argued to be the melted transmitter.

The government's rifle narrative has no reason to care where the mic sat. The exploding-mic conclusion most citizen investigators have reached depends on it — which is why this one-line observation belongs in the evidence. Full hub: **[Microphone — The Exploding Mic Theory](/Mic/overview)**.

## What would settle it

1. Establish from TPUSA's AV records who specified the mic placement — the operator, the AV lead, or standard practice.
2. Obtain TPUSA's AV setup documentation or rider for the campus tour — a written standard would settle "was this normal for them" in one page.
3. Survey Kirk's prior outdoor events systematically and count over-shirt versus under-shirt mountings, rather than citing selected examples.
4. Confirm which RØDE model was actually used — the file elsewhere records an unresolved **conflict between the Wireless PRO and the Wireless ME**, and the dimensional arguments depend on it.
5. Recover and examine the transmitter itself.
