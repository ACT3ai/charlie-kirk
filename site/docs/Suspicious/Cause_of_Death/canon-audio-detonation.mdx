## What is alleged

This is the acoustic backbone of the exploding-mic conclusion, so it is worth stating carefully. According to the analysis, a professional broadcast camera — a **Canon XA55** — was recording at UVU on September 10 from roughly **46 metres** from the tent, capturing **uncompressed audio on four channels at 48,000 samples per second**. The argument is that every phone in the crowd used compressed audio that smeared the events into a single noise, while the Canon's distance and fidelity separated them.

The claimed separation is three distinct low-frequency events: **+114ms**, early energy attributed to a Mach cone; **+202ms**, a muzzle blast placing the rifle approximately **120 metres away**; and **+321ms**, described as a **detonation originating approximately 46 metres from the camera** — that is, at the tent, where Kirk was seated. The analysis states this third event is the **loudest of the three** and asserts: "It's not an echo. It's not a reflection."

From that the argument runs: a rifle 120 metres away and a detonation at the tent are **two events at two locations**, and a single shooter cannot produce both. Supporting claims include **733 supersonic N-wave signatures** captured by the Canon's shotgun microphone against **123** on the best phone recording, and the shotgun mic clipping **122,844 audio samples** because the source was in its line of fire while the built-in mics stayed clean.

## The government's narrative, and the investigators' answer

The government's account needs every loud sound in that courtyard to come from one rifle on one rooftop. Its defenders say the third arrival must be an echo — a reflection off the buildings around the courtyard. Investigators answer that point directly:

* **The third event is the loudest.** A reflection has travelled further and lost energy against a surface; the analysis finds the +321ms event is louder than the muzzle report it would supposedly be echoing. The analyst states plainly: "It's not an echo. It's not a reflection."
* **It locates at the tent.** The timing places the source roughly 46 metres from the camera — exactly where Kirk was seated — not at a building face.
* **The shotgun mic clipped.** The Canon's directional shotgun microphone clipped **122,844 samples** while the built-in mics stayed clean, which is what a very loud source directly in its line of fire produces.
* **Phones could not see it.** Compressed phone audio smears these events into one noise. Only the uncompressed four-channel 48kHz Canon track has the fidelity to separate them — which is why the crowd recordings sound like "one shot."

The same analysis puts a muzzle report roughly 120 metres away. Investigators read that as **two events at two locations**: a distant report, and a louder detonation at the tent. A sound from the rooftop does not show that the rooftop is where the harm came from — see [the acoustic two-event split](/Proof_Not_Tyler/Acoustic_Two_Event_Split) and [no muzzle flash or recoil](/Roof/no-muzzle-flash-or-recoil) on the roof footage. The loudest thing that happened in that courtyard happened at the tent.

## Why Investigators Conclude It Was the Exploding Mic

Citizen investigators have largely concluded that Charlie Kirk was killed by an explosive at his
microphone, not by the government's .30-06 rifle narrative. The main reasons:

* **The shirt and fabric move first** — [video frame analysis](/Mic/mic-explosion-video-analysis) shows the shirt expanding outward, spherically, before the neck wound appears.
* **The audio separates two blasts** — the [Canon XA55 recording](/Mic/Canon_XA55_Audio) reportedly shows a louder detonation at the tent distinct from a distant muzzle report.
* **Eyewitness veterans called it an explosion** — [Rob O'Neill and Gen. Blaine Holt](/Mic/rob-oneill-navy-seal-testimony) said it looked like an explosion, not a rifle wound.
* **No exit wound, and an energy mismatch** — a .30-06 at that range would be expected to exit and destroy tissue; see [six forensic physics phenomena](/Mic/six-forensic-physics-phenomena).
* **The mic was routed under the shirt** — [unlike prior events](/Mic/AV_Team_Mic_Setup), placing the transmitter against his chest and neck.
* **Physical debris** — a [burnt object in the SUV](/Mic/burnt-object-rode-transmitter) and a [neck fragment that may be charred plastic](/Mic/neck-fragment-charred-plastic).
* **The residue evidence was removed, not tested** — dirt, clothing, the SUV and the necklace were [hauled, cleaned or seized](/Mic/Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview) with no public residue test.
* **Means existed** — an [AES contract for miniaturized anti-personnel charges](/Mic/DoD_Contract) sits on the same timeline.

Full evidence hub: **[Microphone — The Exploding Mic Theory](/Mic/overview)**.

## What would put it beyond dispute

1. **Release the original uncompressed Canon XA55 file** so any acoustician can examine the waveform independently.
2. Publish the full method alongside the analysis, including the reflection-path geometry at +321ms.
3. Conduct an **on-site acoustic survey**: fire a reference source from the Losee rooftop and measure what actually arrives at the Canon's position. If the rooftop cannot reproduce a louder third arrival, the government's echo explanation fails.
4. Have a named, credentialed forensic acoustician review the recording — and ask why no government acoustic analysis has been released at all.
