## What is alleged

The silver box chain and cross snapping and flying upward — in some accounts to ear level — is one of the clearest signs investigators point to of a sudden pressure event at the collar.

[@cletus_jethro](/Influencers/x/cletus-jethro), who describes himself as a former USMC explosive breacher and "assault" section leader specializing in urban mobility charges, argues the pendant itself was the weapon. His reasoning, as reproduced in the investigation file: the cross would have been **anchored by the thick chain** around Kirk's neck, "allowing the force of the explosive to push off; while anchored in place by the necklace since blasts tend to follow the path of least resistance." Addressing the objection that the cross was too small, he writes that if the **center column** of the cross held a small amount of PETN "built in a cone shape... to utilize the Monroe effect, it would have been an extremely powerful upward blast relative to its size." He notes PETN's relative effectiveness of 1.66 — one gram equivalent to 1.66 grams of TNT — and argues that a shaped charge without rifling would lose speed rapidly, sufficient to cause the wound but **not to cause an exit wound**.

Separately, Candace Owens and others report that federal investigators were **urgently searching for Kirk's necklace**, and read that search as a hunt for explosive residue on an item that sat directly on his chest. It appears on a widely circulated list of anomalies the rigged-mic theory is said to explain.

Dr. Chris Martenson ([@peakprosperity](/Influencers/x/peakprosperity)), cited secondhand, reads the same fact the other way. His reported analysis attributes the snapped chain to a **high-speed cavitation event** — overpressure from a projectile expanding the neck — which requires no explosive.

**The investigators' answer:** Martenson's own description — sudden overpressure at the neck strong enough to snap a chain upward — is exactly what a small charge at the collar produces, and it arrives *before* any blood, as the [shirt-before-blood frames](./shirt-before-blood) show. A projectile's cavity follows the bullet's path; the chain went **up**, toward the ear, not along a line from the rooftop. See the [exploding-mic evidence hub](/Mic/overview).

## The government's narrative, and the investigators' answer

Defenders of the government's narrative say a rifle round's temporary cavity could snap a chain, and that collecting a victim's jewelry is routine evidence handling. Investigators answer:

* **The chain went up, not back.** A round from the government's rooftop would drive tissue along its path; the chain and cross reportedly flew **upward**, toward the ear — the direction [@cletus_jethro](/Influencers/x/cletus-jethro) says a charge anchored at the chest would push.
* **The search was urgent.** Federal investigators were reportedly **urgently searching for the necklace**. Routine collection of personal effects is not a hunt. An item sitting directly on the chest is exactly where explosive residue would be found.
* **No residue test has ever been published.** The necklace, the clothing, the soil under the tent and the SUV were all seized, hauled, or cleaned — see [the residue cover-up](/Mic/Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview) — and no result, positive or negative, has been released. The one test that would answer the question has never been shown to the public.
* **The details are about placement, not about whether.** Investigators debate whether the charge sat in the RØDE transmitter at the collar or in the pendant itself; @cletus_jethro argues the pendant, anchored by the chain. Both readings are an explosion at the collar, and neither is the government's rifle.

[@cletus_jethro](/Influencers/x/cletus-jethro) himself argues against the microphone placement. He writes that an unanchored lapel mic "would not have been very accurate" and that a charge there would push the mic *left, not right*.

**The investigators' answer:** his objection is to an *unanchored* charge clipped loosely to the outside of a shirt. On September 10 the RØDE transmitter was reportedly [routed under the shirt](./mic-under-shirt) and clasped magnetically against the chest, which anchors it against the body — the same anchoring he says makes the pendant work. His point is about where the charge sat at the collar, not about whether there was one. See [the exploding-mic evidence hub](/Mic/overview).

## Why Investigators Conclude It Was the Exploding Mic

Citizen investigators have largely concluded that Charlie Kirk was killed by an explosive at his
microphone, not by the government's .30-06 rifle narrative. The main reasons:

* **The shirt and fabric move first** — [video frame analysis](/Mic/mic-explosion-video-analysis) shows the shirt expanding outward, spherically, before the neck wound appears.
* **The audio separates two blasts** — the [Canon XA55 recording](/Mic/Canon_XA55_Audio) reportedly shows a louder detonation at the tent distinct from a distant muzzle report.
* **Eyewitness veterans called it an explosion** — [Rob O'Neill and Gen. Blaine Holt](/Mic/rob-oneill-navy-seal-testimony) said it looked like an explosion, not a rifle wound.
* **No exit wound, and an energy mismatch** — a .30-06 at that range would be expected to exit and destroy tissue; see [six forensic physics phenomena](/Mic/six-forensic-physics-phenomena).
* **The mic was routed under the shirt** — [unlike prior events](/Mic/AV_Team_Mic_Setup), placing the transmitter against his chest and neck.
* **Physical debris** — a [burnt object in the SUV](/Mic/burnt-object-rode-transmitter) and a [neck fragment that may be charred plastic](/Mic/neck-fragment-charred-plastic).
* **The residue evidence was removed, not tested** — dirt, clothing, the SUV and the necklace were [hauled, cleaned or seized](/Mic/Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview) with no public residue test.
* **Means existed** — an [AES contract for miniaturized anti-personnel charges](/Mic/DoD_Contract) sits on the same timeline.

Full evidence hub: **[Microphone — The Exploding Mic Theory](/Mic/overview)**.

## What would put it beyond dispute

1. **Produce the necklace and cross from evidence and test them for explosive residue** in front of an independent examiner. One lab result ends the question. None has been published.
2. Release the autopsy findings on **burns, soot, stippling or blast injury** around the wound.
3. Test the shirt, the RØDE transmitter and clasp, and the SUV debris for PETN and other explosive residue.
