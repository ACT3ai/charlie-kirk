A fragment that can be neither matched nor excluded leaves the state without physical proof of the weapon
it says killed Charlie Kirk — the gap that pushes most citizen researchers toward the
[explosive-device reading](/Tyler_Robinson_Not_Assassin/explosive-mic-killed-charlie) instead.

## What is alleged

This item rests on a document — the government's own laboratory report. According to the summary of the ATF report reproduced in the investigation file and the PDF released by Andrea Burkhart, **one bullet jacket fragment and four lead fragments** were recovered during Charlie Kirk's autopsy. The jacket is identified as coming from a **.30-caliber class** bullet. It reportedly shared class characteristics with the Mauser 98 the State attributes to Tyler Robinson, so the rifle **could not be excluded** as having fired it. But the fragment reportedly **lacks individual characteristics permitting identification of one rifle to the exclusion of all others in the class**, and the ATF wrote that firearms producing similar general rifling characteristics "include numerous makes and models."

Three further details are documented. First, the State reportedly now wants **Virtual Comparison Microscopy** at the FBI lab — a 3D scan compared against a test fire — which may require an analyst to unfold deformed portions of the fragile jacket using pliers or similar gripping tools, potentially leaving tool marks and affecting the fragment's structural integrity. Second, an FBI analyst who received the fragment from the ATF reportedly **noted that a portion had already become detached in the packaging**. Third, the defense asked to photograph the jacket in its current state and to attend or photograph future testing; those requests were reportedly **denied**, prompting the January 9 motion. The report also reportedly notes the cartridge casing engravings are consistent with a rotary tool like a Dremel, and that the State seeks unspecified metallurgical testing of a lead fragment.

[@ProjectConstitu](/Influencers/podcasts-project-constitution) goes further, writing that it "proves the fragment is a planted prop designed to roughly match the narrative of the gun they are framing the patsy with." That is ProjectConstitu's reading; the report itself does not address where the fragment came from, and no named person has been shown to have tampered with anything.

## The government's narrative, and the investigators' answer

Defenders of the government's narrative say "inconclusive" is a common result for a deformed jacket fragment, and that class-characteristic agreement is consistent with the Mauser. Investigators answer:

* **Consistent is not proof.** The ATF itself wrote that firearms with similar rifling "include numerous makes and models." The government's narrative names one rifle; its own lab could not tie that rifle to the wound.
* **The chain of custody is already damaged.** A portion of the fragment reportedly became detached in the packaging before the FBI received it, and the State now wants destructive testing that may require unfolding the jacket with pliers.
* **The defense was kept out.** Requests to photograph the fragment and attend testing were reportedly denied, and the report was fought over under seal.
* **A fragment is not a cause of death.** Nothing public shows where in the body the jacket and lead pieces were found, or rules out that they arrived by some other route. A metal fragment does not answer why the shirt moved outward first, why there was no exit wound, or why the loudest sound on the Canon track was at the tent.

The documented result is simple: the physical link between the rifle in the government's narrative and the object taken from Kirk's body is **not individually established**. That gap is a large part of why most citizen researchers have moved to the exploding-mic conclusion.

## Why Investigators Conclude It Was the Exploding Mic

Citizen investigators have largely concluded that Charlie Kirk was killed by an explosive at his
microphone, not by the government's .30-06 rifle narrative. The main reasons:

* **The shirt and fabric move first** — [video frame analysis](/Mic/mic-explosion-video-analysis) shows the shirt expanding outward, spherically, before the neck wound appears.
* **The audio separates two blasts** — the [Canon XA55 recording](/Mic/Canon_XA55_Audio) reportedly shows a louder detonation at the tent distinct from a distant muzzle report.
* **Eyewitness veterans called it an explosion** — [Rob O'Neill and Gen. Blaine Holt](/Mic/rob-oneill-navy-seal-testimony) said it looked like an explosion, not a rifle wound.
* **No exit wound, and an energy mismatch** — a .30-06 at that range would be expected to exit and destroy tissue; see [six forensic physics phenomena](/Mic/six-forensic-physics-phenomena).
* **The mic was routed under the shirt** — [unlike prior events](/Mic/AV_Team_Mic_Setup), placing the transmitter against his chest and neck.
* **Physical debris** — a [burnt object in the SUV](/Mic/burnt-object-rode-transmitter) and a [neck fragment that may be charred plastic](/Mic/neck-fragment-charred-plastic).
* **The residue evidence was removed, not tested** — dirt, clothing, the SUV and the necklace were [hauled, cleaned or seized](/Mic/Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview) with no public residue test.
* **Means existed** — an [AES contract for miniaturized anti-personnel charges](/Mic/DoD_Contract) sits on the same timeline.

## What would put it beyond dispute

1. Release the **complete, unredacted ATF report** rather than the four-page summary, including the examiner's bench notes and photographs of the fragment on receipt.
2. Require any further testing to be **videotaped with a defense expert present** — the relief the January 9 motion seeks.
3. Order **independent examination** of the jacket fragment and the four lead fragments before any further destructive testing — including a test of the fragments and clothing for explosive residue.
4. Release the autopsy's record of exactly where each fragment was recovered.
