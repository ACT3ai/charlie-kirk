## What is alleged

This is the single most-repeated point against the government's account of how Charlie Kirk died, and the arithmetic behind it is not in dispute. [@HolonCitizen](/Influencers/x/holoncitizen) lays it out: a .30-06 firing a 180-grain bullet at roughly 2,700 fps carries approximately **2,914 ft-lbs** at the muzzle, with published values hovering between 2,800 and 3,000+ — about **3,950 joules**. A 9mm at 115 grains and 1,180 fps carries roughly **356 ft-lbs, or 482 joules**. The .30-06 is, in his framing, a round that "won't stop until it is blasting through and past enough to finally scrub the energy to zero."

From that, the argument runs: such a round striking the neck should have destroyed the cervical spine, exited catastrophically, and thrown blood and tissue across the tent and onto the people seated beside Kirk. [@HolonCitizen](/Influencers/x/holoncitizen) argues the videos instead show a comparatively **contained neck wound**, with a jacket fragment reportedly recovered from the neck at autopsy rather than an exit — an energy mismatch the official account, on this reading, never reconciles.

Dr. Chris Martenson ([@peakprosperity](/Influencers/x/peakprosperity)), cited secondhand, also concluded that a .30-06 does not fit the injury — "too much energy, would've knocked him flying." His reported conclusion is that the injury reflects a **high-speed cavitation event** — overpressure expanding the neck, snapping the necklace, and producing the clenched-fist posture — from a **lower-velocity fragmenting round** rather than a .30-06.

**The investigators' answer:** Martenson agrees the government's .30-06 does not fit. The signs he points to — overpressure at the neck, the chain snapping, the posture — are the signature of a sudden pressure event at the collar, which a small charge at the microphone produces without any bullet at all, and which the [shirt-before-blood frames](./shirt-before-blood) show starting at the mic position. No second, lower-velocity round has been found or identified. See the [exploding-mic evidence hub](/Mic/overview). [Candace Owens](/Influencers/podcasts-candace-owens) has argued the mismatch supports a rigged-microphone explanation, and whokilledck.com builds its exploding-mic conclusion partly on the same expectation about what a rifle round should have done.

## The government's narrative, and the investigators' answer

Defenders of the government's narrative say rifle rounds sometimes fragment on bone and fail to exit, so a retained fragment and no exit wound can happen. Investigators answer:

* **A .30-06 at roughly 130 metres is built to penetrate.** It is a hunting round for elk and moose. Through the soft tissue of a human neck — with no body armour and nothing to stop it — investigators say the expected result is an exit wound and massive tissue destruction, not a contained wound.
* **The video shows no spray.** People seated beside Kirk and the tent behind him were not showered with blood and tissue in the first frames, as a round carrying nearly 4,000 joules through the neck would be expected to cause.
* **The injuries are in the wrong places.** A court filing quotes the medical examiner on [hemopericardium, bilateral hemothorax and subarachnoid hemorrhage](/Medical/charlies_injuries) — damage spread across the chest and skull base that investigators say fits a pressure wave from a blast at the collar better than one bullet path through the neck.
* **The fragment does not name the rifle.** The ATF could not tie the recovered jacket fragment to the Mauser — see [the ATF fragment](./atf-fragment-class-only).
* **The autopsy is withheld.** If the wound track matched a .30-06, releasing it would settle the question. It has not been released.

Investigators do not rest this on the idea that a bullet would "knock him flying" — the energy point is about what the round would do *inside* the neck and on the way out, and the wound does not show it.

## Why Investigators Conclude It Was the Exploding Mic

Citizen investigators have largely concluded that Charlie Kirk was killed by an explosive at his
microphone, not by the government's .30-06 rifle narrative. The main reasons:

* **The shirt and fabric move first** — [video frame analysis](/Mic/mic-explosion-video-analysis) shows the shirt expanding outward, spherically, before the neck wound appears.
* **The audio separates two blasts** — the [Canon XA55 recording](/Mic/Canon_XA55_Audio) reportedly shows a louder detonation at the tent distinct from a distant muzzle report.
* **Eyewitness veterans called it an explosion** — [Rob O'Neill and Gen. Blaine Holt](/Mic/rob-oneill-navy-seal-testimony) said it looked like an explosion, not a rifle wound.
* **No exit wound, and an energy mismatch** — a .30-06 at that range would be expected to exit and destroy tissue; see [six forensic physics phenomena](/Mic/six-forensic-physics-phenomena).
* **The mic was routed under the shirt** — [unlike prior events](/Mic/AV_Team_Mic_Setup), placing the transmitter against his chest and neck.
* **Physical debris** — a [burnt object in the SUV](/Mic/burnt-object-rode-transmitter) and a [neck fragment that may be charred plastic](/Mic/neck-fragment-charred-plastic).
* **The residue evidence was removed, not tested** — dirt, clothing, the SUV and the necklace were [hauled, cleaned or seized](/Mic/Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview) with no public residue test.
* **Means existed** — an [AES contract for miniaturized anti-personnel charges](/Mic/DoD_Contract) sits on the same timeline.

Full evidence hub: **[Microphone — The Exploding Mic Theory](/Mic/overview)**.

## What would put it beyond dispute

1. **Release the autopsy report and the wound-track findings**, including any test of the wound and clothing for explosive residue.
2. Release the ATF and FBI analyses of the recovered jacket fragment and four lead fragments, and the record of where each was found in the body.
3. Put the question to a board-certified forensic pathologist, on the record and by name: is this injury within the normal range for a .30-06 neck wound at roughly 130 metres, or does it fit a small charge at the collar better?
