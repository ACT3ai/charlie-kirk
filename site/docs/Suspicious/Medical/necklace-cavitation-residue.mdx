# The Necklace, the Blast and the Residue (Claims)

:::caution Attributed claims only
**No one is accused of anything on this page.** The snapped necklace is one of the physical details citizen investigators point to when they conclude Charlie Kirk was killed by an explosive at his chest and neck — most likely at his microphone — rather than by the government's .30-06 rifle narrative. The necklace was reportedly sought by federal investigators, and no residue test on it has ever been made public.
:::

## What is alleged

**Dr. Chris Martenson ([@peakprosperity](/Influencers/x/peakprosperity))** is cited secondhand for a **"high-speed cavitation event"** in which overpressure expands Kirk's neck, **snaps his necklace**, and produces a **"pugilist pose"** — clenched fists attributed to midbrain trauma. But Martenson is cited as concluding it was **not a .30-06**, because that round carried **"too much energy, would've knocked him flying,"** proposing instead a **9mm +P round at 1,330 fps** that "fragments like hell, dumps energy inside."

Running in the opposite direction, **[@cletus_jethro](/Influencers/x/cletus-jethro)** — self-described as a former USMC explosive breacher and assault section leader — argues for a **cone-shaped PETN charge in the crucifix pendant's center column**, anchored by the heavy silver box chain and pointed directly at the neck area where the injury appears. He argues specifically that **a lapel mic could not have worked** because an unanchored charge "would move left, not right."

**@Blase** adds a third argument: the necklace snapping and the shirt reverberation occur **before any blood appears**, which he calls impossible for a gunshot — "the 1st frame would & should show some degree of entry wound / blood / splatter."

The file also raises that **federal investigators were reportedly searching for Kirk's necklace**, and asks whether that search was for **explosive residue**.

## Why investigators read the necklace as blast evidence

**Most investigators place the charge at the microphone.** The RØDE lavalier and its transmitter were routed under the shirt that day, against the chest and neck — the same zone where the necklace snapped and the fabric expanded. The pendant variant argued by [@cletus_jethro](/Influencers/x/cletus-jethro) and the mic version share the core conclusion: a small explosive device at the chest, not a rifle round from 140+ yards. The detailed mic case is at [Microphone — The Exploding Mic Theory](/Mic/overview).

**The government's account leans on cavitation.** The official .30-06 narrative would explain the necklace as cavitation from a high-energy round. Investigators point out that a .30-06 at that range would be expected to leave an exit wound and heavy tissue destruction — neither has been publicly shown — and that the necklace, shirt and neck move outward together in the way a blast front pushes, before any entry wound or blood appears ([six forensic physics phenomena](/Mic/six-forensic-physics-phenomena)).

**The frame order is the point.** @Blase's observation — necklace snapping and shirt reverberation before any blood — is one of the details investigators treat as consistent with an explosion at the body, and it matches the [video frame analysis](/Mic/mic-explosion-video-analysis) of the shirt expanding spherically first.

**The necklace was taken, not tested.** Federal investigators were reportedly searching for the necklace. Collecting a victim's effects is routine — which is exactly why investigators ask where the residue results are. No public explosive-residue test exists on the necklace, the clothing, the SUV or the courtyard soil; see [the residue evidence that was removed](/Mic/Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview).

**The smoke-signature objection.** [@troofevades](/Influencers/x/troofevades), quoting [@Trillion0x](https://x.com/Trillion0x/status/2070699686961520802), posted a [home demonstration of a 2.5-gram shaped charge](/Videos/Vid_Exploding_Microphone/Vid_Shaped_Charge_Counterargumen/Vid_Troofevades_Demonstration_Real_Shaped_2071072) and argues that a real shaped charge produces a large, visible smoke and blast signature that does not appear in the event footage. Investigators answer that the demonstration was fired in open air onto a padlock, where the blast vents into a visible cloud; a charge seated against a body under a shirt drives its energy into the target instead — and the same demonstration shows that a few grams are enough to punch clean through solid brass. See [Microphone — The Exploding Mic Theory](/Mic/overview).

**The "lapel mic could not have worked" objection.** @cletus_jethro's argument that an unanchored lapel charge "would move left, not right" is his case for the pendant over the mic. Investigators answer that the transmitter was not loose: it was held against the chest by a magnetic MagClip through the shirt fabric, which anchors it the same way the chain anchors a pendant — see [mic versus pendant](/Mic/Mic_vs_Pendant).

Martenson's own reading differs in its details — he is cited as proposing a smaller, fragmenting round — but he, too, is cited as concluding the wound does not fit a .30-06, which is the government's account.

## Why Investigators Conclude It Was the Exploding Mic

Citizen investigators have largely concluded that Charlie Kirk was killed by an explosive at his microphone, not by the government's .30-06 rifle narrative. The main reasons:

* **The shirt and fabric move first** — [video frame analysis](/Mic/mic-explosion-video-analysis) shows the shirt expanding outward, spherically, before the neck wound appears.
* **The audio separates two blasts** — the [Canon XA55 recording](/Mic/Canon_XA55_Audio) reportedly shows a louder detonation at the tent distinct from a distant muzzle report.
* **Eyewitness veterans called it an explosion** — [Rob O'Neill and Gen. Blaine Holt](/Mic/rob-oneill-navy-seal-testimony) said it looked like an explosion, not a rifle wound.
* **No exit wound, and an energy mismatch** — a .30-06 at that range would be expected to exit and destroy tissue; see [six forensic physics phenomena](/Mic/six-forensic-physics-phenomena).
* **The mic was routed under the shirt** — [unlike prior events](/Mic/AV_Team_Mic_Setup), placing the transmitter against his chest and neck.
* **Physical debris** — a [burnt object in the SUV](/Mic/burnt-object-rode-transmitter) and a [neck fragment that may be charred plastic](/Mic/neck-fragment-charred-plastic).
* **The residue evidence was removed, not tested** — dirt, clothing, the SUV and the necklace were [hauled, cleaned or seized](/Mic/Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview) with no public residue test.
* **Means existed** — an [AES contract for miniaturized anti-personnel charges](/Mic/DoD_Contract) sits on the same timeline.

Full evidence hub: **[Microphone — The Exploding Mic Theory](/Mic/overview)**.

## What would settle it

1. **Run explosive-residue testing on the necklace and pendant**, with chain of custody. One lab result resolves most of this page.
2. Establish whether the pendant was recovered intact or fragmented, and photograph it in evidence.
3. Release the autopsy's wound-track findings: entry, any exit, and any foreign material recovered from the neck.
4. Release the autopsy findings on **burns, soot, or blast injury to the skin** surrounding the wound, and test the clothing for explosive residue.
