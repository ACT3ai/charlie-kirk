## What Is Said

**The method statement, first ten minutes.** This is the most valuable part of the stream and it is unusually careful. He explains what Google Trends is — that it "purports to reflect what searches were conducted and where and when those searches were conducted" — and then states his own limits: "I take that at face value. I don't have access to the underlying data." He repeats his standing rule: "I draw no conclusions from what I find. I draw no conclusions from what I choose to present here." He also concedes he is showing a selection: "I cannot present everything I found. It's too numerous, too voluminous."

**The disappearance test.** He claims that terms he covers on air tend to stop returning results afterwards, and — to his credit — describes running a control rather than just asserting it. He says he has tested search terms from unrelated breaking news stories involving violence, and that those "don't tend to disappear, at least not nearly at the pace that the terms associated with Charlie Kirk's story tend to disappear after I cover them." He goes further and distinguishes three groups: queries he runs privately and never mentions; queries he discusses privately with others by text; and queries he covers on the channel. He says the first group persists and the last does not, and leaves the inference to the audience: "I'll leave it for you to decide what that means."

That is a testable design, and it is more rigorous than anything else in this corpus. It is also unverified — nobody has independently reproduced it, and low-volume Trends queries are known to return inconsistently between runs for ordinary technical reasons.

**The Trends walk.** He re-covers ground from his earlier streams for new viewers: the hospital in Orem, the Utah medical examiner Deirdre Amaro with a claimed Israel-geolocated search on June 30, 2025 and no comparable DC or Utah interest, and the Utah Department of Public Safety commissioner appointed on July 17. As before he argues against his own exhibit on the last one, offering the ordinary explanation that a new appointment gets looked up.

**The later frame study.** The middle of the stream turns from charts to event frames and becomes speculative, focusing on a member of the event staff near the stage. This page does not repeat that portion: it singles out a private individual on the strength of heavily zoomed compressed frames, and nothing in it is evidence that anyone did anything.

**What he says about the people he names.** He is explicit and repeated about not accusing anyone. Of Frank Turek he says plainly: "I don't know that Turek was involved at all. I'm not blaming Turek." Near the end he restates it for the whole stream: "I truly am making no claims in this video. I have my thoughts, but I'm making no claims." He also says he believes Tyler Robinson is a patsy — a belief, offered as such.
