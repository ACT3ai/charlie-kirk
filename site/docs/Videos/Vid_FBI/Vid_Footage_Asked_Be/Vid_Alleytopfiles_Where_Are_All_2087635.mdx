### What the clip claims, point by point

Every item below is what he says on camera. None of it has been independently verified here.

## How To Weigh It

This is one man's account of two telephone calls, given on camera. It is his word, and it is the only direct evidence of those calls that the investigation has seen. The FBI has not publicly confirmed, denied, or otherwise addressed it, and no call record, case number, or agent name has been produced on either side. Nothing on this page asserts that any individual — any agent, [Kash Patel](/People/kash-patel), Cub Keller, or the speaker — acted unlawfully, improperly, or in bad faith, and no such finding has been made anywhere.

Ordinary explanations exist and should be stated plainly.

* A field contact suggesting that a witness need not keep distressing footage on a personal phone — particularly footage of someone the witness describes as a friend — is consistent with a **welfare conversation**, and a follow-up call is consistent with routine case note-keeping.
* Once an agency holds a submitted file, asking the submitter not to keep circulating a graphic homicide recording is a **common and lawful** request. Public circulation prejudices a jury pool. Re-encoded copies strip metadata. The request is not, by itself, proof of suppression.
* A request is not an order. On his own telling, nobody seized his phone, and he **kept copies**. That undercuts compulsion. It is also why the account can still be examined.
* **Misunderstanding is possible.** "Do not post this while the investigation is active" can be remembered as "delete it."
* **Self-reported details can be wrong or exaggerated** in the chaotic aftermath of a traumatic event.
* Skeptics in the same October 2025 threads called him a "grifter," noted the later account deletion, or argued that if the FBI had wanted the file gone they would have taken the phone.

The Cub Keller posts add a second ordinary explanation that has to sit next to those: that the **event footage he presented as his own was not his**, that he later owned up, and that any FBI contact — if it happened — may have been about a file the bureau already knew was someone else's. Jan Love's harder version, that he admitted he was not at the event at all, is her characterisation; Cub's on-the-record posts are the ones that name the videos as "ours" and describe the Instagram-to-TikTok path.

What is documented and is not in dispute:

* A man has said this on camera, more than once, in substantially the same words.
* He describes responding by copying the footage widely rather than deleting it.
* The same monologue was the highest-engagement "FBI asked a witness to delete" item of late October 2025, then returned in July and August 2026 as cleaner copies.
* Cub Keller has said, in his own name, that the event video was his, that Ryne claimed it, and that Ryne owned up.
* The FBI has still said nothing public about the calls.

Whether the advice was welfare, a copy-management request, a contact about a file that was not his, or something else is exactly what a disclosure of the tip-line record would settle. That is why this account keeps being raised, and it is why [Fix Laws](/Fix/overview) targets FBI logs of witness contact and any instruction to delete media.

A parallel, thinner claim names a second attendee. Per [@ProjectConstitu](/Influencers/podcasts-project-constitution), **[Skyler Baird](/Videos/Vid_People/Vid_Skyler_Baird_5834/Vid_Project_Constitution_EXPOSED_Charlie_1987224)** said federal agents asked him to delete phone footage, also framed as a "PTSD" rationale. That post is rhetorical and uncorroborated. It is noted only because it is the one other named witness raising the same delete-the-footage wording. One case, even if accurate, does not establish a pattern. A mass order covering the roughly 3,000 attendees has not been shown.
