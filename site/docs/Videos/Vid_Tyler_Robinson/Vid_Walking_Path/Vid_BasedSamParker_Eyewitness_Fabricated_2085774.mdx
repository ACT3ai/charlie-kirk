## What This Video Is

Eleven minutes and forty-seven seconds published by **@BasedSamParker** on **August 7, 2026**,
post identifier 2085774401988125026. The narrator is never on camera. He builds the piece out of
borrowed material — dispatch audio, Channel 4 and CNN broadcast segments, *USA Today* reporting
by Will Carless, a TMZ-watermarked residential camera clip, and citizen roof footage — arranged
in the order of the escape and annotated with yellow arrows marking where each reporter was
standing.

He credits the underlying work to **@SammyTherese**, **@Villgecrazylady** and
[Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens), and addresses the piece to Owens directly. Trial attorney
Patricia Gifford ([@pattygifflaw](https://x.com/pattygifflaw/status/2085794603819544692)) shared
it the same day: *"Eye witness recall helps show the texts are fabricated."*

The structure is unusual and effective. He states the contradiction in the first ninety seconds,
then offers the viewer a choice — *"we could stop there... or we can go back to the beginning and
see how we arrived at this point"* — and spends the remaining ten minutes laying the foundation.

## What Is Said

The claim is narrow and it is about **clothing**.

The alleged confession messages attributed to [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) and read
aloud in the video include: *"I worry about prints. I had to leave it in a bush where I changed
outfits, didn't have the ability or time to bring it with."* Everything after that line in the
sequence should therefore show a differently-dressed man.

The construction-site testimony, quoted from broadcast footage of an electrician identified as
**Dylan Hope**, relaying his coworker's encounter:

> "Yeah, black sunglasses. He had a black COVID mask. He had a black trench coat, black cargo
> pants, and he was wearing a small backpack, and he also had long, greasy black hair."

Two problems follow, in the narrator's words: *"the alleged gunman, who's fleeing through the
construction zone, still has all the black clothes on,"* and *"the photo the FBI released of
allegedly Tyler Robinson that day, [shows] a person with short hair, not long, greasy black
hair."*

The reconstruction that fills the middle of the video runs: dispatch describing *"all black,
black long gun, black tactical helmet, a black mask, possibly wearing a tactical vest"* — a
rooftop figure dropping into what the narrator calls a *"lateral military crawl"* — a witness
with firearms experience reportedly telling investigators the shooter was in tactical gear and a
mask and looked *"highly trained"* — the run along the roofline and the jump down — the wooded
area with evidence markers, cones and FBI forensics — the construction site and the canine
pursuit — and finally the TMZ house camera showing a man in black crossing a field away from
campus.

Two smaller observations are offered as questions rather than findings. On an object visible in
the wooded-area footage: *"Is that an Allen wrench?"* — noting a Mauser can be partly
disassembled with one, and asking whether that explains early reports of a screwdriver near the
rifle. And on the phone photographs themselves: the damage to Lance Twiggs's screen is *"a nearly
perfect circle exactly over his time display,"* which he calls *"very convenient."*

The closing tally is the outfit count — maroon shirt and shorts in the morning, black long-sleeve
and jeans leaving campus, all-black tactical on the roof, an alleged change in the woods, still
all black at the construction site, still all black on the TMZ footage, and back to the maroon
shirt and blue jeans at the surrender. *"Is anybody getting dizzy from all these outfit changes?"*
His proposed readings — **body doubles**, or **others involved alongside** the defendant — are his
hypotheses and are not adopted here.

## What The Footage Shows

Almost none of it is original camera work; the value is in the assembly. Rooftop citizen footage
of a prone figure. Broadcast standups from a hillside neighborhood and from below a construction
bank. Evidence markers, orange cones, crime-scene tape and a ladder against a building. A dirt
path and an open field on a doorbell-style residential camera. Screenshots of the alleged
messages. Yellow arrow overlays throughout, keying each speaker to a map position.

The weakness is inherited from the sources. The construction-site description is **second-hand** —
Hope relaying his coworker — and no one in the footage confirms the visitor was the gunman; the
reporting says only that police thought it worth following with dogs. An eyewitness recalling
clothing under stress is an imperfect record. The video does not close that gap, and does not
claim to. What it does is put the conflict on the record in a form the official sequence has to
answer.
