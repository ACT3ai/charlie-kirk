## What This Video Is

A three-minute-forty-six-second clip posted to X on August 17, 2026 by the X account **[@BlakeBednarz](https://x.com/BlakeBednarz)**. It is built entirely out of the Utah Valley University surveillance exhibit that was played in open court during the [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) preliminary hearing — the camera labelled on screen as **"Computer Science Building Campus Dr / CS - Roof - West Overview Camera 4."**

The clip has one editorial idea, stated in a caption burned into every frame: **"Watch the timestamp."** Bednarz cut the exhibit so the camera's own embedded date-and-time counter stays visible in the top-left corner throughout, and he loops the same passage several times so a viewer can read the numbers as they change.

His caption on the post makes the argument explicit:

> "If the Sloppers are so sure of their Slop, join with us &amp; call for the release of the missing minutes in the original quality! 12:03:09pm to 12:15:20pm is missing! 12:16pm is missing! &amp; 12:17:17pm to 12:22:10pm is missing! We know this was recorded in 4K too, not 480p! Do it!"

Two separate claims are stacked there, and they should be kept apart. The first is a **counting claim** about where the timestamp jumps — that can be checked frame by frame against the clip itself. The second is a **provenance claim** about the resolution the original was recorded at, and about who should release what. That one is an assertion by the poster, not something the footage itself proves.

## The Three Jumps, Read Off The Timestamp

Reading the embedded counter frame by frame through this clip, the counter does not run continuously. It advances normally for a stretch, then skips forward. Three skips are visible, and they land on the three intervals named in the caption:

| # | Last frame before | First frame after | Interval not shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12:03:08 PM | 12:15:20 PM | ~12 minutes |
| 2 | 12:15:59 PM | 12:17:04 PM | ~1 minute |
| 3 | 12:17:17 PM | 12:22:10 PM | ~5 minutes |

The scene on either side of each jump is the same fixed camera pointed at the same parking lot and parking structure, so the discontinuity is not a change of angle. Between the jumps the counter ticks second by second in the ordinary way, which is what makes the skips legible at all.

The clip presents this twice — once in the raw screen-capture form at the top, and again from about 2:00 onward as a courtroom camera filming the same exhibit projected on a **ViewSonic** monitor beside the witness stand, played back inside a Windows viewer window. That second half is what establishes the provenance of the material: this is the exhibit as the court saw it, not a re-cut someone assembled from press clips.

## What The Testimony Says Over The Top

The audio is investigator testimony from the hearing, narrating the route. The automated transcript is imperfect — the Losee Center is rendered variously as "Losi," "Losey," "Loti," "Loki," and "low sea," and one answer contains an obvious self-correction of "December" to "September." Read past those artifacts and the substance is a straightforward walk-through of the state's camera timeline:

> "He comes up around Campus Drive, as you can see on the video here. There is a cut through there north of the Los\[ee\] building, and that's Mr. Robinson coming up the sidewalk. He has the noticeable gate \[gait\] or limp in his walk."

> "This is an image looking across the parking lot to the Los\[ee\] building, and Mr. Robinson is appearing on the exterior staircase, which gives access to the Los\[ee\] building roof and the area where the railing was."

> "And what time is this?" — "It's just afternoon, about 12:15." — "On what day?" — "On \[September\] 10th, 2025."

> "The individual actually kind of rolls over the railing onto the roof of the Los\[ee\] building... the individual is now seen running across the rooftop... to what would be the southwest corner of the building, crouching down and then crawling to the corner."

That is the state's account of what its cameras captured. It is testimony given under oath at a preliminary hearing, and it is reported here as testimony — the hearing decided only that the case proceeds. Robinson is charged, not convicted.

## Why The Gaps Are Worth Asking About

The intervals the counter skips are not idle ones. **12:03 to 12:15** brackets the approach and the climb described in the very testimony playing over the clip, and **12:17 to 12:22** covers the window in which the state says the suspect crossed the roof and left it. In other words, the minutes not shown in this exhibit are minutes the narration is actively describing.

That is the reason the observation carries weight beyond a formatting quibble: an exhibit that skips the intervals its own narrator is characterising leaves the outside observer unable to check the characterisation against the picture. This is the same disclosure question raised on [UVU HD Surveillance Never Publicly Released](/CoverUp/UVU_HD_Footage_Withheld) and pursued through the records requests catalogued on [UVU GRAMA Records](/CoverUp/UVU_GRAMA_Records).

## What This Clip Does Not Establish

Several ordinary explanations would produce exactly the same on-screen behaviour, and none of them requires anyone to have hidden anything:

* **Exhibit selection.** Courtroom exhibits are routinely cut to the relevant passages. A prosecutor playing four excerpts of a continuous recording produces a jumping timestamp on the screen while the full file sits intact in discovery.
* **Playback skipping.** The second half of the clip shows a Windows viewer with a scrub bar. An operator dragging the playhead to the next passage produces a jump that looks identical to a missing segment.
* **Recording mode.** Motion- or event-triggered DVR recording writes only clips containing activity, so a fixed camera over a quiet parking lot can legitimately have no frames for a span.
* **Re-encoding and scale.** The 480p appearance of the exhibit is consistent with a compressed export played on a courtroom monitor and re-filmed. It is not, by itself, proof about what the camera natively recorded, and this clip contains nothing that independently establishes a 4K original.

None of that answers the question — it just means this clip raises it rather than settles it. The thing that would settle it is the item Bednarz is asking for: the original-quality export of that camera for the full 12:00–12:25 window, with its embedded timestamps intact and a documented chain of custody. Until that is produced, the gaps are an open question about disclosure, not a demonstrated act of concealment by any named person or institution.
