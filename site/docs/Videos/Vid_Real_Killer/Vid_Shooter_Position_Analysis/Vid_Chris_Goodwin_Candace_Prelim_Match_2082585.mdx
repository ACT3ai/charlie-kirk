## What This Video Is

Three minutes seven seconds, posted to X by Chris Goodwin on 29 July 2026. It is not
original footage and does not claim to be. It is an assembly: two copies of the same
rooftop recording, played one after the other, so that a viewer can compare them.

The first stretch is the copy of the rooftop video that circulated after Candace Owens
published it. From roughly the 1:28 mark the clip switches to the version the public saw
at the preliminary hearing — the courtroom screen, filmed by media cameras pointed at the
television monitors in the room. Goodwin describes that second portion as the raw video as
shown by the FBI at the hearing.

In his own words: "Here is the @RealCandaceO Video matched up with the footage from the
preliminary hearing the public viewed from media recording court room TV screens. After
1:28 is the FBI footage of the raw video."

## What Is Said

There is no narration by Goodwin. The audio in the second half is courtroom audio: an
investigator being walked frame by frame through the rooftop recording by counsel. The
transcript is imperfect — automatic speech recognition renders "Losee" variously as "low
sea," "loggie" and "lowest" — but the substance is legible.

The witness narrates the movement: the figure "actually kind of rolls over the railing onto
the roof of the Losee building," and then "in the top left corner, the individual is now
seen running across the rooftop of the Losee building, to what would be the southwest corner
of the building, crouching down and then crawling to the corner of the building."

Counsel presses for times. A "quick flash" is placed at "around 12:23," with the witness
adding, "I think a timestamp on the video is 12:22." The date is confirmed on the record as
September 10.

The witness is then asked whether the figure is still visible: "Yes, you can, lying prone."
The narration continues: "Here we wait for what we expect to be some sort of a kickback.
This must have been when our source began recording."

Counsel asks the identification question twice. The second time the answer is given: "You
believe this to be Mr. Robinson? — I do, yes." That is testimony, an opinion offered by a
witness at a preliminary hearing. Tyler Robinson has been charged and has not been convicted;
the identification is the witness's belief and is contested by others in this investigation.

Duration is then established: the figure remains "just until the reported shot was fired at
12:23 and 28," after which "individual then stands up and then moves across the building to
the north."

## What The Footage Shows

The forensic value of this clip is comparative rather than evidentiary. Neither half is new
footage. What the assembly allows a viewer to do is check, without taking anyone's word for
it, whether the copy that reached the public through Candace Owens and the copy displayed in
the courtroom are the same recording, and whether they begin and end at the same points.

That question matters here because the start point of the rooftop video is itself disputed.
Researchers have argued the released clip begins after the running figure was already in
motion, which would place part of the sequence outside anything the public has been shown —
see [rooftop video trimmed](/CoverUp/Rooftop_Video_Trimmed) and
[UVU HD footage withheld](/CoverUp/UVU_HD_Footage_Withheld).

Two limits should be stated plainly. The courtroom half is a camera pointed at a television
screen, so it is a second-generation image: resolution, colour and frame timing are all
degraded relative to whatever file the court was actually playing. And the on-screen
timestamp read aloud as 12:22 against a flash placed "around 12:23" is a discrepancy the
clip surfaces but does not resolve.
