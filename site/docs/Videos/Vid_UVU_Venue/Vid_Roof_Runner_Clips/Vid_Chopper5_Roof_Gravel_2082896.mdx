## What This Video Shows

Thirteen seconds of aerial news footage of the **[Losee Center](/UVU/Losee_Center) roof** at Utah Valley
University, shot from a helicopter roughly an hour after the September 10, 2025 shooting. The **CHOPPER 5**
bug sits in the upper left of frame and the **KSL** station logo in the lower left, which identifies it as
KSL's news helicopter.

The camera holds one continuous high-oblique pass. The roof deck fills the centre of frame; the parapet edge,
the pedestrian bridges and the surrounding plaza are all visible at the same time. There is no cutaway. That
matters, because the whole value of the clip is that it gives an uninterrupted look at the roof surface from
above rather than the low-angle phone footage most of this section is made of.

The clip has rotor and ambient noise but no narration — a transcription pass returned no speech. Nobody is on
the roof. A few small figures are visible on campus walkways at a distance and none are identifiable.

## The Claim Attached To It

@Villgecrazylady's post, in full:

> That's exactly what I thought too. Plus check out this Chopper 5 footage from about an hour after. The
> disturbance in the roof gravel is significantly farther back than described. I always wondered how he got so
> far from the edge before the audience even ducked in the Hall of Flags clip… makes a lot more sense if he
> was laying 10 feet back to begin with.

That is her reading of the footage, reported here with attribution and not adopted as a finding. The argument
has two parts, and they are worth keeping apart:

1. **Position of the gravel disturbance.** She reads the disturbed area as sitting materially farther back
   from the parapet than the state's account describes. This is an eyeball estimate from a moving helicopter
   at an oblique angle, without a scale reference or a survey. It is a question raised, not a measurement.
2. **What that would explain.** If the firing position were roughly ten feet back from the edge rather than at
   it, she argues, the figure would already be well clear of the edge in the Hall of Flags clip by the moment
   the audience reacts. That is an inference from the first point and inherits its uncertainty.

The related line-of-sight argument — whether a shooter set back from the parapet could see the seating
position at all — is worked through on [roof line of sight](/Roof/roof-line-of-sight) and
[wound trajectory and angle](/Gun_Bullet/Wound_Trajectory_Angle).

## No Screwdriver Visible On The Roof

This footage is also directly relevant to a separate dispute, and it is worth stating plainly: **the news
helicopter did not see any screwdriver on the roof.** Nothing resembling a hand tool is visible anywhere on
the roof surface in this clip, and no yellow crime-scene tape is visible at the railing either.

That is the same absence citizen reviewers have cited from other KSL helicopter footage. Officer
[Chris Bagley](/People/chris-bagley) is reported to have testified that he reached the roof around 12:44 p.m.,
found a red-and-black screwdriver roughly 10–20 feet past the guardrail, and put up yellow tape around the
southwest roof area. If that account is accurate, there should be a window in which tape is present at the
railing.

Two cautions belong on that observation, and they pull in opposite directions:

* **Tape is the stronger test.** Crime-scene tape is large, high-contrast and strung at height. Aerial video
  of this quality should resolve it if it were present at the moment of the pass. Its absence here is
  meaningful evidence about *that moment*.
* **A screwdriver is the weaker test.** A hand tool lying on grey gravel, seen from a helicopter, is close to
  the resolution floor. Not seeing it in this clip is weak evidence that it was not there.

Timing is the unresolved variable in both cases. This footage is described by the poster as "about an hour
after" the shot — roughly 1:23 p.m. if the shot is taken at 12:23 p.m. Bagley is reported to have reached the
roof at about 12:44 p.m. Whether the tape had already been taken down, had not yet gone up in the frame the
camera covers, or was never at the railing at all is not settled by thirteen seconds of video. The full
version of that argument, with the competing accounts laid out, is on
[News Helicopter and the Screwdriver](/Roof/news-helicopter-screwdriver) and
[The Screwdriver and the Crime-Scene Tape](/Roof/screwdriver-and-crime-scene-tape).

Nobody on this page is accused of wrongdoing. Officer Bagley is a living witness, not a suspect, and a gap
between aerial footage and courtroom testimony has ordinary explanations — timing, altitude, camera framing —
before it has extraordinary ones.

## What This Clip Does And Does Not Establish

**Does:** a news helicopter filmed the Losee Center roof from above roughly an hour after the shooting, the
roof surface is visible in that footage, and in the thirteen seconds released here no crime-scene tape at the
railing and no screwdriver on the gravel can be seen.

**Does not:** measure how far back the gravel disturbance sits, establish where a shooter lay, establish the
exact time of the overflight, or establish that tape and a screwdriver were never on that roof. Absence in one
short aerial clip is a question for the record to answer, not an answer in itself.
