## What This Video Is

A 20-second vertical phone capture — 432 × 768, roughly 2 MB — filmed from an elevated position on the edge of the Utah Valley University plaza, looking down and across the crowd on **September 10, 2025**.

It reached wide circulation on **August 10, 2026**, almost eleven months after the event, when **[@DangerousConspr](https://x.com/DangerousConspr/status/2086628729975943324)** reposted it from **[@am_tortl](https://x.com/am_tortl/status/2086342071049236486)** with the note:

> "New video of Charlie at the time of assassination (crowd shot). Going to be hard to watch 100 times but could be something good hiding in here."

That framing is worth taking at face value. The person who circulated it is not claiming to have found anything in it — they are saying it is a previously uncirculated angle and inviting other people to look. This site takes the same position: the clip is filed here as **raw footage of the seconds around the shot**, not as evidence for or against any theory. Whether anything is "hiding in here" is an open question, and the honest answer today is that nobody has demonstrated that it is.

## What The Footage Shows

A continuous handheld capture, no cuts, held above head height and panning across the crowd rather than at the stage:

* **00:00 – 00:06** — a high, wide view down onto a packed courtyard. Several thousand people fill the concrete plaza and the grass. A tall lamp standard sits in the middle of frame; the mountains and the campus buildings close the background under a clear sky. A canopy tent is visible at the left edge of frame.
* **00:06 – 00:14** — the camera pans right along the crowd. The **"AMERICAN COMEBACK"** event tent comes into frame at the right, with the barricade line, camera operators and a vehicle behind it. Handmade signs are held up in the near crowd.
* **00:14 – 00:20** — the pan continues back across the plaza. The clip ends abruptly, on the audio reaction rather than on any visible event.

The camera is filming **the audience, from the side**, which is exactly what the person holding it says on the microphone. That places this clip in the same family as the other wide crowd captures in this cluster: it is the wrong angle for the table and the right angle for the crowd. Its distinct value is the **vantage point** — the side of the plaza, elevated, looking across — which is not duplicated by the front-on and rear captures already filed here.

Ordinary attendees, including young people, are visible throughout. None of them are identified on this site.

## What Is Said

There is no narration. The microphone picks up the person holding the phone and the people immediately around them. The full recovered speech pass is short:

> "My son's good. There's Charlie over there, guys. I can't really see it because I'm like on the side."
>
> ... "What the f---?"

Two things follow from that, and only two.

First, **"There's Charlie over there, guys ... I can't really see it because I'm like on the side"** establishes the filmer's own position and intent: standing off to the side of the crowd, aware of where Charlie Kirk was, and filming anyway because the view was poor. That is a self-description of vantage point, recorded on the tape, which is useful when placing this camera on a map of the plaza.

Second, the clip **ends on an unfinished exclamation** rather than on a narrated observation. The speaker does not say what they saw or heard. A machine speech pass on 20 seconds of open-air crowd audio is not a reliable instrument for anything finer than that, and one mid-clip fragment came back garbled and is not quoted here. Anyone who wants to work the audio should work from the file itself, which is pinned to IPFS above and can be pulled with the commands in `IPFS/ipfs.txt`.

## What This Does And Does Not Establish

It **does** add a previously uncirculated side-angle capture of the plaza in the seconds around the shot, with a self-stated camera position, to the raw footage record.

It **does not** show the table, the wound, the tent interior, a rooftop, or a shooter. It does not resolve the shot count, the timing dispute, or the direction question — this crowd audio is far too short and too noisy to bear on any of them. Claims about what the clip contains are the claims of the people making them; nothing on this page adopts them.
