# Security Knew It Was Coming

> **The claim:** Kirk's security **knew** — they didn't flinch, or used hand signals right before the shot.

**Verdict: UNSUPPORTED** (the "hand signal" specifics are **FALSE**).

## What fuels it
Slow-motion readings of ambiguous video (a guard "not flinching"; a gesture read as a "signal"), amplified by the fact that there **were** real security failures that day.

## What the evidence shows
- Kirk's head of security, **Brian Harpole**, stated publicly that the team uses **comms devices, not hand signals**, and gave **no advance signal**. On the [[2025-11-17 - Brian Harpole on the Shawn Ryan Show|Shawn Ryan Show]] he walked through the specific clips: the "woman handed him something" was **"Chrissy," a staffer handing out mints** (the only thing he pocketed was a fallen American flag); a teammate ("Gibby") **reached back to fix a radio** that came loose; and the FBI asked — *"there wasn't a key exchange… nothing got exchanged."* The Frank Turek "hat adjust" was a misread. See [[The Medical Examiner and the Wound]] for his role as a source.
- **"Didn't flinch"** is an **unfalsifiable** reading of ambiguous footage.
- Separately — and importantly — post-incident reviews **did** find real security **failures**: an **unsecured rooftop**, staffing gaps. **That is negligence, not complicity.** Negligence is what a *civil* suit targets; it is not evidence of foreknowledge.

## The rooftop-security dispute (and the "stand down" claim)
A stronger, recurring version — amplified by Megyn Kelly — is that TPUSA security was **"told to stand down"** and that **UVU police falsely claimed they had the roof secured**. What the sources actually support is narrower:
- **Harpole's account** ([[2025-11-17 - Brian Harpole on the Shawn Ryan Show|Shawn Ryan Show]]): the Monday before, his team messaged **UVU's [[Jeff Long|Chief Long]]**, flagging a **student roof-access point** and asking to control it or post one of his guards; the chief's last reply was **"I got you covered."** Harpole says the chief has never answered since; both sides invite a **FOIA of the chief's communications**. He aims the blame at **UVU police**, not his own team.
- **The failure itself is real and documented.** Chief Long said on the day: *"This is a police chief's nightmare… you try to get your bases covered, and, unfortunately, today, we didn't."* An **AP investigation** found UVU PD deployed only **6 of its 23 officers**, **flew no drone** (clear weather; drones ~$2,000), ran **no bag checks or metal detectors**, and **never asked** Orem PD or the Utah County Sheriff — both of which had drones and a mutual-aid agreement — to help. The shot came from the accessible **[[The Rifle|Losee Center]]** roof. See [[Roles and Responsibilities at the Scene]] and [[Gaps in the Investigation]].
- **The counter:** those texts are Harpole's *side* of a **blame dispute** (critics note the messages didn't specifically say "we have the Losee roof covered," and that a diligent detail should have verified the roof itself). And **Harpole never says anyone was "told to stand down"** — that framing is Megyn Kelly's, not his. A documented **coordination-and-resourcing failure** is what it is; it does not, by itself, imply an ordered stand-down.
- **The expert view cuts against a conspiracy:** Kirk's *prior* security director, **[[Greg Shaffer]]**, said on the day that a **~200-yard rooftop shot is "almost impossible to defend against"** for a private detail without countersniper teams, and that blame would fall on the private firm. A hard-to-defend shot plus a rooftop-coverage failure is **negligence and a blame fight**, not evidence of an ordered stand-down.

So the **rooftop was genuinely unsecured** (real), and there is a real **who-was-responsible dispute** — but "told to stand down / establishment cover-up" is an **overreach** the record doesn't support. See [[Kirks Security Detail]] and [[Gaps in the Investigation]].

## Bottom line
The specific "hand-signal" claim is false on the record; the broader "they knew" reading is unsupported. There were genuine security lapses — which cut *against* the idea of a smoothly run inside job, not for it.
