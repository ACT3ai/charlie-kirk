This investigation runs on acronyms — Army programs, flight-tracking data, Utah court rules, forensic lab
methods, explosive compounds. This page decodes the ones that matter and skips the ones everyone already
knows. It starts with the three acronyms that surfaced in Google searches traced to the Fort Huachuca area,
then gives the full glossary by category. Each row links (→) to the page where the term does its work.

## The Fort Huachuca Search Acronyms

Researchers pulling Google search-trend data geolocated to the **Tucson / Sierra Vista, Arizona**
area — home of [Fort Huachuca](./Fort_Huachuca/overview), the U.S. Army Intelligence Center of
Excellence — keep finding the same thing: the search strings are not written the way ordinary people
search. They carry **uppercase military and corporate acronyms** bolted onto a person's name. The
working theory among those researchers is that people on or near the installation were running
image searches to pull up and identify faces around the time of a
[possible September 9, 2025 meeting](/meeting/overview), and that the acronyms are shorthand from
their own working vocabulary. That theory is unproven; the acronyms themselves are the checkable
part, and this page is the glossary.

### ISRTF, PLTR, JDA

| Acronym | Stands for | What it is | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ISRTF** | Intelligence, Surveillance and Reconnaissance Task Force (U.S. Army) | An Army organization that modernizes ISR capabilities, pushes new tools to the field faster, aligns resources, and supports the shift toward large-scale operations against peer competitors. Associated with Fort Huachuca activities and exercises (per AFCEA reporting). | An internal Army term. It is not something a member of the public would normally type into Google. |
| **PLTR** | Palantir Technologies (NYSE stock ticker) | Supplies data-integration, analytics and AI platforms used by U.S. Army intelligence — including the Army Intelligence Data Platform, the Maven Smart System for targeting, and TITAN ground stations — and has a strategic partnership with Israel's Ministry of Defense for war-related missions (per Bloomberg reporting). | Appears joined to ISRTF as **ISRTF-PLTR**, which reads like a program or contract label rather than a stock query. |
| **JDA** | No single documented public expansion | In U.S. military intelligence usage the letters can appear in discussions of joint data / analytics support or deployable systems, but no official Army or Fort Huachuca document publicly ties a "JDA" program to these searches. | **Open question.** Readers who can document what JDA means in a Fort Huachuca context should send the source. |

### Where They Showed Up

The reported search strings combine these acronyms with **Iza Bendianishvili**, reported to be the
birth name of commentator and reporter **Elizabeth Lane** ([@imelizabethlane](https://x.com/imelizabethlane)),
who has publicly commented on the Charlie Kirk case (see [Railroading the Defense](/CoverUp/Railroading_Defense)).
Users on X, including **@Nirnegil**, reported Google search volume for strings such as
**"ISRTF-PLTR Iza Bendianishvili"** and **"JDA Iza Bendianishvili"** appearing to originate from the
Tucson / Fort Huachuca area.

What the searches can and cannot show:

* **They can show** that someone in that geographic area searched those exact keyword combinations,
  if the Trends data is reproduced.
* **They cannot show** who searched, whether the searcher was military, a contractor, or a civilian,
  or why. A birth-name search is also exactly what a curious member of the public might run.
* **No public source confirms** a formal "JDA" or "ISRTF-PLTR" program, unit, or tasking connected to
  Elizabeth Lane. The combined ISRTF-PLTR string makes sense as vocabulary only because Palantir's
  documented work spans Army intelligence modernization, which ISRTF oversees.

The same pattern — geo-specific image searches for named figures, from the Sierra Vista area, in the
weeks before September 9, 2025 — is laid out with screenshots at
[Pre-Sept 9 Search Spikes](/GoogleSearches/fort-huachuca-sept-9-searches) and
[Image Results Reportedly Wiped](/GoogleSearches/fort-huachuca-image-searches).

### Run The Search Yourself

Open [Google Trends](https://trends.google.com/), set the region to Arizona, and try the acronyms on
their own and in combination (**ISRTF**, **ISRTF-PLTR**, **JDA**). Note the date range and the metro
area. A term that only ever spikes from one military town is the signal worth documenting; a term
that spikes everywhere is not. Save screenshots with the date you pulled them — Trends data has
been reported to change after the fact (see [Trends deletion and suppression](/GoogleSearches/trends-deletion-suppression)).
