## The post

On **August 9, 2026**, **[@BasedSamParker](https://x.com/BasedSamParker/status/2086526755087769715)** posted the evidence composite above with the following text, reproduced verbatim:

> Whenever I stash murder bullets in a towel in the bushes so that they won't be found—I first spend hours engraving the bullets hoping that they'll be found.
>
> And I also unnecessarily take them out of my backpack or pockets to dump them—even though I still take the backpack & pants with me.
>
> But they're the only thing from my backpack & pockets that I leave behind—nothing else. 😭
>
> Who thought this up? And why isn't a single government or TPUSA employee questioning this retarded story? Do some people just "escape" differently? Lol

The post reached roughly **121,000 views, 3,439 likes, 649 reposts, and 228 bookmarks** — one of the more widely circulated ballistics arguments of that week.

## What the image shows

The composite is built from four published evidence photographs, each showing a **.30-06 cartridge laid against a ruler beside a handwritten evidence card**. The cards read **`2025-W-000401(1)`**, initials **`SK`**, date **`09/11/25`**, and lab/ATF exhibit numbers — **Lab 2 / ATF 2**, **Lab 3 / ATF 3**, and **Lab 5 / ATF 5**. Scratched lettering is visible on the brass of each round. The source watermarks on the panels indicate the photos came from mainstream outlets — **sltrib.c[om]**, **abc4.co[m]**, and one beginning **wj…** — meaning these are **publicly released case photographs**, not material originating with the poster.

## The argument, restated

Sam Parker's point is not about ballistics but about **behavioral self-contradiction**. As he frames it, the official account requires the same person to have done four things that work against each other:

1. **Spent significant time engraving messages** into cartridges — an act whose only purpose is to be read by someone later.
2. **Then concealed those same cartridges**, wrapped in a towel with the rifle, in the bushes — an act whose only purpose is that they *not* be found.
3. **Removed the rounds from a backpack or pockets** in order to dump them, rather than simply carrying them away.
4. **Kept the backpack and the pants** — so the ammunition became the single category of item deliberately left behind, while everything else went with him.

Taken together, he argues, the sequence has no coherent motive: it is simultaneously the behavior of someone who wants a message found and someone who wants evidence hidden.

## Where this fits in the ballistics record

This page records an argument about **plausibility**, and plausibility arguments are weaker than physical evidence — a real person under stress does incoherent things, and the fact that behavior looks strange is not proof it did not happen. That caveat is important and should be read alongside the claim.

What gives the argument weight is that it does not stand alone. The rifle recovered near campus has its own unresolved problems documented elsewhere in this section: the **September 17, 2025 ATF report** could not match the autopsy jacket fragment to the seized rifle ([ATF Fragment Inconclusive](./ATF_Fragment_Inconclusive)), testimony described **dust and debris inside the seized barrel** ([Barrel Dust & Bullet Diameter](./Barrel_Dust_And_Diameter)), reporting has raised **fingerprint questions** ([Rifle Fingerprints & Mismatch](./Rifle_Fingerprints_Mismatch)), and the circumstances of where the weapon was found are examined on [Gun Dropped Location](./Gun_Dropped_Location). An item of evidence that is forensically inconclusive *and* whose discovery story is behaviorally incoherent invites more scrutiny than either problem would on its own.

## Open questions

* **Who engraved the cartridges, and when?** Nothing published establishes the timing relative to September 10, 2025.
* **Was the engraving tool ever recovered?** Hours of engraving implies a tool and a workspace; neither appears in public filings reviewed for this page.
* **Chain of custody for the towel bundle** — who found it, at what time, and photographed in place or after removal?
* **Were the cartridges tested for prints or DNA**, and with what result? See [Rifle Fingerprints & Mismatch](./Rifle_Fingerprints_Mismatch).
* **Do the engraved rounds match the chambering and lot** of the round alleged to have been fired?
