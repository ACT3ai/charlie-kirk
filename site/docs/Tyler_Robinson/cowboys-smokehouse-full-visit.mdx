This page holds the **full 34-minute video** of citizen journalist
[@DiligentDenizen](/Influencers/x/diligent-denizen-2)'s return visit on **July 11, 2026** to
**Cowboy's Smokehouse** in Panguitch, Utah — the restaurant tied to the
[Cowboy's Smokehouse receipt](./cowboys-smokehouse-receipt) circulated as a possible alibi for
[Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) on the night of the
[Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) assassination — Charlie Kirk was assassinated hours earlier that day at Utah Valley University, and the [record of official withholding](/CoverUp/overview) is why a diner receipt in a small Utah town became an object of citizen forensics at all. In the video, according to the recording,
the restaurant's owner hands the journalist the physical **9/10/25 receipt** ("that IS the
ticket in question"), explains why no security camera was working that night, and points out a
staff member who says he is **"100%"** certain the customer he helped check out between
**8:55 and 9:47 PM on September 10, 2025** was Tyler Robinson. That staff member then gives an
on-camera interview (face cropped, name redacted at his request).

**Nothing on this page is presented as proven.** The receipt has not been authenticated by any
court, the customer's identity has not been officially confirmed, and every claim below is
attributed to the person who made it on the recording.

## Key statements from the video

All statements below are quoted or paraphrased from the transcript of the recording and are the
speakers' own claims, not findings of this site.

**The owner, on the cameras.** The owner says the old camera system "has never worked," that he
removed the dining-room camera when repainting in the winter of 2023, and that the only camera
at the time was a consumer unit watching his firewood on the patio, later moved inside "after
the whole Tyler Robinson thing." He says a full new security system is being installed. He also
describes harassing phone calls to the restaurant — including one where a caller screamed
expletives at a 14-year-old girl who answered — and says he reported a number to police. This
connects to the broader [camera questions](/cameras/overview) around the case.

**The owner, on the receipt.** On the recording the owner retrieves the printed ticket, tells
the journalist "you can have it," and says "that's the ticket." He says the card was run as a
debit transaction, which prints no customer name, and states that he gave "everything" to the
FBI. See the dedicated [receipt analysis page](./cowboys-smokehouse-receipt) for the receipt's
visible fields (Check #211, ordered 8:55 PM, paid 9:47 PM).

**The owner, on whether it was Tyler Robinson.** He was not there that night. He says: "as far
as I know, that wasn't him. My staff thinks it is… it would make sense that it could be," and
notes authorities "never told us it was or wasn't."

**The eyewitness, on-camera (31:00).** The employee — about a year and a half at the
restaurant — says he was working the night of September 10, 2025, remembers the customer
sitting near the end of the bar, ordering a steak and a couple of beers, quiet and on his phone
the whole time. He says he personally took the man's card at checkout ("I was the one who
helped him check out") near closing, consistent with the 9:47 PM receipt time. Asked how
certain he is that it was Tyler Robinson: **"100%."** He says the voice was "unforgettable" and
that he recognized it the next day when a video of Robinson's college-acceptance reaction was
released: "there was no doubt… that was him."

**The FBI follow-up claim.** The witness says the restaurant reported the sighting, the owner
contacted the [FBI](/FBI/overview), agents took the staff's contact information — and then,
according to him, **no one ever followed up**: "Never heard anything from them." He says that
silence is what prompted him to send a tip to [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens)' team. He
also states that, as of the recording, neither the prosecution nor the defense had reached out
to him to testify, despite his claimed direct contact with the customer. These are the
witness's claims; the FBI and the parties in [the trial](./Trial/overview) have not publicly
responded to them.

**The journalist's stated opinion on Lance Twiggs.** During small talk, the journalist offers
his personal opinion that [Lance Twiggs](/People/lance-twiggs) may have "set up" Robinson,
citing claimed physical resemblance and DNA arguments. This is the journalist's speculation
only — his conversation partner immediately pushes back ("that's what was reported, but
there's no evidence of that"), and no evidence establishing any such thing has been presented
in court. Twiggs has not been charged with any crime, and nothing on this page should be read
as asserting his involvement.

## Why this matters

If the receipt is authentic and the customer was Tyler Robinson, an **8:55–9:47 PM presence in
Panguitch** — roughly 3.5 hours' drive from [UVU](/UVU/overview) — would have to be reconciled
with the official [surrender timeline](./Surrender) and the
[Discord/text-message timeline](./Discord_Messages) attributed to that evening. The witness's
claim that federal investigators collected the lead and never followed up also feeds the
section's broader questions about the [FBI's handling of evidence](/FBI/overview). The
counterargument is equally simple: an unauthenticated debit receipt with no printed name, and a
single staff identification from a brief interaction, prove nothing on their own — which is
why the lack of any official follow-up, if true, is the part investigators on X consider most
significant.
