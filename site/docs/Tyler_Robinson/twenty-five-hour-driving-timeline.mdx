Most arguments against the state's case against [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) attack one
piece of it — the [ballistics](/Proof_Not_Tyler/ATF_Inconclusive_Ballistics), the
[confession timestamps](/court/mirandize/custody-timeline-fake-confession), the
[stairwell figure](./Stairs_Guy). This video does something different and, for that reason,
harder to answer: it accepts every published claim at face value, lines them up on one clock,
and adds up the hours. What comes out the other end is a **25-hour waking day** containing
roughly **13 hours and 20 minutes of driving**, performed by a 22-year-old in the middle of what
the speaker calls the largest federal manhunt of his lifetime — and performed, throughout, in the
same maroon T-shirt.

The speaker is described by the account that circulated the clip as **someone formerly on Charlie
Kirk's film crew team**. The video was posted by [**@ArtifexMemor**](/Influencers/x/artifexmemor),
an account whose bio reportedly lists prior work with **@tpusa** and **@charliekirk11**, and was
quoted on August 12, 2026 by **@CharlotteLee_88** with the framing: *"Here's an ethical video by
someone formerly on Charlie's film crew team. One that doesn't try to memorialize Charlie as Jesus
or a martyr."*

**Nothing on this page is a court finding.** It is one commentator's arithmetic applied to
publicly reported claims, delivered in a sardonic register. Robinson is **charged, not convicted,
and is presumed innocent**. Several inputs to the arithmetic — most notably the
[Panguitch identification](/Tyler_Robinson_Not_Assassin/panguitch-breaks-the-timeline) — are
themselves **contested and unauthenticated**, which cuts both ways: if Panguitch is wrong, the
driving total shrinks; if Panguitch is right, the state's night-of case is in far worse trouble
than a long day at the wheel.

## The day as the state reportedly tells it

The following is the sequence as the speaker narrates it, in his order, with his emphases.

* **Before 4:00 a.m., September 10, 2025** — wakes himself up and leaves St. George early enough
  to reach campus. *"They want us to believe that Tyler Robinson slept at home and then woke
  himself up before 4 a.m."*
* **8:07 a.m.** — caught walking past the neighbor's house in the footage TMZ released. He gets
  back in the car and pulls into the garage; the speaker notes the surveillance cameras in that
  garage.
* **Late morning** — approaches a Turning Point employee to ask whether this is where the
  [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) event will be held. He is wearing his favorite shirt:
  *"It's like I'm going to wear my favorite shirt to approach a turning point employee because I
  intend to kill their boss."*
* **Chick-fil-A on campus** — *"We haven't seen any footage from that yet. We haven't been told
  much about that."* The speaker assumes a meal before, in his phrase, it was time to do crime.
* **Off campus** — parks, changes into a long-sleeve black shirt and blue jeans.
* **11:28 a.m.** — sends his Wordle score to a friend. The word of the day was **POUTY**.
  *"Before we do crime, we do Wordle. That's a fact."*
* **Midday** — *"he shoves a broken down German 98 Mauser into his pants and he starts limping
  back onto campus with a new hat, a new backpack, and we all know what happens next."* Compare
  the [backpack and rifle concealment analysis](/Proof_Not_Tyler/Backpack_Rifle_Impossibility)
  and the [Mauser rifle page](./Weapon_Rifle).
* **After the shooting** — jumps off the building and lands on solid ground *"like he had been
  practicing that maneuver."*
* **Then** — changes **back** into the maroon shirt, but leaves the jeans on.
* **~6:38 p.m.** — the [Dairy Queen sighting](/Locations/Dairy_Queen_Orem). *"He really loves
  that shirt."*
* **Evening** — departs Orem and drives roughly **three hours** south to Panguitch, seats himself
  at the bar at [Cowboy's Smokehouse](./cowboys-smokehouse-full-visit), and orders a steak combo
  with a soft drink. The speaker lingers on the drink: *"no alcohol, presumably because he
  doesn't want to drive irresponsibly. And, you know, risking getting a DUI. He probably doesn't
  want to hurt anybody."*
* **After the steak** — drives roughly **three hours back up** to Orem to retrieve the rifle,
  *"during what is the largest federal manhunt of my lifetime."*
* **At the scene** — reportedly stops his car to **ask a police officer a question**.
* **~1:35 a.m., September 11** — gives up on the rifle and drives roughly **four hours** back to
  St. George, arriving **5:00 a.m. at the earliest**.

## The arithmetic

That sequence is where the video stops being a retelling and becomes a measurement:

> *"He's been awake for 25 hours straight, of which he has driven ... an approximate 13 hours and
> 20 minutes."*

The speaker converts the figure into distances a reader can feel:

* **13 hours 20 minutes** is New Orleans, Louisiana to **Chicago**.
* It is also Atlanta, Georgia to **New York City**.

His summary: *"Tyler Robinson? He's just driving about. He decided he just was gonna go around
Utah after committing his 1st assassination."* And the obvious question it raises, asked
seriously in the middle of the joke: *"I'm just wondering if anybody tested Tyler Robinson for
cocaine or meth."*

## And then he does not sleep

The most-repeated beat of the clip is what the narrative asks for **after** hour 25:

* Home for roughly **one hour** — no sleep.
* Drives about **an hour** to **Cedar City**, where he is captured on gas-station footage filling
  his tank. *"We don't actually have an answer for why he went to get gas that far away. But
  apparently, this young man just loves to drive. So why get gas local when you can get gas an
  hour away?"*
* Drives home again. Per the testimony attributed to roommate [Lance Twiggs](./lance-twiggs-drawings),
  Lance sees him **face to face** in the apartment when he wakes. Still, the speaker says, in the
  same shirt.

He closes on the shirt:

> *"I think that one day many books will be written regarding the stupidity, the sheer stupidity
> of the fed slop. And I'm ... probably going to write a book entitled, I went all the way to Orem
> to commit an assassination, and all I got was a maroon t-shirt? ... If you are going to commit a
> capital offense, please remember to wear your 2nd favorite shirt."*

## Why this matters, and what it does not prove

The clip's method is its argument. It does not introduce new evidence, name a different shooter,
or dispute a single item of the state's reported record — it simply refuses to read those items
one at a time. Read as a whole day, the reported timeline requires a person to remain awake for
more than a full day, drive the equivalent of Atlanta to New York, voluntarily return to an active
crime scene, converse with an officer there, and then decline sleep in favor of an hour-long drive
for fuel. Each individual leg has been reported somewhere. The composite is what the speaker
argues no one has been asked to defend.

The counterargument is straightforward and should be stated. Fatigue and erratic behavior are not
alibis — people under extreme stress do improbable things, and prosecutors are not obliged to make
a defendant's day sound reasonable. Several legs of the drive rest on **contested** inputs: the
Panguitch identification is claimed by restaurant staff and has not been authenticated in court,
and the restaurant itself later said it has no evidence establishing it. Remove Panguitch and
roughly six hours of the total disappears. The speaker's figures are also his own reconstruction,
not a stipulated record — no official body has published a driving-hours total for that day.

What survives both readings is the question the video is really asking: **the state's timeline has
never been published as one continuous clock, and every attempt by citizens to build one produces
a day that is hard to say out loud.** That is the same gap the
[Panguitch page](/Tyler_Robinson_Not_Assassin/panguitch-breaks-the-timeline) reaches from the other
direction — one demanding 98–99 mph, this one demanding 25 hours without sleep.
