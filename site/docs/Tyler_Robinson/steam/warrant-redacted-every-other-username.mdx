This page belongs in the Steam section for one reason: it explains **why a public gaming comment
wall is the best list of names anyone has.**

## What the Screenshot Shows

A composite circulated publicly, pairing excerpts from a search warrant affidavit with a screenshot
of the leaked message. Reading the text off the capture:

> "**AFFIDAVIT FOR SEARCH WARRANT** &mdash; Executed on: 17th day of September, 2025 @ 04:04 PM by
> /s/ JUSTIN BOWEN"

> "On 9/12/2025, at approximately 0100 hours, FBI Special Agent Lange conducted an interview with
> the boyfriend of Tyler Robinson at the Washington County Sheriff's Office."
> &mdash; *Page 5 of 9, Search Warrant No. 3217131*

> "Lance showed FBI Special Agent Lange additional messages on his cellphone from a social media
> app called 'Discord'. In one of the messages that Lance showed to investigators, the user
> '**tylor.kum**' posted the following message in a group chat: 'Hey guys, I have bad news for you
> all. It was me at UVU yesterday...'"
> &mdash; *Page 7 of 9, Search Warrant No. 3217131*

And beneath, the leaked screenshot itself, dated **9/11/25, 8:57 PM**, over the name
**zealous_monkey_55095**, with the caption:

> "Screenshot of leaked Discord message from Tyler Robinson (username: **zealous_monkey_55095**
> &mdash; **other usernames redacted**)"

Sourced to [Ken Klippenstein](/Tyler_Robinson/discord/confession-sourcing-klippenstein). We do not
assert the capture is authentic; we record who published it and when.

## The four words that matter here

**"other usernames redacted."**

One account is named. Everyone else in that group chat &mdash; every person who was in the room
when the message landed, every person who might have known something before it &mdash; is a black
bar. Not sealed pending release. Redacted out of the document the public sees.

The warrant also disagrees with itself about the one name it does give: the affidavit says
**tylor.kum**, the screenshot says **zealous_monkey_55095**. That mismatch has its own page &mdash;
[zealous_monkey vs. tylor.kum](/Tyler_Robinson/discord/zealous-monkey-vs-tylor-ikum).

## Why It Matters — for the Steam side

Put this next to the second closed door. Prosecutors have stated they **do not intend to offer**
evidence obtained through forensic extractions of the seized electronics &mdash;
[The Device Record Nobody Is Offering](./device-record-not-being-offered).

So: the associates' names are redacted out of the warrant, and the device record that would carry
the account lists is not being offered. The roster of the people around him is not in the public
record and is not scheduled to enter it.

Which leaves exactly one source. **The Steam comment wall**, where visitors typed their own display
names and, in two cases, their own Discord handles, in the open, of their own accord. Those are
collected on [The Steam Names Roster](./steam-friends-and-handles-roster).

That is a thin, accidental, entirely public substitute for a record that exists in full on a
private company's servers. It is also all there is. And it points at the record that would settle
it: a **Steam friend list is chosen one person at a time**, and Valve holds the login history for
every name on it &mdash;
[The Friend List and What Valve Holds](./steam-friend-list-and-what-valve-holds).

**None of this is saying that any of these people did anything illegal or immoral.** Being in a
group chat, or commenting on a gaming profile, is not evidence of anything.
[Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is **charged, not convicted, and presumed innocent**.

## What Would Settle It

* The **unredacted** group-chat roster from Search Warrant 3217131, or a public explanation of the
  legal basis for withholding it.
* **Valve's subpoena return**: the Steam friend list resolved to account identifiers, and the
  login IP history for each.
* Whether any account in either circle was operated from **outside the United States**, and from
  which country.
* Reconciliation of **tylor.kum** and **zealous_monkey_55095** as account identifiers rather than
  display names.

## The Fix

Redaction is a decision made inside the Department of Justice, reviewable by nobody the public can
reach. That is precisely the gate the
[Charlie Kirk disclosure laws](https://whoassassinatedcharliekirk.com/Fix/overview) remove.

* **Law 1 &mdash; DoJ / FBI Forced Disclosure** forces out what the Bureau already pulled, including
  what sits under the black bars.
* **Law 4 &mdash; Trusted Investigations** takes DoJ out of the gatekeeper role entirely and lets
  trusted investigators compel records from a **private company** such as Valve and take testimony
  under threat of perjury.
* **Law 2** answers the foreign question; **Law 3** makes the inquiry mandatory rather than
  discretionary.

[Read the four laws &rarr;](https://whoassassinatedcharliekirk.com/Fix/overview) &middot;
[The explainer video &rarr;](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1RbDMiEReys&t=133s)
