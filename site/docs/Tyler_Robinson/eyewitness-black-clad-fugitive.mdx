The alleged text-message confession attributed to [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) says
he **changed outfits in the woods** where he left the rifle. An electrician working at a
construction site *above* those woods described the man who walked up to his crew — after that
point in the sequence — as still wearing **all black**, with a **black trench coat, black COVID
mask, black cargo pants, a small backpack**, and **long, greasy black hair**.

That is the collision this page documents. It is presented as a reported conflict between two
pieces of publicly circulated material, not as a court finding. The messages have not been
authenticated in open court, the eyewitness has not been cross-examined, and Tyler Robinson is
entitled to the presumption of innocence.

The argument was assembled by [@BasedSamParker](/Influencers/x/based-sam-parker) in an 11:47
video published **August 7, 2026**, crediting earlier work by **@SammyTherese**,
**@Villgecrazylady** and [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens). Trial attorney
**Patricia Gifford** ([@pattygifflaw](https://x.com/pattygifflaw/status/2085794603819544692))
shared it the same day with the comment: *"Check this out! Eye witness recall helps show the
texts are fabricated."*

## The claim in one paragraph

According to the video, the alleged confession texts to
[Lance Twiggs](/People/lance-twiggs) read in part: *"I worry about prints. I had to leave it in
a bush where I changed outfits, didn't have the ability or time to bring it with."* If the man
who fled the [Losee Center](/campus_university/overview) roof changed clothes in the woods
where the rifle was dropped, then every sighting *after* that point should show him in
different clothes. The construction-site testimony, and the TMZ-watermarked residential camera
footage that follows it, both reportedly show him **still in the black tactical outfit**.

## The eyewitness description

The description quoted in the video comes from an electrician working near the university,
identified in the segment as **Dylan Hope**, who was also quoted in *USA Today*. He was
relaying what a coworker operating an excavator at the site experienced. As quoted on camera:

> "Yeah, black sunglasses. He had a black COVID mask. He had a black trench coat, black cargo
> pants, and he was wearing a small backpack, and he also had long, greasy black hair."

The reporter's account of the encounter, per the video: *"the young man came up here, spoke to
him, asked him if it was okay to come through the construction site, and that he said, 'Sure,
go ahead,' and he said that there'd been a shooting, and he wanted to get out of it."* The
timing detail the video treats as important — *"this all happened long before any of the sirens
happened, and before they evacuated"* — is what places the encounter early in the escape rather
than among the general evacuating crowd.

Sam Parker draws two problems from this. First, the clothing: *"the alleged gunman, who's
fleeing through the construction zone, still has all the black clothes on."* Second, the hair:
*"the photo the FBI released of allegedly Tyler Robinson that day, [shows] a person with short
hair, not long, greasy black hair."*

## The escape path the video retraces

Rather than stopping at the contradiction, the video walks the sequence from the beginning,
stitching together dispatch audio, broadcast news footage and citizen video. The stops, in order:

1. **Dispatch description.** Radio traffic described a possible gunman *"wearing all black,
   black long gun, black tactical helmet, a black mask, possibly wearing a tactical vest."*
2. **The roof.** Footage narrated as a figure running onto the rooftop, dropping into a
   *"lateral military crawl,"* a brief flash, and something *"flung forward."*
3. **A witness's own account.** A man with firearms experience reportedly told investigators the
   shooter was in **tactical gear and a face mask**, not jeans, and struck him as *"highly
   trained"* — a description the video contrasts with the image released to the world less than
   24 hours later.
4. **Off the roof.** Channel 4 and *USA Today*'s Will Carless reporting the figure running the
   roofline, jumping down, landing in grass and heading toward the treeline.
5. **The woods.** Evidence markers, FBI forensics teams, cones and crime-scene tape on the hill.
   The video pauses on an object in the footage it reads as an **Allen (hex) wrench** — noting a
   Mauser can be partly disassembled with one — and asks whether that is the source of early
   reports of a *"screwdriver"* found near the rifle. It presents this as a question, not a fact.
6. **The construction site.** CNN's Ed Lavandera above the scene, Will Carless below it, and the
   electrician's account of the encounter. Police reportedly brought canines to this property.
7. **The TMZ house camera.** A homeowner's footage, checked the next day, showing a man in black
   crossing a field and running a dirt path away from campus — after the alleged clothing change.

## The outfit-change tally

The video's closing argument is a count of the wardrobe the official sequence requires in a
single day:

| Stage | Reported clothing |
|---|---|
| Morning on campus | Maroon shirt, shorts, black Hurley hat, blue backpack |
| Leaving campus | Black long-sleeve shirt, jeans, blue or black hat, black backpack |
| On the Losee Center roof | All-black tactical-style outfit |
| After the woods (per the texts) | Changed clothes — different outfit |
| At the construction site | **Still all black**, trench coat, mask, small backpack |
| On the TMZ house camera | **Still all black** |
| Surrender in Washington County | Maroon shirt and blue jeans |

*"Is anybody getting dizzy from all these outfit changes?"* the narrator asks. His proposed
readings are that **body doubles or additional people** were involved in setting Tyler Robinson
up, or were involved in the plot alongside him. Those are his stated hypotheses, offered as
possibilities, and are not adopted as conclusions here.

## The phone-screen detail

The video closes on the physical evidence for the messages themselves: the damage to Lance
Twiggs's phone screen in the circulated photographs is described as *"a nearly perfect circle
exactly over his time display,"* leaving the timestamp of the screenshots unreadable. The
narrator calls this *"very convenient."* No independent forensic examination of that screen has
been published, and the observation is his.

## What this does and does not establish

It does not establish that the texts are fabricated. An eyewitness recalling clothing under
stress is a notoriously imperfect record, the "long greasy black hair" detail is
second-hand — Hope relaying a coworker — and the man at the construction site may simply not be
the same person as the roof figure. The video itself never resolves whether the excavator
operator's visitor was the gunman; the reporting it quotes says only that police thought it
worth following with dogs.

What it does establish is that the **prosecution's own sequence has to absorb the conflict**.
If the confession texts are the foundation of the case, and a contemporaneous witness places a
man in the original black outfit downstream of the alleged change, then either the witness is
describing someone else, or the texts describe something that did not happen. Both readings
carry consequences the official narrative has not publicly addressed.
