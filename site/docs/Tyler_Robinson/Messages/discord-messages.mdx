Of the four message sets, this is the one stored furthest from anyone's hands: on
**Discord's own servers**, under a company that responds to warrants and keeps server-side
timestamps. That is what makes the set both the most checkable in principle and the most disputed
in practice — because what the public has actually been shown is photographs, not exports.

The account is **zealous_monkey_55095**. That is the username on the confession screenshot, the
one Discord said it identified before reporting it found no planning, and the one the platform
later removed. It is the only Discord handle attached to Robinson anywhere in the public record —
see [Discord Friends, Servers and Handles](/Tyler_Robinson/discord/discord_friends).

## The Messages As Quoted In A Court Filing

In late July 2026 a legal filing excerpt circulated on X showing two Discord messages quoted
directly, each cited to **State's Exhibit 16.2**. The excerpt was posted by attorney
[@aburkhartlaw](https://twitter.com/aburkhartlaw/status/2082616890149789847) (Andrea Burkhart, who
describes herself as a trial and appellate litigator) with the note "Also another Discord message."

The two quoted messages read:

> "Hey guys. I have bad news for you all. it was me at UVU yesterday. im sorry for all of this. im
> surrendering through a sheriff friend in a few moments." — State's Ex. 16.2 at 1.

> "it was [me]. ... look at the pictures from the surveillance footage. it was me. ... im serious."
> — State's Ex. 16.2 at 2.

The bracketed "[me]" and the ellipses are in the filing itself, which means the quotation is a
partial rendering of a longer message rather than a full line.

The Exhibit 16.2 quotations did not surface on their own. They come from a **28-page State
memorandum supporting bindover** of Tyler Robinson, which
[@aburkhartlaw](https://x.com/aburkhartlaw/status/2082615978207445283) published in full on
July 29, 2026. The post that introduced it read:

> "Although we didn't get the autopsy report, the State has shared a lot of details of its findings
> in their memorandum supporting bindover of Tyler Robinson. The complex entry wound coupled with
> fragmentation is consistent with the bullet destabilizing before impact, such as due to yaw.
> There are many other very specific details of the damage caused to Charlie that are consistent
> with extensive fragmentation of a rifle bullet."

The excerpt image accompanying it reads: "The medical examiner concluded the manner of death 'to be
homicide.' State's Ex. 11 at 1. A forensic examination conducted by the ATF revealed that the
bullet that killed Mr. Kirk was a .30 caliber class round. State's Ex. 32 at 2. It created a
'complex entrance gunshot wound' and fragmented in Mr. Kirk's neck. State's Ex. 11 at 1, 3."

That passage is a **ballistics and wound finding, not a message finding** — it is reproduced here
because it is the excerpt that identified the document, and the wound analysis belongs with
[the ballistics section](/Gun_Bullet/overview). What matters for this page is that the whole
memorandum is now public and the message exhibits can be read in their filed context rather than as
loose screenshots.

## The Exhibit 16 Series — How The State Numbered The Messages

Reading the memorandum answers a question this site had been circling: the four message categories
are not an interpretation imposed from outside. **The State numbered them that way.** Exhibit 16
and its sub-exhibits break down as:

| Exhibit | What it is |
|---|---|
| **State's Ex. 16** | Transcript of the Lance Twiggs police interview (cited by page: Tr. at 3, 19, 20, and 29:10–30:20) |
| **State's Ex. 16.1** | The Robinson–Twiggs **text messages** — at least 17 pages, cited page by page |
| **State's Ex. 16.2** | The **Discord messages** — 2 pages |
| **State's Ex. 16.3** | The **handwritten note** |
| **State's Ex. 16.4** | Cited alongside the surveillance screen captures (Exs. 12.1, 12.4) |

The memorandum's own summary sentence puts all three message forms together: "Defendant also
admitted to Mr. Twiggs and other friends that he had shot Mr. Kirk. State's Exs. 16.1 at 1-2, 16.2
at 1-2, 16.3."

Note the phrasing there — **"and other friends."** That is how the Discord set is characterized in
the filing: messages to a group, distinct from the one-to-one text thread with Twiggs.

## What Exhibit 16.2 Is Used To Prove

The memorandum leans on the Discord exhibit for one specific evidentiary purpose beyond the
confession itself. Under Element 1 (identity), it states that "not only did Defendant admit that he
was the one depicted in the published screen captures from the UVU surveillance footage, State's
Ex. 16.2 at 1, Defendant's roommate and romantic partner confirmed that the individual in those
screen captures showing the individuals full face 'definitely' looked like Defendant."

So the state reads the "look at the pictures from the surveillance footage. it was me" line as a
self-identification with the published campus images — which is why that particular Discord message
carries weight in the filing out of proportion to its length. Whether the figure in those captures
is Robinson is contested elsewhere in this investigation; what the memorandum establishes is what
the state intends to do with the message.

For the far longer text-message exhibit (16.1) and what the state quotes from it — the planning
window, the rifle-retrieval attempts, the engraved cartridges, and the instruction to Twiggs to
"delete this exchange" — see [Texts to Lance Twiggs](./text-messages-twiggs). For Exhibit 16.3 see
[the handwritten note](./handwritten-note).

Circulating the excerpt on July 30, 2026, [@MsJaneDoeUKnow](https://x.com/MsJaneDoeUKnow/status/2082642579070546381)
wrote: "DISCORD MESSAGES — Tyler Robinson: ... Tyler Robinson is GUILTY! It was him on the roof of
the Losee Building taking the shot."

That last line is the poster's own conclusion, not a court finding. Robinson has been **charged and
has not been convicted**, and the identification of the figure on the roof is contested elsewhere
in this investigation. What the filing establishes is narrower and still significant: these
specific message texts are in the record as a numbered state exhibit, not merely as a screenshot
circulating online.

## Where The Messages Were Stored

Warrant materials reported by [The Post Millennial](https://thepostmillennial.com/revealed-utah-officers-demanded-discord-turn-over-server-records-containing-tyler-robinsons-communication-affidavit)
and circulated on [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/1024574137/Discord-Warrant) describe the
Utah State Bureau of Investigation seeking server records from **Discord**, targeting activity
linked to usernames including:

- **Craftopia**
- **Gamin**
- **The Corruption**

The FBI reportedly looked into 20 or more people in that Discord circle per BBC summaries.

## How They Reached Investigators

The earliest route described in the charging affidavit did not go through Discord at all. According
to the Officer [Brian Davis](/People/brian-davis) affidavit quoted in the investigation record,
investigators identified Robinson's **roommate**, who said Robinson had "made a joke on discord."
When asked to show the messages, the roommate reportedly **opened his own device and let
investigators photograph the screen** as each message was displayed.

So the first evidentiary form of this material was a photograph of a third party's phone screen.
A warrant to the company came later. That sequence is the origin of most of the authentication
argument on this site.

## The Custody-Timing Objection

The messages are reported to have appeared at **7:57 PM on September 11**, while
[Miranda materials](/Timeline/Tyler_Robinson/Mirandizing) place Robinson in custody at **6:25 PM**.
Washington County protocol is described as taking a suspect's phone once they are in custody and
Mirandized. If that holds, a post timestamped 7:57 PM would have to have come from someone else or
from a device not in Robinson's possession.

Commentators including [Kevin Steele](/People/kevin-steele) and Baron Coleman have pressed this
publicly. It is a contested timeline argument, not a proven fact, and it depends on the accuracy of
both timestamps. The [Discord Custody Timeline](/Proof_Not_Tyler/Discord_Custody_Timeline) page
works through it, and [Surrender](/Tyler_Robinson/Surrender) ties it to missing bodycam and lobby
CCTV.

## What Would Settle It

Native Discord JSON exports carrying message IDs and account tokens, server admin logs for the
named servers, and correlation of IP logins against custody timestamps. None of those have been
shown publicly.

The memorandum does sharpen one point that had been left open on this site. The reported state
filing saying it did not intend to offer evidence obtained through forensic extractions of seized
electronics sits directly against a bindover memorandum that cites the message exhibits on nearly
every page of its identity and mental-state arguments. Those two things can only be reconciled if
the exhibits are being authenticated some other way — through the Twiggs interview transcript
(Ex. 16), through Twiggs' own device, or through testimony rather than extraction. Which of those
is a docket question, and the memorandum does not answer it. The full authentication and contradiction analysis — including the internal
inconsistency table drawn from X commentary — remains on the original
[Discord Messages](/Tyler_Robinson/Discord_Messages) page.
