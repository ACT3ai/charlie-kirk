# Steam Profile and Comments — Stored on a Public Gaming Platform

This is the one category on this page that is **not confession material** and is not part of the
state's core case. It is background: an eleven-year gaming profile attributed to
[Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson), archived by citizen researchers and circulated online as
character evidence. It is included here because it is a distinct storage location with its own
distinct problems, and because leaving it off the list would let it be quietly treated as if it
belonged with the rest.

## Not In The Exhibit Series

Worth stating plainly, because it is checkable: the
[State's memorandum supporting bindover](/court/bindover/state-memorandum-supporting-bindover.pdf)
files the message material as State's Exs. 16 through 16.4 — the Twiggs interview transcript, the
texts, the Discord posts, the note, and one further exhibit tied to the surveillance captures.
**Steam material does not appear anywhere in that series.** Whatever weight the archived profile
carries online, it is not carrying weight in the filing.

## Where It Was Stored

On Steam, Valve's gaming platform, on **publicly visible profile pages**. No warrant was needed and
none was involved. Researchers reportedly scraped the material from pages anyone could load, and
report that those pages were subsequently scrubbed.

The profile is described in the investigation record as running under the handle **craftin247**,
later renamed **milk**, listed as Utah-based, with roughly 1,370 items owned and an eleven-year
history. The archived material includes profile art, aliases, screenshots from games including
Fallout, and captions attached to them.

## Who Archived It

A citizen research thread published nine key screenshots and credited a group of X accounts
including [@joshuajdonnelly](https://x.com/joshuajdonnelly), @TheLFLounge, @MrDiscordeous,
@CamelotJack1963 and @VoxExVeritas, along with contributors who chose to stay anonymous. The thread
states the archive was assembled from public pages rather than through any intrusion.

## How To Read It

The circulated framing reads the profile as showing isolation, dark humour and "red flags in plain
sight." That is interpretation, and it is interpretation performed **after** the event it is being
used to explain. Gore-themed screenshots and odd captions are unremarkable across tens of millions
of gaming profiles; they acquire significance here only because of what is alleged to have happened
afterwards. Hindsight framing of ordinary online material is one of the easiest errors to make in a
case like this one, and it is worth naming rather than working around.

Two narrower points survive that caution and are worth recording. The first is the alias history
itself — a documented rename from craftin247 to milk, which is a checkable fact about an account
rather than an interpretation of its contents. The second is the reported scrubbing: if publicly
visible pages were taken down after the event, when and by whom is a legitimate question, and the
archived copies are the only reason it can be asked at all.
