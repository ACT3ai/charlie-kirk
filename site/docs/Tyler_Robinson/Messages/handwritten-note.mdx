# The Handwritten Note — Stored Under a Keyboard

One message in this case was not stored on any server. According to the charging materials, a
handwritten note was left **under a keyboard** at the apartment
[Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) shared with [Lance Twiggs](/People/lance-twiggs). It is the
earliest piece of alleged confession material in the case and the one furthest from being tested.

## Where It Was Stored

A sheet of lined notebook paper, under a keyboard, in a private apartment. No server logs, no
account, no message ID, no timestamp, no metadata of any kind. Everything that makes the Discord
set checkable in principle is absent here, and so is everything that makes it disputable. There is
no 7:57 PM to argue about, because paper does not record when it was written.

## How It Entered The Record

The note surfaced through the text thread rather than through a search of the apartment alone. The
exchange recovered from Twiggs' phone contains the instruction to look under the keyboard and the
roommate's reply as he read it — which is why
[the text messages](./text-messages-twiggs) and this note have to be read together. The reaction
quoted in the message record is what dates the note in practice: it places the reading of it inside
the Sept 10–11 window covered by the extraction that Agent Davis testified about.

The note itself has been shown publicly as an **enhanced photograph** on lined paper, addressed to a
nickname and containing a statement about an intention to take out
[Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk). The image is catalogued in the
[Letter photo cluster](/Photos/Tyler_Robinson/Letter/overview).

## Filed As State's Exhibit 16.3

The [State's memorandum supporting bindover](/court/bindover/state-memorandum-supporting-bindover.pdf),
published July 29, 2026 by [@aburkhartlaw](https://x.com/aburkhartlaw/status/2082615978207445283),
numbers the note **State's Ex. 16.3**. Unlike the text exhibit (16.1) and the
[Discord exhibit](./discord-messages) (16.2), it is cited without page numbers — consistent with a
single sheet.

It is also the exhibit the memorandum reaches for first when it argues identity. The lead quotation
under Element 1 is drawn from it:

> "I had the opportunity to take out Charlie Kirk, and I took it." — State's Ex. 16.3

The same exhibit is cited again on mental state, and once more for a line about the expected
outcome of the act — that he would be "likely dead, or facing a lengthy prison sentence."

There is a wrinkle worth noting in how the memorandum describes it. In places the filing frames
Ex. 16.3 as something Robinson "wrote to Mr. Twiggs," which reads more like a message than like a
note left under a keyboard. Whether that is loose drafting, or whether Ex. 16.3 is a photograph of
the note as it appeared in the message thread rather than the physical paper itself, is not
resolved by the document. It is the kind of distinction that matters for authentication, because a
photograph of a note and the note are not the same exhibit.

## What Researchers Have Pressed On

The objections here are about form, not content. Commentators quoted in the investigation record
note that the public has been shown photographs of the note and of the messages rather than the
note, the devices, or the full extraction, and that this material was released in **stages, months
apart**. They also point to the state filing reportedly stating that the state did not intend to
offer material obtained through forensic extractions of the seized electronics, which sits
awkwardly beside a public case built substantially on that material.

Against that: the defense holds the underlying data and is not publicly alleging fabrication, and
no examiner and no court has found the note to be anything other than what the state says it is.
Both of those things are true at once and should be held together rather than traded off.

## What Would Settle It

Authentication of the note is a physical-evidence question, not a digital one: ink analysis, paper
analysis, and a handwriting examiner comparing it against known exemplars. None of that has been
presented publicly. Until it is, the note's date and authorship rest on the surrounding testimony
and on the text thread that points to it.

When you look at the published image, it is worth separating three things deliberately: what is
actually legible on the paper, what is the product of enhancement, and what is inference layered on
top by whoever posted it.
