# Text Messages — Recovered From Lance Twiggs' Phone

This is the message set with the clearest forensic chain, and the fact worth stating first is that
the chain does not run through a device attributed to [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson). The
Sept 10–11 exchanges were recovered from the phone belonging to his roommate and partner,
[Lance Twiggs](/People/lance-twiggs).

## Where They Were Stored

On Twiggs' handset, in the ordinary messaging store, alongside everything else on that phone. That
matters in both directions. It means there is a physical device that was seized, imaged and
testified about — a far better position than a screenshot. It also means every message in the set
is a copy as received, and the sending device is not the one that produced the record.

## How They Were Extracted

According to the investigation record, the extraction used **Cellebrite** forensic tooling, and the
recovery of the Sept 10–11 Robinson–Twiggs texts — including the thread referencing the note left
under the keyboard — was **confirmed in open court through Agent Davis' testimony**. That is the
strongest evidentiary footing any of the four message sets has on this site.

Cellebrite DI Ltd. is an Israeli company headquartered in Petah Tikva, and its presence in this
case has drawn commentary given the Israel questions raised elsewhere in the investigation. That
commentary is worth recording and worth keeping in proportion: Cellebrite tooling is standard in
police work in all fifty states and 150-plus countries, and it appears in enormous numbers of cases
with no Israel angle whatever. The one substantive precedent commentators cite is the Karen Read
case, where Cellebrite's parsing of a Google search timestamp was disputed and the company
subsequently updated its software to flag ambiguous timestamp artifacts. That is an example of
parsing fallibility — a derived value presented with more confidence than the underlying data
supports — and it is routinely overstated as "Cellebrite altered metadata," which is not what
happened.

## Filed As State's Exhibit 16.1

The [State's memorandum supporting bindover](/court/bindover/state-memorandum-supporting-bindover.pdf),
published July 29, 2026 by [@aburkhartlaw](https://x.com/aburkhartlaw/status/2082615978207445283),
numbers this material **State's Ex. 16.1** and cites it page by page up to at least page 17 — making
it by far the largest of the message exhibits. The related [Discord set](./discord-messages) is
Ex. 16.2 (two pages) and [the note](./handwritten-note) is Ex. 16.3.

The memorandum quotes the following from Ex. 16.1, attributing each to Robinson:

- "I am [the one who did it], I'm sorry." *(at 2)*
- "I had hoped to keep this secret till [I] died of old age." *(at 2)*
- "Why did I do it? … I had enough of his hatred. some hate can't be negotiated out." *(at 3)*
- "[I have been planning this] a bit over a week I believe." *(at 3)*
- "if I am able to grab my rifle unseen, I will have left no evidence. going to attempt to retrieve
  it again, hopefully they have moved on. I haven't seen anything in the news about them finding
  it." *(at 3)*
- "im wishing I had circled back and grabbed it as soon as I got to my vehicle, because it sounds
  like the police couldn't get in for a long while because of the traffic." *(at 5)*
- "remember how I was engraving bullets? the fuckin messages are mostly a big meme. if I see
  'notices bulge uwu' on fox news I might have a stroke." *(at 8)*
- "judging from today I'd say grandpas gun does just fine idk." *(at 9)*
- "delete this exchange" *(at 9)* — and an instruction not to speak to police *(at 17)*
- "I'm going to turn myself in willingly." *(at 15)*

Those are the state's quotations from its own exhibit, offered in support of probable cause. They
are not findings, and Robinson is charged rather than convicted. The last two items are notable
because they are what Counts 5 and 6 — the obstruction and witness-tampering counts — rest on: the
memorandum argues the demand to delete the exchange and the demand not to speak to police each
establish the elements of those counts.

## What The Messages Reportedly Contain

The affidavit-derived summary in the investigation record describes content referencing:

- A need to **retrieve a rifle from a drop point**
- Leaving the rifle **in a bush**, **wrapped in a towel**
- **Watching the area** where the rifle was left
- **Engraving bullets**, a **scope**, and the rifle being **"unique"**
- Having **changed outfits**

Screenshot threads circulating in July 2026 added further exchanges framed as Robinson confessing
to Twiggs, including a claim summarized by [@ginamilan_](/Influencers/x/ginamilan) that a rifle was
dropped at a drop point and could not be retrieved because vehicles were still lingering. Those are
screenshots in circulation, not authenticated exhibits, and should be read as such.

The short exchange most often quoted — "look under my keyboard," answered with "I am. I'm sorry" —
is the hinge between this page and
[the handwritten note](./handwritten-note).

## Twiggs' Status

Twiggs is treated in the record as a **cooperating witness** and **has not been charged**. Nothing
on this page alleges that Twiggs knew of or participated in any attack. Where his messages are
quoted, they are quoted as a witness's own words.

## Open Questions

The device seizure logs and write-blocker forensic reports have not been shown publicly, and the
same state filing that shadows the Discord material — the one reportedly stating the state did not
intend to offer evidence obtained through forensic extractions of seized electronics — sits
awkwardly beside a testified extraction. Whether that filing was superseded, narrowed, or applies
only to particular devices is a docket question readers should check rather than assume.

For the courtroom treatment of the device itself, see [Twiggs Phone](/court/Twiggs_Phone). For the
screenshot transcripts in circulation, see the
[Lance photo cluster](/Photos/Tyler_Robinson/Lance/overview).
