# The Messages Attributed to Tyler Robinson

The case against [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) rests heavily on messages. They did not
all arrive the same way, they were not all stored in the same place, and they did not all reach
investigators through the same route. This page separates them by **where each one lived** before
anyone in law enforcement saw it, because that storage question is what determines how each set can
be authenticated. Open a kind below for the record, the storage location, and the disputes attached
to it. None of these messages establishes how Charlie Kirk was killed — that question turns on the
[physical evidence](/Cause_of_Death/overview), where the majority citizen finding points to an
explosive device rather than the government's rifle.

## The State Numbered Them The Same Way

This split is not a framing invented here. The **State's memorandum supporting bindover** — a
28-page filing published July 29, 2026 by attorney
[@aburkhartlaw](https://x.com/aburkhartlaw/status/2082615978207445283) — files the message material
as a single numbered series:

| Exhibit | What it is |
|---|---|
| **State's Ex. 16** | Transcript of the Lance Twiggs police interview |
| **State's Ex. 16.1** | The Robinson–Twiggs **text messages** (17+ pages) |
| **State's Ex. 16.2** | The **Discord messages** (2 pages) |
| **State's Ex. 16.3** | The **handwritten note** |
| **State's Ex. 16.4** | Cited alongside the surveillance screen captures |

Steam material appears nowhere in the series, which is the clearest available confirmation that it
is not part of the state's case.

**[Read the full memorandum (PDF, 28 pages)](/court/bindover/state-memorandum-supporting-bindover.pdf)**

## Where Each Set Was Stored

Four storage locations, four different evidentiary problems.

**Discord — a third-party server.** The alleged confession posts lived on Discord's
infrastructure, in servers reported under usernames including Craftopia, Gamin and The Corruption.
Records of that kind are obtained by warrant served on the company. The Utah State Bureau of
Investigation reportedly did serve such a warrant. What the public has been shown, however, is
photographs and screenshots rather than a native export, and the earliest capture described in the
charging affidavit is a photograph taken of a **roommate's own phone screen** while he scrolled
through the messages.

**Text messages — a second person's phone.** The Robinson-Twiggs exchanges were not recovered from
a device attributed to Robinson. They were extracted from the phone belonging to
[Lance Twiggs](/People/lance-twiggs), using Cellebrite forensic tooling, and Agent Davis testified
about that extraction in open court. This is the one message set with a described forensic chain
and courtroom testimony behind it.

**Paper — under a keyboard in an apartment.** The handwritten note had no server, no timestamp and
no metadata. According to the charging materials it was left under a keyboard at the apartment
Robinson shared with Twiggs. Its date and authorship can only be established by handwriting
analysis, physical examination and the surrounding testimony — which is precisely why researchers
have pressed on it.

**Steam — a public gaming profile.** Archived Steam material (the "craftin247" and "milk" profiles,
captions and comments) was scraped from publicly visible pages by citizen researchers before those
pages were reportedly scrubbed. It is not confession material and is not part of the state's core
case; it is background circulated online as character evidence, and it should be read that way.

## Why The Storage Question Matters

Every dispute on these pages traces back to storage. The custody-timing objection — that a Discord
post timestamped after Robinson was already Mirandized could not have been typed by him — only
works because Discord posts carry server-side timestamps. The note has no such problem and no such
check. The texts sit in between: a real forensic extraction, but of somebody else's device.

There is also a filing that cuts across all of it. A state filing opposing a defense continuance
reportedly stated that the state did **not** intend to offer evidence obtained through forensic
extractions of the electronics seized in the case. If that is accurate, the strongest
authentication path for the digital material may not be the path the prosecution actually walks at
hearing, even while public reporting leans on that material heavily. Readers should verify against
the current docket rather than take that summary as settled.

## How To Read These Pages

Nothing here alleges that any message was fabricated. No examiner and no court has found the note
or the messages to be anything other than what the state says they are, and the defense — which
holds the underlying data — has not publicly claimed forgery. What this section documents is that
authentication and timing remain open questions on the Discord set, that the text set has the
clearest chain, and that the note has the least testable form of all. Those are three different
evidentiary positions and they should not be collapsed into one.

For the fuller authentication and custody-timing argument as it developed, see the original
[Discord Messages](/Tyler_Robinson/Discord_Messages) page, which remains the long-form treatment of
that dispute.
