# The Discord Evidence

Almost everything the public believes about [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson)'s state of
mind traces back to **one screenshot** of a chat app. This section takes that screenshot apart
document by document: what it says, who it names, where it came from, what the platform itself said
about it, and why its timestamp does not fit the custody record.

Two facts frame the whole section. **Discord reviewed its own servers and found no plotting** &mdash;
and then said the messages reported as planning details **were not Discord messages at all**. And
the confession is timestamped **after** Robinson was already Mirandized on video.

None of this establishes how Charlie Kirk died. That question turns on the
[physical evidence](/Cause_of_Death/overview), where the majority citizen finding points to an
explosive device rather than the rifle account these messages are used to support.

## Why This Section Exists

The Discord confession did more work than any other single item in this case. It supplied the
motive, it supplied the admission, and it arrived early enough to set the shape of every story
written afterwards. It deserves to be examined at the level of detail normally reserved for
ballistics.

When you do examine it, four problems surface and none of them has been answered:

**1. It is a photograph, not a log.** The probable-cause affidavit describes investigators
photographing a roommate's phone screen *"as each message was shown."* The search warrant's own
exhibit is a screenshot **cited by URL to a Substack post**. There is no export, no message ID, no
account token anywhere in the public record.

**2. The platform label is contested.** The affidavit says Discord. The search warrant, describing
the same interview, repeatedly says **text messages**. Discord says the planning content was **not
on Discord**. Two of the three agree, and the odd one out is the document that established probable
cause.

**3. Two usernames, one post.** Page 5 of Search Warrant 3217131 attributes the confession to
**tylor/kum**. Page 7 of the same warrant shows a screenshot attributing it to
**zealous_monkey_55095**. Nothing in the document reconciles them.

**4. The clock does not fit.** Bates-stamped discovery shows Robinson **Mirandized at ~6:25 PM** on
September 11. The confession is dated **7:57 PM** in filings and **8:57 PM** on the screenshot
itself. Under Washington County protocol his phone would already have been taken.

## What Would Settle It

* Native Discord exports with message IDs, edit flags and account tokens.
* Discord's subpoena return, and login IP records for September 10&ndash;12.
* The investigators' photographs in sequence, with metadata.
* A single released frame of **Bates 003996-R2** showing its timestamp.
* Bodycam and lobby CCTV from the surrender.

Every one of these exists somewhere. None is public.

## The Exhibit 16 Series

The state's own filing numbers the message evidence, and its numbering matches the four categories
this section is organised around. From the **memorandum supporting bindover**:

| Exhibit | Content | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **16** | Transcript of the Lance Twiggs police interview | cited by page |
| **16.1** | Robinson&ndash;Twiggs **text messages** | **17+ pages** &mdash; the largest by far |
| **16.2** | The **Discord messages** | **2 pages** |
| **16.3** | The **handwritten note** | &mdash; |
| **16.4** | Cited with the surveillance screen captures | &mdash; |

**Steam material appears nowhere in the series** &mdash; the clearest available confirmation that
the archived gaming profile is not part of the state's case, however widely it circulated as
character evidence.

Two details in that table are worth sitting with. The Discord exhibit &mdash; the confession that
shaped every story written about this case &mdash; is **two pages**, the smallest message exhibit
of the four. And the state's summary sentence characterises it as messages to a group:
*"Defendant also admitted to Mr. Twiggs **and other friends** that he had shot Mr. Kirk."* Those
other friends are the accounts the warrant redacted.

The full document is hosted here:
[State's memorandum supporting bindover](/court/bindover/state-memorandum-supporting-bindover.pdf).

## The Location Field Is Typed In. The IP Address Is Not.

On Discord, on Steam, and on every platform in this section, the country shown on a profile is
**a text box the user filled in**. Anyone can type Utah, or Egypt, or nothing at all, and change
it again in seconds. Whether it is shown publicly is a setting. It is not evidence of where a
person was sitting when they typed a message.

What is **not** typed in is the **IP address**. Every session, every login, every message send is
written to the platform's own logs with the address it arrived from, alongside device
fingerprints and session timing. Discord holds that record for the servers named in the Utah State
Bureau of Investigation's warrant &mdash; **Craftopia**, **Gamin** and **The Corruption** &mdash;
and for the account that posted the confession.

That is the question this section keeps returning to: **is it known where the people in these
servers actually were?** The FBI reportedly examined twenty or more people in Robinson's Discord
circle and charged none of them, and nothing in the public record states where any of them were.
To be explicit, because it matters: **being in another country is not wrongdoing**, no one in
those servers has been accused by any authority of anything, and nothing on this site suggests
any of them did anything improper. The point is narrower &mdash; that a location nobody has
verified should not be treated as if it were verified, in either direction. Self-reported locations circulated widely &mdash; see [the Egypt claim on a Steam profile](/Tyler_Robinson/steam/steam-ankha-egypt-profile) &mdash; and self-reported locations are worthless.

The answer exists. It is in Discord's subpoena return and in the login-IP history for
September 10&ndash;12 &mdash; records that investigators can obtain through lawful process and
the public cannot. **What we are asking for is that the record for the account that posted the
confession be established through that process and, so far as the law allows, made public.**
Until it is, every claim of foreign involvement in these chats &mdash; and every denial &mdash;
rests on a field somebody typed.

We are **not** asking anyone to identify, locate, contact or expose any private individual, and
readers should not attempt it. We name no one and we accuse no one. This is a request for a
record, not an allegation against a person.

## The Steam Friend List May Matter More

Discord is where the confession appeared. **Steam is where the friendships actually lived.**
A server can hold hundreds of people who have never spoken to each other; a **friend list is
chosen one person at a time**, and the people on it are the ones you spend hundreds of hours in
voice chat with, night after night, for years. If anyone in Robinson's online life knew him well
enough to matter, they are likelier to be on that list than on a server roster.

The two circles overlap directly: visitors to the Steam profile posted their **Discord handles**
on its public comment wall. And Steam stores exactly what Discord stores &mdash; **the IP address
each session came from** &mdash; no matter what country a profile claims.
