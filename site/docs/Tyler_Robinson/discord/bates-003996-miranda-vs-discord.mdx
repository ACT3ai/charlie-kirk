If the Discord confession has one genuine, document-backed problem, this is it.

## The exhibit

> "**'Bates 003996-R2'** is a Bates-stamped discovery exhibit (standard numbering used by the
> prosecution/Utah County Attorney's Office when producing large volumes of materials to the
> defense). It refers to a **video recording** (likely bodycam, stationhouse interview, or
> surveillance footage) of Tyler Robinson's encounter with law enforcement. The video shows him
> being **read his Miranda rights at approximately 6:25 p.m. MDT on September 11, 2025** (with an
> internal video timestamp around 03:36:53 or similar, depending on the exact frame)."

> "The exhibit has been referenced in recent defense filings and analysis (particularly around late
> March 2026) to highlight alleged **timeline discrepancies**: Robinson was **already in custody
> and mirandized (and reportedly invoked his right to counsel) hours before** the official
> 'surrender' narrative and a Discord confession message attributed to him.
> Podcaster/investigator **Baron Coleman** has prominently discussed and quoted from it."

## The arithmetic

| Time, September 11, 2025 | Event |
|---|---|
| **~6:25 PM MDT** | Robinson Mirandized on video, per Bates 003996-R2; reportedly invokes counsel |
| **7:57 PM MDT** | Time cited in charging documents and reporting for the Discord confession |
| **8:57 PM** | Time shown on the confession screenshot itself |
| 09/12, **04:00** | Arrest date/time stamped on the probable-cause affidavit |

Take the earlier confession figure and the gap is **92 minutes**. Take the screenshot's own clock
and it is **two hours and thirty-two minutes**. Either way the message postdates the Miranda
warning.

## The phone-custody argument

Washington County protocol is described as **taking a suspect's phone once they are in custody and
Mirandized**. If that happened at 6:25 PM, the device would have been held until Utah County
Officer [Brian Davis](/People/brian-davis) arrived to transport Robinson roughly **260 miles**
north.

Under that reading, the person Mirandized at 6:25 PM did not have the phone at 7:57 PM, and
therefore did not post the message. **Baron Coleman** has framed this as the core of the
"made-up Discord messages" claim.

## What would resolve it, in one step

The video. Bates 003996-R2 exists, is numbered, and is in the defense's hands. A single frame with
its timestamp, released publicly, settles whether Robinson was in custody before the message.

It has not been released. Neither has the **bodycam** nor the **lobby CCTV** from the surrender
&mdash; see [Surrender &mdash; Sept 11](/Tyler_Robinson/Surrender), which ties the missing footage
to precisely this question.

## The honest counter-argument

Two possibilities keep the official account intact:

1. **The 6:25 PM figure is wrong** &mdash; misread frame, timezone offset, or an internal video
   clock not synced to wall time. The explainer itself hedges: *"or similar, depending on the exact
   frame."*
2. **Someone else had the account.** If a friend or family member had access, the message could
   post while Robinson was in custody. But that undoes the confession as a confession.

Both remain open. What is not open is that the two timestamps, as they currently stand in the
record, cannot both describe the same man with the same phone.

## Why this page sits at the centre of the Discord section

Every other page here is about authentication &mdash; who wrote it, on what platform, sourced from
whom. This page is about **physical possibility**. It is the one argument that does not require
anyone to have lied, only for a clock to be right.

## The Location Field Is Typed In. The IP Address Is Not.

On Discord, on Steam, and on every platform in this section, the country shown on a profile is
**a text box the user filled in**. Anyone can type Utah, or Egypt, or nothing at all, and change
it again in seconds. Whether it is shown publicly is a setting. It is not evidence of where a
person was sitting when they typed a message.

What is **not** typed in is the **IP address**. Every session, every login, every message send is
written to the platform's own logs with the address it arrived from, alongside device
fingerprints and session timing. Discord holds that record for the servers named in the Utah State
Bureau of Investigation's warrant &mdash; **Craftopia**, **Gamin** and **The Corruption** &mdash;
and for the account that posted the confession.

That is the question this section keeps returning to: **were one or more of the people in these
servers outside the United States?** The FBI reportedly examined twenty or more people in
Robinson's Discord circle and charged none of them, and nothing in the public record states where
any of them actually were. Self-reported locations circulated widely &mdash; see [the Egypt claim on a Steam profile](/Tyler_Robinson/discord/steam-ankha-egypt-profile) &mdash; and self-reported locations are worthless.

The answer exists. It is in Discord's subpoena return, in the login-IP history for
September 10&ndash;12, and in the traffic records that US intelligence and federal investigators
can obtain and the public cannot. **We want that answer found and published.** Until it is, every
claim of foreign involvement in these chats &mdash; and every denial &mdash; rests on a field
somebody typed.

We name no one and we accuse no one. We are asking for the record that would settle it.

## The Steam Friend List May Matter More

Discord is where the confession appeared. **Steam is where the friendships actually lived.**
A server can hold hundreds of people who have never spoken to each other; a **friend list is
chosen one person at a time**, and the people on it are the ones you spend hundreds of hours in
voice chat with, night after night, for years. If anyone in Robinson's online life knew him well
enough to matter, they are likelier to be on that list than on a server roster.

The two circles overlap directly: visitors to the Steam profile posted their **Discord handles**
on its public comment wall. And Steam stores exactly what Discord stores &mdash; **the IP address
each session came from** &mdash; no matter what country a profile claims.
