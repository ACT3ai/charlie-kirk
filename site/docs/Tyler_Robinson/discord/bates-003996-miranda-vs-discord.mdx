If the Discord confession has one genuine, document-backed problem, this is it.

## The exhibit

> "**'Bates 003996-R2'** is a Bates-stamped discovery exhibit (standard numbering used by the
> prosecution/Utah County Attorney's Office when producing large volumes of materials to the
> defense). It refers to a **video recording** (likely bodycam, stationhouse interview, or
> surveillance footage) of Tyler Robinson's encounter with law enforcement. The video shows him
> being **read his Miranda rights at approximately 6:25 p.m. MDT on September 11, 2025** (with an
> internal video timestamp around 03:36:53 or similar, depending on the exact frame)."

> "The exhibit has been referenced in recent defense filings and analysis (particularly around late
> March 2026) to highlight alleged **timeline discrepancies**: Robinson was **already in custody
> and mirandized (and reportedly invoked his right to counsel) hours before** the official
> 'surrender' narrative and a Discord confession message attributed to him.
> Podcaster/investigator **Baron Coleman** has prominently discussed and quoted from it."

## The arithmetic

| Time, September 11, 2025 | Event |
|---|---|
| **~6:25 PM MDT** | Robinson Mirandized on video, per Bates 003996-R2; reportedly invokes counsel |
| **7:57 PM MDT** | Time cited in charging documents and reporting for the Discord confession |
| **8:57 PM** | Time shown on the confession screenshot itself |
| 09/12, **04:00** | Arrest date/time stamped on the probable-cause affidavit |

Take the earlier confession figure and the gap is **92 minutes**. Take the screenshot's own clock
and it is **two hours and thirty-two minutes**. Either way the message postdates the Miranda
warning.

## The phone-custody argument

Washington County protocol is described as **taking a suspect's phone once they are in custody and
Mirandized**. If that happened at 6:25 PM, the device would have been held until Utah County
Officer [Brian Davis](/People/brian-davis) arrived to transport Robinson roughly **260 miles**
north.

Under that reading, the person Mirandized at 6:25 PM did not have the phone at 7:57 PM, and
therefore did not post the message. **Baron Coleman** has framed this as the core of the
"made-up Discord messages" claim.

## What would resolve it, in one step

The video. Bates 003996-R2 exists, is numbered, and is in the defense's hands. A single frame with
its timestamp, released publicly, settles whether Robinson was in custody before the message.

It has not been released. Neither has the **bodycam** nor the **lobby CCTV** from the surrender
&mdash; see [Surrender &mdash; Sept 11](/Tyler_Robinson/Surrender), which ties the missing footage
to precisely this question.

## The honest counter-argument

Two possibilities keep the official account intact:

1. **The 6:25 PM figure is wrong** &mdash; misread frame, timezone offset, or an internal video
   clock not synced to wall time. The explainer itself hedges: *"or similar, depending on the exact
   frame."*
2. **Someone else had the account.** If a friend or family member had access, the message could
   post while Robinson was in custody. But that undoes the confession as a confession.

Both remain open. What is not open is that the two timestamps, as they currently stand in the
record, cannot both describe the same man with the same phone.

## Why this page sits at the centre of the Discord section

Every other page here is about authentication &mdash; who wrote it, on what platform, sourced from
whom. This page is about **physical possibility**. It is the one argument that does not require
anyone to have lied, only for a clock to be right.

## The detail inside the video that dates it

The strongest argument that the Bates 003996-R2 encounter happened on **Thursday September 11**,
not Friday the 12th, is not the timestamp at all. It is a sentence Robinson says on the recording.
Asked whether he wants to proceed, he answers:

> "I'd like to speak with my legal counsel. I'm hoping for a fella named Doug Terry. Told he's
> pretty good attorney here. Think my folks are trying to get a hold of him. **I think his office
> is closed for the night.**"

Baron Coleman's reading of that line: a lawyer's office is *closed for the night* on a Thursday
evening. On a Friday evening at 6:25 PM it would be closed **for the weekend**, and a person
waiting on a callback would say so. The same sentence also places a **recent conversation with his
parents** &mdash; "my folks are trying to get a hold of him" &mdash; which does not fit a man who,
on the official account, had by then been in custody for almost twenty-four hours.

This is an argument from the content of the recording rather than from its clock, which is what
makes it hard to dismiss as a misread frame. It is one commentator's reading of a quoted
transcript, not a court finding.

## The booking sheet points the same way

The Utah County Sheriff's Office inmate booking sheet and probable cause statement record:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Arrest date | **09/11/2025, 10:00 PM** |
| Arresting officer | **[Brian Davis](/People/brian-davis)** &mdash; State Bureau of Investigation |
| Date into Utah County | **09/12/2025**, roughly four hours later |

Davis would have travelled from Orem, Salt Lake City or Provo &mdash; about three and a half
hours. To arrest Robinson in Washington County at 10:00 PM he had to have started north of there
at roughly **6:30 PM**, which is minutes after the Miranda timestamp. The return leg took about
four hours, which is consistent. The unanswered question is how Davis knew to begin driving south
if, on the official timeline, Robinson was not yet identified or in custody.

## What St. George residents reportedly heard before 7:57 PM

Two reported details sit in the same window, and both predate the confession timestamp:

* Residents of **St. George** &mdash; Robinson's own town, 260 miles from UVU &mdash; report
  receiving messages at approximately **7:00 PM** saying the person who shot Charlie Kirk lived
  there and was in custody.
* Media outlets reportedly understood he was in **Washington County Sheriff's Office** custody,
  which is given as the reason they were seeking the surveillance video beginning at 6:00 PM.

If either holds up, word of a St. George man in custody would have been moving through his own
town **before** the account [zealous_monkey_55095](./confession-screenshot-zealous-monkey)
announced he was surrendering "in a few moments." Both are reported claims from citizen
compilations rather than documents, neither has been confirmed by any official source, and both
are checkable by anyone who received such a message. We record them as unverified.

The author's characterisation &mdash; that they have not publicly addressed it &mdash; is that

deputies present at the 6:25 PM Miranda reading &middot; Utah Attorney General **Derek Brown**
&middot; Department of Public Safety Commissioner **Beau Mason**.

We want to be as clear as words allow about what this is. It is one commentator's list and one
commentator's inference. It is **not** an allegation that any person named committed a crime,
lied, withheld information, or took part in a cover-up. None of them has been charged with or
accused by any authority of anything arising from this timeline. Every one of them is presumed
innocent of any wrongdoing, and there are ordinary, entirely lawful reasons an official may not
publicly discuss a pending capital prosecution &mdash; including that they are prohibited from
doing so. Not commenting on a disputed timeline is not evidence of anything. Readers should not
contact, harass or draw conclusions about any person named here.

Two further items of public record are sometimes mentioned alongside it. Washington County
Sheriff **Nate Brooksby** held a press conference re-affirming the 33-hour manhunt account, and
**resigned in March 2026**; no reason was announced, and **no connection between his resignation
and this case has been established or alleged by any official body** &mdash; officials resign for
many reasons and we draw no inference from it. Separately, the Washington County Sheriff's Office
has stated that it did not interview or question Robinson. A Miranda warning is not an interview,
so that statement and the recording may well both be accurate; we note it as something that has
not been publicly reconciled, not as evidence that anyone said anything untrue.

## The Location Field Is Typed In. The IP Address Is Not.

On Discord, on Steam, and on every platform in this section, the country shown on a profile is
**a text box the user filled in**. Anyone can type Utah, or Egypt, or nothing at all, and change
it again in seconds. Whether it is shown publicly is a setting. It is not evidence of where a
person was sitting when they typed a message.

What is **not** typed in is the **IP address**. Every session, every login, every message send is
written to the platform's own logs with the address it arrived from, alongside device
fingerprints and session timing. Discord holds that record for the servers named in the Utah State
Bureau of Investigation's warrant &mdash; **Craftopia**, **Gamin** and **The Corruption** &mdash;
and for the account that posted the confession.

That is the question this section keeps returning to: **is it known where the people in these
servers actually were?** The FBI reportedly examined twenty or more people in Robinson's Discord
circle and charged none of them, and nothing in the public record states where any of them were.
To be explicit, because it matters: **being in another country is not wrongdoing**, no one in
those servers has been accused by any authority of anything, and nothing on this site suggests
any of them did anything improper. The point is narrower &mdash; that a location nobody has
verified should not be treated as if it were verified, in either direction. Self-reported locations circulated widely &mdash; see [the Egypt claim on a Steam profile](/Tyler_Robinson/steam/steam-ankha-egypt-profile) &mdash; and self-reported locations are worthless.

The answer exists. It is in Discord's subpoena return and in the login-IP history for
September 10&ndash;12 &mdash; records that investigators can obtain through lawful process and
the public cannot. **What we are asking for is that the record for the account that posted the
confession be established through that process and, so far as the law allows, made public.**
Until it is, every claim of foreign involvement in these chats &mdash; and every denial &mdash;
rests on a field somebody typed.

We are **not** asking anyone to identify, locate, contact or expose any private individual, and
readers should not attempt it. We name no one and we accuse no one. This is a request for a
record, not an allegation against a person.

## The Steam Friend List May Matter More

Discord is where the confession appeared. **Steam is where the friendships actually lived.**
A server can hold hundreds of people who have never spoken to each other; a **friend list is
chosen one person at a time**, and the people on it are the ones you spend hundreds of hours in
voice chat with, night after night, for years. If anyone in Robinson's online life knew him well
enough to matter, they are likelier to be on that list than on a server roster.

The two circles overlap directly: visitors to the Steam profile posted their **Discord handles**
on its public comment wall. And Steam stores exactly what Discord stores &mdash; **the IP address
each session came from** &mdash; no matter what country a profile claims.
