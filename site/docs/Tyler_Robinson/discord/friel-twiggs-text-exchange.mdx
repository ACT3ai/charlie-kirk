This is the opening of the longest sustained critique of the message evidence, and it is where the
Discord-vs-SMS confusion is first pinned down.

## The excerpt

> "**Lance Twiggs and his Lover's Texts.** Lance Twiggs was said to be the boyfriend of Tyler
> Robinson, and the recipient of an incriminating text exchange sent on the evening on which
> Charlie Kirk was killed, continuing into the next day when Robinson 'willingly' turned himself
> in. According to official search warrants, Twiggs was then interviewed in the early hours of the
> following morning (Friday, 12th September, around 1 am) in which he showed images of that text
> exchange (**described as being on the Discord platform in the PCA**). The text exchange was
> partially disclosed in the indictment/information document and forms the basis for the
> 'official' line on what Twiggs knew about Robinson's deeds ... **Our view is that the text
> exchange was fabricated, and fabricated in stages**, so that initially only that part of the
> text exchange detailing the Wednesday events (the rifle retrieval) had been thought up when the
> FBI saw Twiggs, with the consequence that the official line on Twiggs must be false."

## The platform confusion, stated plainly

The parenthesis is the load-bearing clause: an exchange that the search warrants present as
**text messages** is **described as Discord in the probable-cause affidavit**. Note what that
mislabel costs: the only material the record actually ties to a Discord account &mdash; the
account **zealous_monkey_55095** &mdash; is the confession post, not the rifle-retrieval
exchange the affidavit was describing. That is the same
error Discord itself later identified from the other direction, when it told TMZ the planning
messages
[were not Discord messages](./discord-denies-planning-messages).

Two independent sources &mdash; a hostile analyst and the platform's own review &mdash; land on
the same conclusion about the same mislabeling.

## The sequencing claim, in Friel's own framing

Friel's argument is a sequencing argument. Its structure:

1. At the **1:00 AM interview** on September 12, only the Wednesday rifle-retrieval material
   existed.
2. The **keyboard-note framing** ("drop what you are doing, look under my keyboard") was added
   later, because the exchange reads more naturally beginning *"I am still ok my love..."* &mdash;
   a line that presumes Twiggs already knows something is wrong.
3. The **private surrender text** ("one of my neighbors here is a deputy for the sheriff") surfaced
   later still, in the indictment, and is absent from the earlier warrant &mdash; see
   [Two Different Surrender Messages](./two-surrender-messages).

Supporting oddities Friel raises: Twiggs asks *"you weren't the one who did it right????"* even
though he has supposedly just read a note saying exactly that; and Twiggs has to **ask about the
motive** and appears to have **forgotten the bullet engravings** that the same exchange says
Robinson had been doing.

## What this is and is not

This is a **textual and sequencing argument** built from published documents, and it is one
private commentator's opinion. It is not a court finding and this site does not adopt it.

Stated as plainly as we can: **nothing on this page is an accusation that anyone fabricated
evidence.** It is not an accusation against [Lance Twiggs](/People/lance-twiggs), who has not
been charged with anything, was given use-immunity, and has given statements under oath. It is
not an accusation against the FBI, the Utah State Bureau of Investigation, the Utah County
Attorney's Office, or any investigator, agent, officer or prosecutor named or unnamed &mdash; no
court has found that any of them did anything improper, none has been charged with or accused by
any authority of misconduct in this case, and every one of them is entitled to the presumption
that they acted properly.

The word "fabricated" in the block quotation above is **Friel's word**, reproduced so readers can
judge his argument for themselves. Documents can be disclosed in stages for entirely ordinary
reasons &mdash; investigations develop, material is reviewed for redaction, and filings are made
when the rules require them rather than when a reader would find them convenient. That innocent
explanation fits the same facts. We record the argument with attribution, and the state's position
stands opposed to it.

## The Location Field Is Typed In. The IP Address Is Not.

On Discord, on Steam, and on every platform in this section, the country shown on a profile is
**a text box the user filled in**. Anyone can type Utah, or Egypt, or nothing at all, and change
it again in seconds. Whether it is shown publicly is a setting. It is not evidence of where a
person was sitting when they typed a message.

What is **not** typed in is the **IP address**. Every session, every login, every message send is
written to the platform's own logs with the address it arrived from, alongside device
fingerprints and session timing. Discord holds that record for the servers named in the Utah State
Bureau of Investigation's warrant &mdash; **Craftopia**, **Gamin** and **The Corruption** &mdash;
and for the account that posted the confession.

That is the question this section keeps returning to: **is it known where the people in these
servers actually were?** The FBI reportedly examined twenty or more people in Robinson's Discord
circle and charged none of them, and nothing in the public record states where any of them were.
To be explicit, because it matters: **being in another country is not wrongdoing**, no one in
those servers has been accused by any authority of anything, and nothing on this site suggests
any of them did anything improper. The point is narrower &mdash; that a location nobody has
verified should not be treated as if it were verified, in either direction. Self-reported locations circulated widely &mdash; see [the Egypt claim on a Steam profile](/Tyler_Robinson/steam/steam-ankha-egypt-profile) &mdash; and self-reported locations are worthless.

The answer exists. It is in Discord's subpoena return and in the login-IP history for
September 10&ndash;12 &mdash; records that investigators can obtain through lawful process and
the public cannot. **What we are asking for is that the record for the account that posted the
confession be established through that process and, so far as the law allows, made public.**
Until it is, every claim of foreign involvement in these chats &mdash; and every denial &mdash;
rests on a field somebody typed.

We are **not** asking anyone to identify, locate, contact or expose any private individual, and
readers should not attempt it. We name no one and we accuse no one. This is a request for a
record, not an allegation against a person.

## The Steam Friend List May Matter More

Discord is where the confession appeared. **Steam is where the friendships actually lived.**
A server can hold hundreds of people who have never spoken to each other; a **friend list is
chosen one person at a time**, and the people on it are the ones you spend hundreds of hours in
voice chat with, night after night, for years. If anyone in Robinson's online life knew him well
enough to matter, they are likelier to be on that list than on a server roster.

The two circles overlap directly: visitors to the Steam profile posted their **Discord handles**
on its public comment wall. And Steam stores exactly what Discord stores &mdash; **the IP address
each session came from** &mdash; no matter what country a profile claims.
