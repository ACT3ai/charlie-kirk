This is the opening of the longest sustained critique of the message evidence, and it is where the
Discord-vs-SMS confusion is first pinned down.

## The excerpt

> "**Lance Twiggs and his Lover's Texts.** Lance Twiggs was said to be the boyfriend of Tyler
> Robinson, and the recipient of an incriminating text exchange sent on the evening on which
> Charlie Kirk was killed, continuing into the next day when Robinson 'willingly' turned himself
> in. According to official search warrants, Twiggs was then interviewed in the early hours of the
> following morning (Friday, 12th September, around 1 am) in which he showed images of that text
> exchange (**described as being on the Discord platform in the PCA**). The text exchange was
> partially disclosed in the indictment/information document and forms the basis for the
> 'official' line on what Twiggs knew about Robinson's deeds ... **Our view is that the text
> exchange was fabricated, and fabricated in stages**, so that initially only that part of the
> text exchange detailing the Wednesday events (the rifle retrieval) had been thought up when the
> FBI saw Twiggs, with the consequence that the official line on Twiggs must be false."

## The platform confusion, stated plainly

The parenthesis is the load-bearing clause: an exchange that the search warrants present as
**text messages** is **described as Discord in the probable-cause affidavit**. That is the same
error Discord itself later identified from the other direction, when it told TMZ the planning
messages
[were not Discord messages](./discord-denies-planning-messages).

Two independent sources &mdash; a hostile analyst and the platform's own review &mdash; land on
the same conclusion about the same mislabeling.

## The "fabricated in stages" claim

Friel's argument is a sequencing argument. Its structure:

1. At the **1:00 AM interview** on September 12, only the Wednesday rifle-retrieval material
   existed.
2. The **keyboard-note framing** ("drop what you are doing, look under my keyboard") was added
   later, because the exchange reads more naturally beginning *"I am still ok my love..."* &mdash;
   a line that presumes Twiggs already knows something is wrong.
3. The **private surrender text** ("one of my neighbors here is a deputy for the sheriff") surfaced
   later still, in the indictment, and is absent from the earlier warrant &mdash; see
   [Two Different Surrender Messages](./two-surrender-messages).

Supporting oddities Friel raises: Twiggs asks *"you weren't the one who did it right????"* even
though he has supposedly just read a note saying exactly that; and Twiggs has to **ask about the
motive** and appears to have **forgotten the bullet engravings** that the same exchange says
Robinson had been doing.

## What this is and is not

This is a **textual and sequencing argument** built from published documents. It is not a court
finding, and it does not accuse [Lance Twiggs](/People/lance-twiggs) of any crime &mdash; Twiggs
has not been charged, was given use-immunity, and has given statements under oath. We record the
argument with attribution and note the state's position stands opposed to it.
