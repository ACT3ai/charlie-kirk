than the framing suggests.

**@journoaus (quoted), Nov 6:**

> "The Charlie Kirk case gets a new twist with **Phil Lyman commenting on X 2 weeks before Charlie
> Kirks assassination** on a post detailing CIA **Alice in Wonderland MK Ultra style conditioning**
> through means including **Discord groups**. 'You've now got the blueprint for a radicalised...'"

The capture also shows the profile card for **Just Phil Lyman** (@phil_lyman) &mdash; *"Done with
being lied to,"* Blanding UT, joined March 2013, 22.1K followers &mdash; and a graphic captioned
**"TWO WEEKS BEFORE CHARLIE KIRK ASSASSINATION &mdash; Alice in Wonderland Psy-Ops in Your Social
Feed."**

## What this does and does not show

**It shows:** a Utah political figure engaged publicly with a post about online radicalization
techniques, including Discord, about two weeks before September 10. That is a checkable fact about
a public post.

**It does not show:** foreknowledge, involvement, or any connection to Tyler Robinson. Commenting
on a widely shared post about social-media manipulation is something thousands of accounts did. If
the post was popular, engagement two weeks before any given date is close to statistically
inevitable.

We state this plainly because [Phil Lyman](/distraction_people/Phil_Lyman) is a **living person** and no
allegation of wrongdoing against him is supported by this material. Nothing here suggests he knew
anything about the events of September 10.

## The part that is worth keeping

The underlying subject &mdash; whether Discord communities are used as radicalization vectors, and
by whom &mdash; is a serious question independent of who commented on it. The state's own theory of
this case is that a young man was radicalized online. If that is true, the mechanism matters.

We cover the substantive version of that question at
[MKUltra & Digital Radicalization](/Gov_Mind_Control/mkultra-program) and
[Mind Control (Tyler?)](/Consciousness_Control/overview), where the argument stands on its own
without needing a coincidence of timing to prop it up.

## The post he commented on, in full

The framing above turns on a coincidence of timing. The post itself is worth reading on its own,
because the mechanism it describes is the same mechanism the state's own theory of this case
depends on. Its central claim:

> "You've now got the blueprint for a **radicalised sleeper army, built in plain sight**.
> Individuals believe they're acting on their own convictions, but in reality they're following a
> narrative carefully injected into their psyche."

Its stated method is the CIA's **"Alice in Wonderland technique"** &mdash; an interrogation method
the post traces to L. Ron Hubbard and then to the original MK Ultra programs. The objective, as
the post describes it, is to overwhelm a subject with contradictions, chaos and fear until their
sense of reality collapses, at which point the brain seizes on the first coherent narrative
offered and adopts it as truth.

Applied to a phone rather than a cell, the post lays out three stages:

1. **Algorithmic chaos** &mdash; a feed flooded with conflicting information and outrage, where
   the goal is disorientation rather than clarity.
2. **Behavioural analytics** &mdash; every click, scroll and pause logged, until the model knows
   what destabilises that specific person.
3. **Narrative injection** &mdash; after the noise, a clean story or ideology arrives and feels
   like relief, and repetition plus peer reinforcement locks it in.

## The claim inside it that is checkable

One assertion in the post is not theory and can be looked up:

> "The **Buffalo** mass shooter and the **Uvalde** mass shooter were both members of the **same
> Discord group**. That group was reportedly run by a **former FBI agent**."

We reproduce it as stated and flag it as **unverified here**. We have not confirmed it, it names
no individual, and readers should not treat it as established. If it were confirmed it would be
the most consequential line in the thread; if it is wrong, the thread's central historical example
collapses. Either way it has a factual answer, unlike a coincidence of timing &mdash; and it is
the part of the thread nobody arguing about the comment has gone and checked.

## Why the compilation singled him out

The compilation's point is not merely that he commented, but that he was reportedly **one of only
37 accounts to comment** on the post. The thread closes by invoking a line attributed to Erika
Kirk, *"There are no coincidences."*

**We do not adopt that reasoning, and we think it is wrong.** A person who comments on a post
about social-media manipulation is demonstrating an interest in social-media manipulation, which
is what public commentary is for. A comment count is not evidence.
[Phil Lyman](/distraction_people/Phil_Lyman) is a living person. Nothing here connects him to
Tyler Robinson, to September 10, or to any wrongdoing of any kind; he is not accused by anyone of
anything, and no inference adverse to him is supported by this material or drawn by this site.
The page exists so that readers who encounter the claim elsewhere find it answered rather than
amplified.

## The Location Field Is Typed In. The IP Address Is Not.

On Discord, on Steam, and on every platform in this section, the country shown on a profile is
**a text box the user filled in**. Anyone can type Utah, or Egypt, or nothing at all, and change
it again in seconds. Whether it is shown publicly is a setting. It is not evidence of where a
person was sitting when they typed a message.

What is **not** typed in is the **IP address**. Every session, every login, every message send is
written to the platform's own logs with the address it arrived from, alongside device
fingerprints and session timing. Discord holds that record for the servers named in the Utah State
Bureau of Investigation's warrant &mdash; **Craftopia**, **Gamin** and **The Corruption** &mdash;
and for the account that posted the confession.

That is the question this section keeps returning to: **is it known where the people in these
servers actually were?** The FBI reportedly examined twenty or more people in Robinson's Discord
circle and charged none of them, and nothing in the public record states where any of them were.
To be explicit, because it matters: **being in another country is not wrongdoing**, no one in
those servers has been accused by any authority of anything, and nothing on this site suggests
any of them did anything improper. The point is narrower &mdash; that a location nobody has
verified should not be treated as if it were verified, in either direction. Self-reported locations circulated widely &mdash; see [the Egypt claim on a Steam profile](/Tyler_Robinson/steam/steam-ankha-egypt-profile) &mdash; and self-reported locations are worthless.

The answer exists. It is in Discord's subpoena return and in the login-IP history for
September 10&ndash;12 &mdash; records that investigators can obtain through lawful process and
the public cannot. **What we are asking for is that the record for the account that posted the
confession be established through that process and, so far as the law allows, made public.**
Until it is, every claim of foreign involvement in these chats &mdash; and every denial &mdash;
rests on a field somebody typed.

We are **not** asking anyone to identify, locate, contact or expose any private individual, and
readers should not attempt it. We name no one and we accuse no one. This is a request for a
record, not an allegation against a person.

## The Steam Friend List May Matter More

Discord is where the confession appeared. **Steam is where the friendships actually lived.**
A server can hold hundreds of people who have never spoken to each other; a **friend list is
chosen one person at a time**, and the people on it are the ones you spend hundreds of hours in
voice chat with, night after night, for years. If anyone in Robinson's online life knew him well
enough to matter, they are likelier to be on that list than on a server roster.

The two circles overlap directly: visitors to the Steam profile posted their **Discord handles**
on its public comment wall. And Steam stores exactly what Discord stores &mdash; **the IP address
each session came from** &mdash; no matter what country a profile claims.
