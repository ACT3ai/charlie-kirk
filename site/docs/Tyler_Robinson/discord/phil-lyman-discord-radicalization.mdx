A timing observation that circulated widely. We set out what it actually establishes, which is less
than the framing suggests.

## The posts

**@ProjectConstitu:**

> "Hmm. Pretty Weird that @phil_lyman commented on an X post about **CIA radicalizing kids using
> discord and CIA Voice to skull technology 2 weeks before Charlie Kirk was assassinated** by
> someone allegedly radicalized in discord and myself and @stevendenoon believe they may have used..."

**@journoaus (quoted), Nov 6:**

> "The Charlie Kirk case gets a new twist with **Phil Lyman commenting on X 2 weeks before Charlie
> Kirks assassination** on a post detailing CIA **Alice in Wonderland MK Ultra style conditioning**
> through means including **Discord groups**. 'You've now got the blueprint for a radicalised...'"

The capture also shows the profile card for **Just Phil Lyman** (@phil_lyman) &mdash; *"Done with
being lied to,"* Blanding UT, joined March 2013, 22.1K followers &mdash; and a graphic captioned
**"TWO WEEKS BEFORE CHARLIE KIRK ASSASSINATION &mdash; Alice in Wonderland Psy-Ops in Your Social
Feed."**

## What this does and does not show

**It shows:** a Utah political figure engaged publicly with a post about online radicalization
techniques, including Discord, about two weeks before September 10. That is a checkable fact about
a public post.

**It does not show:** foreknowledge, involvement, or any connection to Tyler Robinson. Commenting
on a widely shared post about social-media manipulation is something thousands of accounts did. If
the post was popular, engagement two weeks before any given date is close to statistically
inevitable.

We state this plainly because [Phil Lyman](/distraction_people/Phil_Lyman) is a **living person** and no
allegation of wrongdoing against him is supported by this material. Nothing here suggests he knew
anything about the events of September 10.

## The part that is worth keeping

The underlying subject &mdash; whether Discord communities are used as radicalization vectors, and
by whom &mdash; is a serious question independent of who commented on it. The state's own theory of
this case is that a young man was radicalized online. If that is true, the mechanism matters.

We cover the substantive version of that question at
[MKUltra & Digital Radicalization](/Gov_Mind_Control/mkultra-program) and
[Mind Control (Tyler?)](/Consciousness_Control/overview), where the argument stands on its own
without needing a coincidence of timing to prop it up.
