Two official documents describe the same moment &mdash; Robinson announcing he was giving himself
up &mdash; and they quote **two entirely different sentences**.

## Version A: the indictment

> "Im gonna turn myself in willingly, one of my neighbors here is a deputy for the sheriff."

This is a private text to Lance Twiggs.

## Version B: the search warrant's Discord post

> "Hey guys, I have bad news for you all. It was me at UVU yesterday. [i]m sorry for all of this.
> [i]m surrendering through a sheriff friend in a few moments. [thanks for all the good times and
> laughs[, you've all been so amazing. Thank you all for everything.]"

The bracketed characters in the excerpt mark where the warrant's text departs from the screenshot.

## The editing observation

> "he author of the search warrant, **Justin Bowen**, has used **upper case** at times where the
> Discord post had had **lower case**, and has **omitted the last few words** included above."

That is a real, checkable difference. The screenshot is in all-lowercase sentence starts &mdash;
*"im sorry," "im surrendering," "thanks for all"* &mdash; and the warrant renders them
capitalised, then stops short of the closing words. Retyping rather than quoting is not
misconduct, but it means the warrant's version of the confession is **a transcription, not the
artifact**, and small transcription choices are exactly what a defense expert would probe.

## The part the warrant does not contain

The analysis makes a further point:

> "the search warrant seems to know nothing about the **personal surrender note to Lance himself**,
> only the post written for everyone (and which contains the word 'all' **five times!**)."

So Version A &mdash; the private "one of my neighbors here is a deputy for the sheriff" line
&mdash; appears in the **indictment** but not in the earlier **warrant**. The sequencing argument
that follows is that the public Discord post existed first, and the private surrender text
surfaced afterwards.

## The deputy detail

> "It's interesting that only here do we have any notion that the sheriff friend
> ([Mike Mitchell](/People/mike-mitchell)) was a (former) deputy ... that piece of information
> (that the facilitator was a deputy) had **never been placed in the public domain prior to the
> 15th** (with the indictment) ... and it was indeed Mitchell, confirmed by the search warrants,
> was only revealed by [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens) around the 17th or so. To underline
> this point, **the PCA knows nothing about any deputy**."

Three documents, three states of knowledge about the same intermediary, arriving in an order that
the analysis reads as accretion rather than discovery. We present it as a contested reading of the
document sequence, not as a proven fabrication.
