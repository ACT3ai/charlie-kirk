A small vocabulary difference that the analysis argues is not small.

## The observation

> "Again, and perhaps a minor point, while the indictment has Tyler **'turning himself in,'** the
> Discord post has **'surrendering.'** More significant, in our view, is the awkwardness of the
> **'willing' surrender**. It is unnatural for anyone to use such a phrase speaking in the first
> person, but makes a lot of sense when we enter the mentality of the fabricator who has at one
> point Tyler wanting to go out on his own terms."

## Why "willingly" is the odd word

Nobody narrating their own surrender says *"I'm going to turn myself in **willingly**."* The
adverb has no function for the speaker &mdash; of course it is willing, he is the one doing it.
The word does work for a **third party** describing the event afterwards, where "willingly" is
doing legal and narrative labour: it forecloses the alternative that he was already in custody.

That alternative is exactly what the Bates-stamped Miranda footage raises. See
[Bates 003996-R2 &mdash; Miranda at 6:25 vs. the Discord Clock](./bates-003996-miranda-vs-discord).

## The "own terms" thread

The same excerpt points to the other half of the message set, in which Robinson allegedly wrote
that he would be *"going out on my own terms"* and had *"no intention of being drug through the
courts in front of the country"* &mdash; see
[Going Out On My Own Terms](./warrant-twiggs-texts-own-terms).

The excerpt cites a post by **Sam Parker** on X reading:

> "OMGOSH: The original plan was for Tyler Robinson to be k*lled in a shootout! Just as we've
> suspected."

That is an X commentator's inference from the released text messages, not a finding by anyone with
subpoena power. We record it with attribution.

## The internal contradiction it points at

Read the two message sets together and the same author, within roughly a day, is said to have:

* wanted to keep the secret *"till I died of old age,"*
* wanted to *"go out on my own terms"* rather than face court,
* and then **surrendered willingly** through a friend so he could face court.

A single coherent author producing all three in sequence is possible &mdash; people in crisis are
not consistent. But the analysis argues the pattern reads more like separately drafted material
than like one person's collapsing plan. We present it as a textual argument, not a proven one.
