The Discord confession attributed to [Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson) is timestamped
**7:57 PM on September 11, 2025**. The defense filing quoting discovery exhibit
**Bates 003996-R2** places him Mirandized on video at **6:25 PM the same evening**. Those two
clocks have never been reconciled.

What this page adds is the part that had been missing: **the state's own witness, on the record,
putting September 11 as the day Robinson turned himself in** - and the underlying filing page
itself, rather than a paraphrase of it.

## What the witness said

The clip is short enough to quote in full. It is cross-examination about the Cellebrite
extraction of Lance Twiggs' phone:

> **Q.** We mentioned a Cellebrite extraction of Mr. Twiggs' phone. Do you know what time that
> last text was sent?
>
> **A.** Yeah, I recall it was just before 8 p.m. on September 11th. It was, I think, 7:42 PM is
> my recollection.
>
> **Q.** I'm sorry, September 11?
>
> **A.** Yes.
>
> **Q.** Um, when September 12th &mdash; make more sense, or you're saying September 11th?
>
> **A.** So September 11th.
>
> **Q.** Was when he turned himself in, September 11th?
>
> **A.** Yep.
>
> **Q.** Okay, thank you.

Two separate things come out of those thirty-five seconds.

**First**, the last message on the phone is placed at roughly **7:42 PM on September 11** &mdash;
inside the window the official account says Robinson was still at large and had not yet posted the
confession.

**Second**, and more directly, the witness was handed an explicit opportunity to move the date to
**September 12** and declined it, then agreed that September 11 "was when he turned himself in."

We are describing what a recording of a public hearing appears to show. We are **not** stating that
any witness gave false testimony. A witness can misremember a date under questioning; recollection
offered from memory in a hearing is not a document, and the transcript, not this clip, is the record.
@JohPersso's post asks "Did the agent lie under oath?" &mdash; that is the poster's question, framed
as a question, and nothing here answers it. What can be said is narrower and stronger: **on this
point the state's witness and the defense filing agree with each other, and both disagree with the
7:57 PM confession.**

The site has carried the 6:25 PM figure for some time, but as an explainer's paraphrase. Both
underlying pages are now captured.

**The index.** Row 15 of the discovery exhibit list reads **"Video at WCSO on 9/11/2025"**, Bates
number **3996-R1**, disclosed **11/25/2025**. WCSO is the Washington County Sheriff's Office. The
exhibit's existence, and its September 11 date, are printed on a numbered index page produced in
discovery &mdash; not asserted by a commentator. The rows immediately below it are the ones this
whole section turns on: **16** the recorded 1102 statement of Lance Twiggs, **16.1** "Text messages
between TR and LT", **16.2** "Discord chats", **16.3** "Photograph of note".

**The filing.** Page 70 quotes the companion exhibit **003996-R2** at internal video timestamp
**03:36:53**, and reproduces the exchange:

> **06:25 PM / 03:36:53** &mdash; "So Tyler, um, before we begin, I'm just gonna read you your
> rights. &hellip; You have the right to remain silent. &hellip; So are you, are you good to
> proceed? I'd love to talk to you."
>
> **06:26 PM / 03:37:38** &mdash; "Uh, I would like to speak with my, uh, legal counsel. I'm hoping
> for a fellow in Utah named [Doug Terry](/People/doug-terry)."
>
> **06:26 PM / 03:37:39** &mdash; "Uh, told he's a pretty good, uh, attorney here, but I, I think my
> folks are trying to get ahold of him, but I think **his office is closed for the night**."
>
> **06:26 PM / 03:37:52** &mdash; "But, uh, until I've had the opportunity to speak with someone, I
> would like to remain silent."

The same page then makes the defense's own point about what followed: that officials who told the
media Robinson "was refusing to cooperate" were describing a man exercising a Fifth and Sixth
Amendment right, and that no adverse inference should be drawn from that silence.

The quoted explainer adds a third timestamp, read into the record by defense counsel at the same
hearing. From the transcript of the clip:

> "The nearest timestamp on this text message is on page 10, which says **yesterday at 5:55 p.m.**
> If the court looks at the text messages that come just before it, what the court sees is somebody
> who the state alleges is Mr. Robinson saying to Mr. Twiggs, quote, **'I'm going to turn myself in
> willingly, one of my neighbors here is a deputy for the sheriff.'**"

Counsel's argument from there is a sequencing one. By that point in the state's own account, the
person it says is Robinson had left Orem, driven back to St. George, spoken with his father, and
arranged through **Mike Mitchell's** 1102 statement for Mitchell to help him surrender to the
Washington County Sheriff.

> "By 5:55 p.m., according to these messages and according to Mike Mitchell's 1102, he and Tyler
> Robinson had already worked out the details of turning Tyler in to the Washington County
> Sheriff's Office. So what are we supposed to believe &mdash; that they then waited for several
> hours for Tyler to then confess to the Discord chat before turning himself in? Or did he actually
> turn himself in around 6:25 p.m., like the Bates documents said."

## The evening, as three independent records now describe it

| Time, September 11, 2025 | What the record says | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **~5:55 PM** | Text: "I'm going to turn myself in willingly, one of my neighbors here is a deputy for the sheriff" | Read into the record at the pretrial hearing, page 10 of the exhibit |
| **~5:55 PM or earlier** | Surrender already arranged with Mike Mitchell, after a call with Robinson's father | Mitchell's 1102 statement, per preliminary-hearing testimony |
| **6:25 PM** | Mirandized on video at WCSO; invokes counsel one minute later | Defense filing p.70, quoting Bates 003996-R2 at 03:36:53 |
| **~7:42 PM** | Last text off Lance Twiggs' phone | State witness, under cross-examination |
| **7:50 PM** | State press conference | Contemporaneous reporting |
| **7:57 PM** | Discord confession timestamp (8:57 PM on the screenshot's own clock) | Charging documents and reporting |
| 09/12, **04:00** | Arrest date and time stamped on the probable-cause affidavit | [Affidavit](/Timeline/Tyler_Robinson/Affidavit_Probable_Cause) |

Read downward, the first four rows describe a surrender that was planned, executed, and recorded
before the confession was posted. The last three describe a manhunt that was still running.

## What this does and does not establish

**It does** establish that the September 11 turn-in is no longer only a citizen-investigator
reading of a leaked exhibit. It is now supported by a numbered discovery index, a filed defense
brief quoting the video verbatim, and sworn testimony from the state's side that names the same
date.

**It does not** establish who typed the Discord message, and nothing here should be read as an
accusation against any named person. Two ordinary explanations remain live and are recorded on
[the Bates 003996-R2 page](./bates-003996-miranda-vs-discord): the 6:25 PM reading could be a
misread frame or an unsynced internal video clock, or someone other than Robinson could have had
access to the account &mdash; which would undo the confession as a confession by a different route.

**The single record that would settle it** has still not been produced: Discord's own login-IP
history for the account that posted the message on September 11. That is obtainable by
investigators through lawful process and by nobody else. Until it is produced and, so far as the
law allows, made public, the two clocks stay unreconciled.
