This capture is not evidence of the shooting. It is evidence of **how the confession entered the
public record**, and it is useful precisely because it lays the chain out in one place. It
reproduces the same chat as the
[confession screenshot](./confession-screenshot-zealous-monkey), then names the four routes by
which that image became public.

## The four routes it names

**1. Court documents and unsealed search warrants.** Prosecutors put the Discord messages into
charging documents and warrants. The capture states plainly that *"Robinson's roommate showed
investigators the chats on his screen and allowed photos to be taken as evidence"* &mdash; which
matches the probable-cause affidavit. See
[The Affidavit: Photographing a Roommate's Screen](./pca-affidavit-roommate-screen-photos).

**2. Mainstream reporting.** The Washington Post, CNN, the New York Times, the BBC, ABC News and
Deseret News all ran the message, sourcing it to law enforcement, court records, and screenshots
they reviewed.

**3. FBI Director Kash Patel's congressional testimony**, in which the Discord confession was
confirmed.

**4. Ken Klippenstein's Substack**, September 16, 2025 &mdash; *"Exclusive: Leaked Messages from
Charlie Kirk Assassin."* The capture states he obtained the chat logs **directly from a source
connected to the group** and also interviewed one of Robinson's childhood friends, and that
**his article is the primary public source of the actual chat images**.

All four routes carry the same artifact: the post made under the Discord username
**zealous_monkey_55095**. Route 4 is where the image of it came from, and routes 1 through 3 are
downstream of route 4 &mdash; which is the whole problem this page sets out.

## Why point 4 matters more than the other three

Read the four together and a circle appears. Klippenstein published the screenshots. The outlets
in point 2 cite "screenshots they reviewed." The images referenced in the court documents match
Klippenstein's. So the same underlying artifact &mdash; a photograph of a friend's screen, handed
over by an anonymous source in the group &mdash; is doing the work in all four places while
appearing to be four independent confirmations.

That is not an accusation against any journalist. It is a chain-of-custody observation: an item
does not become better authenticated by being republished.

## The timestamp discrepancy inside this very capture

The capture's own text says the confession is *"timestamped at 7:57 PM MDT."* The chat image
directly above that sentence reads **9/11/25, 8:57 PM**. The document contradicts its own
illustration and does not notice. Neither figure clears the
[6:25 PM Miranda problem](./bates-003996-miranda-vs-discord).

## What would settle it

* A native Discord export with message IDs, edit history and account tokens.
* Server-side login IP records for the account around 7:57&ndash;8:57 PM on September 11.
* The device-seizure log showing where Robinson's phone was at that hour.

None of those has been shown publicly. Until one is, the confession's provenance runs through a
single anonymous source in a private friend group.

## The archive leak and where it came from

A second sourcing chain runs parallel to the Klippenstein route and produced far more material,
with far less scrutiny. It did not come from the court file at all.

The source is described as **a close friend who gamed with Robinson and Lance Twiggs for roughly
six years**, holding archives going back to **2020**, who leaked raw Discord logs together with
photographs of the apartment. That material reached the public through two channels:

* **anonbirdd's Substack**, which published the write-up of the logs. [Jack Posobiec](/People/jack-posobiec)
  pointed audiences to it as a primary dump of alleged chat content.
* **@TurkeyTom on YouTube**, whose video breakdown carried it to a much larger audience, and which
  the circulating X threads were in several cases screen-recording rather than sourcing directly.

The distinction matters for this page. The confession screenshot has a documented, if unsatisfying,
route into the record: roommate to investigator to filing to Substack. The archive leak has no such
route. It is a private party publishing private group chat, and the reader has no way to check what
was included, what was left out, or whether the archive is complete &mdash; while the FBI, which
reportedly examined **twenty or more people** in the same circle, has published nothing at all.

The content of that archive is set out on
[Discord Friends, Servers and Handles](./discord_friends), with the same caution attached: it is a
leaker's account, not a document.

## The Location Field Is Typed In. The IP Address Is Not.

On Discord, on Steam, and on every platform in this section, the country shown on a profile is
**a text box the user filled in**. Anyone can type Utah, or Egypt, or nothing at all, and change
it again in seconds. Whether it is shown publicly is a setting. It is not evidence of where a
person was sitting when they typed a message.

What is **not** typed in is the **IP address**. Every session, every login, every message send is
written to the platform's own logs with the address it arrived from, alongside device
fingerprints and session timing. Discord holds that record for the servers named in the Utah State
Bureau of Investigation's warrant &mdash; **Craftopia**, **Gamin** and **The Corruption** &mdash;
and for the account that posted the confession.

That is the question this section keeps returning to: **is it known where the people in these
servers actually were?** The FBI reportedly examined twenty or more people in Robinson's Discord
circle and charged none of them, and nothing in the public record states where any of them were.
To be explicit, because it matters: **being in another country is not wrongdoing**, no one in
those servers has been accused by any authority of anything, and nothing on this site suggests
any of them did anything improper. The point is narrower &mdash; that a location nobody has
verified should not be treated as if it were verified, in either direction. Self-reported locations circulated widely &mdash; see [the Egypt claim on a Steam profile](/Tyler_Robinson/steam/steam-ankha-egypt-profile) &mdash; and self-reported locations are worthless.

The answer exists. It is in Discord's subpoena return and in the login-IP history for
September 10&ndash;12 &mdash; records that investigators can obtain through lawful process and
the public cannot. **What we are asking for is that the record for the account that posted the
confession be established through that process and, so far as the law allows, made public.**
Until it is, every claim of foreign involvement in these chats &mdash; and every denial &mdash;
rests on a field somebody typed.

We are **not** asking anyone to identify, locate, contact or expose any private individual, and
readers should not attempt it. We name no one and we accuse no one. This is a request for a
record, not an allegation against a person.

## The Steam Friend List May Matter More

Discord is where the confession appeared. **Steam is where the friendships actually lived.**
A server can hold hundreds of people who have never spoken to each other; a **friend list is
chosen one person at a time**, and the people on it are the ones you spend hundreds of hours in
voice chat with, night after night, for years. If anyone in Robinson's online life knew him well
enough to matter, they are likelier to be on that list than on a server roster.

The two circles overlap directly: visitors to the Steam profile posted their **Discord handles**
on its public comment wall. And Steam stores exactly what Discord stores &mdash; **the IP address
each session came from** &mdash; no matter what country a profile claims.
