The tone changes completely in this block, and it is the change that the "original plan was a
shootout" reading is built on.

## How the warrant frames it

> "Lance showed FBI Special Agent Lange **additional text messages** between Tyler Robinson and
> himself. The text messages were **photographed by investigators as Lance showed them the text
> messages**. The following is the text content of **one of the pictures** that investigators took."

Read that carefully: the warrant is transcribing **a photograph of a screen**, and says so.

## The exchange

| Speaker | Message |
|---|---|
| Tyler | "I'm sorry, but I think this might be it. If the way is clear I can try and come your way to say goodbye, after that I have a spot in mind that I'll call the police to" |
| Lance | "are there cops outside?" |
| Lance | "and why are you calling the police anywhere?" |
| Tyler | "not yet, but I have no doubts they will be on me within the hour. And I will be giving my location up and **going out on my own terms**. I have no intention of being **drug through the courts in front of the country**. Can I come see you? After that I can call my dad and see if he's willing to help me or not" |
| Lance | "im just at my parents rn, maybe I could make an excuse to head home for a bit" |
| Tyler | "you would need to do that right now" |
| Lance | "any ideas?" |

## Why commentators read this as a shootout plan

"Going out on my own terms," combined with "say goodbye," "this might be it," and calling police to
a chosen spot, reads to many commentators as a plan to die rather than to surrender. **Sam Parker**
posted on X that *"the original plan was for Tyler Robinson to be k*lled in a shootout."* That is
an X commentator's inference from these words, not a finding by any authority. We record it with
attribution.

## The contradiction it creates with the Discord post

Within hours, on the same account of events, the author allegedly:

* refuses to be *"drug through the courts in front of the country"*, then
* [surrenders "willingly"](./surrendering-vs-turning-himself-in) through a sheriff's friend, to
  face exactly those courts.

Analysts argue a single author does not usually reverse a life-or-death intention that cleanly in
that window. Others argue that people in acute crisis reverse themselves constantly, and that the
reversal is the most human thing in the whole record. Both readings are on the table; neither has
been tested in open court.

## Note on the "dad" line

The final Robinson line mentions calling his father to *"see if he's willing to help me or not."*
The family's own account of that night is disputed &mdash; see
[Family Denies Confession &mdash; Baron Coleman](/Tyler_Robinson/baron-coleman-family-denial).
