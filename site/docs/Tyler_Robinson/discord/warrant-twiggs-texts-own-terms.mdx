The tone changes completely in this block, and it is the change that the "original plan was a
shootout" reading is built on.

## How the warrant frames it

> "Lance showed FBI Special Agent Lange **additional text messages** between Tyler Robinson and
> himself. The text messages were **photographed by investigators as Lance showed them the text
> messages**. The following is the text content of **one of the pictures** that investigators took."

Read that carefully: the warrant is transcribing **a photograph of a screen**, and says so.

## The exchange

| Speaker | Message |
|---|---|
| Tyler | "I'm sorry, but I think this might be it. If the way is clear I can try and come your way to say goodbye, after that I have a spot in mind that I'll call the police to" |
| Lance | "are there cops outside?" |
| Lance | "and why are you calling the police anywhere?" |
| Tyler | "not yet, but I have no doubts they will be on me within the hour. And I will be giving my location up and **going out on my own terms**. I have no intention of being **drug through the courts in front of the country**. Can I come see you? After that I can call my dad and see if he's willing to help me or not" |
| Lance | "im just at my parents rn, maybe I could make an excuse to head home for a bit" |
| Tyler | "you would need to do that right now" |
| Lance | "any ideas?" |

## Why commentators read this as a shootout plan

"Going out on my own terms," combined with "say goodbye," "this might be it," and calling police to
a chosen spot, reads to many commentators as a plan to die rather than to surrender. **Sam Parker**
posted on X that *"the original plan was for Tyler Robinson to be k*lled in a shootout."* That is
an X commentator's inference from these words, not a finding by any authority. We record it with
attribution.

## The contradiction it creates with the Discord post

Within hours, on the same account of events, the author allegedly:

* refuses to be *"drug through the courts in front of the country"*, then
* [surrenders "willingly"](./surrendering-vs-turning-himself-in) through a sheriff's friend, to
  face exactly those courts.

Analysts argue a single author does not usually reverse a life-or-death intention that cleanly in
that window. Others argue that people in acute crisis reverse themselves constantly, and that the
reversal is the most human thing in the whole record. Both readings are on the table; neither has
been tested in open court.

## Note on the "dad" line

The final Robinson line mentions calling his father to *"see if he's willing to help me or not."*
The family's own account of that night is disputed &mdash; see
[Family Denies Confession &mdash; Baron Coleman](/Tyler_Robinson/baron-coleman-family-denial).

## The Location Field Is Typed In. The IP Address Is Not.

On Discord, on Steam, and on every platform in this section, the country shown on a profile is
**a text box the user filled in**. Anyone can type Utah, or Egypt, or nothing at all, and change
it again in seconds. Whether it is shown publicly is a setting. It is not evidence of where a
person was sitting when they typed a message.

What is **not** typed in is the **IP address**. Every session, every login, every message send is
written to the platform's own logs with the address it arrived from, alongside device
fingerprints and session timing. Discord holds that record for the servers named in the Utah State
Bureau of Investigation's warrant &mdash; **Craftopia**, **Gamin** and **The Corruption** &mdash;
and for the account that posted the confession.

That is the question this section keeps returning to: **were one or more of the people in these
servers outside the United States?** The FBI reportedly examined twenty or more people in
Robinson's Discord circle and charged none of them, and nothing in the public record states where
any of them actually were. Self-reported locations circulated widely &mdash; see [the Egypt claim on a Steam profile](/Tyler_Robinson/discord/steam-ankha-egypt-profile) &mdash; and self-reported locations are worthless.

The answer exists. It is in Discord's subpoena return, in the login-IP history for
September 10&ndash;12, and in the traffic records that US intelligence and federal investigators
can obtain and the public cannot. **We want that answer found and published.** Until it is, every
claim of foreign involvement in these chats &mdash; and every denial &mdash; rests on a field
somebody typed.

We name no one and we accuse no one. We are asking for the record that would settle it.

## The Steam Friend List May Matter More

Discord is where the confession appeared. **Steam is where the friendships actually lived.**
A server can hold hundreds of people who have never spoken to each other; a **friend list is
chosen one person at a time**, and the people on it are the ones you spend hundreds of hours in
voice chat with, night after night, for years. If anyone in Robinson's online life knew him well
enough to matter, they are likelier to be on that list than on a server roster.

The two circles overlap directly: visitors to the Steam profile posted their **Discord handles**
on its public comment wall. And Steam stores exactly what Discord stores &mdash; **the IP address
each session came from** &mdash; no matter what country a profile claims.
