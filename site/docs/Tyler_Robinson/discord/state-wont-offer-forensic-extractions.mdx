One sentence in a filing about scheduling, and it changes what the message evidence can be.

## The quote

> "With the exception of a photograph of a burnt note, doorbell surveillance from a single
> property, a DNA report regarding items found in (Robinson's) apartment, and a toolmarks report,
> each of the witnesses and exhibits has been known to the defense for at least six months before
> the preliminary hearing," prosecutors argue. "Most of the evidence to be presented at the
> preliminary hearing, including the DNA report regarding the rifle found at the scene, was
> provided to the defense in October of 2025. **The state does not intend to offer evidence
> obtained through forensic extractions of electronics seized in this case**."

## Why that last sentence matters

A forensic extraction &mdash; a Cellebrite-style image of a phone &mdash; is the only thing that
can turn a **photograph of a screen** into **authenticated data**. It produces message IDs,
database timestamps, deletion records, and a hash that proves the file was not altered afterwards.

Without it, the message evidence at the preliminary hearing rests on:

* photographs taken of [a roommate's screen](./pca-affidavit-roommate-screen-photos),
* a screenshot [republished from a Substack post](./confession-sourcing-klippenstein), and
* [Lance Twiggs' testimony](./twiggs-immunity-and-statements) about what he was shown.

That is witness recollection and pictures, not device data.

## Read it alongside Discord's position

Discord says the planning messages
[were not Discord messages](./discord-denies-planning-messages) &mdash; which pushes
authentication onto the phones. The state then says it will not offer the phone extractions.

The two statements together close both doors:

| Authentication route | Status |
|---|---|
| Discord server-side logs | Company says the content was not on its platform; account removed |
| Forensic phone extraction | State says it does not intend to offer it |
| Photograph of a witness's screen | This is what remains |

## The careful caveat

This is a filing about a **preliminary hearing**, which has a low evidentiary bar and is not the
trial. The state is entitled to hold evidence back at that stage and produce it later. Nothing
here proves extractions do not exist.

But the preliminary hearing is where probable cause is tested, and the state has said the device
data will not be there. Readers should verify the current docket, since positions in an active
case change.

## The filing sits against the bindover memorandum

There is a direct tension here that has not been publicly reconciled, and it is worth stating
plainly because it cuts both ways.

The **State's memorandum supporting bindover** cites the message exhibits &mdash; **16.1** (texts),
**16.2** (Discord), **16.3** (the note) &mdash; on nearly every page of its identity and
mental-state arguments. Its central summary sentence is built on them: *"Defendant also admitted
to Mr. Twiggs and other friends that he had shot Mr. Kirk. State's Exs. 16.1 at 1-2, 16.2 at 1-2,
16.3."*

A prosecution that says it will not offer forensic extractions, while resting a bindover
memorandum on message exhibits, is only consistent if those exhibits are authenticated some other
way. Three routes are available in principle:

* through the **Twiggs police interview transcript** (State's Ex. 16), where a participant in the
  conversations describes them under questioning;
* through **Twiggs' own device** rather than Robinson's, which is where the texts were recovered
  and which is the message set with the best chain of custody;
* through **testimony** rather than extraction &mdash; a witness saying *these are the messages*.

The memorandum does not say which. And each of those routes authenticates the **texts** far better
than it authenticates the **Discord posts**, because Twiggs was the other party to the texts and
was not the other party to a group post. The Discord exhibit is the one that most needs a
platform-side record, and it is the one furthest from having one.

## The Location Field Is Typed In. The IP Address Is Not.

On Discord, on Steam, and on every platform in this section, the country shown on a profile is
**a text box the user filled in**. Anyone can type Utah, or Egypt, or nothing at all, and change
it again in seconds. Whether it is shown publicly is a setting. It is not evidence of where a
person was sitting when they typed a message.

What is **not** typed in is the **IP address**. Every session, every login, every message send is
written to the platform's own logs with the address it arrived from, alongside device
fingerprints and session timing. Discord holds that record for the servers named in the Utah State
Bureau of Investigation's warrant &mdash; **Craftopia**, **Gamin** and **The Corruption** &mdash;
and for the account that posted the confession.

That is the question this section keeps returning to: **is it known where the people in these
servers actually were?** The FBI reportedly examined twenty or more people in Robinson's Discord
circle and charged none of them, and nothing in the public record states where any of them were.
To be explicit, because it matters: **being in another country is not wrongdoing**, no one in
those servers has been accused by any authority of anything, and nothing on this site suggests
any of them did anything improper. The point is narrower &mdash; that a location nobody has
verified should not be treated as if it were verified, in either direction. Self-reported locations circulated widely &mdash; see [the Egypt claim on a Steam profile](/Tyler_Robinson/steam/steam-ankha-egypt-profile) &mdash; and self-reported locations are worthless.

The answer exists. It is in Discord's subpoena return and in the login-IP history for
September 10&ndash;12 &mdash; records that investigators can obtain through lawful process and
the public cannot. **What we are asking for is that the record for the account that posted the
confession be established through that process and, so far as the law allows, made public.**
Until it is, every claim of foreign involvement in these chats &mdash; and every denial &mdash;
rests on a field somebody typed.

We are **not** asking anyone to identify, locate, contact or expose any private individual, and
readers should not attempt it. We name no one and we accuse no one. This is a request for a
record, not an allegation against a person.

## The Steam Friend List May Matter More

Discord is where the confession appeared. **Steam is where the friendships actually lived.**
A server can hold hundreds of people who have never spoken to each other; a **friend list is
chosen one person at a time**, and the people on it are the ones you spend hundreds of hours in
voice chat with, night after night, for years. If anyone in Robinson's online life knew him well
enough to matter, they are likelier to be on that list than on a server roster.

The two circles overlap directly: visitors to the Steam profile posted their **Discord handles**
on its public comment wall. And Steam stores exactly what Discord stores &mdash; **the IP address
each session came from** &mdash; no matter what country a profile claims.
