# Witnesses

Firsthand accounts from people who were at Utah Valley University on September 10, 2025, when [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) was shot. This section collects on-camera and on-record interviews from named or pseudonymous witnesses and indexes their specific testimony — what they saw, what they heard, and from which direction. Almost none of this testimony
appears in the state's prosecution of [Tyler Robinson](/Tyler_Robinson/overview), who is
charged rather than convicted, which is precisely why the witness record is gathered and
preserved here.

:::note Event witnesses vs. hearing witnesses
The accounts in this section are **interviews, not sworn testimony**. For people who took the stand
under oath in [State v. Robinson](/court/case-overview) — subject to cross-examination — see
**[Who Testified](/court/hearing-witnesses)** and **[Testimony](/court/testimony)**. The two carry
very different evidentiary weight, and conflating them is the most common error in public commentary.
:::

According to [@CharlotteLee_88](https://x.com/CharlotteLee_88/status/2069566053353463951),
one of the earliest on-camera witnesses first described Charlie Kirk being shot **in
the chest** — repeating the chest/heart description several times — then gave a
**neck-wound** account on a different station roughly an hour later. A separate
witness in the same lineup describes the neck from the start. The poster frames this
as a contradiction; this site records the accounts as-given and treats the apparent
shift as an open question, not an accusation. Witnesses under acute stress routinely
misremember and revise, and a chest-versus-neck distinction is easy to confuse in a
chaotic moment. The full clip, verbatim quotes, and the competing readings are laid
out on the dedicated page: [Shot in Chest, Then Neck](./Shot_in_Chest_Reversal). See
also the related [Wrong Witnesses](./wrong_witnesses) page.

## What this area covers

Witness testimony is the front line of any shooting investigation. This area gathers interviews with people who were at UVU during the event and who have spoken on the record about what they experienced — the sounds they heard, the direction of fire, the reactions of the crowd, and anything else that might inform whether the official single-shooter, single-rifle account is consistent with what was on the ground. Witnesses are presented one per page so each account stays self-contained and so a reader can compare them side by side.

## Key patterns to watch

Across the witness interviews collected here, several recurring observations stand out and bear comparison with the official narrative. According to several reported accounts, the sound profile most witnesses describe is closer to "pops," "fireworks," or smaller-calibre or pistol-like reports rather than the heavy crack of a 30-06 rifle (the calibre the alleged Mauser 98 would have produced). Reported descriptions of crowd reaction at the moment of the shot — including a group reportedly cheering near the top of the venue — are also documented here. Where witnesses describe the trajectory of fire, those descriptions are recorded as-stated and cross-referenced against the [Gun & Bullet](/Gun_Bullet/overview) and [Shooting Locations](/Suspects/Shooting_Locations) sections.

## How to use this section

Start with any single witness page to read that person's account verbatim and watch the embedded interview. Then compare across witness pages for points where multiple people independently report the same detail — those convergences carry more evidentiary weight than any single account. Where a witness account conflicts with the official report or with other witnesses, the page notes the discrepancy rather than reconciling it; reconciliation is left to the [Proof Not Tyler](/Proof_Not_Tyler/overview) and [Shooting Locations](/Suspects/Shooting_Locations) pages where multi-source synthesis happens.

## A witness who says he was advised to erase what he filmed

The pattern above concerns accounts that change. This one concerns an account that has stayed identical across every retelling — and what the witness says he was asked to do with his footage.

He filmed the event on his phone and sent it to the [FBI](/FBI/overview) as a tip, believing he was helping. He describes the contact escalating from a local field office to someone at the national level, and says that contact advised him to erase the video from his phone because the victim had been his friend and keeping it would only give him PTSD. A second call, he says, asked him to confirm he had erased it.

For readers working through witness material, this one is unusually easy to assess on its own terms. It is a single first-person account of two phone calls, told the same way each time it has surfaced, with no corroborating record produced by anyone. He says he did not delete the file, and that the confirmation call is what made him publish and back it up widely. The FBI has not publicly addressed it; ordinary welfare and evidence-handling explanations fit the same facts, and no wrongdoing by any individual is asserted here.

## Pattern Map Citizen Investigators Use

1. **Sound** — pop / fireworks language vs .30-06 crack expectations.
2. **Wound language** — early heart/chest vs later neck.
3. **Crowd control** — who silenced or redirected on-camera witnesses.
4. **Trajectory** — left/right disagreements vs Losee geometry.
