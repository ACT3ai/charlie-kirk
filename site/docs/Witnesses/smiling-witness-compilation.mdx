# The Smiling Witness Compilation

:::caution These are private individuals
The people in this compilation are private individuals interviewed by local and national news crews at
Utah Valley University on September 10, 2025. **They are not named here**, nothing on this page accuses any
of them of anything, and their interviews are **not sworn testimony**. A facial expression on camera is an
observation, not evidence of what anyone knew or intended.
:::

A 73-second compilation posted to X on **September 13, 2026** by
[@CharlotteLee_88](/Influencers/x/charlottelee-88) strings together television interviews with witnesses
at UVU on the day [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) was shot. Across the clips, **several witnesses have
the appearance of smiling** — some broadly — while describing the shooting, the blood, and the panic in the
crowd.

## What @CharlotteLee_88 posted

According to [@CharlotteLee_88 on X](https://x.com/CharlotteLee_88/status/2098970186166534186), 2026-09-13:

> The Charlie Kirk assassination smiling witness compilation.

Every clip carries the banner **"The Smiling Witness Compilation"** across the top of the frame. The post
makes no further argument in words; the edit itself is the argument.

## What the video shows

The compilation cuts between roughly half a dozen interviews, most of them carrying local news chyrons —
"Charlie Kirk shot and killed on UVU campus," "President Trump reports Charlie Kirk is dead," "Charlie Kirk
killed in UVU shooting," "Shooting at Charlie Kirk event." The footage includes a young woman interviewed in
a car, two women standing together outdoors, a young woman in a parking lot, a man on a remote video call
with a news anchor, a young woman on a campus sidewalk, and several students interviewed together in front
of a brick archway.

**The appearance of smiling is the thread that ties the clips together.** Several of the witnesses have the
appearance of smiling, grinning, or laughing lightly while they speak about what they saw — including while
describing the shot hitting him and the blood that followed. In other moments the same witnesses look
serious or shaken. The edit selects and places the smiling moments one after another.

## What the witnesses say

From the machine transcript of the compilation's audio:

> It was pretty terrifying. Probably the scariest thing I've ever been a part of.

> I figured a shot would be a little bit louder, but I just saw it hit him in the chest, and then, all of a
> sudden, just tons of blood gushing right outwards.

> As the gun went off, we both hit the floor, started praying.

> It was a pretty traumatic experience. But we've had friends around all day, which has really been helpful,
> but we're definitely getting flashbacks every once in a while from it.

Note the second quote: one witness describes the shot hitting Charlie Kirk **in the chest**, with blood
"gushing right outwards." That matches the chest-and-heart accounts collected on
["It Hit His Chest" — Second-Row Accounts](/Witnesses/hit-his-chest-blood-squirt) and
[Shot to the Heart](/Witnesses/shot-to-the-heart), and conflicts with the neck wound in the state's account.
See [Witness Account Conflicts — Chest vs Neck](/Medical/Witness_Account_Conflicts).

## Why the smiling draws attention

Citizen investigators on X have pointed to the smiling as one more strange detail in the first-day witness
record — alongside the repeated chest-wound descriptions and the fact that
[none of these witnesses has been called to testify](/Witnesses/wrong_witnesses). Some go further and argue
the interviews look rehearsed. That is a theory held by some investigators, not something the video proves.

There are also ordinary explanations, and they belong next to the observation. People in acute shock often
smile or laugh involuntarily; nervous laughter under stress is well documented. Many of these witnesses were
college students speaking to cameras for the first time, hours after a traumatic event, and several say in
the same clips that they were terrified and are having flashbacks. A compilation also shows only the moments
the editor chose. Readers should watch the full original broadcasts before drawing conclusions about any
individual.

What the compilation does show, reliably, is that the earliest on-camera witness accounts from UVU are
unusual enough — in content and in delivery — that they deserve to be preserved and examined rather than
forgotten. That is why they are collected in this [Witnesses](/Witnesses/overview) section.
