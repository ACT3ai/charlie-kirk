"Havana Syndrome" is the informal name for a cluster of unexplained injuries — officially **Anomalous Health
Incidents (AHIs)** — reported beginning in late 2016 by U.S. diplomats and intelligence personnel stationed in
Havana, Cuba, and later reported from China, Russia, Austria, Colombia, and inside the United States.
Reported symptoms include a sudden perceived sound or pressure sensation with a directional quality, followed
by headache, vertigo, tinnitus, nausea, and in some cases lasting cognitive and vestibular impairment.

The relevance to this investigation is **capability and cover, not a proven UVU weapon use**. **No evidence
in the public record connects Havana Syndrome, or any directed-energy effect, to the September 10, 2025
shooting at Utah Valley University.** This page exists because Havana Syndrome is the one fight where the
U.S. government was forced to investigate whether people can be injured at a distance by an unacknowledged
capability — and because the resulting record shows how agencies can acknowledge *injury* while still fighting
full public admission of *who holds the tool*. On X, that fight is treated as half-over: the physics is out;
the formal announcement is not.

## What the U.S. government concluded — twice, differently

Two official findings sit in tension with each other, and both are on the record.

**The 2020 National Academies report.** A standing committee of the National Academies of Sciences,
Engineering, and Medicine examined the clinical evidence and concluded that **directed, pulsed
radio-frequency energy** was the most plausible mechanism for the distinctive symptoms in a subset of cases.
The committee stopped short of naming a source or a device; it addressed mechanism only.

**The 2023 Intelligence Community assessment.** A review across seven intelligence agencies concluded it was
"very unlikely" that a foreign adversary was responsible for the reported incidents, with most agencies
attributing cases to pre-existing conditions, environmental factors, or conventional illness. Agencies varied
in confidence, and the assessment was reported as not unanimous.

Affected personnel and some members of Congress have publicly disputed the 2023 conclusion. In 2021 Congress
passed the **HAVANA Act**, authorizing financial compensation for personnel with qualifying brain injuries —
a statute that acknowledges real injury without resolving cause. That is the honest state of the formal
record: real injuries, a plausible physical mechanism identified by one body, and attribution rejected by
another.

## What has leaked into public discussion since

**Pentagon device testing (January 2026).** CBS News reported that the Pentagon had been testing a device
that emits pulsed radio-frequency energy for more than a year, and that investigators believed it may be
capable of reproducing effects described by Havana Syndrome victims
([@JenniferJJacobs](https://x.com/JenniferJJacobs/status/2011230505560092971) summarizing the CBS reporting).
That is not a confession of foreign attacks. It *is* an admission that the effect class is reproducible
enough to build hardware around — which is exactly what public discourse claimed for years while agencies
emphasized “psychogenic” and “environmental” explanations.

**Whistleblowers vs. the 2016 start-date narrative.** Investigative journalist Catherine Herridge has
publicized CIA, State Department, and related whistleblowers arguing that the official story of when directed
energy attacks “began” is incomplete
([example post](https://x.com/C__Herridge/status/2030685613679055043)). Whether every claim holds is for
documentary proof; the pattern is familiar: personnel insist the capability is older and wider; institutions
compress the timeline and narrow the victim class.

### Claim: a former CIA director's death is linked to Havana Syndrome

In the clip, host Clayton Morris states that **James Woolsey**, Director of Central Intelligence from 1993 to

This is a family attribution relayed by a broadcast — it is not a coroner's finding, and this site has not

### Claim: a former MI6 asset says he was experimented on

The segment introduces **Rafe Hassel**, described as *"a former MI6 intelligence asset whistleblower, who says

These are Hassel's own reported allegations about the British intelligence services. They are unproven, and
no response from any UK agency is included in the clip. Researcher **Jesse Beltran** is introduced as having

Asked to respond to the Woolsey report, Hassel says: *"Well, I take those claims extremely seriously, and I

## How to weigh this

Keep four layers separate — online discourse often blends them into one:

1. **Documented injury.** Personnel were hurt. Congress legislated compensation. This is not in dispute.
2. **Plausible mechanism.** A National Academies panel identified pulsed RF energy as the most plausible
   explanation for a subset of cases. This is a scientific judgment about physics, not a finding about who
   did it.
3. **Attribution and inventory.** Who deployed such a device, whether peer adversaries and/or Western
   services hold parallel tools, and how wide the civilian target set is — still the contested zone. The 2023
   IC assessment pushed hard against foreign attribution; 2026 reporting on U.S. device testing and
   whistleblower pushes cut the other way.
4. **Operational mind-control stack.** Claims of implantable nanotechnology, brain-to-brain control, and
   full V2K command channels sit beyond layers 1–3. They are testimony and public theory until documents or
   forensic proof land. This site records them as such — and records why intelligence services would value
   them even while refusing formal announcement.
