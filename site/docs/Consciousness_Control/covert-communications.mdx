# Covert Communications

Before reaching for exotic explanations, the ordinary ones deserve a page of their own. **Covert
communications** — the established tradecraft for passing instructions without leaving an obvious trail — is
the mechanism that has actually been used, historically and repeatedly, in operations where one party directs
another. It requires no implants, no beamed audio, and no unacknowledged physics. It requires only patience
and discipline.

The term also appears in the whistleblower testimony discussed on the [Havana Syndrome](./havana-syndrome)
page, where a former British intelligence asset alleges he was subjected to experiments involving *"implantable
nanotechnology, covert communications, and technologies that allegedly left him permanently changed."* That
usage is a claim about hardware inside a body. This page uses the term in its conventional sense: methods of
message-passing designed to be unattributable.

## The conventional toolkit

**Dead drops.** A physical location where one party leaves material for another, so the two never meet.
Documented in prosecutions of Aldrich Ames and Robert Hanssen, among many others.

**One-way voice links.** Numbers stations — shortwave broadcasts of recited digit groups readable only by a
holder of the matching one-time pad. Because reception is passive, the recipient emits no signal and cannot be
located by transmission.

**Steganography.** Concealing a message inside an innocuous carrier — an image file, an audio track, a
document's whitespace. The carrier looks and functions normally; nothing about it invites inspection.

**Burner devices and cut-outs.** Single-use phones, prepaid accounts, and intermediaries who each know only
one link in the chain. This is the most common real-world method and by far the hardest to reconstruct after
the fact.

**Ordinary encrypted platforms.** Signal, WhatsApp, Telegram, and — relevant to this case — Discord. In
practice these carry the overwhelming majority of concealed communication today. They are not covert in the
tradecraft sense; they are simply private, and private is usually enough.

## What would actually be needed to establish it here

The public record in the Charlie Kirk case contains a Discord message exchange attributed to the accused,
which prosecutors have cited and which is examined separately on the [Discord Confession
Timing](/Gov_Mind_Control/discord-confession-timing) page. It does not contain evidence of a covert channel
between the accused and any handler, foreign or domestic.

Establishing that one existed would require at least one of the following, and none has surfaced publicly:

* Device forensics showing an application, account, or protocol inconsistent with the accused's ordinary use.
* Financial records indicating payment through an untraceable channel.
* Physical evidence of a drop site or of materials passed.
* Testimony from a participant in the chain.
* Signals intelligence — which, if it exists, would sit with agencies that have not disclosed it.

The absence of these is not proof that no such channel existed. It is, however, the reason this page makes no
claim that one did. What can fairly be said is that the [FBI's handling of evidence
requests](/FBI/overview) and the [sealed material](/CoverUp/overview) in this case mean the public cannot
currently rule the question either way — which is itself an argument for the disclosure laws proposed in the
[Fix](/Fix/overview) section.

## Why this page sits in a consciousness-control section

Deliberately. When an online argument moves from "he may have been directed" to "he may have been controlled
by a device," it usually skips this page entirely. Covert communications is the far likelier mechanism for
any real direction, it is fully documented, and it has an evidentiary signature that investigators know how
to look for. An investigation that reaches for neurotechnology before exhausting tradecraft has made a
methodological error, not a discovery.
