A man filming himself on a phone at Utah Valley University on **September 10, 2025** captured the
seconds after [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk) was shot. In the clip he identifies himself by a
social-media handle — **"Outer TikTok"** — announces that shots have been fired, and repeatedly directs
viewers to follow and subscribe to his account while the crowd around him is still reacting. The clip
circulated on X in August 2026 and is included here because it is another figure whose behaviour in that
window pulled attention and camera time toward himself rather than toward the shot.

:::caution Presence and self-promotion only — no claim of foreknowledge
Nothing on this page says, suggests, or implies that this man knew the assassination would happen, was
involved in it in any way, or acted with anyone else. Filming an event you are attending, and promoting
your own channel while filming, are ordinary and lawful. He is unidentified beyond a handle, and this
site makes no finding of wrongdoing against him.
:::

## What the video shows

The footage is a single continuous vertical phone clip, roughly 52 seconds long. The filmer:

* States that there was **just a gunshot** and that shots were fired at the Charlie Kirk event in Utah.
* Repeats "**this is not a joke**" and "**this is not a drill**" several times.
* Names himself on camera as "**your boy, Outer TikTok**."
* Interleaves religious appeals — "make sure you go to church on Sunday," "read the Book of Mormon,"
  "Jesus is Lord" — with the reporting.
* Closes by asking viewers to "**subscribe to Outer TikTok on Instagram**."

## Why he is catalogued here

This section documents figures who **absorbed attention** in the minutes around the shooting — from
police, from cameras, or from the online audience that formed within hours. As the
[section overview](./overview) states, the word "distraction" here means that attention and
investigative time went toward them; it does **not** mean any of them knew anything in advance.

His clip belongs in that category for a narrow reason: it is one of the earliest pieces of on-scene
footage in which the filmer's own channel promotion, rather than the event, is the dominant content. That
matters when researchers are trying to build a clean visual record of those seconds from crowd footage —
see the [Distraction Timeline](./Distraction_Timeline) and the
[Investigation Index](./Investigation_Index).
