The [exploding-mic theory](./mic-explosion-video-analysis) does not claim an ordinary firework or a
battery fire. It claims a **high explosive** — the class of energetic material that detonates rather
than burns, propagating a supersonic shock front through itself. That distinction is the whole
argument. A deflagration pushes; a detonation shears. The wound signature proponents point to on the
[Mic vs Pendant](./Mic_vs_Pendant) and [shape charge vs bullet](/Medical/shape_charge_vs_bullet)
pages only makes sense with the second category.

## The three compounds that keep appearing

Three names recur across the citizen-investigator threads, and they are the same three that
forensic laboratories treat as the standard post-blast panel:

* **RDX** (cyclotrimethylenetrinitramine) — the base of C-4 and of most military demolition
  charges. High detonation velocity, mouldable when plasticised, and the compound most often
  recovered in published post-blast fabric studies.
* **PETN** (pentaerythritol tetranitrate) — the explosive in detonating cord and in many
  miniature initiators. [Candace Owens](/People/candace-owens) has raised PETN by name in her
  coverage of what the federal search of Kirk's effects may have been looking for; that framing is
  summarised on the [Removing Explosive Residue Cover-Up](./Explosive_Residue_Coverup/overview) hub.
* **TNT-related compounds** — trinitrotoluene and its degradation and manufacturing byproducts,
  which persist as chemical markers even when the parent charge is fully consumed.

None of these is exotic. All three are routinely detected by the laboratory methods described on the
[Explosive Residue](./explosive-residue) page, and all three are in the standing reference library of
any federal forensic lab.

## Why the size argument matters here

The recurring objection to the mic theory is scale: a lavalier transmitter is small. Proponents
answer that high explosives are **energy-dense enough that "small" is not disqualifying** — a
few grams of RDX or PETN in a shaped geometry directs almost all of its output along one axis, which
is the mechanism demonstrated on the [Demonstration of a Shaped Charge](./Demo_of_shape_charge) page
and argued in detail on the [Exploding Pendant](./exploding-pendant-shaped-charge) page. Skeptics on
those same pages argue the opposite: that a real shaped charge produces a smoke and flash signature
absent from the UVU footage. Both arguments are documented on this site; neither is settled here.

The procurement thread runs in parallel. A paralegal who says she has handled DoD procurements
flagged an [April 2025 Accurate Energetic Systems contract](./DoD_Contract) described as covering
**miniaturized anti-personnel demolition charges**, and the company profile and subsequent
[factory explosion](./aes-explosion-factory) are documented on their own pages. That contract is a
public record. It establishes that miniature anti-personnel charges are a procured item — nothing
more. **It does not establish that any such device was present at Utah Valley University.**

## What a high explosive leaves behind

This is where the theory becomes testable rather than rhetorical. A detonation of RDX, PETN or a
TNT-class compound never consumes itself completely. Unreacted particulate and characteristic
byproducts deposit on every nearby surface — fabric, skin, upholstery, soil, and any object carried
on the body. In the frame at right, several people are within arm's length of Kirk's chest; if a
charge functioned there, their clothing is a substrate.

According to [@jonaaronbray](https://x.com/jonaaronbray/status/2083042911617200350) on X, July 31,
2026, quoting the forensic literature:

> "Residues of military/high explosives (e.g., RDX, PETN, TNT-related compounds) have been detected
> on fabric after detonations or simulated post-blast conditions. One HPLC study recovered RDX at low
> concentrations (around 3–4 μg/g) from nylon and cotton cloth exposed in post-blast scenarios;
> recovery varied strongly by fabric type (better from nylon than cotton; none from denim in that
> work)."

The recovery numbers, the fabric-by-fabric differences, and the laboratory methods are broken out on
the [Explosive Residue](./explosive-residue) page. The short version: a high explosive is one of the
easiest things in forensic chemistry to find, if anyone swabs for it.

## What is not established

- **No explosive residue result has been publicly released** for any item connected to September 10,
  2025 — positive or negative, for RDX, PETN, TNT compounds, or anything else.
- **No agency has stated that a high explosive was present** at the event. The official finding
  remains a rifle gunshot wound.
- **The AES contract is a procurement record, not evidence of a device at UVU.** Nothing on this page
  alleges wrongdoing by Accurate Energetic Systems, by any federal employee, by any member of the
  security detail, by [TPUSA](/TPUSA/overview), or by any person visible in the photograph above.
- **The compound list is a forensic panel, not a finding.** RDX, PETN and TNT appear here because
  they are what a laboratory would test for — not because any of them has been detected.
