# Security Team Clothing Seized

This page documents questions and allegations raised in public commentary — not findings of fact. [Candace Owens](/Influencers/podcasts-candace-owens) reported on July 30, 2026 that,
according to information passed to her, **the FBI took the clothing of certain members
of [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk)'s security detail — down to their underwear** in the
hours after the September 10, 2025 shooting at Utah Valley University. She named no
individual as the source and stated plainly that she does not know what happened to the
items afterward. This page records that attributed account and the questions it raises.
It does not assert that any agent destroyed evidence.

## The Reported Account

Owens framed the claim against a detail that had already been public for months: that
**[Frank Turek](/People/frank-turek)** and others from the detail ended up in **hospital
scrubs**. Her question was why a person would surrender everything they were wearing at a
hospital unless they were covered in blood — and, if so, how that squares with the
conflicting accounts of who performed CPR in the transport vehicle. In her telling, the
clothing seizure supplies the missing explanation for the scrubs.

Her own words on the podcast: *"I did hear that the feds, the FBI took the clothes from
certain members of Charlie's security team down to their boxer briefs, took their clothes.
Down to their boxer briefs, the FBI took their clothing."*

The account was amplified by **[@ProjectConstitu](/Influencers/podcasts-project-constitution)**
on X on July 30, 2026.

## Why This Is an FBI Thread

Seizing the clothing of everyone who handled a shooting victim is, on its own,
**textbook evidence collection**. Blood spatter pattern, transfer, fiber, and residue all
live on fabric, and a competent investigator bags it. Read in isolation, this item is
evidence of the FBI doing its job.

The reason it is on this site at all is the pairing Owens drew: the same federal posture
that reportedly collected the detail's clothing is the posture that, per separate reported
claims, had the [transport SUV towed, cleaned and prepared for resale](/CoverUp/SUV_Destroyed).
Her argument is one of internal inconsistency — an agency that preserves boxer briefs does
not release the vehicle. Either the vehicle disposal claim is wrong, or the clothing was
not collected for the reason collection is normally done. That is an argument, not a
finding, and it depends entirely on the vehicle claim holding up.

## The Residue Question

Investigators who favor a device-based mechanism over a rifle round (see
[Mic & Explosive claims](/CoverUp/Mic_Explosive) and the
[Cause of Death](/Suspicious/Cause_of_Death/overview) section) note that clothing worn in
close contact with a victim is exactly the substrate on which **explosive residue** would
be recoverable, and that synthetic fibers hold such residue well. If that theory is right,
the seized garments are among the most testable objects in the case. If the conventional
account is right, they are ordinary blood-evidence exhibits.

Either way the same disclosure settles it: an itemized property receipt and a lab report.
Neither is public.

## What Would Settle It

- The **FD-597 property receipts** (the FBI's receipt-for-property form) issued to each
  member of the detail, if any were issued.
- The **evidence inventory** showing whether the garments were logged, and under what case
  number.
- Any **laboratory examination request** and result for those garments — serology, DNA,
  fiber, or residue.
- Whether the items are still in custody, were returned, or were destroyed.

Owens noted that the party with standing to compel that disclosure is
**[Tyler Robinson](/People/tyler-robinson)'s defense team**, through discovery. See
[Discovery & Access Delays](./Discovery_Delays).

## Counterargument

The straightforward reading is that this is normal practice and proves nothing. Clothing
worn by people who carried a gunshot victim is evidence; hospitals hand out scrubs to
people whose clothes have been taken or ruined; nothing about scrubs requires a sinister
explanation. The account is also **secondhand and unnamed** — Owens did not identify who
told her, and no member of the detail has confirmed it on the record. Turek's name was
explicitly *not* attached to the claim by Owens herself.

## Open Questions

1. Which members of the security detail had clothing taken, and by which agency?
2. Was the clothing taken at Timpanogos Regional Hospital, at UVU, or elsewhere?
3. Was a property receipt issued to each person?
4. Has any of that clothing been examined, and by which laboratory?
5. Does the defense's discovery inventory list these items?

## Laws (Charlie Kirk)

* Property receipts and evidence inventories for clothing taken from the security detail, and any laboratory examination of those garments, are things that the [Charlie Kirk Investigation Laws](/Fix/overview) may result in powerful truths coming out that aren't out yet.
