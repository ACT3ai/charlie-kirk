On **August 2, 2026**, two X accounts reported the same thing within minutes of each other: an attempt
to publish a **poll** asking whether people still believe the initial [Charlie Kirk](/People/charlie-kirk)
shooting narrative would not go through as a poll. This page records what they said, what the screenshot
shows, and what it does and does not establish.

## The post

According to [@SwordTruth](https://x.com/SwordTruth/status/2083966318534180990), an account that
describes itself as an "Investigative America First Journalist twice de-monetized by X":

> X will not allow this to be created as a poll as they protect the fedslop narrative on the Charlie
> Kirk murder. Try it for yourself it just posts and does not make a poll

Four minutes earlier the same account had posted the poll text itself — which, per the account's own
description, went out as **plain text with no poll attached**:

> 🗳️: Given where we are now, do you still believe the Charlie Kirk 30.06 narrative and what was
> stated initially? Or do you believe there's more to it? Maybe..you dont know what to think? Please
> share and vote. Thank you.
> (Feel free to leave YOUR personal ideas in the comments.) 🇺🇸 @QueenOfRespawn poll

The **"30.06"** reference is to the **.30-06 cartridge** named in the initial official account of the
shooting — see [Gun & Bullet](/Gun_Bullet/overview) for the ballistics record and the disputes around it.
The poll was asking users whether they still accept that rifle account of how Charlie Kirk was killed, at a point when the majority view among investigators on X had already shifted toward the [explosive device at the microphone](/Tyler_Robinson_Not_Assassin/explosive-mic-killed-charlie).

## What the screenshot shows

The attached image is a screenshot of a post by **@QueenOfRespawn** ("Savage"), who reportedly wrote
the poll first:

> Theyre blocking my poll about Charlie. Just says failed to send and goes to drafts.

Nested inside that post is a phone screenshot of the **X poll composer**: the audience selector set to
"Everyone", an **Update** button, the full poll question typed into the body, and the visible
answer-choice fields reading **"I believe it."** and **"Unsure."** The composer is populated and
apparently ready to send.

So the two accounts describe **two different failure modes** for the same poll:

| Account | Reported behavior |
|---------|-------------------|
| @QueenOfRespawn | Poll "failed to send" and was moved to **drafts** |
| @SwordTruth | Poll posted, but **as plain text** — no poll attached |

## What this does and does not establish

This is a **user-reported product failure**, and it should be read that way.

**What is on the record:** two accounts, on the same day, reporting that a poll about the Charlie Kirk
narrative would not publish as a poll, with a screenshot of the populated composer.

**What is not on the record:** no platform log, no error code, no moderation notice, and no statement
from X. Neither account published a control test — for example, the same poll wording with "Charlie
Kirk" removed — which is the check that would separate topic-based filtering from a generic composer
fault. An ordinary poll-composer bug, a client-version problem, or an account-level posting restriction
would look identical from the user's side. @SwordTruth's characterization that X is acting to "protect
the fedslop narrative" is that poster's stated view of motive, not an established fact, and this site
does not assert it as one.

**Why it is logged anyway:** it fits a pattern this section already tracks. Reports of reduced reach on
Kirk threads ([X — Deboosting Investigators](./X_Deboosting_Investigators)) and of account actions tied
to engagement with [Candace Owens](/Influencers/podcasts-candace-owens) Kirk posts
([X — Candace Engagement Bans](./X_Candace_Engagement_Bans)) are also user-reported rather than
platform-documented. Individually each is weak evidence. The value is in whether the reports accumulate
and whether anyone runs the control test.

## The open question

The claim is unusually **easy to test**, and @SwordTruth invited exactly that — "Try it for yourself."
Anyone with an X account can attempt a poll with this wording, then attempt the same poll with the
subject changed, and record whether one publishes and the other does not. Until someone posts that
side-by-side result, the episode remains **two people reporting a failure**, not a demonstrated
suppression of polling on a topic. Readers who run the test should archive screenshots and timestamps —
the same method investigators use elsewhere in this section, alongside
[IPFS pinning](/Your_Actions_Fix_It/overview) of anything that might disappear.
